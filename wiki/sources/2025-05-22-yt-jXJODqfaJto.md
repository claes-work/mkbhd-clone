# Why Apple, Samsung and Google Need Each Other (@mkbhd, 2025-05-22)

## Summary
An essay on the **money behind the tech rivalries** — "companies that hate each other but need each other, where the
economics more than outweigh the public rivalry." Three relationships: **(1) Apple + Samsung** — **Samsung makes the
iPhone's high-end OLED displays** (the only supplier that can hit Apple's quality at scale), and it's so lucrative that
**the iPhone X made Samsung more money than their own Galaxy S8** (~180–200M panels). Samsung *could* refuse to sell
and trigger an Apple supply crisis — but the money they'd lose dwarfs the extra phones they'd sell, so "money talks"
(the risk to Samsung is Apple eventually switching to micro-LED, "the same way Apple ditching Intel hurt Intel"). **(2)
Apple + Google** — the **power of defaults**: **Google pays Apple ~$20 billion a year to stay the default search in
Safari** (most people never change defaults), a win-win now threatened by the **DOJ antitrust trial**. Three scenarios
if the payment stops: nothing changes (you probably meant to Google it); it jumps to ChatGPT (maybe also illegal); or —
the interesting one — **Apple builds its own search engine** (the "code red" that made Google strike the deal in the
first place, because Apple devices are **>half of Google's search business**). He notes he now uses **ChatGPT/Gemini
over Google search "kind of every single time"** for knowledge/in-depth queries. **(3) Google + Samsung** — a weird
tell: **Google demos new Android features on *Samsung* phones at Google's own events** (Gemini Live on the S24/S25
Ultra, minutes before unveiling a Pixel); **Circle to Search debuted on Samsung**; and court docs show **Google pays
Samsung "enormous sums" to pre-install Gemini.** The mutual dependence: **Samsung needs Google (Android is the
backbone), and Google needs Samsung** (Samsung has ~5× the Pixel's US share — "the conduit for distributing Google
services to the world"). "All worth keeping an eye on in 2025."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2025-05-22] **Samsung makes the iPhone's OLEDs — the economics outweigh the rivalry.** "'Most people don't realize
  Samsung makes the high-end OLED displays for the iPhone — the one company that can supply that quality at scale. The
  iPhone X made Samsung more money than their own Galaxy S8 (~180–200M panels). They could refuse and trigger an Apple
  supply crisis, but the money they'd lose dwarfs the extra Samsung phones they'd sell. Money talks — the risk is Apple
  eventually going micro-LED, like they ditched Intel.'"
- [2025-05-22] **Google pays Apple ~$20B/year for default search — the power of defaults.** "'Google pays Apple roughly
  $20 billion every year to stay the default search in Safari, because most people never change defaults. It's a
  no-brainer for both — free money for Apple, secured #1 for Google — and $20B off the top of each company's ~$100B
  profit. Now the DOJ trial argues it's anti-competitive.'"
- [2025-05-22] **If the payment stops: nothing, ChatGPT, or Apple's own search (the "code red").** "'Three things could
  happen: nothing changes (you probably meant to Google it); it jumps to ChatGPT (maybe also illegal); or — the
  interesting one — Apple builds its own search engine. That's the "code red" that made Google strike this deal in the
  first place, because Apple devices are over half of Google's search business. Meanwhile I now use ChatGPT and Gemini
  over Google search kind of every single time for in-depth stuff.'"
- [2025-05-22] **Google demos Android on Samsung phones — and pays Samsung to pre-install Gemini.** "'Google keeps
  demoing new Android features on Samsung phones at their own events — Gemini Live on an S24/S25 Ultra, minutes before
  unveiling a Pixel. Circle to Search debuted on Samsung. And court docs show Google pays Samsung enormous sums to
  pre-install Gemini.'"
- [2025-05-22] **Samsung needs Google; Google needs Samsung.** "'Samsung needs Google — Android is the backbone, the
  Play Store, the services. But Google needs Samsung more than people realize: Samsung has ~5× the Pixel's US market
  share and is how most of the world gets Android in their pocket — the conduit for distributing Google services
  globally.'"
