# The iPad's Odd New Feature (@mkbhd, 2022-07-28)

## Summary
A commentary built around a theory about **iPadOS 16's Stage Manager**: **Apple never seems to want the iPad to do
anything the same way the Mac does — it always finds "an iPad way."** The setup: the iPad hardware has raced ahead
(a $1,000 iPad Pro with the same M1 as the Macs), but **the software has stayed "remarkably consistent" (a nice way
of saying it hasn't kept pace)** — the biggest knock for years. Stage Manager is the last big multitasking hurdle
toward "a real computer" — and it's the first windowed, simultaneous multitasking (finally extending to an external
display) — **but it's M1-only, ~"the fifth different way to multitask," and not the most intuitive.** He invokes the
**Craig Federighi calculator/weather-app quote** ("we'd want to do something distinctly great; we just haven't
gotten around to it") and Apple's own headline — **"incredibly capable, unmistakably iPad"** — to argue Apple is
walking a tightrope between powerful and over-complicated. Ends still confused about **"the what-is-a-computer
question."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-07-28] **The theory — Apple never wants the iPad to work like the Mac.** "'The more I use Stage Manager, the
  more I crystallize this theory: **Apple doesn't seem to want the iPad to do anything the same way the Mac does —
  they always find a new "iPad way."**' The iPad started as a giant iPhone and got powerful (a 13" mini-LED, an M1)
  — **but the software stayed remarkably consistent (a nice way of saying it hasn't advanced at the same rate as
  the hardware)**, which is the biggest knock on it (people want it to be their only, fully-capable computer). Even
  trackpad support became the unique floating circle pointer — "very much the iPad way."'"
- [2022-07-28] **The Federighi quote.** "'When I asked Craig Federighi why there's no default weather/calculator app,
  he said **"we'd want to do something really distinctly great; it's easy to make a calculator, but one that feels
  like the greatest iPad calculator — we honestly just haven't gotten around to doing it great."** Two years later
  it sounds even more like "we haven't gotten around to doing it the iPad way yet." Apple's own headline:
  **"incredibly capable, unmistakably iPad."**'"
- [2022-07-28] **Stage Manager — the first windowed multitasking, but complicated.** "'Multitasking is the last big
  hurdle to feeling like a real computer — and in line with the theory, **Apple won't let it look or feel like the
  Mac; it needs an iPad way.** Stage Manager: an on-stage windowed app (a set of predetermined sizes, not fully
  fluid) with off-stage recents; **the first time you can have more than one windowed app open at once** (up to 4),
  and **finally an external display extends instead of mirroring.**'"
- [2022-07-28] **The downsides — M1-only and unintuitive.** "'It's not perfect: **M1-only (it uses memory swaps)**;
  and it's **~the fifth different way to multitask** (split-screen, slide-over with its own carousel, etc.), buried
  behind obscure gestures and off by default. Opening an app already in a group opens the whole group; apps don't
  auto-maximize; iPhone-size apps (Instagram) don't resize; you can't drag a window straight to another display;
  and it won't drive an external display with its own screen off (no clamshell mode).'"
- [2022-07-28] **The tightrope + the what-is-a-computer question.** "'The state of the iPad is fascinating: amazing
  hardware, features to use it — but **in the effort to do it the iPad way every time, they're walking a tightrope
  between intuitive/powerful and too-complicated/hard-to-remember, landing right down the middle.** I'll continue
  to be confused about the what-is-a-computer question.'"
