# OnePlus 15 Review: This is Not Normal!

## Summary

A high-praise-with-one-big-caveat review. The OnePlus 15 (they skipped 14 — "four is super
unlucky") could have just been a chip-refresh of the already-liked OnePlus 13, but instead
OnePlus "sprinkled in a whole bunch of overkill" — the runaway theme being **"this is not
normal."** New Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5, a ceramic-coated matte-black finish that "does the
damage" rather than takes it, IP69K, a 7,300mAh silicon-carbon battery, custom Wi-Fi/touch
chips, 165Hz. The single big letdown, as always with OnePlus: **the cameras are a substantial
downgrade**, and the Hasselblad partnership appears over.

## Key claims

- **[2025-11-13]** "I'm not going to bury the lead. This phone is so good." A chip-refresh would
  have been fine (the **Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5** is "already goated," 20–40% faster, 4.6GHz),
  but OnePlus added "overkill... stuff even the most insane enthusiasts would barely notice — the
  type of stuff people who watch an MKBHD video of a phone they're not even going to buy might
  appreciate." **"This is not normal."** (quote)
- **[2025-11-13]** The **matte-black finish** uses **micro-arc oxidation** (a ceramic coating,
  ~8–9 on the Mohs scale) — "one of the most impressive matte blacks I've ever seen," doesn't
  fingerprint, and the "scratches" that appeared were **other objects' material rubbing off onto
  it**: "this phone isn't taking the damage, it's doing the damage. That's not normal. Call an
  ambulance, but not for me." (quote)
- **[2025-11-13]** Certified **IP66/68/69/69K** — survives high-pressure 80°C water jets, "could
  literally survive a laundry cycle" — funny given OnePlus's history of skipping IP certification
  to save money. (paraphrase)
- **[2025-11-13]** **7,300mAh silicon-carbon** battery (up from the 13's already-praised 6,000mAh)
  while slightly *thinner* — "twice as much battery as some phones a few years ago." He gets **7–8h
  screen-on easily**, sometimes ending a day at ~50%. 80W wired in the US (100W elsewhere; brick in
  box), 50W wireless. (paraphrase) He reiterates (from the Waveform podcast) that **he doesn't do
  battery benchmarks** because "so few of them actually represent real-world usage." (quote)
- **[2025-11-13]** Custom silicon: a new **G2 Wi-Fi chip** (their answer to Apple's C1/C1X) and a
  dedicated **touch-response chip** enabling a **3,200Hz touch sample rate** and a **165Hz display —
  but only for gaming** (it's 1–120Hz otherwise). He's "the type that's just happy to have a higher
  refresh rate and high resolution on at the same time," not chasing 165 vs 120. (paraphrase)
- **[2025-11-13]** Small downsides: slightly **lower resolution** than the 13 (1272 vs 1440p — "you
  probably won't notice"); **the alert slider is gone again** (replaced by a customizable button —
  "I really like being able to know the state without turning on the screen"); a paper haptic-motor
  downgrade you won't notice. (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-11-13]** **The big one — cameras.** "This has been the story of OnePlus phones forever...
  they always botch the cameras," and this is **"a pretty substantial downgrade"** — smaller sensors/
  apertures, the primary "the same one that's in the $400 Honor 200," dull colors, too much noise.
  **The Hasselblad partnership appears over** (no logo, no orange shutter button, no XPAN mode).
  (quote)
- **[2025-11-13]** Verdict: he loves it for the same reason he loves OnePlus phones — "so fast and
  smooth," confidence-inspiring, all-day battery, customizable (OxygenOS has "devolved into a Color
  OS clone, which is also an iOS clone," but stayed buttery). But **"they have a longer way to go to
  get me to daily a phone like this if the camera is going to be lagging so hard."** Competitively
  priced at **$899** ($999 for 16GB). "Can you guys make an ultra version with a really nice camera,
  please?" (quote)
