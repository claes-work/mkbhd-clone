# So This is Peak Foldable ★★

## Summary

Marques declares the **Oppo Find N6** "peak foldable" — the point where his long-standing test
is finally met: **a foldable makes sense for regular people only when the only difference from a
normal phone is that it folds, with no other compromises.** He walks the seven-year list of
foldable compromises (thickness, bezels, cameras, battery, durability, the crease) and shows the
N6 has engineered each away — normal slab-phone dimensions, a 6,000mAh silicon-carbon battery, a
flagship chip and cameras, IP59, and a genuinely convincing **"No-Feel Crease."** He closes on
the obvious next move: **the folding iPhone**, and Apple's sit-on-the-sidelines-then-Apple-ify
playbook. A landmark for the foldable arc. ★★.

## Key claims

- **[2026-03-30]** The standing thesis, now met: **"foldables are for early adopters only… they
  don't make sense for regular people until the only difference between a foldable and a regular
  phone is the fact that one of them happens to fold in half, and there's no other compromises."**
  Seven years of compromises (Fold 1's bezels, subpar cameras/battery, durability, the crease) have
  been "engineered away" one by one. (quote)
- **[2026-03-30]** **No-compromise dimensions**: closed, the N6 is **just under 9mm and ~230g —
  effectively identical to the iPhone 17 Pro Max** — so "you'd hardly know it's not a normal 6.6"
  slab phone," and you actually *want* to use it closed. Flagship cover specs (3,600 nits peak, 1–
  120Hz LTPO). (paraphrase)
- **[2026-03-30]** The old physics problems, solved by 2026 tech: a **6,000mAh silicon-carbon battery
  split in half** ("more than a lot of slab phones") + 80W/50W charging; the **S25 Edge's shallow-
  Z-axis 200MP ISOCELL HP5** main sensor + 50MP ultrawide + 50MP 3x; a **Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5**
  (binned, one less core) — "for all intents a flagship chip"; and **IP59.** (paraphrase)
- **[2026-03-30]** **The "No-Feel Crease" is the pièce de résistance.** He was skeptical (the
  marketing videos were sponsored), but in hand it's "actually super impressive — basically no
  crease." The engineering: they **laser-scan each individual titanium hinge, 3D-print a liquid
  polymer to fill the micro-gaps, then UV-harden it**, cutting surface variation **from 0.2mm to
  0.05mm (less than half a human-hair thickness)** — plus thicker top glass. Rated for **600,000
  folds** without a significant crease. On the crease generally: "it's like bezels — most of the
  time you don't see it, but every once in a while it catches the light and it just hits." (paraphrase
  + quote)
- **[2026-03-30]** Cherry on top: **pen support** (unlike Samsung's Fold) via a snap-on case that
  wireless-charges the pen, working on both screens with 4,000+ pressure levels. (paraphrase)
- **[2026-03-30]** Verdict: **"the Oppo Find N6 is complete… aside from the price tag, this is
  effectively a regular phone that just happens to fold in half. Peak foldable — we've arrived."**
  (quote)
- **[2026-03-30]** The elephant in the room: **the folding iPhone** (rumored for late 2026). "That's
  what Apple does with emerging tech — sit on the sidelines while the daring companies work out the
  compromises, then jump in with an **Apple-ified** version" (**Vision Pro** into VR, **HomePod** into
  smart speakers, the original iPhone into phones). Apple's usual cheat code — "make the version that
  works well with the iPhone" — is odd here since it *is* an iPhone, so **what's the trick?** (paraphrase)
- **[2026-03-30]** The rumor he flags: the **iPhone Fold plays with aspect ratio** — a short,
  **passport-style ~5.5" closed** display opening to an **~8" widescreen** ("a portable iPad mini"),
  vs the square that most modern folds open to. "We'll see how much of the no-feel crease / super-thin
  / silicon-carbon / flagship-camera stuff makes it in." (paraphrase)
