# Blue Bubbles vs Green Bubbles: Explained! (@mkbhd, 2022-01-24)

## Summary
A landmark explainer on **iMessage lock-in** — "one of the biggest walls in Apple's walled garden." Traces the
messaging history (SMS 1992 → MMS 2002 → iMessage 2011 → RCS 2018) and shows why the US blue/green divide exists:
**most people use the default messaging app**, and **~70% of US 18–24-year-olds carry an iPhone**, so iMessage
becomes the social default and Android users get the degraded green-bubble experience (no encryption, compressed
"dumpster fire" videos, broken group chats). The durable point: **software can be a wall too** — and Apple knows
it (the Epic-case testimony that "iMessage on Android would hurt us more than help us"). He weighs the alternatives
(a single multi-platform app — but should Meta hold all the world's messaging? and defaults never move) and lands
on **fragmentation being both an upside and a downside**, with a simple ask: **Apple should add RCS to iMessage —
there's no downside; every day they don't, they signal they value the lock-in.**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-01-24] **Software is a wall — and iMessage is a big one.** "'We knew about hardware walls (Apple Watch,
  AirPods, AirTags only-really-work-with-iPhone) — but **software can be those walls too, and iMessage is a really
  big wall.** Switching to Android means not just losing features and becoming a green bubble, but that Apple built
  such a social network of iPhone-only features that **Android users feel genuinely ostracized** — a
  self-fulfilling prophecy that pressures people onto the iPhone.'"
- [2022-01-24] **It's driven by defaults + iPhone dominance.** "'Most people just use the default messaging app
  (also the default browser and email). And **~70% of US 18–24-year-olds carry an iPhone.** So iMessage becomes
  the default, it's good for groups and social dynamics, and everyone else feels left out. The green-bubble
  experience on an iPhone is trash (SMS/MMS fallback: no encryption, "Marques liked your text," a 1080p video
  compressed to ~100 px) — **and Apple is fine with this.**'"
- [2022-01-24] **Apple knows the value of the lock-in.** "'From the Epic v. Apple documents: Eddy Cue testified
  Apple could have made iMessage for Android, but **Craig Federighi and Phil Schiller noted it would "remove an
  obstacle to iPhone families giving their kids Android phones" and "moving iMessage to Android will hurt us more
  than help us."** So it's crystal clear the higher-ups recognize the value of iMessage lock-in — it's not going
  anywhere.'"
- [2022-01-24] **The multi-platform problem — and who should hold it.** "'A single multi-platform app sounds great,
  but two problems: **(1) people overwhelmingly stick to the default (try getting your parents — or a whole social
  circle — to switch); (2) should the entire planet's messaging be in one company's hands — and should that
  company be Meta?**' (WhatsApp = 2B users; when Meta went down for 6 hours, Telegram gained 70M.) Google's tried
  and failed many times.'"
- [2022-01-24] **Fragmentation is an upside and a downside — and "Apple should add RCS."** "'Messaging
  fragmentation is **both an upside and a downside — a downside because we're not all on one thing, a good thing
  because there's still competition.** My take: **Apple should add RCS support to iMessage — there's no downside**
  (bigger files, reactions, real group chats, and crucially end-to-end encryption for DMs, which a
  privacy-preaching company should want over unencrypted SMS). They won't, because they move slowly — but
  **every day Apple doesn't add RCS, they signal they value the lock-in just a little more.**'"
