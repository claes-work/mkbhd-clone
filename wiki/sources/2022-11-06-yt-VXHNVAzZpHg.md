# The Electrification of Supercars! (Ferrari 296GTB)

## Summary

**The origin of his "electrification of the sports car" thread.** Marques uses the **Ferrari 296 GTB**
(the first V6-badged Ferrari — a 3.0L V6 + e-motor, ~830 hp) to explain the choice every sports-car
maker now faces: how to keep the loud-engine/gear-shifting feel enthusiasts love while adding electric
instant torque. His framing recurs across the corpus (the Artura, the E-Ray, the 911 GTS T-Hybrid).
And a key sub-point: **the quietly-leaving-your-neighborhood benefit may matter as much as the
performance gains.** Fully Marques-narrated.

## Key claims

- **[2022-11-06]** ⭐ THE THESIS: "For 100 years the best performance cars were big gas engines in a
  light package. Then EVs came out of nowhere and smoked everything in a straight line — but enthusiasts
  weren't swayed; they still love the exhaust, the shifting gears, the noise (the literal definition of
  inefficiency, but it's fun). **So sports-car makers have a choice: how do we give people the instant
  electric torque AND still give them the loud engine and shifting they love?**" The 296 is "the perfect
  example." (quote)
- **[2022-11-06]** The company map: "McLaren's doing it with the Artura, Acura with the NSX, the Porsche
  918, a hybrid 911 is coming, hybrid Lamborghinis soon — this is now the Ferrari way." The 296: 3.0L V6
  (rear) + an e-motor (~150 hp) between the rear wheels, ~830–900 hp rear-wheel-drive, ~15 mi EV range. (paraphrase)
- **[2022-11-06]** ⭐ The sub-point: **"The electric-drive-leaving-your-house-without-disturbing-your-
  neighbors part is actually just as important as the performance benefits (filling the torque gaps
  while the turbos spool). But that's just me."** (E-drive mode: silent, up to ~80 mph.) (quote)
- **[2022-11-06]** ⚠️ The gripe (his consistent one): **"tons of haptic buttons — the ignition, the drive
  modes, the menus. A baffling choice. Cars should have physical switches for anything you do more than a
  few times while driving, especially climate."** (quote)
- **[2022-11-06]** ⭐ The boundary: **"Electric muscle cars (big, heavy, batteries-are-heavy) — that's
  been great for electric. But sports cars are typically super-lightweight and dance all over a track,
  so it's a more delicate balance. This is why I keep looking at the Model S Plaid — incredible in a
  straight line, but I don't see it as a track car; it's a 4-door sedan that happens to be incredibly
  powerful. The 296 balanced the added weight well — a well-done hybrid, and I'm a big fan."** (quote)
