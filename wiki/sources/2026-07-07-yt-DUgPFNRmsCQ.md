# Nothing Phone 4b: They Can't Say It!

## Summary

A budget-phone review of the **Nothing Phone 4b** (€330, not in the US) whose running joke is
that **Nothing won't just call it a budget phone** — Marques reads out the marketing-speak
non-answer to "what does the B stand for?" and concludes **"B safely stands for budget."** It
validates his prior theory (Nothing can't make a competitive flagship right now) and adds a new
one: this was likely meant to be a **CMF Phone 3 Pro**, bumped up a tier because the **memory-
price spike** pushed the bill of materials too high. His verdict: a genuinely good taste of the
Nothing experience at the price — "the 4b" as "a bold and bare brilliant bargain."

## Key claims

- **[2026-07-07]** The bit: Nothing's head of marketing gives "a lot of marketing words to not tell us
  what the B stands for," so Marques answers it himself — **"B, I think, safely stands for budget."**
  "I don't know why Nothing has this allergy to just calling this a cheaper budget phone." (paraphrase
  + quote)
- **[2026-07-07]** It **validates his prior theory** (the 2026-03 Nothing video): "they really aren't
  able to make a high-end competitive flagship phone — their costs are just too high at their volume —
  so they just don't. And they still don't." (quote)
- **[2026-07-07]** **New theory: the 4b was supposed to be a CMF Phone 3 Pro**, but **"this insane
  memory price spike we're in now pushes the bill of materials even higher,"** which would have made it
  too expensive for the sub-brand — so they converted it to a slightly-pricier, slightly-more-premium
  **Nothing** phone. (Tells: it would've had "a useless third camera because *Pro*" and a MediaTek
  chip.) (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-07-07]** The core framing — a cheap Nothing phone is **"a balancing act of including premium
  fundamentals but finding ways to save money in ways you either won't notice or won't mind."** The
  display (6.77" 120Hz LTPS OLED, high-freq PWM dimming) is "a microcosm" — good fundamentals, but
  uneven bezels, a worse anti-glare finish, and a slow optical fingerprint reader. (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-07-07]** Cameras: a **dual 50MP main (OIS) + 8MP ultrawide** — "the correct choice for two
  cameras" (no fake third lens); acceptable in good light, dull/noisy when it's gone. "If you really
  want much more camera, you end up with a Pixel 10a, just not a Nothing Phone." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-07-07]** Performance: a **Snapdragon 6 Gen 4** (mid/low-end — "benchmarks around the OnePlus
  7 Pro, a flagship seven years ago… close to the Tensor G2, which says more about Tensor"), but **the
  software makes it feel like it overperforms the chip** ("Nothing software is one of my favorite skins
  in the Android world" — a custom CPU scheduler, a 1000Hz touch sample rate); it still stutters
  sometimes. All the Nothing software/AI-toolkit/Glyph features trickle down "all the way to the
  B-series." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-07-07]** His buying framework: **"a list of perks that are really nice for this price, and
  some shortcomings they had to use to get to this price. If the perks interest you and none of the
  shortcomings are dealbreakers, then it fits — put it at the top of your list."** Perks: the OLED,
  IP64, **true stereo speakers** ("not something you can say about every $900 iPhone"), and the
  **biggest battery ever in a Nothing Phone** (5,200mAh / 6,000 in India — 1.5–2 days). Shortcomings:
  no wireless charging, **no NFC in the India version** ("surprising"), a mushy vibration motor, a
  plastic build that scuffs, and slower UFS 2.2 → "won't age amazingly in 5–6 years." (paraphrase +
  quote)
- **[2026-07-07]** Verdict: "a pretty good set of features for this price and a good taste of the
  Nothing experience. **Hey Nothing, embrace it — it's okay to have a lineup that's cheap, cheaper,
  cheapest**… it's the Nothing Phone 4b." (a "bold and bare brilliant bargain.") (quote)

## Notable verbatim quotes
