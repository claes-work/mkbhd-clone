# This Phone is $169 - What's the Catch? (@mkbhd, 2023-01-12)

## Summary
A review of the **$169 Moto G Play (2023)** framed as a hunt for "the catch," anchored to his recurring line:
**"good phones are getting cheap, and cheap phones are getting good."** Category by category, the phone
holds up better than its price: the **big 6.5" 90 Hz LCD** (only 720p, dim, but "plenty of screen") isn't
the catch; the **plastic build** (water-resistant IP52, rear fingerprint) isn't; the **battery is genuinely
excellent** (5,000 mAh, day-and-a-half easy); the **software is near-stock Android with Moto's perfected
gestures** — "virtually the same experience as their $1,000 Edge"; the camera is merely "functional" (with a
hilariously bad portrait cutout — "the zigzag preschool-scissors method"). **The catch is that it's slow —
really slow** (MediaTek Helio G37, 3 GB RAM) — "it might be the slowest phone I've ever used." That triggers
the video's real question: **new cheap phone vs. old formerly-flagship?** Using a **OnePlus 7 Pro** (~$200
used) as the foil, the old flagship wins almost everything (chip, OLED, cameras, build). Two feathers for
the new phone: **longer future software updates** (though the advantage evaporates once a flagship is ~3+
years old) and — but this cuts the *other* way — **the environment favors buying and keeping an older phone
(less e-waste).** His rule: **"the lower the price of the phone, the more I would actually want to buy the
older [flagship] phone."** At $169 → old flagship; ~$400 → debatable; ~$600 → a brand-new Pixel 7 is great.
Aside: **don't skip software updates — the security patches matter.**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-01-12] **Good phones getting cheap, cheap phones getting good.** "'I've said it before and I'll say
  it again: good phones are getting cheap, and cheap phones are getting good. This video is about the second
  half — how do you end up at a phone that's a 10th the price of the ones I've been enjoying?'"
- [2023-01-12] **The catch is speed.** "'The build's fine, the battery's great, the cameras are functional,
  the software's good — but this phone is slow, really slow. The Helio G37 and 3 GB of RAM: it's slow to
  scroll, unlock, and open even normal apps; it's almost never near its 90 Hz. It might be the slowest phone
  I've ever used, and I'm constantly reminded of it. That's absolutely the catch.'"
- [2023-01-12] **New budget vs. old flagship.** "'Compare the $169 Moto to a used OnePlus 7 Pro (~$200) — the
  2019 phone of the year — and dollar-for-dollar the old flagship is just so much better across the board:
  better chip, faster storage, more RAM, a much nicer OLED at real 90 Hz, better cameras, glass build,
  30 W warp charging.'"
- [2023-01-12] **Software updates favor the new; the environment favors the old.** "'One distinct budget-
  phone advantage: being new, it'll get updates further into the future — but even that evaporates once a
  flagship is more than ~3 years old. And a feather in the old phone's cap is the environment: buying an
  older phone built to last and getting an extra few years out of it is less e-waste. (Also — don't avoid
  software updates; the security patches are actually pretty important.)'"
- [2023-01-12] **The rule: the cheaper the tier, the more I'd buy the old flagship.** "'The lower the price
  of the phone, the more I'd want to buy the older flagship. At $169, I'd definitely rather have the old
  flagship (a used Pixel 5, a OnePlus 7 Pro). At ~$400 it's debatable. At ~$600 there are really good
  brand-new phones — not a lot beats the Pixel 7 right now — and you keep the software-update advantage.'"
