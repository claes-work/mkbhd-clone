# CMF Phone 1: Fun Modular Budget Phone! (@mkbhd, 2024-07-08)

## Summary
A review of the **$200 CMF Phone 1** — CMF being **Nothing's budget sub-brand**, taking Nothing's "average budget phone
but with a focus on software and design" thesis and pushing it further. On paper it doesn't stand out in the sea of
$200 Androids, but **the design absolutely does**: a **completely removable, swappable plastic back** (black/orange/
blue/light-blue, held by visible metal screws, with a matching swappable SIM tray and dial) — a callback to the old
Moto Maker days but changeable — and a **screw-on accessory "knob"** in the corner that mounts a **kickstand, a card-
holder wallet, or a lanyard** ("not a gimmick — the kickstand I'd actually want"). He clocks it as **"an ultra-budget
version of MagSafe"** (screws instead of magnets) — but then discovers the magnet plate connects to a **suspiciously
familiar magnet ring**, and **a real MagSafe puck sticks to it** ("the more you know"). In use it **"feels just like a
Nothing phone"** (Nothing OS 2.6, missing zero features besides the Glyph lights — cloned apps, Game Mode, the
one-handed pull-down, notification history), with a bright 120 Hz AMOLED and a decent Dimensity 7300 — **"stretches
where I'd forget I was using an ultra-cheap phone."** The trade-offs are honest, not complaints: no wireless charging,
**no NFC**, a crappy haptic motor, a mono speaker, a slowish fingerprint reader, flimsy detached backs, IP52, and a
serviceable-not-great 50 MP Sony camera (no OIS). Targeted mainly at **India**. Verdict: **"the $200 phone engineered
to look cool"** — a set of trade-offs made in the name of really good design and software, and **"I like it."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-07-08] **CMF = Nothing's budget sub-brand pushing the design thesis further.** "'Nothing came along saying "what
  if we made an average budget phone but focused on software and design?" — and it worked. CMF is their budget
  sub-brand doing the same thing but even more. This is a $200 phone that doesn't stand out on specs but absolutely
  does on software and design.'"
- [2024-07-08] **Removable/swappable backs + a screw-on accessory knob.** "'The plastic back is completely removable
  and replaceable in a few colors (with a matching SIM tray and dial) — a callback to Moto Maker but changeable. And
  there's a screw-on knob that mounts accessories: a kickstand, a card-holder wallet, or a lanyard. Is it a gimmick? I
  vote no — the kickstand is one I'd actually want.'"
- [2024-07-08] **An ultra-budget MagSafe — that fits real MagSafe.** "'It's like an ultra-budget MagSafe — you screw
  the thing on instead of using magnets. But the wallet's magnet plate connects to a suspiciously familiar magnet ring,
  and a real MagSafe puck stuck to it really well. So you could actually buy a MagSafe car mount for this. The more you
  know.'"
- [2024-07-08] **It feels just like a Nothing phone.** "'Running Nothing OS 2.6, it's not missing a single feature the
  premium Nothing phones have except the Glyph lights — cloned apps, Game Mode, the one-handed pull-down, notification
  history. There were stretches where I'd forget I was using an ultra-cheap phone.'"
- [2024-07-08] **Honest budget trade-offs — "the $200 phone engineered to look cool."** "'You can get it to show its
  price: no wireless charging, no NFC, a crappy haptic motor, a mono speaker, a slowish fingerprint reader, flimsy
  detached backs, IP52, a serviceable-not-great 50 MP Sony sensor with no OIS. These aren't complaints — they're the
  trade-offs to hit $200. I like the set of trade-offs they made in the name of design and software. It's the $200
  phone engineered to look cool.'"
