## Summary
WWDC 2026 recap: iOS 27 / the "Snow Leopard" performance-and-polish framing, Liquid Glass opacity sliders, platform updates, and the headline **new Siri ("Siri AI")** verdict. Marques attended the event.

## Key claims (Marques-attributed only)
- [2026-06-12] **iOS 27 = "the Snow Leopard of Apple releases" — and he welcomes it.** A bunch of small incremental fixes, attention to detail, performance increases, no big design overhaul. His clean take: **"making older iPhones much faster without adding any new features is a great software update"** (iOS 27 supports the iPhone 11, ~7 years old, apps launching ~30% faster — "big thumbs up"). Corroborates his substance-over-flash / fix-the-fundamentals stance. (consumer-tech-culture, tech-reviews)
- [2026-06-12] **Liquid Glass opacity sliders — "I love this."** Users can dial glassiness from clear to opaque (answering the months of Twitter mockups asking for a slider); the universal **edge-sharpening on all Liquid Glass interfaces makes it more readable — "better across the board by far."** (tech-reviews)
- [2026-06-12] **Keynote-production read (his media-literacy lens).** Noticed this WWDC felt **"less produced / more human"**: more handheld shots, people walking in the background, no drone shots, broad daylight instead of diffused softboxes. His theory: with an hour of AI to announce, **they deliberately made it feel human** (vs the usual "sterile, robotic, beautiful, sweeping" style). Notes the Tim-Cook-final-WWDC "ode" happened in the in-person pre-livestream (25 thank-yous), not on the broadcast. (production-filmmaking, consumer-tech-culture)
- [2026-06-12] **New Siri ("Siri AI") verdict — "not that crazy; about what we expected."** Its one differentiator vs Gemini/Claude/ChatGPT (all already on iPhone) is **deep personal-context integration** — it indexes iMessage/calendar/notes/email/group-chats and can take actions in first-party apps (send messages, add events/reminders). His **immediate skeptical question: "what if I don't use all those Apple apps?"** (WhatsApp, Google Calendar, Spotify) — third-party support via app defaults is the open question. (tech-reviews, tech-industry-commentary)
