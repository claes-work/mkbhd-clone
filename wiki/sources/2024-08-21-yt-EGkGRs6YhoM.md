# Google Pixel 9/Pro Review: Gimmick or Good? (@mkbhd, 2024-08-21)

## Summary
The full two-week review of the **Pixel 9 / 9 Pro** — the phones that "finally feel like real flagships" in hardware
(brighter screens, even bezels wrapping the OLED corners, a fast/reliable **ultrasonic** fingerprint reader, clicky
buttons, squared-off sides, the new camera "shelf island," and a welcome **Pro-that-isn't-a-Pro-Max** size). But a
sharp observation opens it: **the Google event "felt like a software showcase that just also happened to have phones,"**
which did the opposite of inspiring confidence. On the inside, the **Tensor G4 isn't much faster** — what's
significant is a **much more powerful NPU and a big chunk of RAM literally reserved for AI**. Cameras are shared across
base/Pro (Pro adds the 5x telephoto + better selfie), and the photos are **"the Pixel look — very processed, punchy,
lots of HDR/sharpening"** (great for social, less natural). His gripes ($1,000 earns them): same-ish batteries, still
slow charging (~37 W — "50–60 W would be nice"), and **128 GB base — "Thanks, Apple, I guess that's the standard
now."** He hammers the software absurdity: **the phones ship with Android 14 (the second-latest)** despite Google
making Android. The AI story he triages into **useful / meh / gimmick**: **useful** = Gemini (genuinely good, rapidly
improving; extensions that see your Docs/Drive/calendar are the killer use), Call Notes, Video Boost, **Add Me**
(genuinely useful group-selfie compositing); **meh** = AI weather summaries, Magic Editor **Reimagine** ("what is a
photo anyway?"), the Screenshots app (should've been in Photos); **gimmick** = Zoom Enhance ("I'm not buying it") and
Pixel Studio. He re-flags the **two-assistants mess** (Gemini vs the still-present Google Assistant, with Gemini oddly
showing an Assistant logo when doing new tasks) — and the punchline: **Gemini works on any Android (Google demoed it on
an S24 Ultra), so it's not unique to Pixel.** Verdict: **"the reason to buy a Pixel is the same as ever — it's the
smartest smartphone in the room"** — now in genuinely nice hardware, with AI on top you can take or leave. "Some of
their finest work yet."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-08-21] **Finally real flagship hardware — but the event was a software showcase.** "'The hardware is finally
  what makes these feel like flagships in a good way — brighter screens, even bezels, an ultrasonic fingerprint reader
  finally, clicky buttons, and a Pro that isn't a Pro Max. But watching Google's event, it felt like a software
  showcase that just also happened to have phones — which did the opposite of giving me confidence.'"
- [2024-08-21] **The G4 isn't much faster — the NPU and reserved RAM are the point.** "'The Tensor G4 isn't that much
  more powerful — you won't notice for 99% of what you do. What's significant is a way more powerful NPU and a big
  portion of RAM literally reserved for AI. In a year where the meta is minor improvements + a bunch of AI, I'm glad
  the Pixel also got meaningful hardware.'"
- [2024-08-21] **They ship with Android 14 — the second-latest.** "'Google makes Android, so the Pixel should be the
  vessel for the latest Android — yet these launch with Android 14, the second-latest version, out of the box. That's
  not a joke, and it's hilarious. And it's a $1,000 phone starting at 128 GB — thanks, Apple, I guess that's the
  standard now.'"
- [2024-08-21] **The AI features, triaged: useful / meh / gimmick.** "'Useful: Gemini (genuinely good, improving fast —
  the extensions that see your Docs/Drive/calendar are where it becomes like talking to a person), Call Notes, Video
  Boost, and Add Me (genuinely useful group-selfie compositing — "another moment that never happened"). Meh: AI weather
  summaries, Magic Editor Reimagine ("what is a photo anyway?"), the Screenshots app (should've been in Photos).
  Gimmick: Zoom Enhance ("I'm not buying it" — it just adds a clarity filter and fake texture) and Pixel Studio.'"
- [2024-08-21] **Gemini isn't unique to Pixel — and the two-assistants mess persists.** "'Gemini is deeply integrated
  (the boot animation turns into the Gemini logo — these are basically Gemini phones). But Google Assistant still
  exists alongside it — the home-screen mic is the old Assistant, and Gemini even shows an Assistant logo when doing
  new tasks. And you can do all of this Gemini stuff on any Android — Google demoed it on an S24 Ultra at their own
  event. So the reason to buy a Pixel is the same as ever: it's the smartest smartphone in the room, now in nice
  hardware, with AI on top you can take or leave.'"
