# The New Siri is... Google (Explained) ★★

## Summary

Marques breaks down the confirmed news that **the long-awaited Siri overhaul, coming in
2026, will be powered by Google's (Gemini) models.** He frames it as the headline everyone
can point to and say "yeah, Apple is losing the AI race" — but also a sensible move given
Apple couldn't get Siri competitive. The video's durable idea is a reframe: **"who owns the
models is not as important as who controls the interface"** (the Raycast analogy), and the
whole deal is ultimately a **switching-cost play** — make sure built-in AI is no longer a
reason to leave the iPhone. A tech-industry-commentary landmark that resolves the 2025
Apple's-AI-Crisis thread. ★★.

## Key claims

- **[2026-01-13]** The confirmed headline: **the big Siri update (2026) will be powered by
  Google.** Apple's statement: "Google's technology provides the most capable foundation for
  Apple foundation models." He calls it "hilarious news that Apple hasn't finally figured out
  AI — they'll be partnering with a company who has." (paraphrase + quote) It's ~2 years after
  the big Siri update was first announced.
- **[2026-01-13]** **"This feels like the headline we can all point to to say they're losing
  the AI race"** — a $4T company everyone assumed was on the bleeding edge, now partnering out
  its AI. "And honestly, I don't blame them." (quote) [Direct payoff of the 2025-03-29 "Apple's
  AI Crisis" video.]
- **[2026-01-13]** The precedent: **Google has long paid Apple billions to be Safari's default
  search** (Apple doesn't make a search engine, so it partners to give users the best one) — but
  **here it's probably Apple paying Google.** Same logic: Siri exists but is bad, Apple couldn't
  get it competitive, so a partnership using Google's models as a foundation "was the right
  answer." (paraphrase)
- **[2026-01-13]** The twist: Apple recently partnered with **OpenAI** (Siri kicks complex
  requests out to ChatGPT). If a Google-powered Siri can handle those on its own, **"what happens
  to that OpenAI partnership? I don't know."** (quote)
- **[2026-01-13]** He expects the result to be **"pretty good"** — Gemini on Android is already
  strong, "comfortably top three" with GPT and Claude. Open questions for WWDC 2026: **how much
  Apple builds on top** (a big privacy focus is guaranteed — using Google's models on-device
  doesn't mean kicking data out to Google), and whether the iPhone finally gets **real Android-
  style circle-to-search** ("one of the absolute most useful AI features of the last 5+ years").
  (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-01-13]** The reframe: **"maybe who owns the models is not as important as who controls
  the interface."** Analogy — he uses **Raycast** (a Spotlight replacement, "Spotlight has been
  dog water for years") whose AI-chat lets you pick any model (ChatGPT/Gemini/Grok); "it doesn't
  matter which model I choose — the interface I use is still Raycast, so **Raycast gets the user**
  because it delivers the experience I need." Likewise **Apple does this deal because it keeps the
  user on the iPhone.** (quote)
- **[2026-01-13]** The switching-cost conclusion: **if built-in AI was one of the reasons you'd
  leave the iPhone for a Pixel/Samsung, this move is specifically to make sure that's no longer
  one of the reasons you'd switch** — and Apple gets the PR boost of "Siri's good again." He asks
  viewers how much LLMs actually factor into their phone-buying. (paraphrase)
