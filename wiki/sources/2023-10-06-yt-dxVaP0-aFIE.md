# Can You Trust Google? (@mkbhd, 2023-10-06)

## Summary
A commentary springing off the Pixel 8's **seven-years-of-updates** promise — "industry-leading and super awesome" —
against **Google's rich history of killing products people depend on.** The paradox: you'd think relying on a giant
is safer than a startup, but a service you invest in **"might just be a small rounding error for them that they get
rid of at any time."** The rule (from a friend): **"Google is willing to spin up new projects aggressively, but if
it doesn't get a billion users or make a billion dollars, it's liable to get axed at any time."** The receipts:
just-killed Google Domains, Jamboard, Podcasts, Pixel Pass; the long "Killed by Google" list (Google+, Inbox,
Hangouts, Stadia...). And **personal ones**: he'd **just moved mkbhd.com to Google Domains a week before Google
killed it** (and sold the domains back to Squarespace); his old **NY Empire ultimate team uses Jamboard** for
scouting; **Pixel Pass** was quietly killed a month before the first free-phone upgrade so nobody got theirs. His
thesis: **"launching ambitious new things is easy; supporting them for a long time is hard"** — so seven years is
"awesome and hard to believe at the same time" — **"it's a promise, and Google breaks promises all the time."** He
ties in a broader **"features coming soon" trend** (Pixel's Video Boost, iOS's Journal/AirDrop, the Watch's
double-tap all shipped incomplete — like the games industry releasing half-finished games), and restates his
standing rule: **"never buy a piece of tech based on the promise of future software updates — buy it for what it is
today; delivered updates are a bonus."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-10-06] **A giant can rug-pull you too — the billion rule.** "'You'd think relying on a big company is safer
  than a startup, but the same problem happens with Google, because something you get invested in might be a small
  rounding error they get rid of anytime. As a friend says: Google is willing to spin up new projects aggressively,
  but if it doesn't get a billion users or make a billion dollars, it's liable to get axed at any time.'"
- [2023-10-06] **The receipts, including personal ones.** "'Just in the past few weeks: Google Domains, Jamboard,
  Podcasts, Pixel Pass — all killed. There are whole webpages chronicling it. Personal ones: I'd just moved
  mkbhd.com to Google Domains a week before they killed it and sold the domains back to Squarespace; my old NY Empire
  ultimate team uses Jamboard for scouting; and Pixel Pass was quietly killed a month before the first free-phone
  upgrade, so nobody got theirs.'"
- [2023-10-06] **Launching is easy; supporting is hard.** "'Launching ambitious new things — which is what Google
  does — is easy. Supporting them for a long time is hard. Seven years of updates is the most ambitious support plan
  we've ever seen in smartphones, which is what makes it so awesome and so hard to believe at the same time. I want
  it to be real — but it's a promise, and Google breaks promises all the time.'"
- [2023-10-06] **The "features coming soon" trend — half-finished releases.** "'A mini-trend lately: devices ship but
  the new features aren't available at launch, so reviewers can't even test them (Pixel Video Boost, iOS
  Journal/AirDrop-over-internet, the Watch double-tap). It feels like the games industry releasing half-finished
  games to test the waters. I don't love it — it makes things harder to review.'"
- [2023-10-06] **Never buy for future updates.** "'I'll stick with what I've said from the beginning: never buy a
  piece of tech based on the promise of future software updates. Buy it for what it is today. If the promised stuff
  actually gets delivered, it's a bonus — an extra value-add for something you're already happy with.'"
