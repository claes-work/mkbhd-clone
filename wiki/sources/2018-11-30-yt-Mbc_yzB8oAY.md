# Let's Talk About Tesla 'Killers!' (@mkbhd, 2018-11-30)

## Summary
An EV-commentary essay (prompted by the Rivian R1T/R1S reveal) laying out **why there's no "Tesla killer" yet** —
via a reusable four-pillar framework (**design / price / specs / convenience**) and the argument that
**convenience is the moat** startups can't cross and legacy makers lack the ambition for.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2018-11-30] **Rivian is "the most promising EV that isn't a Tesla" — but not a killer.** The R1T (quad-motor
  ~800 hp, 180 kWh / 400 mi, 0–60 in 3 s, tows 11k lb, $69k, H2 2020) is "**pretty damn cool** as an electric
  truck" (huge frunk, gear tunnel). "But is this the Tesla killer? **No.**" A calibrated hype-check.
- [2018-11-30] **THE FRAMEWORK — a great EV needs design + price + specs + CONVENIENCE.** "**These four pillars are
  what you need to compete, and they're harder than you think.**" Startups nail the first three: **flashy sexy
  design** (no 100-year heritage holding them back), **optimistic specs** on paper, and even **headline prices**
  (Lucid Air's 1,000-hp limo at $59k; Rivian at $69k). "**But what they all lack is the biggest pillar:
  convenience.**" A durable lens for judging any EV startup.
- [2018-11-30] **Convenience = mass production + autonomy data + a charging network (the moat).** "**Mass
  production** (factories are brutally hard — see how long Tesla struggled); **autonomous driving** ("**Tesla just
  passed a billion Autopilot miles — that's a data moat; you have to be a software company on top of a car
  company**"); and a **charging network** ("**Tesla's Supercharger network — nothing is even close**; the startups
  *can* use it, Tesla invites them, and for some reason no one does"). Startups (Faraday, Lucid) "aren't doing so
  hot and aren't selling yet." His central EV thesis.
- [2018-11-30] **Legacy makers COULD be the killer — but lack ambition.** "The Toyotas/BMWs/Fords have the
  manufacturing, scale, and design/fit-and-finish heritage. **But they don't seem to want it that bad** — the flood
  of 'coming in 2–3 years with ~200-mile range' announcements is **ridiculous; that's way too little for 2020.**
  And they still lack the Supercharger network and the billion miles of data." His recurring hunger-vs-heritage
  read. (Also: **stop treating 'electric' as a whole new aesthetic to prove.**)
- [2018-11-30] **The verdict — "there is no Tesla killer, but we don't need one."** "The most promising/complete
  competitors are the **Jaguar I-Pace** and the coming **Porsche/Audi** EVs — which **I'm rooting for. Tesla needs
  as much competition as possible, because competition makes us all better off** — better products, a more diverse
  choice. **There's plenty of room in the market — and that's what works.**" His durable rooting-for-competition
  value, applied to EVs.

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "To make a great electric car you need a good design, a good price, great specs, and what I'll call convenience —
> and convenience is the biggest pillar."
> "Tesla just passed a billion miles driven on Autopilot — that's a lot of data to learn from. You have to be a
> whole software company on top of a car company to make this good."
> "Tesla's built out a charging network like no other — nothing is even close."
> "There is no Tesla killer, but we don't need one. There's plenty of room in the market for competition, and
> that's what works."
