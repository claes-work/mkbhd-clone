# The Problem with these Smartphone Batteries ★★

## Summary

An explainer resolving a thread Marques has pushed all year: **why Apple, Samsung, and
Google keep skipping silicon-carbon batteries** while Chinese makers ship 10,000mAh in
iPhone-thin bodies. After industry sources independently reached out, he lands the answer:
**silicon-carbon has a swelling/longevity risk** (silicon expands up to 3× on charge, which
eventually cracks and can lead to thermal runaway), and at Apple/Samsung's tens-of-millions
scale even a tiny failure rate is "a dozen exploding Note 7s." Plus a market reason: the
**US is software/ecosystem-locked**, so there's little hardware-competition pressure to take
the risk. A landmark technical-and-strategic explainer. ★★.

## Key claims

- **[2026-02-06]** The setup: the **Honor Power 2** is thinner than an iPhone 17 Pro Max yet
  has a **10,000mAh battery** (vs ~5,000) — "the size we used to only see in power banks" —
  thanks to **silicon-carbon.** Yet Samsung, Apple, and Google keep skipping it. "I finally
  think I know why." (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-06]** How it works: silicon-carbon **swaps graphite for silicon**, which holds far
  more energy → **higher energy density** (the OnePlus flagship jumped 5,400→6,000→7,300mAh in
  the same footprint). On the surface "a no-brainer." (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-06]** The debunked theory: it's **not just supply volume** (that the giants can't
  source enough) — because **Xiaomi, Realme, Honor, Oppo, Huawei are huge and ship silicon-carbon
  in high volume**, while Google ships far fewer Pixels and still skips it. (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-06]** The real reason — **swelling and longevity.** Multiple industry sources (whose
  companies he won't name) independently told him the same concern: during charging the **silicon
  expands up to 3× its volume** (like a sponge that triples and shrinks every cycle), and
  eventually things **crack** → potential **internal damage → worst case, thermal runaway "in your
  pocket."** The carbon is there to **absorb the expansion**; the silicon is finely ground to
  reduce stress; **some devices even use a literal steel cage** around the battery. (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-06]** On testing: companies verify **~80% capacity after 1,000 cycles**, but
  real-world use adds heat/cold/drops/pressure/humidity that testing can't fully capture. A source
  said getting issues under **1 in 250,000** "feels pretty good" — **but at tens of millions of
  phones, that's still enough for "a dozen exploding Note 7s like 2016."** So some companies "sit
  it out and keep developing regular lithium-ion." (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-06]** The market reason: the non-adopters (Samsung/Apple/Google) all have a **big US
  presence**, and **the US is a software-focused, ecosystem-locked market** — "people just want the
  blue bubbles." **If nobody switches phones over a battery, there's no reason for Apple to take the
  risk yet.** In other markets (where people happily use WhatsApp) there's more hardware competition,
  so **a 40%-bigger battery makes people move.** (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-06]** The verdict: **in 2026 silicon-carbon is still bleeding-edge and a bit riskier;
  lithium-ion is still the safe bet.** The first silicon-carbon phones are ~3 years old, with far
  more in the wild in the past year — "we'll check in in a year or two with more real-world data."
  (paraphrase)
