# Dear YouTube! (@mkbhd, 2021-11-11)

## Summary
A "Dear YouTube" commentary opposing YouTube's decision to hide public dislike counts. His media-literacy frame:
**you critique the thing you love harder because you want it to be better** — and YouTube is "one of the only
platforms that actually listens to creators and does something about it" (after his earlier thumbnail "Dear
YouTube," they reached out and reacted). His core argument: **the like/dislike ratio is a glanceable piece of
information** that helps you decide if a video is worth your time — and **YouTube is uniquely a search engine, not
just a recommendation feed** (the second-largest search engine in the world), so removing that signal hurts the
user experience more than it helps. He grants the mental-health/review-bomb concerns are real but argues for a
**nuanced solution over "carpet removal,"** noting the change **just leaks the negativity into the comments** —
which is worse.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-11-11] **You critique what you love — and YouTube listens.** "'When you really love something you don't go
  easy on it — you critique it more because you want it to be better. **After my earlier "Dear YouTube," they
  reached out, they listened, they reacted — YouTube is one of the only platforms that actually listens to its
  creators and does things about it.** That's why I want to talk about this.'"
- [2021-11-11] **The ratio is glanceable, useful information.** "'Put likes and dislikes together and you get a
  ratio — **the immediately glanceable thing I look at to know if a video is worth my time.** A how-to install
  video with a high dislike ratio is my cue to scroll to the comments and find out it was done wrong or it's an
  old version. **It's less a quality score than a review of your experience — did it satisfy what you came for.**
  Hide the counter and it's no longer a ratio; you hide a very useful signal.'"
- [2021-11-11] **YouTube is a search engine, not just a feed.** "'Matt at YouTube says other platforms don't have
  a dislike button — technically true, but **those (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok) are content-recommendation
  engines. YouTube is also the second-largest search engine in the world.** It's more like Netflix, Amazon, or
  Etsy — you search, you land on a thing, and you need a way to review whether it was what you were looking for.
  **Imagine Etsy removing 1-star ratings to protect sellers' mental health — it'd work, but it'd wreck the
  usability of the whole site.** On Etsy you decide where to spend money; on YouTube, your time.'"
- [2021-11-11] **I'm here for the dislike button — nuance over carpet removal.** "'You won't hear many creators say
  it, but **I'm here for the dislike button and the public count.** As a creator, yes, the counter racking up can
  be mentally taxing — but you weight it less over time, and **hiding it just leaks all that negativity into the
  comments section, which is worse.** Put the energy into better comment tools instead. A more nuanced solution
  (e.g. only letting people rate after watching a proportion of the video) would beat **carpet-removing the
  dislike counter from every video.** Well-intentioned, but it does more harm to the experience than it solves.'"
