# iPhone 15 Review: Welcome to the Club! (@mkbhd, 2023-10-02)

## Summary
A review of the base **iPhone 15** where **"nothing inside is new"** — and that's the point: **"you can be a really
good phone and not really new; they aren't mutually exclusive."** It's Apple's **"textbook late-mover advantage"** —
take something we've already seen (USB-C, Dynamic Island, the A16, a 48 MP camera) and **"do it the Apple way."**
The design is "familiar" (Apple says "all new" — "all new? really?"), but the softened corners and first satin
non-Pro back are nice (and the pale colors are the most fingerprint-resistant iPhones in years). The most
fascinating critique: **it's an $800 phone that still has a 60 Hz display in 2023.** His nuanced take — **the target
buyer genuinely doesn't notice** (he's handed people 120 vs 60 Hz side by side and they can't tell), but Apple, the
richest tech company, **makes subtle improvements all the time that regular people never notice** (LCD→OLED,
A15→A16, 5G) — and **a higher refresh rate is *more* noticeable than most of those** (a $170 Moto G is 90 Hz). So
withholding 90 Hz is **a deliberate tool to widen the Pro gap** ("ProMotion for the Pro"), even if it makes this
"the most expensive phone in the world with a 60 Hz display." Cameras are weird-but-good (a new 48 MP → 24 MP
default that last year's Pro *doesn't* get despite the same chip — software-locked). Verdict: the **subtle
improvements stack** into an appreciably better phone — **"just get the boring new iPhone with nothing that's
actually new; it's just gonna work"** (and don't buy a Lightning iPhone now).

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-10-02] **You can be a good phone and not new — the late-mover advantage.** "'Nothing inside is new or
  anything we haven't seen before somewhere. That doesn't mean it's not good — you can be a really good phone and not
  really new; they aren't mutually exclusive. It's Apple's textbook late-mover-advantage strategy: take something
  we've already seen and do it the Apple way.'"
- [2023-10-02] **The $800 60 Hz display — the target buyer doesn't notice.** "'It's 2023 and this $800 phone still
  has a 60 Hz display. For most people buying a baseline iPhone, it's totally fine — they don't care, they can't tell
  120 from 60 side by side. Apple makes subtle improvements regular people never notice all the time (LCD→OLED,
  A15→A16, 5G).'"
- [2023-10-02] **But 90 Hz is withheld to widen the Pro gap.** "'A higher refresh rate is actually *more* noticeable
  to more people than most of those — a $170 Moto G is 90 Hz because Motorola knew people would feel it. It's wild
  how easy it'd be for Apple to make this 90 Hz, but they refuse because they've attached "Pro" to that feature
  (ProMotion). It's a tool to widen the gap between Pro and non-Pro — even if it makes this the most expensive phone
  in the world with a 60 Hz display.'"
- [2023-10-02] **The software-locked camera — same chip, different features.** "'The new 48 MP main gives a 24 MP
  default via binning + detail transfer — but last year's Pro has the same A16, same ISP, same neural engine, and
  doesn't get it. So my base 15's non-zoomed photos actually look a little better than last year's Pro. Same with
  the new auto-portrait detection. Software-locked, kind of weird.'"
- [2023-10-02] **Just get the boring new iPhone — it's USB-C and it works.** "'The subtle improvements stack up
  enough that it feels appreciably better even though it looks the same. If you have a 14 you don't have to upgrade;
  but if you're on an older phone, I wouldn't buy last year's to save money — just get the 15. You don't buy a base
  iPhone for the bleeding edge; you buy it because it works and it'll be familiar for years. And now that we've moved
  to USB-C, I wouldn't buy a Lightning iPhone. Just grab the boring new iPhone with nothing that's actually new — it's
  just gonna work.'"
