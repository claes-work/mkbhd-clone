# I shrunk down into an M5 chip

## Summary

An ambitious educational/visualization video (his self-described "highest fact-dense video
ever") that shrinks Marques down 100× at a time — from human scale to the 3nm transistors in
a 2026 M5 MacBook Pro — to make the incomprehensible transistor counts on Apple keynote
slides tangible. Built entirely in **Blender with explicitly no generative AI**, in collab
with creator **Epic Spaceman**. Contains a notable **appearance/biography anchor** (he states
his height: **6'3"**) and reinforces his self-identification as **"a numbers person."**

## Key claims

- **[2025-12-26]** The premise: keynote slides flash "impossibly big numbers of transistors,"
  and, like "scale of the universe" videos help you grasp cosmic size, this shrinks *down* to
  make chip density tangible. "Isn't it crazy how we taught rocks to do math?" (quote)
- **[2025-12-26]** ⬥ APPEARANCE/BIOGRAPHY: **"I'm 6'3", so around 2m tall, basically."** (quote)
- **[2025-12-26]** ⬥ Self-identification: **"I'm a numbers person, so I really enjoy those
  'scale of the universe' type videos."** (quote)
- **[2025-12-26]** PRODUCTION/VALUES: **"No generative AI is being used to create this video.
  Just a whole lot of Blender."** (quote) — a deliberate, stated stance consistent with his
  broader anti-AI-slop position.
- **[2025-12-26]** The scale walk: at 1/100 he's a keyboard key on a MacBook that's now an
  apartment building; a 1948 transistor is "the size of a single transistor" at that scale;
  **Moore's law** (~doubling every 2 years) "has stayed about true since the beginning of the
  semiconductor industry — which means we have a long way to go." Down through micrometer scale
  (200µm — twice a hair's thickness; paper ~100µm), 2µm (bacteria scale, invisible to the eye,
  where physics goes weird — air molecules too big to breathe, visible light as tall as he is),
  to **20nm**, where a 2026 M5 transistor is "about the size of a garage" and atoms are marbles.
  (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-12-26]** **3nm transistors aren't "built" like objects** — they're made via
  **photolithography**, "like developing a photo with light projected through a microscope but
  onto atoms instead of film," yielding billions per chip. He's honest about limits: the M5
  visualization "most likely isn't 100% accurate because Apple's not going to share their exact
  chip architecture with me making a YouTube video." (quote)
- **[2025-12-26]** The inverse framing (making it big instead of small): the **ENIAC** (1946,
  room-sized, light-bulb vacuum-tube triodes). If you built today's iPhone (~**19 billion
  transistors**) with 80-year-old transistors, it would be "the size of the state of New
  Jersey"; if a modern transistor were a Toyota Prius, that iPhone "would be about the size of
  planet Earth." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-12-26]** Humility on the trend: "I don't really know how much longer Moore's law can
  apply as they get down to the literal atomic level, but I sure do want to appreciate it while
  it's happening." Notes it's "still almost nothing" against the cosmos (100B+ stars in the
  Milky Way). Credits **Epic Spaceman** as "100% of the inspiration." (quote)
