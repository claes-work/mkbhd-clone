# The Electric Car Pre-Order Problem (@mkbhd, 2022-03-30)

## Summary
A commentary/rant on why EV pre-orders are broken. The frame: **a 5-step guide to making an EV company** — (1) a
clever name + snazzy site, (2) become a company (charismatic CEO, poach talent), (3) build ONE working prototype,
(4) parade it around + open pre-orders to raise money/interest, (5) **actually ship it** — and **almost everyone
gets stuck between step 4 and step 5** (the "draw the owl" gap). Pre-orders are **interest-free loans** (the
Roadster took $250K wire transfers in 2017 and still hasn't shipped — that money in Tesla stock would be ~$4M). He
walks the graveyard (Faraday Future, Byton, Canoe, Lordstown's **fabricated pre-orders**) and even Rivian (the
$12–15K price hike on existing pre-orders → backlash → 24-hour walkback). His durable takeaway: **EVs are the
future, not the present** — and **"anytime I see a new EV announcement, I automatically add a couple years and
several thousand dollars, no matter who it's from."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-03-30] **The 5-step guide — and the step 4→5 gap.** "'The five steps to a new EV company: a clever name + a
  site; become a company; **build ONE working prototype** (just demonstrate you can); parade it around and open
  pre-orders to raise money and signal interest to investors; and **ship it.** Spoiler: **pretty much all of them
  reach step 4, and the gap to step 5 is massive — like the "draw the owl" tutorial** (step 1: two circles;
  step 2: draw the rest of the owl).'"
- [2022-03-30] **Pre-orders are interest-free loans — and the opportunity cost.** "'The Roadster took full-price
  wire transfers ($250K) in 2017 to be in the first thousand — **it's 2022 and it still hasn't shipped, so you gave
  Tesla a 5-year interest-free loan of a quarter million** (that same money in Tesla stock would be worth ~$4M
  today). Pre-orders let companies use the count to lure big investors.'"
- [2022-03-30] **The graveyard — and fabricated pre-orders.** "'The stuck ones: **Faraday Future** (a great 2017
  prototype, pre-orders still open, ~$2B, never shipped), **Byton** (raised $1.2B, gone silent), **Canoe** (Hyundai/
  Kia pulled out), and **Lordstown — an investigation found most of its 100k+ pre-orders were fabricated** (they
  allegedly paid a firm to place ~1,000). Even **Rivian** (the furthest along, actually delivering) **raised prices
  $12–15K on people who'd already pre-ordered → massive backlash → walked it back within 24 hours.**'"
- [2022-03-30] **Established companies treat EVs like Kickstarters too.** "'It's not just startups — even
  established companies treat new EVs like Kickstarters (the F-150 Lightning you can pre-order now; Tesla followed
  the exact blueprint with the Cybertruck — one famous prototype, pre-orders in the millions = hundreds of
  millions in interest-free loans, no firm ship date).'"
- [2022-03-30] **EVs are the future, not the present — the buyer's rule.** "'I firmly believe EVs are the future —
  but that's just it, **the future, not the present** (99% of cars on the road are gas). The moral: **beware
  companies parading working prototypes while taking pre-orders with far-away or unknown ship dates.** I'll still
  check out every new EV — but **anytime I see a new EV announcement, I automatically add a couple years and
  several thousand dollars, no matter who it's from.**'"
