# The $129 Smartphone?! (@mkbhd, 2020-12-05)

## Summary
A look at the $129 Poco M3 that turns into a landmark essay on **how phone prices are marketed in the ultra-budget
category.** Two durable "price tricks": **early-bird pricing** (the headline price is a brief limited-volume
teaser that rises permanently — but the headlines stay up) and the **base-version-you-shouldn't-buy** (the
cheapest SKU that isn't the one reviewers are even sent). Plus the broader thesis: **the commoditization of the
smartphone** is a good thing — cheap phones getting this good is an achievement.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2020-12-05] **Budget phones keep getting good — and "embrace the plastic."** "'Budget phones have been getting
  better at $400, $300, even $200 — but **not even cracking $150 shipped is incredibly impressive.** The faux-
  leather plastic is nice — **if you're going to go plastic, embrace the plastic; don't pull a Samsung and look
  me dead in the eyes and call it "glastic."** Plastic is fine on cheap phones.'" A memorable materials line.
- [2020-12-05] **Single-camera is best on budget; the huge battery is the star.** "'The **best budget cameras are
  single-camera systems (Pixel 4a, iPhone SE) — I still would have preferred an ultrawide over the useless 2 MP
  macro + 2 MP depth sensors.** The best spec is the **6,000 mAh battery** (two-day potential) — plus a headphone
  jack, fingerprint-in-power-button. The catch: **it's slow (Snapdragon 662) — I worry how it ages.**'"
- [2020-12-05] **PRICE TRICK #1 — early-bird pricing.** "'The $129 is **early-bird pricing — a limited time/volume
  teaser; then the price rises to normal permanently, but all the "$129 phone" headlines stay up.** There's
  science behind it: they want you to not think too hard and **buy fast.** Do you write "$129 phone" as your
  headline when most readers can't get it at that price? Tough to say.'" A promotable marketing-mechanics call.
- [2020-12-05] **PRICE TRICK #2 — the base version you shouldn't buy (and reviewers aren't sent).** "'The other
  trick: **a cheapest base version with an appealing number that you probably shouldn't buy** (obvious upgrades),
  like "starting at $X" cars with no A/C. **The first Poco made "$300 phone" headlines but they sent reviewers
  the $400 version** — you have to assume it's not the same experience if they chose to send the pricier one.'"
- [2020-12-05] **THE COMMODITIZATION thesis — a net positive.** "'To those who say smartphones have gotten
  boring/all-the-same: **that's one of the benefits of the commoditization of the smartphone — you can now
  mass-produce something pretty decent at an amazingly low price, and leave the innovation and crazy fun stuff
  (folding, flipping, rolling) to the high end.** I applaud that level of accessibility.'" (Asks whether Poco is
  filling the good-cheap shoes OnePlus is leaving.)
