# Nothing Phone 3a/Pro: Now Do a Flagship! (@mkbhd, 2025-03-04)

## Summary
A review of the **Nothing Phone 3a ($379) and 3a Pro ($459)**, opening with a thesis: **"there's something refreshing
about a budget phone from a company that doesn't have a bunch of more expensive phones to protect."** Both **"do a
shockingly good job of feeling like $1,000 flagships, with a few small asterisks."** The design is still the standout —
**"find me a more interesting phone design out there right now, I'll wait"** (the transparent split/glyph look; the Pro
adds a big camera "plateau" that doubles as a finger rest). He reads **Nothing's growing maturity** (a glass back now,
IP64, a 3,000-nit-HDR AMOLED, a Snapdragon 7s Gen 3) but the real value is the **software** — "shout out to Nothing's
software team; six years of updates; the little animations are optimized to feel flagship-smooth." Notably, **Nothing
is one of the last big names *not* heavily pushing AI** (no image generator, writing tools, notification summaries,
Genmoji, or object removal) — **except one thing: the "AI button" / Essential Space** (press to screenshot; hold to add
a voice memo that's transcribed, summarized, and can set reminders — "like the Pixel Screenshots app on steroids"). His
verdict on it invokes a **personal rule: "I won't dump my life into something if it's not multi-platform"** (it's
phone-only, no web version) — "a cool start, maybe someday." A useful buyer note: **the 3a-vs-3a-Pro camera difference
is much smaller than the huge camera bump suggests** (nearly identical primaries; the Pro's periscope 3x is the real
gain). Two closing thoughts: coming straight from the iPhone 16e, **"this spec sheet makes the 16e's look terrible"**
(triple cameras, a bigger/brighter AMOLED, high refresh, a bigger battery — and cheaper); and **"now I'm way more
curious about a Nothing Phone 3, a genuine flagship — now do a flagship!"** (Ridge 12-year-anniversary sponsor.)

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2025-03-04] **Refreshing: no expensive lineup to protect.** "'There's something refreshing about a budget phone from
  a company that doesn't have a bunch of more expensive phones in their lineup to protect. The 3a ($379) and 3a Pro
  ($459) both feel like $1,000 flagships, with a few small asterisks.'"
- [2025-03-04] **The most interesting design + growing maturity + great software.** "'There's still nothing that looks
  quite like a Nothing Phone — find me a more interesting design right now, I'll wait. You can see Nothing's growing
  maturity (a glass back now, IP64, a 3,000-nit-HDR AMOLED). But the real value is the software — six years of updates,
  and the little animations are optimized to feel flagship-smooth. Shout out to Nothing's software team.'"
- [2025-03-04] **Nothing barely pushes AI — except the AI button / Essential Space.** "'Nothing is one of the last big
  names not heavily pushing AI — no image generator, writing tools, notification summaries, Genmoji, or object removal.
  The exception is the AI button / Essential Space: press to screenshot, hold to add a voice memo that gets transcribed,
  summarized, and can set reminders — like the Pixel Screenshots app on steroids. A cool start.'"
- [2025-03-04] **My multi-platform rule.** "'I have a personal rule: I won't dump my life into something if it's not
  multi-platform — and Essential Space is phone-only, no web version, so I'm not diving all in. Maybe someday. (Also,
  you'll press that button accidentally a lot.)'"
- [2025-03-04] **It makes the 16e look terrible; now do a flagship.** "'Coming straight from the iPhone 16e, this spec
  sheet makes the 16e's look terrible — triple cameras, a bigger/brighter AMOLED, high refresh, a massive battery, and
  it's way cheaper. And it makes me way more curious about a Nothing Phone 3, a genuine flagship: if they can make this
  for this value with software this good, imagine a Snapdragon 8 Elite + silicon-carbide battery + wireless charging.
  Now do a flagship. (And the 3a-vs-Pro camera difference is much smaller than the giant camera bump suggests.)'"
