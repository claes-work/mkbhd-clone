# Why Don't More Smartphones Have This? (TCL NXTPAPER) (@mkbhd, 2023-12-07)

## Summary
A look at the **$200 TCL 40 NXTPAPER**, a basic budget phone with **one striking feature: a matte, reflection-free,
"paper-like" display.** It's not real E-Ink — it's an LCD with **textured glass + software tricks** that delivers
almost all the eye-comfort benefits (zero reflections, physically less blue light, a soft-touch feel) without the
slow refresh of an e-reader. His first reaction is the title: **"why doesn't every phone have this?"** — especially
as someone who spends real effort **shielding reflective screens when shooting reviews** ("inside baseball — it's a
whole thing"). Then the catch: **the extra display layers cut light, sharpness, and contrast** — 450 nits max (dim
and washed-out outdoors), a 1080p panel that "looks closer to 720p" (soft text/edges), a weak 1500:1 contrast, and
you can't sensibly put a screen protector on it. So a display advantage becomes a **niche feature**: "most people
aren't willing to sacrifice the downsides for less eye strain and fewer reflections" — **"the perfect addition to
the pile of niche features that are awesome but won't sell a ton of phones."** The e-paper software modes (Color
Paper, Ink Paper) look "silly to most people," but genuinely impressive next to an actual e-reader — which is why
almost nobody buys it, and why not every phone does this.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-12-07] **Why doesn't every phone have a matte no-reflection display?** "'It has one really interesting
  feature — the screen. Put it next to another phone and there are no reflections at all. My first thought: why
  doesn't every phone have this? How is a cheap budget phone the first I've seen to pull it off? As someone who
  spends a lot of effort shielding reflective screens when I shoot reviews (inside baseball — it's a whole thing),
  I'm very into it.'"
- [2023-12-07] **It's not E-Ink — textured glass + software.** "'It's not real E-Ink (rearranging actual ink); it's
  an LCD with textured glass and software tricks that resembles a paper-like display and delivers almost all the
  eye-comfort benefits — zero reflections, physically less blue light through an extra filter, and a soft-touch
  feel.'"
- [2023-12-07] **The catch — it cuts light, sharpness, contrast.** "'The extra display layers cut a lot of light and
  sharpness. Max brightness is 450 nits — fine indoors, quickly washed out outdoors. The 1080p panel looks closer to
  720p (soft text and edges), contrast is a weak 1500:1, and you can't sensibly put a screen protector on it.'"
- [2023-12-07] **A display advantage becomes a niche feature.** "'In a world of big, bright, clear displays, this
  goes from an advantage to a niche feature — most people aren't willing to sacrifice the downsides for a little
  less eye strain and fewer reflections. A phone isn't a desktop monitor you buy purely on the panel; there's a
  bunch of stuff people care about, and it turns out they don't like this trade-off.'"
- [2023-12-07] **The perfect addition to the niche-features pile.** "'I've talked about all kinds of niche features
  that are awesome but won't sell a ton of phones — this is the perfect addition to that pile. The e-paper software
  modes look silly to most people, but next to an actual e-reader they're impressive (full color, 90 Hz, fully
  responsive). It's technically awesome, but almost no one's going to buy it — which is why not every phone does
  this.'"
