# RIP Apollo (for now) (@mkbhd, 2018-11-19)

## Summary
A personal update, not the Tesla video he wanted to make: his Tesla Model S **"Apollo"** got hit by an 80,000-lb
truck on the highway. He's completely unhurt; the car (probably repairable, not totaled) becomes a case study in
the real pain point of Tesla ownership — repair times.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2018-11-19] **BIOGRAPHY — the crash (he's fine).** "On the way from a shoot to the studio, **a truck merged into
  my lane on a 3-lane highway** — I was in his blind spot, matching speed in traffic (~35 mph); an 80,000-lb truck
  clipped the rear-left wheel and scraped forward to the mirror. **I'm totally fine — not scratched, not injured.**"
  A dated life event (posted the photos to Twitter; someone caught it on a dashcam).
- [2018-11-19] **The Tesla-repair pain point (an ownership reality).** "This hits **one of the biggest complaints
  Tesla owners have — repairs take a long time**, not because the work is long, but because **Tesla takes a long
  time to get parts out** to start it. The auto body shop thinks it can be repaired (not totaled — the frame/
  battery/motors seem okay)." Uses his own misfortune to surface a real EV-ownership downside.
- [2018-11-19] **The total-vs-repair economics.** "Whether you total or repair comes down to **repair cost relative
  to car value** — with a **high-value car like a Tesla + high repair cost, it often still makes sense to fix it**
  (glad Apollo isn't totaled)." A quick, clear explainer.
- [2018-11-19] **The service-relationship honesty.** "People point out my **relationship with my Tesla service
  center could look preferential** — we know each other, we talk camera stuff, the Elon interview. **But this is a
  new scenario — a body shop I've never dealt with, new insurance logistics. The real question is how Tesla handles
  it.**" Candid about his own access, and testing the process as a normal owner would. (Ubering everywhere
  meanwhile.)
