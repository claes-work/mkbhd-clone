# The Android Tablet Problem (@mkbhd, 2025-06-20)

## Summary
An essay on why tablets struggle, prompted by testing "the best Android tablet I've used" (the **OnePlus Pad 3**) right
after the biggest iPadOS update ever. The core thesis: **very few people actually need a device *between* a phone and a
computer** — a tablet has to do something **a phone AND a computer can't**, which is niche. He grounds it in the **iPad
app problem**: most developers build for the phone (one version, millions of customers), so **most iPad apps are just
scaled-up iPhone apps** — famously **there's still no Instagram iPad app in 2025** ("the best a trillion-dollar company
with tens of thousands of employees is willing to do"). That's why every high-end iPad Pro review says the same thing:
**"amazing hardware, but I can't do anything special over a base iPad or even an iPhone, because iPadOS is still
iPadOS."** The OnePlus Pad 3 is genuinely **"the whole package"** (Snapdragon 8 Elite, a 13.2" 144 Hz screen, a huge
**12,140 mAh battery** with absurd standby — 85%→84% overnight in a backpack, eight speakers, **$699**) — **"and yet,
who's gonna buy one?"** Because the same problem holds: **"no matter how great an Android tablet is, it will be
incredibly niche."** He frames it as the **Android double-edged sword** (endless variety — foldables, flippables,
tri-folds — but nothing easy to recommend, and an inconsistent experience) and observes **"90% of the iPads I've seen
since 2020 are point-of-sale tip screens or a child watching CoComelon at full blast."** The hopeful note: **iPadOS 26
might finally lift the value cap** (a separate video). He ends by asking viewers what they *actually* use a tablet for —
"I suspect the answers are single-use things these tablets are wildly overpowered for."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2025-06-20] **Few people need a device between a phone and a computer.** "'Very few people actually need a device in
  between a phone and a computer. A tablet has to do something a phone AND a computer can't — and that's really niche.
  It's the same problem the iPad has.'"
- [2025-06-20] **The iPad app problem — no Instagram iPad app in 2025.** "'Most developers build for the phone — one
  version, millions of customers — so most iPad apps are just scaled-up iPhone apps; a tiny fraction (Procreate,
  Pixelmator) are truly great. There's still no Instagram iPad app in 2025 — the best a trillion-dollar company with
  tens of thousands of employees is willing to do. That's why every high-end iPad Pro review says the same thing:
  amazing hardware, but I can't do anything special over a base iPad or even an iPhone, because iPadOS is still
  iPadOS.'"
- [2025-06-20] **The OnePlus Pad 3 is amazing — and yet who's gonna buy one?** "'The OnePlus Pad 3 is the whole package
  — Snapdragon 8 Elite, a 13.2" 144 Hz screen, a huge 12,140 mAh battery with absurd standby (85% to 84% overnight in a
  backpack), eight great speakers, $699. And yet, who's gonna buy one? Because most people have a phone and a computer,
  so the tablet has to do what neither can.'"
- [2025-06-20] **No matter how great, an Android tablet is niche — the double-edged sword.** "'No matter how great an
  Android tablet is, it will be incredibly niche. That's the Android double-edged sword: endless variety (foldables,
  flippables, tri-folds) is sweet, but nothing is easy to recommend to large groups, and the experience isn't
  consistent. 90% of the iPads I've seen since 2020 are point-of-sale tip screens or a child watching CoComelon at full
  blast.'"
- [2025-06-20] **iPadOS 26 might fix the value cap.** "'The value you can get from a tablet has always felt capped. This
  new iPadOS 26 does feel like a game changer (a separate video). Either way — these are super-awesome pieces of tech
  that most people probably won't need to buy. What do you actually use a tablet for? I suspect single-use things
  they're wildly overpowered for.'"
