# How Teslas Upgrade Over Time! (@mkbhd, 2020-05-23)

## Summary
Prompted by his new Model S ("Phantom," replacing "Apollo"), an essay on **how Teslas upgrade over time** — the
best and worst thing about them. The durable thesis: **cars should be like tech and get better over time**, powered
by OTA software updates and fleet learning.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2020-05-23] **BIOGRAPHY — Phantom replaces Apollo.** "'This is my Model S, **"Phantom"** — not Apollo (the lease
  was up). A 2020 Model S Performance.'" A datable personal-car update (Apollo = the one hit by a truck,
  [[2018-11-19-yt-6w6pEXZutoY]]).
- [2020-05-23] **The rolling-updates model — no model years.** "'Tesla doesn't do model-year updates; they do
  **constant, ongoing minor changes** that add up. Tiers: **tiny cosmetic** (matte trim, leather, seats), **medium**
  (faster UI computers, 2→6 cameras for Autopilot), and the **major named ones** (Raven suspension/drivetrain, the
  2016 nose-cone refresh, the P100D — "that's when I decided to buy"). The car you get is the best version ever —
  like a piece of tech.'"
- [2020-05-23] **The manufacturing logic — simplify by removing options.** "'Constant changes streamline
  manufacturing — they remove SKUs and options (every Model S ships the **same battery**; Performance vs Long Range
  is just a motor/inverter difference). A tiny number of options for a luxury car — but it makes each one the best
  version yet.'"
- [2020-05-23] **THE THESIS — cars should improve like tech (and the fleet-learning moat).** "'The **software
  updates are the biggest, most valuable thing — this car got more updates than my phone.** If rule #1 is "never
  buy for the promise of future updates," **Tesla is trying to break that rule — because it helps them for people
  to trust their cars get better.** And **millions of drivers testing features (like Smart Summon) feed data back —
  an underrated competitive advantage; Tesla is a software company as much as a car company. Maybe cars should be
  like tech and get better over time.**'" A promotable framework (ties [[2020-05-02-yt-nxf41fMX_Y4]]).
- [2020-05-23] **Smart Summon — "technically a success, but a nightmare."** "'The car finds you across a parking
  lot — but it **drives like a 5-year-old who can barely see over the wheel**: sometimes ignores pedestrians,
  ignores lane lines, unpredictable. The upside: **all those failed attempts become training data.**'" His honest
  autonomy-still-a-work-in-progress read.

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "Maybe that's not such a bad thing for the car world — maybe cars should be like pieces of tech and attempt to
> get better over time."
> "Since I've gotten that car, it's gotten more software updates than my phone has."
