# F1: Explained! (@mkbhd, 2023-12-11)

## Summary
An explainer on **why he started watching Formula 1** and why you should too — reframing it for a tech audience:
**"while on the surface it's just a car race, it's also the world's highest-end tech competition. It's a science
fair."** The key insight: F1 runs **two championships at once** — the Drivers' and the **Constructors'
(engineering) Championship** — and unlike other sports, **you're encouraged to improve your equipment between every
race**, so teams spend hundreds of millions **fighting for tenths of a second.** Crucially, it works like the tech
industry: **when one team finds a real edge, every other team eventually reverse-engineers and adopts it** — a
constantly-compounding **innovation ecosystem** (wings, KERS→hybrids, DRS, paddle shifters, the monocoque — all
started as one engineer's experiment, then became universal, and some **trickle down to road cars**). He covers the
FIA rulebook (safety + excitement: the mandatory **halo**, the two-tire-compound rule, and his favorite — the
**plank of wood bolted under every car** to police ride height), and the race-day experience (practice/qualifying/
race, pit-stop and tire strategy — "like chess, but the pieces move at 200 mph"). He attended the **Las Vegas Grand
Prix** (Bitdefender/Ferrari) and stresses **cameras don't do the speed justice in person.** "F1 is the pinnacle of
motorsport, and I totally agree — it's art on wheels."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-12-11] **F1 is a science fair — the world's highest-end tech competition.** "'On the surface it's just a car
  race, which is cool — but it's also the world's highest-end tech competition. It's a science fair. There are two
  championships each season: the Drivers' and the Constructors' (the engineering teams). In F1 the best equipment is
  hugely advantageous, and you're encouraged to improve it between every single race.'"
- [2023-12-11] **The compounding innovation ecosystem — like tech.** "'Teams spend hundreds of millions fighting for
  tenths of a second, and they're incredibly secretive — but when a team finds something that gives a real edge,
  eventually every other team picks it up and builds it themselves. It's this ecosystem of constantly-improving
  features stacking on top of each other (wings, KERS → hybrids, DRS, paddle shifters, the monocoque).'"
- [2023-12-11] **The rules — the halo and the plank of wood.** "'The FIA has the impossible job of keeping it fair
  and safe while encouraging innovation. Safety: the iron-reinforced halo (FIA-designed, not the teams') that saves
  lives. My favorite rule: under every car is a plank of wood bolted to the bottom — if it's too worn after a race,
  you rode too low with too much downforce, and you're disqualified. Every carbon-fiber masterpiece has a plank of
  wood on it.'"
- [2023-12-11] **Race strategy is chess at 200 mph.** "'Every car must use at least two tire compounds during the
  race — softest (grippiest, fastest, but cooked in 10–20 laps) to hardest (slower but durable) — so teams build
  pit-stop strategies, and different drivers finish on different tires. It's like chess, but the pieces move at 200
  miles an hour around curves.'"
- [2023-12-11] **Cameras don't do it justice; trickle-down to road cars.** "'Seeing it in person (the Las Vegas
  Grand Prix) gave me a new level of respect — the noise, the smells, the vibration; cameras just don't do the speed
  justice. And the innovations trickle down: DRS, paddle shifters, carbon fiber all originated in F1 and made their
  way to cars we drive today.'"
