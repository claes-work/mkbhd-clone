# On New Cybertruck Updates from Drone Footage... (@mkbhd, 2022-02-17)

## Summary
A commentary on the **drone-over-factories subgenre** — hobbyists flying drones over company campuses to spot
unreleased products — sparked by drone footage of a Cybertruck being worked on in the open at Fremont. The durable
idea: **the same secrecy-vs-openness calculus is the upside and downside of control.** Companies guard secrets
(NDAs, physical walls — "we'll never see an iPhone prototype in public again"), and the **Streisand effect** +
scarcity make any real leak huge. Then the contrast: **Apple sides hard with control** (they hired people to stop
drones over Apple Park), while **Tesla doesn't care at all** — because **Tesla has no PR department and runs no
ads, relying on word-of-mouth and the product speaking for itself** (free, powerful — but a real loss of control
over how people first see the product). His read: leaving the Cybertruck out in the open **"is at least a little
bit marketing"** — Tesla leans into the hype. The plea: **"just don't ruin it for everyone"** (one crash and Tesla
flips the switch).

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-02-17] **Secrecy, the Streisand effect, and the scarcity of real leaks.** "'Companies spend millions on R&D
  and don't want it leaked — it's a loss of control of their narrative (the lost iPhone 4 prototype; interview
  guests' #1 off-limits topic is future products). **The Streisand effect applies — the more you hide something,
  the more attention it attracts** — and real tech leaks are so scarce that any one is a big deal, so a whole
  ecosystem publishes literally anything new.'"
- [2022-02-17] **The drone subgenre — cool but sketchy.** "'The weirdest way to discover company secrets I've seen:
  **flying drones over headquarters and factories.** Cool sometimes, sketchy sometimes — beyond the crash risk,
  there are real FAA limits (5 miles from airports, ≤400 ft, line of sight, not over crowds). **Legally you could
  fly over Apple Park — but Apple absolutely won't let you** (they hired people to stop drones).'"
- [2022-02-17] **Tesla doesn't care — no PR department, no ads.** "'On the other end: **Tesla doesn't care at all**
  — people routinely fly over the gigafactories, workers wave at the drones. Why? **Tesla is a weird company:
  they have no PR department and run zero ads**, so instead of setting aside millions for advertising like
  everyone else, they rely on **word-of-mouth and the product speaking for itself** — which works powerfully for
  them.'"
- [2022-02-17] **The upside and downside of control.** "'This approach has upsides and downsides. **The upside: it's
  free, and Tesla fans spreading the word works great. The downside: a major loss of control** — you don't control
  how people first see your product. Apple sides firmly with control (their image is precious); **Tesla chose to
  take the risk and give up some control.**'"
- [2022-02-17] **"It's a little marketing" — and "don't ruin it for everyone."** "'Leaving the Cybertruck out in an
  empty parking lot in broad daylight, aware of the drones, when they have indoor space? **I'd argue that's at
  least a little bit marketing** — the same company that kept the Roadster/Model Y/Cybertruck total secrets before
  the stage reveals now leans into the hype. **My only thing: please don't ruin it for everyone** — it takes one
  person flying too close, clipping a power line, for Tesla to flip the switch and stop allowing it.'"
