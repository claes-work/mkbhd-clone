# When Smartphones Copy Each Other! (@mkbhd, 2021-06-02)

## Summary
A commentary (hung on the ZTE Axon 30 Ultra vs. the Note 20 Ultra) arguing that **copying is fine — as long as you
copy the good stuff.** The durable ideas: the **second-mover advantage can be just as good as, maybe better than,
the first-mover advantage** (the Axon arrives 10 months later with newer specs at a lower price); his modified
maxim **"good artists copy, great artists copy but change it a little so it doesn't look like a copy"**; and **"if
you get a whole bunch of good things in one place, it doesn't really matter where you got them from."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-06-02] **THE HOT TAKE — "go ahead, copy stuff; I'm totally fine with it."** "'People clown on companies for
  copying, as if everything must be totally original — **but I'm totally fine with companies learning from each
  other's successes and downfalls, as long as they copy the good stuff.**'"
- [2021-06-02] **THE SECOND-MOVER ADVANTAGE — "for my fellow business majors."** "'This is a perfect example of
  why the **second-mover advantage can be just as good as, maybe even better than, the first-mover advantage.**
  The Axon 30 Ultra grabbed the "Ultra" name and the square-camera/boxy design from Samsung — but 10 months later
  it has the **newer Snapdragon 888, faster 65 W charging, a 144 Hz display, for $749 vs the Note 20 Ultra's
  $1,099.** And the high-refresh caveat cuts the other way: the Note couldn't do 1440p+120 Hz at once, so
  high-refresh fans ran it at 1080p anyway — now you just get a smoother 144 Hz.'"
- [2021-06-02] **What's genuinely new comes from elsewhere too.** "'It's not literally cloning — more like **copying
  the homework and changing it so it doesn't look copied.** And the good stuff comes from many places: the 65 W
  charging didn't come from Samsung; ZTE's own **big colorful quick-settings touch targets are well done (Android
  12 is picking that up).**'"
- [2021-06-02] **THE PRINCIPLE — "if the good things are in one place, it doesn't matter where they came from."** "'The
  point: **if you get a whole bunch of good things in one place, it doesn't really matter where you got them from
  — even if a bunch were obviously inspired by a certain other thing.** As customers, we should be okay with
  companies looking around and learning from each other — as long as they copy the good stuff.'" A promotable
  pro-copying value.
- [2021-06-02] **His modified maxim.** "'The saying is "good artists copy, great artists steal" — but I'd modify
  it: **good artists copy, great artists copy but then change it a little to make it look not as much of a copy.**
  I think that'll be my quote.'"
