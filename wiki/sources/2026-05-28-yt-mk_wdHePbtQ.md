# The Hidden Tech Behind an NBA Broadcast!

## Summary

Marques goes behind the scenes of an NBA playoff broadcast to catalog the gear and, more so,
the **craft** of live TV. He tries the ball-tracking rig himself and drives home a recurring
theme: **"intuitive to learn, difficult to master."** The through-line is his deep respect for
**live, unscripted multicam storytelling** — "a symphony of live storytelling on the biggest
stage… and if it's done really well, you don't even think about it once" — contrasted with the
months Hollywood spends for minutes of footage.

## Key claims

- **[2026-05-28]** The tracking that looks easy "is not easy" — a viral Twitter clip of him
  trying it proved the point. It's "a lot of reps and practice and skill to perfectly track the
  ball, keep it center frame, rack focus, and zoom at the same time." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-05-28]** The gear: most arena cameras are **Sony P50 boxes** (sub-1" global-shutter
  sensor, 1080p60, ~$50k each) with **Canon "122" lenses (8–1000mm, 122× optical zoom)** on
  **$25k fluid heads**; a dedicated bank of six on swivel chairs (wide/tight/two player ISOs/coach/
  vertical). Operators get **a cheat sheet of player faces** and ~2 seconds to ID/find/zoom/focus
  on a director's call. (paraphrase)
- **[2026-05-28]** The iconic tip-off shot comes from a **cable cam** (a Sony P50 + Canon 14× on a
  Ronin 2, wirelessly flown by a **two-person tag team** — one moves it in 3D space, one controls
  the camera), "kind of similar to flying a drone… super fun." "Shout out to those carabiners"
  (four of them hold it to the roof cables). (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-05-28]** The **stanchion** is loaded top-to-bottom with cameras/mics (an ultrawide + a
  floor mic for sneaker squeaks; a RED, a Sony a9, a Canon R6; **Sennheiser MKH 416 shotgun mics —
  "which I happen to already know is a great microphone, I have a soft spot for them, I'm literally
  recording with one right now"**; a Nikon D4 for stills with black tape to kill backboard
  reflections); plus an **above-the-rim camera** and a **Steadicam** that runs out on every timeout
  ("the most dynamic camera"). **~40–50 cameras** total. (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-05-28]** The real magic is the **truck pod** (~six trucks for a playoff game, 200 yards
  away) where everything is **cut together live** — engineers white-balancing every camera to match,
  a live audio mix of dozens of mics, live framing/keyframing, and a director "calling out cuts in
  real time." "Hollywood spends months for a few minutes; this is **48 minutes of live, unscripted
  multicam delivered as a finished product as it's happening.**" (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-05-28]** The **EVS replay controller** (a scrub wheel + a smooth speed lever, sources up
  to 180fps) is how "those crisp replays get played exactly at the right moment, seconds after they
  happen." "Intuitive to learn, difficult to master." (paraphrase)
- **[2026-05-28]** The takeaway: it gave him "more appreciation for how different this is than what
  I do" — **"a symphony of live storytelling on the biggest stage for an audience of millions, and
  if it's done really well, you don't even think about it once."** (quote)
