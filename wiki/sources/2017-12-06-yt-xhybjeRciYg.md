# Portrait Mode: Explained! (@mkbhd, 2017-12-06)

## Summary
A canonical teach-the-mechanism explainer on smartphone Portrait Mode — what it's *trying* to emulate (the
shallow depth of field / bokeh of a big-sensor camera) and how well it's gotten. He benchmarks the iPhone X /
Note 8 / Pixel 2 against a **Hasselblad X1D medium-format** — a camera he owns and calls the source of "the
best photos I've ever taken." A strong photography-literacy + gear-credibility node.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2017-12-06] **Teach-the-mechanism: what Portrait Mode emulates.** "A great camera with a big sensor
  (full-frame) or a fast lens gives shallow depth of field — the subject pin-sharp, the background a beautiful
  blur; the quality of that blur is **bokeh**, and higher-quality bokeh usually means a more advanced lens/
  sensor. Portrait Mode is phones trying to emulate that with software." Defines the target before judging.
- [2017-12-06] **The history: the iPhone 7 Plus's first portrait mode was "garbage."** "The first real
  impact was the iPhone 7 Plus's dual cameras + beta portrait mode in iOS 10 — and it was **garbage, really
  trash when it first came out. But Apple stuck with it, worked on it, and a couple software updates later
  it's gotten better and better.** Now in 2017 pretty much every phone has dual cameras — it's the rule, not
  the exception." A stick-with-it-via-software arc (fair credit for iteration).
- [2017-12-06] **GEAR/CREDIBILITY — the Hasselblad X1D medium-format benchmark.** "I'm comparing to the
  Hasselblad X1D — a **medium-format** sensor (43.8×32.9 mm, even bigger than full-frame's 36×24), which
  recently got **the highest-ever DxOMark score, 102**. I've had it a couple months and **I've taken the best
  photos I've ever taken in my life with it** (check the Flickr)." Anchors the phone comparison to a real
  professional camera he shoots — a photography-credibility node. (Consistent with his DxOMark-not-out-of-100
  point: 102 > 100.)
- [2017-12-06] **The test: phones vs. the real thing.** Same photo/angle from the iPhone X, Note 8, Pixel 2,
  and the Hasselblad — "you can see the differences right off the bat, especially how well the Hasselblad does
  it." A blind-test-adjacent, show-don't-tell comparison of software-emulation vs. optics.

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "The quality of that background blur — that's what's called bokeh — and the higher the quality of the bokeh,
> usually the more advanced and higher quality the lens and camera system."
