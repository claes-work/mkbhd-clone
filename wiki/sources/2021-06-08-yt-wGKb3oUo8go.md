# Why iPhone's Features are Always 'Late' (@mkbhd, 2021-06-08)

## Summary
A companion commentary (from WWDC / iOS 15) explaining **why Apple's features arrive later than Android's — but
often better and more integrated.** The durable thesis: **Apple's ecosystem constraint** (every team has to plug
into everything) **multiplies development time but produces features that talk to each other**, while **Google's
more siloed teams innovate faster but produce features that don't connect.** It closes with the reader question:
**do you prefer bleeding-edge-new, or later-but-more-polished-and-plugged-in?**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-06-08] **The pattern — iOS features arrive late, but often better.** "'Every WWDC we see iPhone features
  we've already seen in Android (widgets, app drawer, picture-in-picture were famously late to iOS 14). **iOS 15's
  Live Text is basically Google Lens (around for a while); on-device Siri is catching up to Google's 2019 voice
  recognition; Apple Maps is catching up to Google Maps.** But Apple's tend to arrive **later and arguably
  better.**'" (Cf. the copy-the-good-stuff companion, [[2021-06-02-yt-ZS-LwWzlKdc]].)
- [2021-06-08] **THE KEY THESIS — the ecosystem constraint multiplies dev time.** "'Why is Apple later? **Apple's
  teams have a distinct focus on the ecosystem — not just to make good new stuff, but to make everything plug
  into each other seamlessly** (it makes the products better AND harder to leave). So **even if an Apple team has
  the exact same idea at the exact same time as Google, they have the constraint of making it work with the rest
  of the ecosystem — which multiplies the time, but the end result has plugs the other versions don't.**'" A
  promotable Apple-vs-Google org-structure thesis.
- [2021-06-08] **Google's siloed teams — innovative but disconnected.** "'**Google's teams are more siloed and get
  to work without having to talk to each other, so they churn out amazing new features — that just don't talk to
  anything else.** I don't see Google adding seamless integration between Google Messages and Google News; those
  teams don't seem to work together.'"
- [2021-06-08] **Focus modes + SharePlay + Continuity — the ecosystem payoff.** "'**Focus modes** (like multiple
  profiles) — set "work" on the iPhone and your iPad, Mac, and Watch all switch, and your iMessage status shows
  it. **SharePlay** is better-integrated than generic screen-share (synced playback in FaceTime). And **dragging
  your cursor from Mac to iPad and dropping files between them** is "one of the coolest ecosystem-flex features
  I've ever seen — I'd hold my breath waiting for Google to do that with Chrome OS + Android."'"
- [2021-06-08] **THE QUESTION — bleeding-edge-new vs. later-but-polished-and-plugged-in.** "'So the bottom line
  is: **it's what we've come to expect — we wait for it.** Do you prefer **super-innovative, bleeding-edge new
  features, or a little later but more well-polished and plugged-in features?**'" A promotable framing of the
  Android-vs-Apple tradeoff.

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "Apple's teams... will often have this constraint of having to work with the rest of the ecosystem and plug into
> as many different things as possible, which often multiplies the amount of time needed to make the thing work —
> but the end result is typically something that's got some functionality or some plugs that the other versions
> don't."
> "Google's teams... will often churn out amazing, incredible new features that just don't talk to anything else."
> "Do you have a preference between super-innovative, bleeding-edge features versus a little bit later but a
> little bit more well-polished or plugged-in features?"
