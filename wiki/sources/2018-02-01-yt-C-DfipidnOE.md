# Dear Snapchat... (@mkbhd, 2018-02-01)

## Summary
Another entry in his "Dear X" open-letter format — this time explaining, mechanically, **why Snapchat looks
so bad on Android**, and why that's a symptom of a broader app-developer incentive problem. A strong
teach-the-mechanism / media-literacy node.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2018-02-01] **The mechanism — Snapchat screen-grabs the viewfinder instead of using the camera.** "On
  Android, Snapchat for years didn't tap the real camera API — it essentially took a **screenshot of a live
  preview** and sent that. So you're getting a low-res, compressed image even on a phone with a great camera.
  That's why your Pixel shoots gorgeous photos but your snaps look like a potato." He explains the *why*, not
  just the symptom — his signature move.
- [2018-02-01] **The economics behind it (developer incentives).** "Here's the uncomfortable reason: an app
  developer supports maybe **three new iPhones a year** but **hundreds of Android models** with different
  cameras, sensors, and quirks. The ROI on optimizing for all of them is low — so a lot of apps do the lazy
  cross-platform thing and Android users pay for it." A systems-level explanation of platform fragmentation.
- [2018-02-01] **The "Dear X" ask.** Frames it as a direct, constructive request to Snapchat: use the proper
  camera API, treat Android as first-class — "you have the users; do the work." Constructive-critic register,
  not a dunk.
- [2018-02-01] **Media-literacy takeaway.** "Don't judge a phone's camera by one app — the app can be the
  bottleneck, not the hardware." Consistent with his look-past-the-score / controlled-comparison methodology.

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "Snapchat has basically been taking a screenshot of the viewfinder and sending that — on a phone with an
> amazing camera."
> "An app developer supports maybe three iPhones a year, and hundreds of Android phones — so guess which one
> gets the effort."
> "Don't blame the camera when the app is the bottleneck."
