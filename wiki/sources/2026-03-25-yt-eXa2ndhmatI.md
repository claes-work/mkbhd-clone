# The Windows Laptop Problem ★★

## Summary

Sparked by the MacBook Neo ($599, disruptive) and the M5 Max MacBook Pro (absurdly powerful)
"putting Windows laptops on notice," Marques investigates what's actually wrong on the Windows
side. His frame: **Windows vs Mac laptops is basically iPhone vs Android** — the Windows
advantage is choice and variety, but that's now also the disadvantage, because a great Windows
laptop **requires many separate companies (Dell + Intel + Microsoft) to all execute well at the
same time** — and **Windows itself has become the weak link** (ads, forced sign-ins,
Copilot/Recall). Meanwhile the Neo weaponizes Apple's vertical integration *and* scale at the
low end — sold cheap as a **Trojan horse for services subscribers**. A landmark industry essay. ★★.

## Key claims

- **[2026-03-25]** The setup: the **Neo** ("one of the best deals in tech in a long time") and the
  **$6,000 M5 Max MacBook Pro** (top-of-every-chart multi-core, a GPU stronger than the M2 Ultra in
  his $10k Mac Pro, ~20,000MB/s SSD) are both **"putting Windows laptops on notice"** — Microsoft,
  Dell, HP, Asus, Lenovo, Razer. An Asus exec called the Neo **"a shock to the industry."** (quote)
- **[2026-03-25]** The core frame: **"Windows vs Mac laptops is basically the same as iPhone vs
  Android"** — the Windows advantage is **variety and choice**, but that's now "both an advantage and
  a disadvantage that's getting kind of scary." (quote)
- **[2026-03-25]** Why choice is fragile: a good Windows laptop (e.g. the nice **$2,200 Dell XPS 14**,
  tandem OLED, Intel Panther Lake) **relies on Dell, Intel, AND Microsoft all doing a great job at the
  same time** — "and lately Windows has felt like a weak link, and every single Windows laptop will
  have to suffer for it." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-03-25]** **Windows is the weak link**: setting up the XPS took ~45 minutes of **forced
  updates, mandatory sign-ins, Microsoft 365/OneDrive nags**, then **a McAfee pop-up ad on a $2,000
  laptop**, plus Copilot, the "infamous Recall feature everyone hates," generative AI in Paint, and a
  **required Copilot key** to be an "AI PC." "At the high end, users get deservedly really picky" — and
  that premium lane is where **Apple's vertical integration** (hardware designed for software) has
  played almost exclusively. (paraphrase)
- **[2026-03-25]** At the low end it gets interesting: **the same efficiencies Apple used to dominate
  the high end also work at the low end.** Cheap Windows laptops (the $550 Acer Aspire 16) thrived on
  acceptable trade-offs — **until the Gatorade-colored Neo** brought "that premium, well-integrated,
  efficient experience" to $600. In Windows land you not only need many companies to execute at once,
  **you're also paying all of them (each needs a profit) → it's hard to make good cheap laptops and
  profit.** (paraphrase)
- **[2026-03-25]** **The Trojan horse**: Apple "basically prints A18 Pros for cheap," AND it isn't even
  chasing a big hardware margin on the Neo — it's **"a Trojan horse to create first-time Mac buyers who
  then become Apple services subscribers"** (TV+, iCloud, AppleCare), which is worth more than the
  hardware margin. "It already seems to be working" — a **Tim Cook tweet said Mac had its best launch
  ever for first-time customers.** (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-03-25]** The stakes: "the shock to the industry is very real"; if Windows makers can't
  respond, **"Neo's just going to keep eating their lunch, and they could have a bit of a market-share
  crisis on their hands."** (quote)
