# Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra Review: There's a Catch

## Summary

The full S26 Ultra review, built entirely around **"everything is a trade-off"** — with the
new **Privacy Display** as "one of the most interesting case studies in that in a long time."
Marques (who literally bought a microscope) shows how the display achieves its
shoulder-surfing protection by using **two pixel types** and, when engaged, **turning off half
the pixels** — so it costs resolution, viewing angles, and a little brightness *all the time*.
He's genuinely impressed by the feature ("a real hardware innovation"), catalogs the misses
(**still no Qi2 magnets, still no silicon-carbon**), and lands that **"the price is probably
the most Ultra thing about this phone."**

## Key claims

- **[2026-03-06]** The theme: **"every feature in every phone is a trade-off,"** and the Privacy
  Display "is maybe one of the most interesting case studies of that in a long time." (quote)
- **[2026-03-06]** How the **Privacy Display** works: the panel has **wide-angle pixels** (visible
  from everywhere) and **narrow-angle pixels** (with a focusing lens, visible only straight-on).
  Turning privacy on **shuts off the wide-angle pixels** → **resolution is literally cut in half**
  (blockier text under his microscope), **peak brightness drops** (masked by boosting the pixels
  that stay on), and — since half the pixels permanently have narrow viewing angles — the display
  is **"slightly worse all the time."** It's also **8-bit simulating 10-bit** with a weaker anti-
  reflective coating than the S25 Ultra. (paraphrase)
- **[2026-03-06]** But it's a **genuinely useful hardware innovation** ("we really don't get that
  often") — toggleable, and appliable to **just certain apps / a password field / the notification
  area.** And **most people aren't pixel-peepers** (the 1440p screen even ships at 1080p by default
  to save battery). "That's the trade-off they decided to make for this one really cool feature
  they believe will sell more phones." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-03-06]** Design trade-offs: slightly thinner/rounder (so the **S Pen now only inserts one
  way**); a bigger camera plateau that makes it **"rock viciously on a table"**; and **back to
  aluminum from titanium** (no real difference — "people will make fun of you for copying the
  iPhone's titanium for no reason"). (paraphrase)
- **[2026-03-06]** Chip/battery: **Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy** (benchmarks ~like the
  OnePlus 15, the expected 20–30% CPU bump; Samsung is more focused on the NPU); a redesigned vapor
  chamber; slightly better battery from efficiency (same 5,000mAh); 60W charging. **But: "No Qi2
  magnets? … No silicon-carbon battery?"** — "wouldn't you also want to win an MVP-Phone-of-the-Year
  award, too?" (references Mrwhosetheboss's battery test showing it dunked on by last year's
  silicon-carbon phones). (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-03-06]** Cameras: mostly last year's, but **wider apertures** on the main and 5x (faster
  lenses, softer bokeh — "as a photographer I'll always take the faster lens"); the main camera's
  **minimum focus distance got worse.** Software wins: an **APV Log codec** and a strong **Horizon
  Lock** stabilization (crops the 200MP sensor for very stable QHD/60). He **shot the AutoFocus
  Polestar 4 talking-head footage on it.** (paraphrase)
- **[2026-03-06]** On the AI: "**software** is a word you could use. **Slop** is another word.
  Samsung would prefer **agentic**." A pile of features from useful (**Call Screening**, Audio
  Eraser) to **Photo Assist** (editing photos into scenes "that never actually happened"). "Take it
  or leave it… I'm just not convinced any of this is a reason to buy the phone" (and most should be
  a software update to older devices). (quote)
- **[2026-03-06]** Aside: **the base S26 and S26+ "are not good deals anymore"** — afterthought
  designs, basically the same cameras since the S23, no mmWave, Exynos outside the US, and the $900
  base (they dropped the 128GB but kept 256GB at the same price — "the price of entry is higher").
  **"You probably have to vote with your wallets to get Samsung to do anything."** (quote)
- **[2026-03-06]** Verdict: a really good phone with one **one-of-one** feature, but **"the price is
  probably the most Ultra thing"** — no silicon-carbon, no fast/wireless/magnet charging, no S Pen
  Bluetooth — "this feels like an S26 Plus Plus Pro Max." (quote)
