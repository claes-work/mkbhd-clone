# Pixel 10/Pro Review: Good News and Bad News!

## Summary

The full Pixel 10 / 10 Pro review, structured around a running "good news / bad news"
bit whose punchline is that both are the same: **"It's still a Pixel."** Despite 10th-gen
hype and the first fully-in-house TSMC-built Tensor, Google made no philosophical change —
it's all the things he likes and dislikes about the Pixel, now pushed to the masses via a
celebrity marketing blitz. The standout new feature is **Pixel Snap** (Qi2 magnets); the
disappointment is that the **cameras are slipping** (zoom especially) and **Magic Cue**
under-delivers versus the demos.

## Key claims

- **[2025-08-29]** The thesis, both ways: **"The good news? It's still a Pixel. The bad
  news? It is still a Pixel."** All the liked traits (design, camera bar, software) remain;
  all the long-missing ones (fast charging, silicon-carbon battery, huge sensors) are still
  missing. (quote)
- **[2025-08-29]** He wouldn't have spent the Pixel budget on a **Jimmy Fallon presentation
  + a Jonas Brothers music video** — "but that's why I'm the reviewer and they're the Google
  executives." (quote)
- **[2025-08-29]** **Tensor G5** (first fully Google-designed, 3nm, TSMC-built) is "a really
  good chip, but it's not magic." It benchmarks like a **mid-range** phone — CPU ~30% up but
  still behind Snapdragon (closer to the 8s Gen 4 in the Nothing Phone 3); the **GPU gap is
  even bigger** (Geekbench OpenCL ~half the Pixel 9's, far behind the 8 Elite); no
  hardware ray tracing. **Raw CPU/GPU flagship power "is not even really one of its goals."**
  (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-08-29]** Tensor's real strength is the **TPU — 60% more powerful than last gen,
  and that's intentional**: Google wants these to be "the fastest and most capable AI
  devices," not the raw-performance leaders. (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-08-29]** **Pixel Snap** (Qi2, 15W / 25W on XL) "has genuinely become one of my
  favorite new features" — a huge quality-of-life win for a **"card-carrying member of Team
  No Case."** He thinks the first-party magnets are **slightly stronger than the iPhone's
  MagSafe**. "It still doesn't have fast wireless charging, but it just gained a ton of
  functionality. Thumbs up." (quote)
- **[2025-08-29]** **eSIM-only in the US** — he dislikes being forced off physical SIM
  ("only the carriers win with this"), while admitting he over-indexes it because he swaps
  SIMs across test phones constantly. (quote)
- **[2025-08-29]** **The cameras are slipping.** The Pixel leaned on elite software (HDR+)
  for years, but now "they just don't stand out from the pack nearly as much" and in some
  ways fall behind. Wides and Night Sight are still great, but **zoom is "surprisingly bad"**
  — even Pro shots past ~30x "look like they have a cartoon filter," "smoked by the S25
  Ultra," worse than even the iPhone 16 Pro. Video still trails the flagships. (paraphrase +
  quote)
- **[2025-08-29]** **Magic Cue** (surface info across your Google apps) "rarely seemed to
  work exactly the way I wanted it to... only half as helpful as advertised" — often just
  links you back to the app; once gave wrong flight info and auto-sent it without the flight
  number. "I can see the promise; it's still a work in progress." (quote)
- **[2025-08-29]** **Conversational Editing** (semantic photo edits from text prompts, e.g.
  "add a hot air balloon," "delete the cars and make the sky bluer") "does a surprisingly
  good job" — and is **coming to all phones with Google Photos**, not just Pixel. He asks
  half-seriously: "Google, is video editing next? Should I be concerned?" (quote)
- **[2025-08-29]** Verdict: the Pixel "feels like a finished/complete thought for now" —
  Google isn't changing its philosophy, just re-attacking the masses. If you disliked the
  Pixel before you still will; if you loved it you still will. He'll daily the Moonstone
  Pixel 10 Pro, then rotate off to more interesting hardware, then back again — "the Pixel
  is right where it's always been." (paraphrase + quote)
