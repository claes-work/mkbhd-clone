# The Biggest Problem with AI! (@mkbhd, 2023-02-18)

## Summary
On the new **AI-chatbot search wave** (ChatGPT-powered Bing vs Google) — and its core flaw: **"sometimes it's just
wrong."** The mechanism: **the AI doesn't know if it's telling the truth** — it isn't forming sentences like a human,
it's **generating the next most-likely words**, so it can't fact-check itself. He frames the race as **Bing (nothing
to lose, everything to gain) vs Google (everything to lose** — search + ads are >half its revenue, so shipping a
chatbot that's wrong "all the time" at the top of search is untenable). The famous **100-million-users adoption
chart** (telephone 75 years → ... → TikTok 9 months → **ChatGPT 2 months**) makes the moment feel inevitable. His
test: **"ask it a question you already know the answer to and spot the error"** — the lists look convincing (best
cameras, best EVs) but to an expert are **"C-plus, maybe B-plus at best"** (Jaguar I-Pace over the Rivian). The
verdict/creed: **"anything we do with these AI tools should be a collaboration with the human touch"** — like DALL·E
(inspiration, not finished art) or ChatGPT (a framework for an essay, not the essay) — **"check its work first."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-02-18] **The biggest problem — sometimes it's just wrong, and it doesn't know.** "'One thing follows
  conversational AI everywhere: sometimes it's just wrong. Fundamentally the AI doesn't know if it's telling the
  truth — that's not part of the model. It's not forming a sentence like a human; it's generating a new sentence
  from words most likely to go together, and it's definitely not fact-checking itself.'"
- [2023-02-18] **Bing has everything to gain; Google has everything to lose.** "'On one hand, Bing has everything to
  gain; on the other, Google has everything to lose. Search and ads are more than half of Google's revenue —
  displacing that with a chatbot that gets things wrong all the time is insane; it can't happen. That's what having
  everything to lose looks like. Google's had conversational AI for years (the 2021 IO "talk to Pluto" demo) but
  never shipped it.'"
- [2023-02-18] **The adoption chart makes it feel inevitable.** "'The time-to-100-million-users chart hits hard:
  telephone 75 years, mobile 16, Netflix 10, Twitter 6, Instagram 30 months, TikTok 9 months, ChatGPT two months.
  Looking at that, I buy it — we're clearly on the precipice of something really big.'"
- [2023-02-18] **The test — spot the error; a B-plus answer to an expert.** "'A game I like: ask it something you
  already know the answer to and spot the error. The answers are really convincing to someone who knows nothing —
  but if you're an expert in what you asked about, they're C-plus, maybe B-plus at best (it left the Rivian off a
  best-EV list). So add layers: am I a newb here? Am I going to blindly trust this? Is a B-plus answer good enough?'"
- [2023-02-18] **The creed — collaboration with the human touch; check its work.** "'My take: anything we do with
  these AI tools should be a collaboration with the human touch. You wouldn't take a DALL·E output and call it art —
  it's inspiration for your own canvas; you wouldn't copy-paste a ChatGPT essay and submit it — it's the framework
  for your own piece. Use it as a springboard for your own more-informed research, especially on topics you don't
  know. Maybe check its work first.'"
