# RED Hydrogen One Review: I Wanted this to be Great! (@mkbhd, 2018-11-02)

## Summary
A rare **disappointed review of a brand he loves** — "the most frustrating phone I bought this year." The
standout is his **vertical-integration insight** explaining exactly why the RED-branded cameras are only "okay,"
plus a clear-eyed "wait for the modules / trust-RED-or-not" verdict on a $1,295 phone.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2018-11-02] **"I wanted this to be so good" — the disappointed frame.** "**The most frustrating phone I've bought
  this year.** Parts are pretty good, but as a complete package there's only a tiny sliver of people who should
  buy it as is — and even they should probably wait." He followed its development for months (the Houdini
  prototype), which sharpens the letdown.
- [2018-11-02] **THE VERTICAL-INTEGRATION INSIGHT — why the cameras are "just okay."** "RED **doesn't make this
  sensor** — it's an **off-the-shelf Sony IMX**, the same kind in a bunch of phones. RED's real cameras are
  incredible because **RED makes the silicon AND controls the entire image pipeline end-to-end.** Here they're just
  doing the best they can with off-the-shelf parts + software — so the photos are ~**OnePlus 6T level**, not
  RED-quality." A genuinely clarifying explanation of what makes RED, RED (and why a phone can't inherit it).
- [2018-11-02] **The holographic "4-View" display — hit or miss + real tradeoffs.** "**Don't let 'holographic'
  fool you** — nothing floats out; it's a **glasses-less 3D depth effect**, sometimes good with content shot for
  it, but eye-fatiguing and impossible to show on camera. The tradeoff: the hardware layer makes it **not very
  bright and visibly pixelated in normal 2D** (vs the Pixel 3)." Trusts his own eye (interviewed RED founder Jim
  Jannard, who says software can improve it — "his PR answers").
- [2018-11-02] **The speakers — a letdown.** "Big phone, big spaces — I was ready to love these. But it's **not
  even louder than average, a bit tinny — like a weak laptop speaker.** The '3D audio' toggle makes it worse." An
  honest downgrade from his usual big-speaker enthusiasm.
- [2018-11-02] **Last-year specs + the "it's built like a tank" honesty.** SD835 (not 845 — "at $1,295 you expect
  the latest"), 4,500 mAh (the biggest he'd tested); a modern-industrial carbon-fiber/metal body "**built like an
  absolute tank — don't put a case on it, it already feels like it has one**" (a big reason for the price).
- [2018-11-02] **The only redeeming quality — the modular future (a trust gamble).** "The exposed **pins** are an
  unlocked key: **storage / battery / and a CINEMA module — a real RED-made sensor + interchangeable lens mount —
  all 'coming 2019.' THAT would be the RED camera in your pocket we were excited about. But right now it's a
  complete fantasy, just text on a page.**" The verdict: "**Imagine this phone without the RED name — a hopeless
  ~$400 Android phone with no hope of modules. But with RED behind it, if you trust RED, it could be interesting.**
  No way to recommend it at $1,300 as is — wait for the modules or Hydrogen 2." A clean articulation of a
  bet-on-the-company purchase.
