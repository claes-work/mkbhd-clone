# Apple Vision Pro Review: Tomorrow's Ideas... Today's Tech! (@mkbhd, 2024-02-04)

## Summary
The landmark review, opening: **"I actually love this thing — not because it's flawless (it's far from it), but
because it's actually *interesting*"** — risky and new, after years of "boring spec bump" Apple products. The
recurring theme is the title: **"tomorrow's ideas... today's tech"** — again and again the Vision Pro **runs up
against the technology of today not being advanced enough** to reach the ideal. **Upsides:** the **best micro-OLED
fidelity** he's seen (7.5-micron pixels; no screen door; **foveated rendering** makes only what you look at sharp —
clever, but proof the computer can't render everything at once); the **best passthrough of any headset ever**
(12 ms latency, the R1 chip; he caught thrown objects and played table tennis) — "but as Nilay put it, it's still
cameras and screens; the tech of today isn't magic"; **eye + hand control** ("borderline magical"); and the
ecosystem — **Mac Virtual Display is his #1 feature** ("the number-one thing you can do that you couldn't with any
other Apple product") and **FaceTime "the most only-Apple-could-ship-this in a long time."** **Downsides:** the
**Solo Knit Band is the comfort problem** (all the weight on your face — the **Dual Loop is far better**, he wore it
for a 2-hour podcast); the **field of view** ("ski goggles from the inside" — the Quest 3 is wider); **EyeSight
doesn't really work** (too reflective, low-res, "doesn't feel like eye contact"); **~600 native apps, no
YouTube/Netflix/Spotify/TikTok**; meh battery; $3,500. His verdict: **"a really expensive, really fun toy"** — and,
crucially, **the first-gen-iPhone analogy doesn't guarantee success ("you can't say the others had problems and
succeeded, therefore this will") — there has to be something people actually like, and the sci-fi energy is it.**
"There are way more cool ideas about VR/AR than actual apps." Buy a **Quest 3** instead; he's "already excited for
the next generation."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-02-04] **I love it because it's interesting, not flawless.** "'I love this thing — not because it's flawless,
  it's far from it, but because it's actually interesting. Don't forget, the last two-three years of Apple reviews
  are "boring, just a spec bump, nothing new." This is risky, and most of all it's new — new in a way only Apple
  would try. And just as interesting is the future it implies.'"
- [2024-02-04] **Tomorrow's ideas, today's tech.** "'This is a recurring theme: the Vision Pro runs up against the
  technology of today not being advanced enough to accomplish the ideal. The screens are amazing, but there are so
  many pixels the computer can't render everything sharp at 90 Hz — so foveated rendering only renders what you're
  looking at. Best passthrough I've ever seen — but as Nilay from The Verge put it, it's still cameras and screens;
  today's tech isn't magic.'"
- [2024-02-04] **Mac Virtual Display is my #1 feature; FaceTime is peak Apple.** "'Mac Virtual Display is my number-
  one favorite feature — it re-renders a virtual monitor as big as you want with Vision Pro apps floating around it;
  the number-one thing you can buy it for that you couldn't do with any other Apple product. And FaceTime (the
  scanned persona, the positional spatial audio) is the most only-Apple-could-ship-this I've seen in a long time.'"
- [2024-02-04] **The Solo Knit Band is the comfort problem; EyeSight doesn't work.** "'It's not really the weight —
  it's the Solo Knit Band (the cool one in all the ads) that puts all the weight on the front of your face; the
  uglier Dual Loop is 100% the way to go for long sessions (I wore it for a 2-hour podcast). And EyeSight — the eyes
  on the outside — doesn't really work: too reflective, low-res, it doesn't feel like eye contact, especially for
  darker skin tones.'"
- [2024-02-04] **A really fun toy — and the first-gen-iPhone analogy doesn't guarantee success.** "'Honestly, it's a
  really expensive, really fun toy — and there are way more cool ideas about VR/AR than actual apps (~600 native, no
  YouTube/Netflix/Spotify). The first-gen iPhone/iPad/Watch had the same downfalls and succeeded — but you can't
  just say the others had problems and succeeded, therefore this will. There has to be something people actually
  like, and it has it: the sci-fi energy of controlling things with your hands. This new attention will shine a
  light on the cheaper Quest 3 you should probably buy instead — and I'm already excited for the next generation.'"
