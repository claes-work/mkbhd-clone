# Investigating the Tesla Robotaxi! ★★

## Summary

Marques flies to the geofenced downtown-Austin zone where both Tesla Robotaxi and Waymo
operate and rides both all day, back to back, taking notes — his first-ever rides in
either. The framing is his signature **two-approaches-to-one-problem** tech comparison,
plus a running critique of how autonomous-driving discourse has become tribal sports-team
warfare. His verdict: both perform admirably and drive confidently; Waymo is a bit
further along in the overall ride experience and ~25% more cautious; Tesla is cheaper /
more scalable without LiDAR but leans much harder on a human safety monitor. His governing
principle: **being cautious and safe (geofences, safety drivers, LiDAR) is a good thing —
"if it comes at the expense of any safety, it doesn't matter."** ★★ L3-candidate.

## Key claims

- **[2025-08-08]** Truly autonomous driving is **"one of the hardest technological
  problems to solve on planet Earth"** — millions of variables, no two roads alike, and a
  mistake "could cost someone their life," so the stakes and difficulty are both maximal.
  (quote + paraphrase)
- **[2025-08-08]** He flew to Austin specifically to ride both because **neither is
  available on the East Coast** where he lives; he'd only read headlines and watched
  social videos before. (paraphrase)
- **[2025-08-08]** The **Tesla Robotaxi** is a regular (refreshed) **Model Y with decals**
  and a **safety monitor in the passenger seat** who verifies your app and holds special
  pull-over / stop-in-lane buttons — plus "a rather firm grip on the door-opening button"
  many think is another way to disengage FSD ("a vice grip on a kill switch"). No button
  was ever needed on his rides. (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-08-08]** The **Waymo** is a heavily-sensored (LiDAR) **Jaguar I-Pace** with
  **no one in the car** — greets you by name, explains the ride, and the app UX (unlock,
  trunk, wheel stickers) is "a bit further along in the overall experience." (paraphrase)
- **[2025-08-08]** Driving-wise the two were **very similar in capability**; the Waymo
  felt **~25% less aggressive / more cautious**. Both made an odd maneuver on one ride
  (drove ~50 yards past him; another blew past the destination into an 8-minute
  turnaround) that "a human driver probably wouldn't have done." (paraphrase)
- **[2025-08-08]** Tesla's advantages: no eyesore sensor rig, quieter, visible sunroof,
  300+ mi range, and **cheaper → potentially more scalable without LiDAR**. But it's "more
  reliant on the safety-monitor employee," which "feels a little more silly." (paraphrase)
- **[2025-08-08]** The **technology-adoption S-curve**: new tech improves steeply then
  tapers once it's "good enough for the masses." Both Tesla and Waymo are "insanely early
  on this curve" — small geofences, no highways, still a person in some cars. (paraphrase)
- **[2025-08-08]** Governing principle on scaling: he's heard the Twitter argument that
  Tesla scales faster without expensive LiDAR — "maybe. **But if it comes at the expense of
  any safety, it doesn't matter. It won't matter.**" One bad incident and "you can see the
  headlines coming from a mile away." (quote)
- **[2025-08-08]** Therefore the **geofences and safety drivers are actually a good
  thing**; neither company should rush to "drop these training wheels" — though "they're
  also businesses in competition." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-08-08]** These cars will hit endless novel edge cases (construction, flooding,
  snow, bad markings) — "like how there's a brand new sentence typed into Google every
  day," a car will face driving situations no human ever has. (quote)
- **[2025-08-08]** On the tribalism: **"Robotaxi vs Waymo Twitter is worse than iPhone vs
  Android ever was"** — but look where iPhone/Android landed: they compete, coexist, and
  push each other to be better. "I don't think that's such a bad future here either."
  (quote)
