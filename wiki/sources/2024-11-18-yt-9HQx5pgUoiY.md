# M4 Max MacBook Pro: I'm Convinced! (@mkbhd, 2024-11-18)

## Summary
After **three years on his M1 Max MacBook Pro with "no compulsion to upgrade,"** the **M4 Max is the first generation
that's actually tempted him** — because the right upgrades landed in the places that matter to him. On **performance**:
M2→M3 were incremental (nobody on M2/M3 needs to upgrade), but the M1→M4 gains have **stacked to ~200%, not 30–40%** —
his maxed M4 Max posts the **highest single-core Geekbench of any Mac ever**, GPU bumping against the **M2 Ultra** (the
Mac Pro's chip). His real-world proof is video editing: a mixed 8K RED + action-cam + Snap-glasses Final Cut project
**exports ~2× faster than his M1 Max**, and the new **Magnetic Mask** (AI roto in Final Cut Pro 11) leans on the neural
engine (M1 Max 2:56 → M4 Max 2:13 → M2 Ultra 1:30; the Intel Xeon Mac Pro a woeful 6:00). Beyond speed, the stack that
convinces him: the new **nano-texture display option** ("my favorite option — it eats reflections in any lighting; all
laptops should have something like this"), **Thunderbolt 5** ("overrated and underrated at the same time" — genuine
future-proofing, but the accessory ecosystem is thin/expensive today), quietly-added **quantum dot displays** (on the
way to OLED), a brighter 1000-nit SDR panel, a better Center Stage webcam, and slightly better battery. His verdict: no
single feature justifies the upgrade, but **stacked together, "you can actually justify an upgrade this year if you're
regularly pushing this laptop to its limits"** — though **"M1 Max is gonna be perfectly fine for another year, no
question."** The closing note ties back to the era's theme: **"we basically have Apple Intelligence to thank"** for the
new **16 GB unified-memory floor** that makes every base Mac a better deal.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-11-18] **The first gen tempting me to upgrade from M1 Max.** "'I've used my M1 Max MacBook Pro for three years
  with no compulsion to upgrade. The M4 generation is the first one I've actually been tempted by — they stacked a
  couple of the right upgrades in the right places for things that make a difference to me.'"
- [2024-11-18] **M1→M4 is ~200%, not 30–40%.** "'M2→M3 were incremental — nobody on those needs to upgrade. But the
  gains from M1 to M4 have stacked up: my maxed M4 Max posts the highest single-core Geekbench of any Mac ever, and the
  GPU bumps against the M2 Ultra (the Mac Pro's chip). Real-world: my mixed 8K RED Final Cut project exports about twice
  as fast as the M1 Max. The bottom line is a ~200% bump, not 30 or 40%.'"
- [2024-11-18] **The nano-texture display is my favorite option — all laptops should have this.** "'The nano-texture
  matte display is new and I love it — it eats reflections and removes lighting distractions in an office, a coffee
  shop, an airplane. It started as a delicate $1,000 Pro Display XDR option, but it's gotten more maintenance-friendly
  (a $100 iPad option people touch constantly). On a laptop this is my favorite option — all laptops should have
  something like this.'"
- [2024-11-18] **Thunderbolt 5 is overrated and underrated at once.** "'Thunderbolt 5 is overrated and underrated at the
  same time — a genuine capability bump (three 6K displays instead of two, faster I/O), great for future-proofing, but
  there are very few TB5 accessories yet and they're expensive. It won't affect most people right away.'"
- [2024-11-18] **Justifiable if you push it — but M1 Max is still fine; thank Apple Intelligence for 16 GB base.** "'No
  single thing — performance, nano-texture, TB5, the brighter quantum-dot display, the webcam — justifies upgrading
  alone. But stacked together, you can actually justify an upgrade this year if you regularly push this laptop to its
  limits (or at least that's what I'm telling myself). M1 Max is perfectly fine for another year, no question. And we
  basically have Apple Intelligence to thank for the new 16 GB unified-memory floor, which makes every base Mac a better
  deal.'"
