# I'm Taking a Break (@mkbhd, 2024-08-31)

## Summary
A Q&A video posted while he's flying to Australia to **compete at the World Ultimate Frisbee Championships for Team USA
Mixed** — the grass team he **finally made in 2024 after trying his entire Ultimate career** (junior worlds u20s and
u23s both rejected; 2023 got him the beach-worlds team (bronze); 2021 club worlds gold with PoNY). "Taking a little
break" while he does. The Twitter-sourced Q&A is dense with persona-defining opinions: on **Ronaldo joining YouTube**
("a testament to where YouTube is at... but pump the brakes on passing MrBeast — I want to see what they actually
post"); on the **Vision Pro** ("my usage fell off a cliff; is it a success? of $3,500, of course it hasn't sold well —
but the interesting one is the **second gen: I suspect it'll be non-Pro, a stripped-down, mass-produced "Apple Vision
Air" for more people — that's what I'm interested in"); the **oldest phone he'd still daily** (OnePlus 7 Pro; Pixel 3
XL); **minimal AI use** ("occasionally for brainstorming / twisting an idea — I write out basically everything myself;
no AI editing, no AI music, no AI images"); his **car fleet** ("**~95% dailying the Turbo S** (12k miles, "everything I
hoped"), 4% R1T, 1% Cybertruck — can't sell the Cybertruck for a year"); the **Magic Mouse / Tim Cook interview** ("Tim
Cook is the CEO — his job is strategy, business, supply chain, not Magic Mouse ergonomics; tech CEOs are **media-trained
PR machines**; **I'm probably done with tech-CEO interviews** — I want to talk to **product people** in the nitty-gritty
and non-tech people with interesting perspectives; I cut ~90% of the Ternus interview"); and **why YouTube is the crown
jewel** ("**stability and money** — people go where the money is; it's the world's video library, too big to fail; the
one thing that could kill it is an **adpocalypse** — and that happened, and advertisers came crawling back because
that's where the eyeballs are"). Plus: **Steph Curry replied to his tweet** during the Olympics.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-08-31] **Made Team USA Mixed after years of trying.** "'I'm in Australia competing at the World Ultimate
  Frisbee Championships for Team USA Mixed — the grass team. I've been trying to play for these teams my entire Ultimate
  career: out of high school junior worlds u20s, then u23s, both got tryouts, didn't make it. 2023 I made the beach-
  worlds team (bronze); we got gold with PoNY at club worlds in 2021. In 2024 I got a tryout for the grass team and
  actually made it.'"
- [2024-08-31] **The Vision Pro's second gen is the interesting one.** "'My Vision Pro usage fell off a cliff after the
  beginning. Is it a success? At $3,500, of course it hasn't sold well — not a shock. But the second generation teaches
  you the most about how a company feels about a product. I suspect the next one isn't "Pro" — it's just Vision (an
  "Apple Vision Air"), a stripped-down, mass-produced version for more people. That's the one I'm interested in.'"
- [2024-08-31] **My AI use is minimal — I write everything myself.** "'I use AI occasionally for brainstorming and
  twisting or rewriting an idea, but I write out basically everything in the actual reviews and topical videos myself. I
  don't use AI to edit anything, no AI-generated music, no AI-generated images — it's just a fun tool to play with.'"
- [2024-08-31] **I'm done with tech-CEO interviews — I want product people.** "'Tim Cook is the CEO — his job is
  strategy, business, supply chain, not Magic Mouse ergonomics. Tech CEOs are, let's be honest, media-trained PR
  machines. Talking Tech started as interviews with anyone with interesting tech perspectives (Kobe, Neil deGrasse
  Tyson, Will Smith), but once you do one CEO, all the PR teams reach out and you fall down that slope. I cut ~90% of
  the Ternus interview and kept the 10% about longevity-vs-repairability. I'm probably done with tech-CEO interviews — I
  want to talk to product people in the nitty-gritty, and non-tech people, for their perspectives.'"
- [2024-08-31] **YouTube is the crown jewel — stability and money; the adpocalypse test.** "'YouTube is the crown jewel
  of video platforms because of stability and money — people go where the money is, and it's been the most stable place
  for discovery and monetization for the longest time (that's why TikTokers move to YouTube, why Ronaldo started a
  channel). It's the world's video library now — too big to fail. The one thing that could take it down is an
  adpocalypse that ruins advertiser trust — and that happened, and months/years later they all came crawling back,
  because that's where the eyeballs are.'"
