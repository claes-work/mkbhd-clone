# On 'Quitting' YouTube (@mkbhd, 2024-01-19)

## Summary
A rare, personal creator-business essay, prompted by a wave of OG YouTubers (Tom Scott, MatPat...) retiring or
cutting back. His thesis, in analogies. **"A dream job is still a job"** (per Tom Scott) — being a creator is like
being a pro athlete (you must be willing to do it for free for a long time to turn passion into a job). **But the
missing piece: "creative jobs don't scale like regular jobs"** — when success unlocks bigger opportunities (a brand
trip, hiring a team), you spend more time on the *scaling* (inbox, invoicing, taxes, managing people) and **less
time on the actual creative thing you fell in love with.** There's **no playbook** (the job is too new). The
**treadmill analogy**: to keep it a job you have to keep moving, and **the real skill is finding the speed that
works for you** (a video how often?) — satisfying both external growth and internal creative goals; **push too fast
and you get burnout** (the Daft Punk "Infinity Repeating" video). Concretely: **"I can't review every phone — I'm
one person, I have to pick one"** (the S24, OnePlus 12, Find 7 all at once). The **Mr Beast blueprint trap**:
following it *will* make you the "Mr Beast of your genre," but you don't realize everything that comes with it. His
closing frame — **the octopus analogy**: a creator does eight full-time jobs at once (writer/shooter/editor/inbox/
taxes/PR...), so **cut off the arms you're bad at and hand them to people who are better** — **but keep your "three
hearts,"** the core parts you fell in love with (different for everyone). "If you ever get to live the dream, be
very deliberate about it."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-01-19] **A dream job is still a job — the pro-athlete analogy.** "'This is a dream job — the most popular
  answer when you ask kids what they want to be is now "YouTuber." But as Tom Scott put it, a dream job is still a
  job — one that keeps getting bigger and more complicated, and I am so tired. It's like being a pro athlete: you
  have to be willing to play for free for a long time to turn the passion into the job.'"
- [2024-01-19] **Creative jobs don't scale like regular jobs.** "'The thing missing from that analogy: creative jobs
  don't scale like regular jobs. When success unlocks a bigger opportunity — a brand trip, hiring a team — you now
  spend time on comms, invoicing, taxes, managing people. The more you scale up to address awesome new
  opportunities, the less of your job is the original thing you fell in love with: being creative.'"
- [2024-01-19] **The treadmill — find your speed, or burn out.** "'It's a treadmill: to keep it a job you have to
  keep moving. The skill is finding the speed that works for you — a video how often? — that satisfies both external
  growth and internal creative goals. Push too fast and it falls apart; that's burnout (like the Daft Punk
  "Infinity Repeating" video). I can't review every phone — I'm one person, I have to pick one.'"
- [2024-01-19] **The Mr Beast blueprint trap.** "'A lot of channels look at what Jimmy's doing as the end goal — if
  I follow the blueprint I'll be the Mr Beast of my genre. And they're right, they will be. But they don't realize
  all the other things that come with that.'"
- [2024-01-19] **The octopus analogy — keep your three hearts.** "'Being a creator is like being an octopus doing
  eight full-time jobs at once — writer, cinematographer, on-camera, editor, inbox, invoicing, taxes, PR. Get help:
  cut off the arms you're bad at and hand them to someone who does it 500× better (like an accountant). But fun
  fact — octopuses have three hearts they can't cut out. Figure out, as early and deliberately as you can, the core
  parts you fell in love with, and keep those. The retirees aren't quitting so much as finding their hearts.'"
