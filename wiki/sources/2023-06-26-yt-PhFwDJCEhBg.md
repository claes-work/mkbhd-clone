# Google Pixel Fold Review: Maybe Next Year! (@mkbhd, 2023-06-26)

## Summary
The full review of Google's first foldable — **"a weird phone... so many things about this thing are unusual."** The
strengths: it optimizes hard toward thin and passport-shaped, making it **"the easiest, best foldable to use while
closed"** (a premium 5.8" 120 Hz outer screen — and he's always said the best foldables must be usable as a normal
phone). But it **"has a much lower ranking for using it while open,"** where the weirdness starts: an
average-but-visible crease, a fold that stops **~1–2° short of flat**, and **bigger top/bottom bezels** (Google's
intentional thin-first hinge choice — and "the Pixel was never about pristine, super-engineered hardware; they flex
on software"). Performance is **weirdly the best of any Pixel** despite the same Tensor G2 (probably the 12 GB RAM),
though the usual **accelerated-aging disclaimer** applies. Battery is a **C-plus** (~4 hrs screen-on). The software
is beautiful (tablet-optimized Google apps, the swipe-up dock) with **one weird gripe: you can't set a different
home screen inside vs outside** — it mirrors them (fine for beginners, "Apple would do the same," but it feels
un-Android). Cameras are **a notch down from the flagship Pixels** (smaller sensors for thinness; he shot a whole
AutoFocus video on it). At **$1,800** it's for early adopters and Pixel fans — **but "weirdly enough, I really
like using this phone,"** precisely because it nails being used closed. "Maybe next year" for the rest.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-06-26] **The best foldable to use closed — weird when open.** "'I stand by it: this is the easiest, best
  foldable to use when it's closed — the most reachable, usable outside screen of any folding phone. I've always
  said the best foldables have to be usable as a regular phone too, and this accomplishes that great. But it has a
  much lower ranking for using it while open — that's where things get weird.'"
- [2023-06-26] **The intentional thin-first trade-offs — and Pixel's software-not-hardware DNA.** "'The bezels are
  bigger because Google biased so hard toward thin, moving hinge components above and below instead of behind — which
  let them fit a real selfie camera. And this is a little along the lines of Google: the Pixel has never been about
  pristine, super-engineered hardware; they flex their strengths on the software side.'"
- [2023-06-26] **The weird software gripe — you can't set different inside/outside home screens.** "'Every other
  foldable lets you set up separate home screens inside and outside. On this one you can't — it keeps them exactly
  mirrored. That's fine for beginners (Apple would do the same), but I can't be the only one spending $1,800 who'd
  like apps on the outside and widgets on the inside. It feels kind of un-Android.'"
- [2023-06-26] **Best-Pixel performance, C-plus battery, cameras a notch down.** "'Performance is weirdly the best of
  any Pixel despite the same Tensor G2 — probably the 12 GB RAM. But it's a C-plus for battery (~4 hrs screen-on),
  and the cameras are a notch down from the flagship Pixels — smaller sensors for thinness (I shot a whole AutoFocus
  video on it; a clear step down). Same thing Samsung deals with on its foldables.'"
- [2023-06-26] **First-gen shortcomings — but I really like it.** "'It's a first-gen device: you can tell they
  learned from other foldables, but there are clear first-gen shortcomings — maybe the next one will be better (the
  crease, bezels, battery, charging, the app situation). And yet, weirdly enough, I really like using this phone —
  specifically because it's so well optimized for using it closed. It's getting closer to that ideal folding phone
  for me.'"
