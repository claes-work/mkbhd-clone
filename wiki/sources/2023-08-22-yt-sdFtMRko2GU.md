# How 3D Printing Changed This Dog's Life! (@mkbhd, 2023-08-22)

## Summary
A human-interest tech story about **Cleo**, a three-legged rescue dog, and **3D Pets** (a New Jersey studio) that
builds **custom 3D-printed prostheses** for animals. The workflow surprised even him with how much tech it involves:
**scan the animal with an iPhone's LiDAR / TrueDepth camera** (via apps like Polycam), refine the fit in custom
design software, then **3D-print the prosthesis** (24-hour print, then sand/torch to soften). The framing beat is a
running theme — **"there's tech in everything"** (every story/angle on the news now has tech in it) — but the real
revelation is a **confession**: **"I never really cared about 3D printing"** despite the hype (the studio's own
printer mostly made "useless trinkets"), and **this is the first time he's seen a situation where 3D printing
clearly makes the most sense for a manufacturing process** — because **every animal's needs are a fingerprint
(size, limb height, weight-bearing), so you can't mass-produce one-size-fits-all**; the bespoke, rapidly-iterable
print is the right tool. 3D Pets has helped 300+ animals (dogs, ducks, a tortoise, a pig, a goat helmet, an elephant
leg). The uplift: it's enabled by **relatively accessible tech** (a phone, an app, consumer-to-industrial printers),
and there's an adjustment/muscle-memory process for the pet — "a huge win for pets like Cleo."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-08-22] **There's tech in everything.** "'I've said it before, but there's tech in everything — every story,
  every angle I see on the news has some tech in it. Even this story surprised me at just how much tech was involved
  in helping dogs like Cleo.'"
- [2023-08-22] **The workflow — iPhone scan, then 3D-print the bespoke prosthesis.** "'They use a smartphone (the
  iPhone's LiDAR or TrueDepth camera) with apps like Polycam to 3D-scan the animal in five minutes, refine the fit
  in custom design software, then 3D-print the prosthesis — a 24-hour print, then sanded and torched to soften.'"
- [2023-08-22] **The confession — the first time 3D printing clearly makes the most sense.** "'A confession: I never
  really cared about 3D printing. There was a ton of hype, and every time it was brought up I couldn't find a reason
  to care — we got a printer at the studio and made useless trinkets. This is the first time I've seen a situation
  where 3D printing clearly makes the most sense for a manufacturing process.'"
- [2023-08-22] **Because bespoke, not one-size-fits-all.** "'Every animal's situation is a fingerprint — the reason
  for the missing limb, the size, how far off the ground it ends, how much weight it can bear. You can't mass-produce
  one-size-fits-all, so the 3D-printing process allows rapid iteration of a custom, comfortable fit for each animal.
  That's why it just makes the most sense for once.'"
- [2023-08-22] **Enabled by accessible tech — a huge win.** "'It's super cool that it's enabled by relatively
  accessible tech — a phone in our pocket, a computer app, a couple pieces of software, and 3D printers. And there's
  an adjustment process — like crutches, the pet slowly remaps its muscle memory to trust the limb. A huge win for
  pets like Cleo.'"
