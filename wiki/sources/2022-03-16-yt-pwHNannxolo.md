# iPad Air M1 Review: Don't Choose Wrong! (@mkbhd, 2022-03-16)

## Summary
A review of the M1 iPad Air framed as a lesson in **pricing ladders.** Opening with a study from *The Myth of Fair
Value* (decoy pricing — adding a cheap or an ultra-premium option reshapes which tier people pick, always to
maximize revenue), he argues **pricing ladders are designed to nudge you up rung by rung.** The Air is "one of the
stranger Apple products in a while" because **it overlaps the iPad Pro more than ever** — same dimensions, camera,
battery, and now the same M1 chip. He walks the ladder trap (a stingy 64 GB base → the only upgrade is 256 GB for
$150 → now you're $50 from a Pro). His one real reason to still pick the Pro is **ProMotion** ("the M1 doesn't feel
as fast at 60 Hz"). The twist: **putting a Pro feature (the M1) in the cheaper model removes a reason to upgrade —
and may nudge some people DOWN.** Ends frustrated that **iPadOS still doesn't take advantage of the M1.**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-03-16] **Pricing ladders are decoy-engineered to maximize revenue.** "'From *The Myth of Fair Value*: offer
  a standard + premium and 4/5 pick premium; add a cheap third option and the cheap one is ignored but people shift
  to the middle as the "value"; swap the cheap option for an ultra-premium one and the ratio flips back plus some
  buy the most expensive — the highest total revenue. **Pricing ladders are very real and designed to get you to
  spend as much as possible.**'"
- [2022-03-16] **The Air overlaps the Pro more than ever.** "'This is the most I've ever seen one Apple product
  overlap another. Last year's Air was already "the one true iPad Pro killer" (80% of the tablet for $200 less);
  **now the Air also gets the massively-overkill M1 — one more thing they do exactly the same** (same dimensions,
  cases, primary camera, battery, and chip as the Pro).'"
- [2022-03-16] **The ladder trap — 64 GB base nudges you up to the Pro.** "'Watch the ladder: **the $599 base Air is
  only 64 GB (not great in 2022 — we have 20 GB games), so the only storage upgrade is 256 GB for +$150 → now
  you're in $750 territory → and for $50 more you might as well get a Pro.** They nudge you up rung by rung.'"
- [2022-03-16] **ProMotion is the one real reason for the Pro.** "'The only reason I still pick the Pro as the
  better tablet is **ProMotion — the 120 Hz makes everything smoother; the M1 theoretically closes the performance
  gap, but it doesn't FEEL as fast at 60 Hz** (you could put a 3090 in here and the big animations wouldn't feel as
  smooth). The rest (an extra ultrawide + LiDAR, quad speakers, Thunderbolt, Face ID) are all pretty minor. For me,
  50 extra bucks for ProMotion is worth it.'"
- [2022-03-16] **A trickle-down feature removes a reason to upgrade — and iPadOS wastes the chip.** "'The funny part:
  **putting a Pro feature (the M1) into the cheaper model just removes a reason to upgrade — and may nudge a small
  number of people DOWN** (until the M2 Pro comes out). A great spec bump — but it **makes me even more frustrated
  that iPadOS isn't evolving to fully take advantage of it** (another rant for another video).'"

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "Pricing ladders are very real and very much designed to get you to spend as much as possible and make those
> companies as much money as they can."
> "Putting one of the features from the more expensive model in the less expensive model just removes one reason to
> upgrade — and may actually encourage a small number of people to move down."
