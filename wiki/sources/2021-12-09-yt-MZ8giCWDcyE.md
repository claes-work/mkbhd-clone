# Smartphone Cameras vs Reality! (@mkbhd, 2021-12-09)

## Summary
A landmark essay on **computational photography bending reality.** Starting from a concrete example (the iPhone
13 Pro's 3x button sometimes just crops the main sensor because "the camera thinks it knows better than you — and
usually does"), he argues smartphone cameras have **gone past smart to bending reality.** The core tension:
**enthusiasts want the neutral, information-rich photo so THEY keep control to edit; most people want the finished
photo straight out of camera with zero edits — which means giving up all control, so your photos become "a product
of someone else's vision"** (each company's tuned pipeline reflects its biases). Traces the escalation — the
**Huawei moon-mode** (superimposing a stored moon), Xiaomi beauty filters that reshape your face, the Pixel 6's
background face-swap, FaceTime eye-contact — to a future where cameras output **"captures of moments in time that
never really happened."** The open question: **how far is too far — are you okay with photos further and further
from reality, bending the definition of a photo?**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-12-09] **The camera thinks it knows better than you.** "'Hit 3x on the iPhone 13 Pro and sometimes it just
  crops the main camera instead of switching to the telephoto — because **the camera thinks it knows better than
  you, and it usually does** (the tele's smaller sensor would be noisier in low light). Smartphone cameras are
  smart — but they've gone past smart. **They're bending reality.**'"
- [2021-12-09] **Control vs the finished photo.** "'In the tech/photography world we want the neutral photo with
  more information — the better photo to us, because we can edit it exactly how we want; **we want that control.**
  But most people just want to point, shoot, and post with zero edits — to them that IS a great camera. **The
  danger of giving up all the control is your photos become a product of someone else's vision** — every company's
  image pipeline is tuned by people and reflects their biases. Which one is real? Which is accurate? Maybe it
  doesn't matter.'"
- [2021-12-09] **The moon-mode and how-far-is-too-far.** "'The Huawei P30 Pro's moon mode: everyone's moon shots
  looked suspiciously similar — Android Authority concluded it superimposes a stored image of the moon. Clever
  (the moon is tidally locked, so one stored image works). **But it raises a fair question: how far is too far?**
  Xiaomi phones make skies bluer and grass greener; their beauty filters literally move your hairline and reshape
  your nose, chin, and lips, out of the box.'"
- [2021-12-09] **Captures that never happened.** "'The most powerful adjustments are the ones that happen when you
  don't even know it — the Pixel 6 merging a non-blurry face from the ultrawide onto your subject; FaceTime's eye-
  contact moving your pupils. **I predict a future group-selfie feature that swaps in everyone's best smiling,
  non-blinking face automatically** (a 2012 Nokia Lumia already had a version). Eventually **these cameras are
  outputting captures of moments in time that never really happened** — the whole world becoming AI-recognizable
  QR codes the camera morphs into recognized situations.'"
- [2021-12-09] **What is a photo?** "'I don't have a solution for this eerily dystopian future, but I wonder — **are
  you okay with smartphone cameras spitting out finished images that are further and further from reality? They're
  basically bending the definition of a photo.**'"
