# What Is Right To Repair? (@mkbhd, 2021-05-08)

## Summary
A landmark explainer on the right-to-repair movement, framed by **"once you buy something, do you really own it?"**
The durable theses: **repair benefits users (saves money + the environment/e-waste) while manufacturers want
control** (to keep earning money on a product they already sold); Apple's certified-repair program is **"virtually
worthless"**; the real problem isn't that tech is smaller/harder to repair — it's **manufacturers telling parts
suppliers "you can't sell it to me"**; and **as tech gets better it gets more integrated and less repairable** (a
crossroads, per the M1 Macs). Includes Louis Rossmann + Simone Giertz guest segments (quarantined).

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-05-08] **The frame — accepted limits vs. the repair block.** "'Once you buy something, do you really own
  it? We accept some limits (speed limits, a printer won't copy money). **Right-to-repair asks: if it breaks,
  should you be allowed to fix it? That should be an easy yes — and there's no law against it — but the companies
  that make our stuff try to stop you before you get that far.**' (A slightly misleading name — you ARE allowed;
  the barrier is access.)'"
- [2021-05-08] **The two sides — user (money + environment) vs. manufacturer (control = money).** "'**User side:
  pop off the broken screen, buy a new one, keep using it — repair saves money, cuts e-waste in a throwaway
  economy, and lets someone else benefit from a used phone.** Manufacturer side (Apple as the example): **once
  they sell you the iPhone, their interest is to retain as much control as possible** — the accessory market
  (MFi cut), the services, and the repairs. "In Apple's dream world there are no independent repair shops."'"
- [2021-05-08] **Apple's certified program is "virtually worthless" (via Louis Rossmann — guest).** "'Rossmann
  (quarantined): the program has so many barriers it's "virtually useless" — batteries and screens only, screens
  priced above a used phone, unannounced audits, fines for having chips Apple doesn't want you to have, and
  **Apple can inspect you for up to five years even after you leave** ("imagine drug testing enforced five years
  after you quit").'"
- [2021-05-08] **THE REAL PROBLEM — "you can't sell me the part."** "'Apple (and others) make small changes to an
  off-the-shelf part, then contract the supplier to **not sell it to anyone else** ("we'd like 20 million of this
  chip, but you can't sell to anyone else"). Rossmann's key distinction: **repairs are harder either because of
  technological progress (my responsibility to figure out) OR because the manufacturer just wants them to be —
  the problem is the latter: "don't tell the company that made this part they're not allowed to sell it to me."**'"
  A promotable articulation of the core issue.
- [2021-05-08] **Not just Apple — John Deere and Tesla.** "'**John Deere: farmers own the tractor, but the
  software is copyrighted, so a breakdown locks them out — farmers hack/pirate to repair.** **Tesla: hard-to-get
  parts, blacklisting salvaged VINs from Superchargers/updates.** (Simone Giertz — guest, quarantined — recounts
  the fear of building "truckla" and a service tech saying "I know what you've done to your car.") Tesla's counter
  is safety — a fair argument — but "it's all about control, extending the time you can earn money on a product
  you sold."'"
- [2021-05-08] **THE CROSSROADS — better tech is less repairable (the M1 Macs).** "'As tech gets better it gets
  **more tightly integrated — and less repairable.** The **M1 Macs are the best ever precisely because the SoC
  fuses CPU/GPU/RAM — nothing to pop out and upgrade.** Does better/faster tech mean near-impossible repairs? That
  was my sticking point. **We need well-written legislation that blocks the anti-competitive behavior without
  mandating a design aesthetic — we deserve the right to repair what we own, and so does the environment (one
  planet; these companies paint themselves green).**'"

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "Once you buy something, do you really own it?"
> "As tech gets better... it's getting more well-connected and more integrated, and so as that happens, all the
> tech is getting better — that's also making it less repairable at the same time."
> "We deserve the right to repair the things we own — simple as that. And by the way, the environment deserves
> that too."
