# OnePlus Concept One Phone: Disappearing Cameras? (@mkbhd, 2020-01-07)

## Summary
A CES 2020 hands-on with the OnePlus Concept One (McLaren partnership) — a OnePlus 7T Pro whose rear glass uses
**electrochromatic tech to hide the cameras** (and double as an ND filter). A "classic CES concept" not meant to
ship, but a genuinely forward-thinking idea about the camera-bump problem.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2020-01-07] **The disappearing-cameras tech.** "'They **shrank the electrochromatic glass** from McLaren
  supercar sunroofs (which I showed in the McLaren GT quirks video, [[2019-12-05-yt-nmnTAOU44SI]]) into the phone's
  back glass — **18 months of work.** Current passes through it to create shade; open the camera and **0.7 s later
  the glass goes clear** (a neat cross-dissolve). ~90% opaque when off; **it just makes the phone look cleaner.**'"
- [2020-01-07] **The bonus for video nerds — built-in ND.** "'In pro mode there's an **ND button — three stops of
  physical ND from the electrochromatic glass** (like sunglasses for your camera), useful in bright light so you
  don't have to mess with shutter speed. **Three stops of physical ND built into a phone — that's not something
  we've seen before** (I'd expect it from a RED Hydrogen).'" A production-minded highlight.
- [2020-01-07] **The forward-thinking framing — hiding the camera-bump problem.** "'Selling this now would be
  extremely expensive (why it's a concept). But as **phones get more and more cameras with uglier bumps**, this
  could make sense — **flatten the glass, hide however many cameras you want, and the design never gets weirder.**
  Imagine a future Galaxy with a dozen cameras you never have to see.'" A dated, prescient design observation.
- [2020-01-07] **The CES-concept framing.** "'Classic CES: a **concept of something cool you might never sell** —
  but if you do, that'd be cool — and it makes a nice wave of press.'" His even read on concept-tech theater.

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "Three stops of physical ND built into a camera phone — that's not something we've seen before. That's why it's
> so impressive."
> "As phones get more and more cameras and uglier weirder-looking camera bumps, this becomes something that
> actually makes a little more sense."
