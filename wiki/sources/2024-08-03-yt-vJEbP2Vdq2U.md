# What's going on with Windows Laptops? (@mkbhd, 2024-08-03)

## Summary
A "back from the Windows sidelines" review of the **matte-black Microsoft Surface Laptop with a Qualcomm Snapdragon X
Elite** — an **ARM** Windows laptop — asking: **have Windows laptops finally caught up to the Apple Silicon leap?** He
frames Apple's Intel→ARM transition as "crazy successful" (efficiency + vertical integration), notes Windows tried and
failed before (the Surface Pro X), and finds this attempt **has gone way better**. The **big win is efficiency →
battery life**: a quoted 23 hours translates to worry-free all-day mixed use (high brightness, browsing, video,
research), ending the day at ~40% — it even outlasts the M3 MacBook Air on some synthetic benchmarks. The **second win
is well-optimized everyday performance** — a capable $2,000 14" laptop that handles Affinity photo editing and Office
without breaking a sweat, smooth from full battery to nearly dead. But the catch — same as Apple's transition — is
**app compatibility**, in three tiers: **native** (Photoshop/Lightroom CC, Chrome, Spotify, Zoom — great, easy on
battery), **emulated** (Lightroom Classic, TickTick — works but choppy and battery-hungry), and **broken** (Arc,
Google Drive desktop, VMware, many VPNs, and games with anti-cheat that won't run). His bottom line: **"the main
question you should consider is: does the software you use work on ARM?"** — depending on who you are, you're totally
covered or totally out of luck. Other notes: excellent Surface build quality, but **no OLED option** (you trade OLED
for a 120 Hz LCD), a built-in GPU (not for maxing out Elden Ring), lots-of-cores-but-weaker-single-core, and it's a
**Copilot+ PC** (a dedicated NPU + Copilot button). Verdict: **"a good start — actually what we've been waiting for"**
— if your software runs on ARM.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-08-03] **The battery-life quantum leap is real.** "'The mega leap forward is efficiency — same battery, way
  better battery life. The quote went from 19 hours (last Intel Surface) to 23; for me that's worry-free all-day mixed
  use ending at ~40%. It even outlasts the M3 MacBook Air on some synthetic benchmarks. For a 14" laptop, I just don't
  think about charging.'"
- [2024-08-03] **Everyday performance is well-optimized.** "'Not high-end gaming performance — I mean general smoothness
  and rock-solid consistency across everyday tasks. A $2,000 14" laptop that handles Affinity photo editing and all of
  Office without breaking a sweat, plugged in or not, snappy from full battery to nearly dead.'"
- [2024-08-03] **The app-compatibility problem — same as Apple's ARM transition.** "'The number-one difficulty of
  changing your computer's architecture is app compatibility — three tiers, just like the Mac transition: native
  (Photoshop, Chrome, Spotify, Zoom — great, easy on battery), emulated (Lightroom Classic, TickTick — works but choppy
  and battery-hungry), and broken (Arc, Google Drive desktop, VMware, many VPNs, games with anti-cheat that won't
  run).'"
- [2024-08-03] **The question is whether your software runs on ARM.** "'If you're considering a Windows ARM laptop, look
  up the specific apps you need and make sure they're at least compatible or emulatable. Depending on who you are, you
  could be totally covered or totally out of luck. That's the main question: does the software you use work on ARM?'"
- [2024-08-03] **A good start — but no OLED, and it's a Copilot+ PC.** "'Build quality is awesome, but at this price I'd
  have liked an OLED option — instead you trade OLED for a 120 Hz LCD. It's a built-in GPU, not for maxing out Elden
  Ring; the chip has lots of cores but weaker single-core. And it's one of the first Copilot+ PCs — a dedicated NPU and
  a Copilot button. Overall, a good start — it's actually the premium matte-black ARM Windows laptop we've been waiting
  for.'"
