# I Tried a Secret Google Project! (Project Starline) (@mkbhd, 2023-05-18)

## Summary
A hands-on with **Google Project Starline** — **"this might be the most impressive tech demo I've ever seen"** — a
new, more compact **light-field 3D video-calling booth**: you sit across from a 65" light-field display and array of
color cameras/mics that use AI to build a real-time 3D model of each person, so it feels like the other person is
physically across a table (it shows a different image to each eye, plus head-tracking and spatial audio). The catch:
**it doesn't translate on camera** (depth comes from the gap between your two eyes; a lens is one eye), which is why
Google has guarded it — he rigs a cardboard face with a lens to give viewers the closest-possible glimpse. It's
genuinely immersive ("I felt like I could reach out and high-five him"), with only slight edge-glitching keeping it
out of the uncanny valley. **The real thesis is skeptical, though:** "what is this for? We don't really know yet" —
and, crucially, **"regular people are not very fast to pay extra for higher fidelity"** — **"convenience is king,"
and the cutoff for acceptable quality is surprisingly low for the masses** (Spotify-over-Bluetooth, AirPods,
good-enough phone cameras). So while businesses use booths to avoid flights, FaceTime/Zoom is "fine for most
people." Still, he's excited to watch the tech get cheaper and simpler.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-05-18] **The most impressive tech demo I've ever seen.** "'This might be the most impressive tech demo I've
  ever seen — and one of the hardest to explain on video. A light-field display literally shows a different image to
  my left eye and my right eye, and all the computing renders the person into a real-time 3D model from just camera
  info, with head-tracking. Even knowing it's 3D, I felt like I could reach out and high-five him.'"
- [2023-05-18] **It doesn't translate on camera — depth comes from two eyes.** "'It won't look right on camera
  because depth comes from the distance between your eyes — a camera lens is one eye. That's why Google's been super
  protective. I rigged a cardboard face with a lens through it to give you the closest glimpse, but even that doesn't
  fully capture it.'"
- [2023-05-18] **What is this for? We don't really know yet.** "'You might be thinking what I was: this is super cool,
  but what is it for? The answer is we don't really know yet. A few companies use booths for meetings — better than a
  Zoom call, maybe — but it only tracks one person at a time, and it's still too expensive and hard to get to.'"
- [2023-05-18] **Convenience is king — the masses won't pay for higher fidelity.** "'If I know anything about
  bleeding-edge tech, it's that regular people — the masses — are not very fast to pay extra for higher fidelity. The
  cutoff for acceptable quality is surprisingly low: Spotify over Bluetooth is good enough for most, AirPods are the
  most popular headphones, phone cameras are good enough for 99% of photos. Convenience is king. That's why FaceTime
  and Zoom — a flat, low-res square — are fine for most people.'"
- [2023-05-18] **But the tech will get better.** "'This will get more realistic, cheaper, simpler, and better — from
  the size of a room to the size of an easel, maybe someday a TV with a backpack. I'm really looking forward to
  keeping an eye on this and the whole light-field-display space.'"
