# Who Cares About the Metaverse? (@mkbhd, 2022-10-28)

## Summary
His metaverse thesis, built on hands-on time with Meta's Quest Pro (Zuckerberg showed him before the keynote). The
economic frame: **the internet is controlled by two things — ads and devices.** Google owns ads; Facebook has
peaked (ATT's "ask app not to track" took a multibillion-dollar bite), so **Meta is going all-in on VR/AR to
control the next foundation** — the software, hardware, ads, and what reaches your eyeballs. The Quest Pro is
**"actually pretty good"** (comfortable balance, real face/eye tracking) but chunky with ~1.5 hr battery. He
decodes Meta's whole plan: **step 1, think of what people do on the internet; step 2, make it in VR and pour money
in** — and admits **some VR applications are "scary good"** (photorealistic avatar meetings, virtual multi-monitor
work). His one problem: **one company controlling the foundation of this "second-generation internet" is a terrible
idea** (eye-tracking to serve you ads is too much). His durable value line: **"when companies truly compete, that
is when we win."** Apple's coming headset will make Meta nervous.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-10-28] **The internet is controlled by ads and devices.** "'The internet is mostly controlled by two things:
  **ads and devices.** Google owns ads (for all we talk Pixel/Chrome, it's an ads business at its core). Facebook
  wanted a popular device and never made one — and now, peaked and in slow decline (ATT took a multibillion-dollar
  chunk of their ads), **they're moving to the next big thing to regain control: VR/AR, where they can own the
  software, hardware, ads, and what reaches people's eyeballs.** Cue the rebrand to Meta and billions poured in.'"
- [2022-10-28] **The Quest Pro is actually pretty good.** "'The $1,500 Quest Pro is **actually pretty good** — more
  comfortable (components split so the balance point is centered), an XR2+, 1920×1800/eye at 90 fps, decent color
  pass-through, and **real face/eye tracking** (an avatar mirrors my micro-expressions in real time — pretty cool).
  Still chunky, doesn't fold, ~1.5 hr battery.'"
- [2022-10-28] **Meta's whole plan — make it in VR, pour money in.** "'Watching the (awkward) keynote crystallizes
  the plan: **step 1, think of everything people do on the internet; step 2, make those things in VR and pour tons
  of money in** — over and over (Zoom calls → VR meeting rooms; work → a virtual office; your computer → giant
  virtual monitors, native Mac support). They paid Microsoft (Office in workrooms) and Accenture (thousands of
  headsets). The startup trope: build any VR thing and you'll get acquired by Meta.'"
- [2022-10-28] **Some VR apps are scary good.** "'My hot take: **some of these VR applications are scary good and
  starting to look better than the non-VR version.** The cringey Wii-avatar meetings are a stopgap — but the
  **photorealistic live-avatar demo** (phone scan → a realistic you with eye/face tracking, relative audio, lean-
  over-and-whisper) is next level. If Meta makes enough of these real, they make the metaverse happen.'"
- [2022-10-28] **The one problem — and competition is how we win.** "'My one problem: **one company controlling the
  entire foundation of this second-generation internet is a terrible idea** — Meta wants to build the next
  foundation and own everything that grows on it (imagine eye-tracking to make sure you see certain ads — too much
  to root for). If any company can push VR over the hump it's Meta — but **"when companies truly compete, that is
  when we win"** (real incentive to get better). Apple's coming headset will make Meta nervous.'"
