# What Happened to OnePlus? (@mkbhd, 2021-01-12)

## Summary
An explainer on OnePlus's arc from upstart "flagship killer" to a full multi-phone lineup — framed by the **"you
either die a hero or live long enough to see yourself become the villain"** enthusiast-brand theory. The durable
lessons: **enthusiast brands almost always eventually disappoint** (either they go mainstream, or they stay niche
and die), so **"don't be a fanboy — be a fan of the products themselves."** Plus the structural fact: OnePlus is a
**BBK sub-brand alongside Oppo, Vivo, and Realme.**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-01-12] **The OnePlus One — one of the phones of the decade.** "'The OnePlus One caught on instantly — the
  original **flagship killer**: a young, hungry upstart with startup energy, near-stock CyanogenMod software, no
  huge marketing spend, listening to forums, and an incredible price-to-performance ratio at $299. **If you're
  listing the best phones of the last decade, it has to be on it.**'"
- [2021-01-12] **The structural fact — OnePlus is a BBK sub-brand (like Oppo/Vivo/Realme).** "'Keep this in the
  back of your head: **OnePlus is owned/managed by BBK Electronics, which also runs Oppo, Vivo, and Realme** —
  independently run, but related like brothers. **A lot of OnePlus phones are also Oppo phones** (the One ≈ Oppo
  Find 7; the 6T ≈ Oppo R17; Dash Charge = rebranded Oppo VOOC). OnePlus has taken pages from Oppo's playbook
  since the very beginning.'"
- [2021-01-12] **Why the drift is inevitable — the enthusiast market is a tiny fraction.** "'The one-hard-hitting-
  phone-per-year model **isn't sustainable by itself — the enthusiast market is a tiny fraction of the whole.**
  So prices crept up ($299 → $899), and they added T-versions, Pro/non-Pro, the fashion-focused X, and the Nord
  budget line — leveraging enthusiast status to sell to everyone.'"
- [2021-01-12] **THE THEORY — an enthusiast brand can't not eventually disappoint you (credit: Tech Altar).** "'Per
  Tech Altar's enthusiast-brands theory: **there's almost no way an enthusiast brand doesn't eventually disappoint
  you** — either they move on to the mainstream (a little disappointing) or they keep chasing the fickle, tiny
  enthusiast crowd and can't sustain a business, and they die (also disappointing).'"
- [2021-01-12] **THE MORAL — "don't be a fanboy; be a fan of the products."** "'**Don't become such a fan of a
  company that they can eventually betray you — be a fan of the products themselves.** If a company you like drops
  a bad product, consider other options. Simple, but a lot of people don't have that luxury (many early OnePlus
  people have left the company).' The best-bang-for-buck / most-enthusiast-focused spot is now "new shoes to be
  filled" — a rotating seat (Redmi, Poco, new brands).'"
