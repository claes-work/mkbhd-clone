# Rabbit R1: Barely Reviewable (@mkbhd, 2024-04-30)

## Summary
A review of the **$200 Rabbit R1** — "another AI in a box, a sign of the times," with a lot in common (good and bad)
with the **Humane AI Pin**. A Teenage-Engineering-designed bright-orange plastic cube: one push-to-ask button, a small
touchscreen (mostly unusable — locked to terminal typing), a scroll wheel (no haptics, protrudes, "surprisingly
frustrating"), a swiveling vision camera, and a SIM tray. It answers **quicker than the Humane Pin** (a very low bar),
but the flaws rhyme: **~4-hour battery** that drains while idle, missing basics (**no alarms, timers, photos, video,
email, calendar**), and it **hallucinates confidently**. Its two supposed differentiators: the **$200 no-subscription
price** (vs. the Pin's $700 + $24/mo), and the **Large Action Model (LAM)** — an agent meant to *use apps like a
human* (mouse/keyboard, not an API). He calls the LAM **"a really, really cool idea"** — but reality: only **four apps
(Spotify, Uber, DoorDash, Midjourney)**, already getting things wrong; the promised 800 apps, generative UI, and
**Teach Mode** are all "in the works." The centerpiece is a broader indictment: **"a lot of these tech companies are
developing tech backwards"** — ship at full price, then deliver the half-baked thing and iterate — across games, cars,
phones, and now AI gadgets at "the apex of this horrible trend," which **makes them nearly impossible to review**. His
standing advice, hardest to follow here because the promise is so big: **"buy the product based on what it is today,
not what it's promised to be."** He *wants* a truly personal AI assistant (which **"needs to know everything about
you"**), and sees the Rabbit chasing a **Tesla-Autopilot-style data flywheel** — but its problem is **chicken-and-egg:
unlike early Teslas, there's no other reason to own it today.**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-04-30] **Another AI in a box — quicker but still bad.** "'It's a virtual assistant in a different box — a
  Teenage-Engineering plastic cube you carry like a phone. It answers noticeably quicker than the Humane Pin, but
  that's a very low bar, and it's bad at a lot of the same stuff: ~4-hour battery that drains while idle, no alarms,
  timers, photos, video, email or calendar, and it still hallucinates confidently.'"
- [2024-04-30] **The Large Action Model is a cool idea — barely delivered.** "'An LLM turns your words into words; a
  Large Action Model is supposed to turn your words into actions — use apps like a human would, with a virtual mouse
  and keyboard, not a restrictive API. It's a really, really cool idea. But right now there are exactly four apps
  (Spotify, Uber, DoorDash, Midjourney), already getting things wrong. The 800 trained apps, generative UI, and Teach
  Mode are all "in the works."'"
- [2024-04-30] **Companies are developing tech backwards.** "'It used to be: make the thing, then put it on sale. Now
  it's: put it on sale, then deliver the half-baked thing and iterate. Games ship as full-price "alphas," cars ship
  with features "coming soon," every phone launch has one major feature that isn't ready — and these AI products are
  the apex of the trend. You pay full price for something borderline non-functional versus the promises. It makes them
  nearly impossible to review.'"
- [2024-04-30] **Buy it for what it is today, not what it's promised to be.** "'My advice, and it's the hardest thing
  to do in this category because the promise is so big: buy the product based on what it is today, not what it's
  promised to be in the future.'"
- [2024-04-30] **A good assistant needs to know everything about you.** "'I genuinely want a super-personalized AI
  assistant that can do what a human assistant does. But whether human or virtual, a good assistant needs to know
  everything about you — your preferences, location, what you're doing, every little thing. That's an enormous amount
  of data, and it'll take a lot of time and effort to get there.'"
- [2024-04-30] **The chicken-and-egg vs. the Tesla data flywheel.** "'Rabbit wants to use the low price to get millions
  of units out beta-testing so the model gets good — like Tesla shipped cars people bought for other reasons (good EV,
  good range, good charging) and quietly collected Autopilot data. The Rabbit's problem is it doesn't have that other
  reason to own it today. It's a chicken-and-egg problem.'"
