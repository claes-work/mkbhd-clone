# Why Did LG Phones Really Die? (@mkbhd, 2021-04-06)

## Summary
A landmark commentary on LG exiting smartphones — "LG died a hero." The durable theses: **LG made a lot of
genuinely first-of-its-kind, low-key influential phones** (capacitive touch, dual-core, dual-camera, tap-to-wake,
QHD, ultrawide, triple-focal-lengths) **but never learned to SELL them** (bad naming, forgettable "washer-and-
dryer" marketing); the crucial **innovation-vs-recommendation distinction** (crediting a new idea ≠ recommending
the whole package to buy); the structural reason — **LG had no steady money-maker to subsidize a risk-taking
line**; and **innovation needs a balance of stability and consistency.** Losing a competitor is "an L for all of
us."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-04-06] **"LG died a hero" — and was low-key hugely influential.** "'"You either die a hero or live long
  enough to become the villain" — **LG just died a hero.** People forget LG made phones that directly shaped the
  one you're holding: the **LG Prada (first capacitive touchscreen), Optimus 2X (first dual-core + 1080p),
  Optimus 3D (first dual rear camera), G2 (first tap-to-wake), G3 (first mass QHD), G5 (first ultrawide), G6
  (first 18:9), V40 (first triple-focal-length).** Throwing stuff at the wall — and a lot stuck.'"
- [2021-04-06] **The failure — they couldn't SELL them (naming + forgettable marketing).** "'LG made decent
  phones on paper, but **phone-purchase decisions aren't made on paper, and they did not know how to sell them.
  The most basic marketing art is naming — "LG V60 ThinQ 5G" — the extra words don't mean anything to anyone.**
  And forgettable marketing — "like a washer and dryer," same as their appliances. Hard to characterize.'"
- [2021-04-06] **THE KEY DISTINCTION — innovation vs. recommendation.** "'There's a big difference between **sharing
  a product / crediting a new idea, and reviewing and recommending it to buy for most people — and phones that do
  well in one bucket often don't in the other** (see Sony). I did ~20 LG videos and credited the innovation (the
  self-healing G-Flex, the modular G5, the V40 cameras) — **but as an overall package, LG phones were always a
  little tough to recommend to large groups** (the V60's 60 Hz flagship screen; the Wing's 765G at $1,000; the G8
  "jack of all trades, master of none").'" A promotable reviewing-framework distinction.
- [2021-04-06] **The innovation paradox + the structural reason LG couldn't afford it.** "'**The most innovative,
  unproven stuff you do is almost by definition useful to the smallest number of people.** Normally that's fine —
  a company with money-making flagships designates one line to take the risks (Samsung's foldables, Xiaomi's Mix,
  Oppo group's Vivo). **But LG had no steady money-maker to subsidize a risk-taking line — their main line WAS the
  risk-taking one.** So exiting was a straight business decision (to focus on EV components, appliances, B2B).'" A
  sharp structural analysis.
- [2021-04-06] **The moral + "losing LG is an L for all of us."** "'The moral: **innovation and risk-taking is
  great, but it requires a balance of stability, long-term consistency, and predictability** (LG's dropped-next-
  year features + bad update record undermined that). And the real bummer: **losing LG is an L for all of us —
  one less set of cool ideas tried, one less competitor to keep the big companies sharp.** On "did YouTubers kill
  LG?" — no; the truth is more nuanced; this was a business decision long in the making.'"

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "There's a big difference between sharing a product with the world and giving it credit for something new...
> versus reviewing and actually recommending a product overall to buy for a purchase decision."
> "The most innovative, unique, unproven stuff that you do in your gadget is almost by definition useful to the
> smallest number of people."
> "Losing LG is an L for all of us... that's one less competitor to incentivize the other bigger companies to
> really keep their stuff together and win over our business."
