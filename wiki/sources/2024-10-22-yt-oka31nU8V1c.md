## Key claims (Marques-attributed only)
- [2024-10-22] **Computational-photography thesis:** **"Physics is the reason [phones]
  will never actually look the same [as big-sensor cameras], but compute is the reason why
  they look closer and closer every day."** A bigger sensor + bigger lens gives a shallower
  depth of field every time; phones with tiny sensors "have to do a lot of compute to end
  up trying to look like a big sensor" (sharpening faces, noise reduction). (tech-reviews)
- [2024-10-22] **His tells in the blind test:** big sensors give a **natural, smooth bokeh
  fall-off**; small sensors give a "foggy," compressed out-of-focus gradient ("a layer of
  dew"). The **iPhone reliably brightens faces** "brighter than it probably needs to be"
  (a recurring pattern across ~3 years of his blind tests); at 5×, phones keep more of the
  subject in focus (smaller sensor) where a big camera throws parts out of focus. Marques
  got the hardest one right when others missed. (tech-reviews)
- [2024-10-22] Honest caveat on the whole exercise: the differences require **pixel-
  peeping** — "if I wasn't pixel-peeping, I'd be like 'oh, that's fine'"; on Instagram
  (compression + small screen) it'd be far harder to tell. The studio still shoots on real
  cameras "because we're nerds about that sort of stuff." (tech-reviews — the honest-about-
  the-limits reflex) (attribution: uncertain on exact speaker for this specific caveat, but
  consistent with Marques)
