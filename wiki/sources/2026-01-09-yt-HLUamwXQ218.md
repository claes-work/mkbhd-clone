# The Problem with this "Ultra Luxury" Smartphone

## Summary

An investigative "the problem with…" exposé of the **Vertu Agent Q**, a $5,380 "ultra
luxury" phone marketed as "the world's first AI agent phone for entrepreneurs." Marques
opens with a price-guessing game (the specs read like an $800–1,000 phone), then digs in —
and "the more I dig into it, the funnier it gets." Genuinely nice hardware (calfskin
leather, Swiss-hinge "falcon wing" SIM trays) sits atop a fake-variable-aperture camera, a
made-up chip name, an AI-slop website, and a "24/7 AI concierge" that turns out to be a
human in China who took ~7 hours to order a $29 t-shirt. Verdict: "a crudely skinned
Android phone designed to take advantage of people with way more dollars than cents."

## Key claims

- **[2026-01-09]** The Vertu Agent Q cost him **$5,380 + tax + shipping** for roughly
  flagship-level specs (Snapdragon 8 Elite, 1TB/16GB, a 1080p 120Hz AMOLED, a 5,565mAh
  battery) — Vertu's site goes up to a **$50,000 phone**. Unlike the Caviar phones (whose
  angle was material insanity — diamonds, moon rock), Vertu's pitch is "a luxury AI
  experience." (paraphrase)
- **[2026-01-09]** Real hardware quirks: a V-shaped body, **wine-red calfskin leather** back,
  stainless rails with exposed screws, and **"falcon wing" SIM trays on Swiss hinges** (a
  toolless SIM tray he genuinely likes) — but **no wireless charging and no IP rating.** The
  extra weight "feels nice — the opposite of the iPhone Air effect," but "it's not $5,000
  nice." (paraphrase)
- **[2026-01-09]** The camera claims are fabricated: the site touts a **"world's first
  mechanical zoom lens"** (there's no zoom — it's a fixed lens) and a **"mechanical variable
  aperture f/1.59–f/4"** (the aperture slider just adds **fake software blur** — the EXIF
  claims f/1 and f/16 but shutter/ISO don't change and the photos are identical). The sensor
  is a **Sony IMX906 — the same one in sub-$500 phones** (the A55, Honor 200). The site's
  cope: "we pursue artistic soul over mere specifications." (paraphrase)
- **[2026-01-09]** The real selling point is the **"Ruby key" AI-agent concierge** — but it's
  **not AI, it's a human.** He asked it to order an MKBHD.com shirt: it quoted the wrong price
  ($20.30 for a $29 shirt), asked to be paid via **"PayPal or USD"** (which autocorrects to
  USDT crypto — "a typo, which is a red flag, or crypto, which is more of a red flag"),
  requested a screenshot of payment, asked to wait until 10am Beijing time, and **placed the
  order ~7 hours later.** When he asked "are you a human?" the reply was "it's real, man…"
  (paraphrase)
- **[2026-01-09]** The brand's history: **Vertu was Nokia's London luxury sub-brand, sold to
  private equity, moved to China**, now "a less honest version of what it was" (feels like a
  ZTE phone; credit to Aaron/Mr. Who's the Boss). Its website "reads like **AI slop** designed
  to get picked up by search engines with as many buzzwords as possible" — hence errors like
  a nonexistent **"Snapdragon 8 Elite Supreme"** chip. (paraphrase)
- **[2026-01-09]** The closing thesis: **"That's the thing about phones at this point — it's
  just a phone. They're all doing the same thing… whether it's $50 or $50,000."** This one is
  "a crudely skinned Android phone designed to take advantage of people with way more dollars
  than cents." (quote)
