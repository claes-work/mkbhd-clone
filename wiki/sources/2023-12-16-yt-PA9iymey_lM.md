# The Worst Hot Takes in Tech (part 2) (@mkbhd, 2023-12-16)

## Summary
The second hot-takes reaction (concept credited to Jimmy/basketball hot takes). **(1) "The Cybertruck isn't as
overpriced as everyone says" → good take:** it's **"the biggest Delta between promised and delivered I've ever seen
from Tesla"** (the 2019 promise: $39,900, 500+ mi, 2.9s; the 2023 reality: ~$60k+, 340 mi, 2.6s) — which *feels*
overpriced, but **vs its actual shipping EV-truck competition (F-150 Lightning, R1T, Hummer EV) it's right in line**
— "either they're all overpriced or none are." His real take: **"if the Cybertruck came out this week without that
overpromising event four years ago, it'd be way more universally exciting"** — an argument for **not announcing
until you're ready to ship.** "Overhyped, maybe; overpriced, probably not." **(2) "The iPhone Mini would've been the
most popular phone with the Max's battery" → bad take:** the battery part is right, but **people just don't buy small
phones**; the best-selling iPhone ever is the **iPhone 6** (200M+), and Apple discontinued the Mini and replaced it
with the *Plus* — the opposite. **(3) "4x3 is the best aspect ratio" → "a bad take but a good take":** he's **softened
on vertical video** (it's the default now — TikTok/Reels/Shorts), so if he could shoot only one ratio he'd pick
something **squarish to crop either way** — but VR/spatial video is pushing back toward horizontal (iOS 17.2 spatial
video requires landscape). **(4) "Apple making dedicated GPUs would be over for everyone" → bad take** (Apple was
never into discrete GPUs). **(5) "A flagship smartwatch without 3+ day battery is unacceptable" → bad take**, via a
durable insight: **as long as a device hits the *accepted* standard (18 hrs for a watch, a full day for a phone),
makers keep aiming for exactly that and spend the gains on other features** (brighter screens, more sensors) — "the
Nokia 2310 lasted a month because it did nothing."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-12-16] **Cybertruck — overhyped, not overpriced; don't announce early.** "'It's the biggest overpromise/
  underdeliver Delta I've ever seen from Tesla — but vs its actual shipping competition (Lightning, R1T, Hummer EV)
  it's right in line; either they're all overpriced or none are. My real take: if it came out this week without that
  overpromising event four years ago, it'd be way more universally exciting. That's my argument for not announcing
  things until you're about to ship them. Overhyped, maybe; overpriced, probably not.'"
- [2023-12-16] **The Mini — people just don't buy small phones.** "'The battery part is right, but people just don't
  buy small phones when given the choice — they gravitate to the bigger version. The best-selling iPhone ever is the
  iPhone 6 (200M+); there's no way the Mini outsells the bigger phones even with world-class battery. It was noble
  of Apple to try, but it underperformed even their expectations and got replaced by the Max — the opposite of the
  Mini.'"
- [2023-12-16] **4x3 — I've softened on vertical video.** "'A bad take but a good take. Five-six years ago I was
  super against vertical video; today it's the default (TikTok, Reels, Shorts, texting a video). If I could only
  capture one aspect ratio forever, I'd take something squarish so I could crop it horizontally or vertically. The
  tiebreaker: the future might swing back to horizontal because of VR/spatial video (iOS 17.2 spatial video requires
  landscape — it uses the distance between the lenses to match your eyes).'"
- [2023-12-16] **Apple GPUs — bad take.** "'Apple Silicon was a huge leap, but Apple's never really been into
  discrete GPUs — the M2 Mac Pro has a tiny list of supported cards. Maybe an Apple GPU for their own Mac could be
  great, but "over for everyone" (Nvidia, AMD, Intel making GPUs for PCs)? No shot. They'd be totally fine.'"
- [2023-12-16] **The battery-acceptance thesis.** "'"A flagship smartwatch without 3+ day battery is unacceptable" —
  bad take. The Apple Watch is the most popular watch in the world at ~1.5-day battery, so people accept it. Swap
  "smartwatch" for "smartphone": a full day is the accepted standard, so makers keep aiming for exactly that and
  spend the gains on more power, brighter screens, more sensors. The Nokia 2310 lasted a month because it did
  nothing — today's devices do a lot more.'"

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "That's my argument for not announcing things until you're right about to be ready to ship them."
> "People just don't really buy small phones... they want big phones."
> "As long as they can still hit the full one-day battery life, it will be accepted — that's like the industry
> standard now."
