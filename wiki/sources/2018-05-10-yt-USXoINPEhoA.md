# Let's Talk About Google Duplex! (@mkbhd, 2018-05-10)

## Summary
A commentary essay on **Google Duplex** (announced at I/O 2018) — the Assistant feature that calls a business,
sounds human (ums, pauses), and books an appointment for you. Marques calls it "amazing and kind of terrifying,"
walks the ethics ("does Google owe the person disclosure?"), and lands his durable **"the goalposts keep getting
wider — how far does it go?"** framing about AI.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2018-05-10] **"Did Google Assistant just pass the Turing test?"** Reacting to the on-stage haircut/restaurant
  demos: "That was a real conversation with a real person at a real salon. **Amazing and kind of terrifying.** It
  had all the right inflections and pauses — **if I got that call and wasn't told, I'd have been convinced it was
  human.**" A dated marker of the moment AI voice crossed convincing.
- [2018-05-10] **The deliberate "um" — engineered deception.** "That little **'um'** — it didn't have to do that,
  but Google **programmed it to**, specifically to convince the person they're talking to a human." He flags the
  intentionality as the crux.
- [2018-05-10] **The ethics, argued both ways (his even-handed method).** "If I worked in service, **I'd still want
  to know when I'm not talking to a human.** Does Google have an obligation to disclose? A lot of people say yes.
  But from Google's side, **you have to mimic a human as closely as possible to make it work** — a call that opens
  'hi, I'm Google Assistant' gets hung up on." He genuinely holds both sides rather than dunking.
- [2018-05-10] **The "triggered vs answered" distinction (his actual resolution).** "When *I* trigger the assistant
  on my phone, it can be as human as it wants — **I know it's a robot.** The weird part is when you **answer** a
  call thinking it's a human and find out mid-call you were fooled. **That's** what feels creepy." A precise framing
  of where the discomfort actually lives.
- [2018-05-10] **"The goalposts keep getting wider — how far does it go?"** "It used to be 'just tell me the
  weather.' Now it's 'book my haircut.' **Next it's 'edit my YouTube videos, run my company, drive me to work' —
  how far does it go?**" His recurring AI-trajectory question. Notes he raised this with **Neil deGrasse Tyson**
  (links [[2018-03-09-yt-pqQrL1K0Z5g]]) and would love to ask **Elon Musk** ("hitting me up, Elon"). Frames it as
  narrow AI that's "excellent in its box — but you've got to keep it in the box."
