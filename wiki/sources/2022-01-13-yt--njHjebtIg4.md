# When Tech Companies Lie to Us... (@mkbhd, 2022-01-13)

## Summary
A media-literacy landmark cataloguing the **spectrum of tech-company "lies"** — from harmless framing to genuinely
harmful deception — and framing the reviewer's job as sorting **(1) how real a claim is, and (2) if it's not real,
how harmless or harmful the lie is.** Levels: **clever framing of real numbers** (Tesla's "1.99s 0–60" measured
with rollout subtracted; "wheel torque" vs crankshaft torque — real, but deceiving without context); **vaporware**
(concept cars that'll never ship; Samsung's Galaxy Home; Apple's cancelled AirPower — "a single non-working dummy
unit at the event"); **outdated industry standards** (the **"1-inch sensor" that's actually ~0.63" diagonal** — a
"1.0 type" holdover from vacuum-tube-diameter naming; "a lie by omission" if never clarified, and it ties directly
to the Xperia Pro-I "catch"); and genuinely **harmful lies** (Huawei passing off DSLR shots as phone photos).

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-01-13] **Clever framing of real numbers — rollout-subtracted and wheel torque.** "'Tesla's "1.99s 0–60" (and
  the Roadster's "1.9s, first car under 2s") is measured with **one-foot rollout subtracted** (a drag-racing metric
  that shaves 0.2–0.3s — real-world Model S runs are ~2.15–2.25s). And the Roadster's "10,000 Nm of torque" is
  **wheel torque, not crankshaft torque** (EVs are direct-drive, so the wheel number is much bigger) — legit, but
  deceiving without the context that it's a different measurement than gas cars have reported for decades. Not a
  lie — but the number can mislead without the extra word.'"
- [2022-01-13] **Vaporware — AirPower and Galaxy Home.** "'Vaporware is a product advertised but not buyable. **The
  biggest red flag for AirPower was a single non-working dummy unit in the hands-on area — then everyone went home
  and it quietly disappeared** (later officially cancelled). Samsung's Galaxy Home speaker: announced, never
  shipped, the "coming soon" language quietly removed. Concept cars (Sony's, BMW's e-ink iX) we all know won't
  ship as shown.'"
- [2022-01-13] **The "1-inch sensor" isn't one inch — "1.0 type."** "'No part of a "1-inch sensor" is one inch —
  it's ~13.2×8.8 mm (~0.63" diagonal). **The name is a 50-year-old holdover: old vacuum-tube cameras were named by
  the tube's diameter, and a tube big enough to hold today's ~0.63" sensor would be 1 inch.** Sony's own site says
  "1-inch (1.0 type)" — we should call them **1.0-type sensors.** Is it a lie? Yes and no — harmless if you know,
  but a **lie by omission** if a company never clarifies (DJI Air 2S, Insta360, the Xperia Pro-I headlines).'"
  (Directly extends the Xperia Pro-I "catch," [[2021-11-10-yt-9rvqDxBsJX4]].)
- [2022-01-13] **The harmful end — Huawei's fake photos.** "'On the harmful end: **Huawei got caught multiple times
  claiming DSLR/bigger-camera shots were taken on their newest phone** — that misleads a customer who thinks
  they're getting one thing and doesn't. (The transparent-back-that's-just-a-sticker is a lie, but a harmless one —
  it still looks cool.)'"
- [2022-01-13] **The reviewer's job — sort how real, and how harmful.** "'Part of my job as a reviewer is to sort
  through all these company statements: **one, figure out how real they are; two, if they're not real, find out how
  harmless or harmful the lie is — because it's definitely a spectrum** — so we can all make more informed
  decisions together.'"
