# An Honest Review of Apple Intelligence... So Far (@mkbhd, 2024-11-22)

## Summary
A feature-by-feature review of **everything Apple Intelligence has shipped ~6 months after WWDC** (full rollout not
promised until March 2025) — with a verdict that **"the promise is starting to fade."** He walks each one: **Writing
Tools** ("even as basically a professional writer, I haven't gotten much use — I don't rewrite things that often");
**Notification Summaries** ("almost useless — I turned it off; the memes of it botching things are the best part");
**Genmoji** and **Image Playground** ("fun, but over my head — apps I'll probably never use again after this video");
**Priority Notifications** (Apple's long-standing notification weakness — has promise); the **Photos Clean Up tool**
("Apple added exactly one AI feature to the hated new Photos app — but it's actually **better than Google's Magic
Eraser**, the best background removal I've seen"); **call/recording summaries** ("the transcripts are amazing with
speaker ID — but bafflingly it's not in Voice Memos, where I'd actually use it in a lecture/meeting"); **Visual
Intelligence** ("useful 3–4 times a year for me — but nothing new; I demoed the same thing on the Galaxy S8 with Bixby
in 2017"); and **ChatGPT integration** (Siri with a new animation but "most of the new Siri isn't here yet"). His
sharpest point is the **"could you vs. should you"** critique via the keynote's dog clip: **someone asks the phone what
breed a dog is instead of asking the person walking it** — "you *could* use these features, and they might work well,
but *should* you? You could ask AI to write a friendlier letter, or just write it friendly in the first place." Also
telling: **no Apple Intelligence at all on the Vision Pro**, "their most futuristic platform." His bottom line: it's
"the worst it'll ever be," but the marketing treats it as groundbreaking when it isn't — and **"honestly the best thing
we've gotten from Apple Intelligence is that, since it all runs on-device, they bumped the base memory in everything —
16 GB base RAM now — which made the base Mac mini an incredible deal."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-11-22] **The promise is starting to fade.** "'Apple made this huge promise that Apple Intelligence would change
  everything across the lineup. Nearly six months after WWDC it's still not fully out (March 2025 now), the iPhones
  launched with zero of it, and I think that promise is starting to fade. Let me review every feature that's actually
  shipped.'"
- [2024-11-22] **Most features are meh — a few are genuinely useful.** "'Writing Tools — even as a professional writer,
  I've barely used them. Notification Summaries — almost useless, turned it off. Genmoji/Image Playground — over my
  head, I'll probably never use them again. But the Photos Clean Up tool is actually better than Google's Magic Eraser
  — the best background removal I've seen. And the call/recording transcripts are amazing (with speaker ID) — though
  bafflingly it's not in Voice Memos, where I'd actually use it.'"
- [2024-11-22] **Could you vs. should you — the dog-photo encapsulation.** "'The perfect encapsulation is the keynote
  clip where someone asks the phone what breed a dog is instead of asking the person walking it. You could use these
  features, and they might work well — but should you? You could ask AI for a friendlier version of a letter, or just
  write it in a friendly way in the first place. You could follow an AI trip itinerary blindly, or actually plan it.'"
- [2024-11-22] **Visual Intelligence isn't new; no AI on Vision Pro.** "'Visual Intelligence is useful maybe 3–4 times
  a year (what plant/dog is this) — but it's not new; I demoed the exact same thing on the Galaxy S8 with Bixby back in
  2017. It's more capable and better integrated now, sure. And notably there's zero Apple Intelligence on the Vision
  Pro — their most futuristic platform — which is already kind of fascinating.'"
- [2024-11-22] **The best thing Apple Intelligence gave us is more base memory.** "'This is the worst it'll ever be, and
  parts are really good (the Photos eraser). But it's crazy how the marketing treats it as this massive game-changer.
  Honestly the best thing we've gotten from Apple Intelligence — because it all runs on-device — is that they bumped
  the base memory in everything: more RAM in every iPhone, and 16 GB base on every Mac, which made the base Mac mini an
  incredible deal. Shout out to Apple Intelligence for that.'"
