# 15" MacBook Air M2 Review: The Obvious Thing! (@mkbhd, 2023-06-12)

## Summary
A review of the **15" MacBook Air (M2)** built on one analogy: it's **"the Tesla Model Y of laptops."** The Model 3
was Tesla's most important, most-attainable, best-selling car — but **"sneakily bigger cars are more popular,"** so
Tesla did the obvious thing, built the slightly-bigger Model Y, and it rocketed to the most popular car on Earth.
Same story: the MacBook Air is Apple's most important, most-attainable Mac — so the 15" is **"a MacBook Air but
bigger,"** the obvious thing, and he predicts it becomes the most popular Mac. What the bigger screen buys: mostly
**just bigger** (not more info, not better quality — same 500-nit 60 Hz panel), plus a slightly larger trackpad, a
second set of force-cancelling woofers, and a bigger battery that **cancels out the bigger screen for the same
great battery life.** Thermals: he tested small-vs-big and found a **negligible sustained-performance difference**
(both fanless M2) — if you need sustained workloads, look at the 14" Pro. **The X-factor is price:** the base
1299 has only **8 GB / 256 GB**, and (since you can't upgrade later) it **"quickly becomes a $1,700 laptop"** with
a few clicks. Windows rivals will beat it at one specific thing, but none match **the well-rounded package** — thin,
light, nice display, great battery, M2, the ecosystem — which is what makes it a MacBook Air.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-06-12] **The Tesla Model Y of laptops — the obvious thing.** "'The Model 3 was Tesla's most important,
  most-attainable, best-selling car — but sneakily bigger cars are more popular, so they did the obvious thing and
  made the slightly-bigger Model Y, now the most popular car on Earth. The MacBook Air is the most important Mac; the
  15" is a MacBook Air but bigger — the obvious thing — and I predict it rockets to the most popular Mac.'"
- [2023-06-12] **The bigger screen mostly just feels bigger.** "'Millions of people walk into stores asking for a
  15" laptop, and Apple had to point them at the $2,500 16" Pro. Now there's this. Is there dramatically more info or
  a better-quality display? Not really — it mostly just feels bigger. Bigger videos, movies, text, windows, for a
  little extra size (11.5 mm thin, 3.3 lbs).'"
- [2023-06-12] **The bigger battery cancels the bigger screen; thermals are negligible.** "'Same chipset, bigger
  screen and bigger battery sort of cancel out, so you get roughly the same great battery life. I tested small-vs-
  big on sustained cinebench and there's a slightly smaller dip on the 15" — potentially a bit less throttling over
  time — but the difference is unlikely to be noticeable; both are fanless M2. If you do sustained workloads, you're
  looking at the 14" Pro.'"
- [2023-06-12] **The price is the X-factor — the base config quickly becomes $1,700.** "'The biggest hurdle to being
  the Tesla Model Y of laptops is price. The base $1,299 only has 8 GB and 256 GB — and you can't upgrade these
  later, so you want to get as much as you can up front — which quickly becomes a $1,700 laptop with a few clicks.
  That's pricey.'"
- [2023-06-12] **The well-rounded package is what makes it a MacBook Air.** "'Windows laptops at that price will each
  do one thing better — a nicer XPS display, a better-gaming Zephyrus — but none are quite this package: a thin,
  light, daily 15" laptop with a nice display, great battery, and impressive M2 performance, plus the ecosystem.
  That well-roundedness is what makes it the MacBook Air.'"
