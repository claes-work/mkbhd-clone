## Summary
Ellis explains why big cabinets make bass (Hoffman's Iron Law — a speaker can be small,
loud, or bassy, pick two), then profiles the Brain X (Brain Audio) Bluetooth speaker, which
uses a "magnetic negative spring" to counteract cabinet air pressure and produce big bass in
a small sealed enclosure. Audio-education content authored entirely by the team.

## Key claims (Ellis-attributed — CONTEXT ONLY)
- [2024-05-22] **Hoffman's Iron Law**: a speaker can be small, loud, or bassy — but not all
  three; a ported (non-airtight) cabinet lets the cone move more freely for more bass with
  less power, but you still need a reasonably large cabinet for low resonant frequencies.
- [2024-05-22] The **Brain X** ($599) "breaks Hoffman's law" via a magnetic negative spring
  (permanent magnets + electromagnets keeping alignment); Ellis loves it wired for movies,
  with caveats (Bluetooth latency, not studio-monitor imaging). Frames it as a great time to
  be a "new kind of audiophile" who appreciates clever engineering.
