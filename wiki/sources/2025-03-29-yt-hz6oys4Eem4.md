# Apple's AI Crisis: Explained! (@mkbhd, 2025-03-29)

## Summary
An essay on **Apple's AI predicament** — "giant company is late to a massive technological shift, fails to adapt, falls
behind, dies; a tale as old as time" (Nokia missed smartphones; BlackBerry missed the virtual keyboard; **Skype was the
default until Zoom ate it in ~2 years** — "that's how fast tech moves"). Apple is $3T and **won't die financially** —
but Apple Intelligence is a genuine crisis. His core argument: **Apple's famous second-mover strategy is a *hardware*
strategy** (not first to OLED, wireless earbuds, the tablet, even the smartphone — but executed best); **AI is
different — it's software, an iterate-as-fast-as-possible race**, and every rival shipped *something* to point at
(Circle to Search/Gemini, Galaxy AI, Copilot). Apple announced Apple Intelligence at WWDC 2024, then the **iPhone 16
launched "built from the ground up for it" with literally none of it** — a drip rollout (iOS 18.1 Writing Tools/
notification summaries → 18.2 Genmoji/Image Playground/ChatGPT → 18.3 visual intelligence, but **notification summaries
got disabled for news over mistakes**) — and **the promised new Siri (onscreen awareness, in-app actions,
conversational) still isn't out, with no timetable.** He then reframes his own job — **"we go from tech demo to tech
demo; part one is deciding if it's a good idea, part two is understanding how real the demo is"** (AirPower didn't work
in the hands-on area → never shipped; Tesla's teleoperated robots; the Bixby speaker Samsung never shipped) — and cites
**John Gruber's red flag: Apple has *never* demoed the new Siri to anyone.** "The fact they haven't even gotten to the
cool-tech-demo part makes it feel farther away than we think." Meanwhile Apple is running billboards, and **deleted a
Siri commercial for a feature that doesn't exist** — with internal reorgs calling it "embarrassing." His view: **Apple
Intelligence always felt more like a thing to please investors** than a product regular people will love. Three reasons
it's unlikely to just be fine: **(1) AI isn't at the core of Apple's business** (hard to draw a line to more money,
especially for a privacy-focused company); **(2) their usual advantages don't apply** — the second-mover trick is
hardware, and developers **don't actually *want* Siri reaching into their apps** (the Uber example: it takes away their
control); **(3) the total lack of demos** is very hard to ignore. Apple keeps shipping great hardware — "but this is
something to keep our eye on."
