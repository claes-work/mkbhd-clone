# How Apple is Taking Back the iPhone (@mkbhd, 2023-02-03)

## Summary
An explainer built around **a gradient chart**: every product made of parts sits somewhere between **off-the-shelf**
(Sony camera sensors, Samsung/LG displays, Qualcomm chips) and **fully custom / vertically integrated** (the chassis,
the A16, the Taptic Engine). Two 2023 rumors — Apple making **its own combined cellular/Wi-Fi/Bluetooth radio chip**,
and **its own micro-LED displays** — are, read correctly, **"a very Tim Cook supply-chain optimization story."** Why
custom matters: **optimization** — the memorable analogy is **trying to fill a triangular volume with off-the-shelf
square blocks vs designing every piece yourself** ("that's peak optimization"), exactly what the Apple Silicon Mac
transition proved (Intel off-the-shelf → custom → a quantum leap in speed/efficiency/battery). Key distinction:
**Apple doesn't actually manufacture — it's a world-class design firm** (TSMC builds the chips). The micro-LED move
is different because **Apple wants to fund and own the technology itself** (an exclusive it can dictate to a maker),
which is why supplier stocks dropped. Who it affects: **Apple gets more control + better products; suppliers
(Qualcomm/Broadcom/Samsung) lose a huge customer; users eventually get better products.** The pattern: **spend
mountains of cash on upfront development, reap better products down the line** — and rivals with less cash will try
to follow.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-02-03] **The off-the-shelf → custom gradient.** "'Every product made of parts sits on a gradient from
  off-the-shelf (Sony sensors, Samsung/LG displays, Qualcomm chips anyone can buy) to fully custom, vertically
  integrated. The iPhone is a cacophony — a mixed salad — mostly off-the-shelf sensors, a customized L-shaped
  battery, and on the custom end the chassis and the A16.'"
- [2023-02-03] **Vertical integration is optimization — the square-blocks analogy.** "'The main benefit of custom
  parts, especially in tech, is optimization. Imagine filling a triangular volume as efficiently as possible but you
  only have off-the-shelf square blocks — you won't be efficient. Design all the parts yourself and now we're
  talking: peak optimization. You just saw it with the Macs going from Intel to Apple Silicon — a massive
  improvement, hilariously better than before.'"
- [2023-02-03] **A Tim Cook supply-chain story — and Apple designs, doesn't manufacture.** "'Since 2007 most of
  Apple's revenue is the iPhone, so taking back its parts is a Tim Cook supply-chain optimization story more than
  anything. Apple doesn't really manufacture anything — it's a world-class design firm; it custom-designs the M2 but
  TSMC (the foundry) builds it.'"
- [2023-02-03] **Micro-LED is different — Apple wants to own the tech.** "'With displays today, ~80% of iPhone
  screens are Samsung, ~12% LG — Apple just orders OLEDs those makers already know how to build, with a few
  customizations. Micro-LED is different: Apple wants to fund and own the development in its own facilities, so it's
  an exclusive Apple technology it hands off to a maker — that's why supplier stocks dropped (word is they're trying
  to strategically drop Samsung).'"
- [2023-02-03] **Combining the radios; who it affects.** "'The radio rumor is more interesting — not just their own
  chip but combining Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and cellular into one, which could mean real efficiency and battery gains.
  Both stories are Apple using its cash on upfront development to reap better products later. Apple gets more control
  and better products; the suppliers lose a big customer; and we potentially get better products.'"
