# The Rollable OLED TV: The Potential is Real! (@mkbhd, 2019-01-09)

## Summary
A CES 2019 hands-on with LG's Signature R rollable OLED TV — a rare "radical CES idea that actually became a
product." Marques explains the mechanism, floats a genuinely useful improvement (variable aspect ratio), and is
impressed-but-not-buying.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2019-01-09] **A radical CES idea made real (the value).** "Announced last year behind closed doors as a proof of
  concept; **this year it's on the show floor and they're promising to ship it to real buyers.** It's cool to see
  one of these radical CES ideas — **CES is notoriously full of products that never exist** — actually move forward
  into a real product." A media-literate CES read (vaporware vs real).
- [2019-01-09] **The mechanism (teach-the-mechanism).** A 65" 4K OLED that rolls out of a silver box (with
  **front-facing** speakers, unlike upward-firing soundbars); three modes (full / hidden / **"line mode"** two-
  thirds up, showing a weather/clock UI). "Watching it roll — quiet, ~8–10 seconds. **The panel is ~40–50
  interlocking segments spanning the back, held rigid by an expanding/contracting X-frame.**" A clear explanation.
- [2019-01-09] **The improvement he'd make — variable aspect ratio.** "Line mode gave me an idea: **let you pick
  ANY aspect ratio in between.** If I'm watching a 21:9 movie, I could **roll the TV down to 21:9 so there are no
  black bars** — that would make the whole rollable/modular concept genuinely interesting." A designer's-eye
  suggestion (his best contribution). (Also wishes for a **clear-box demo** to see it work.)
- [2019-01-09] **Impressed, not buying.** "Would I get one? **Probably not — it'll be much more expensive than any
  TV I use, and I already have a wallpaper TV.** But it's cool to see the potential become real." A calibrated,
  self-aware close (confirms he owns the LG Wallpaper OLED).

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "It's neat to see really radical and crazy ideas at CES — which is notoriously filled with products that never
> go on to exist — actually move forward and become a real product."
> "What would be cool is if you could pick any aspect ratio in between — roll the TV down to 21:9 so there are no
> black bars for a movie. That would make the whole concept interesting."
