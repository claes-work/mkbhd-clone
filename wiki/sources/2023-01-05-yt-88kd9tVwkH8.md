# What is Happening with iPhone Camera? (@mkbhd, 2023-01-05)

## Summary
An explainer resolving an apparent contradiction: the iPhone **keeps losing the blind camera tests** (first
round in brackets; middle-of-the-pack in the 21M-vote scientific test) yet Marques **gave the iPhone 14 Pro
his "best overall camera system 2022" trophy.** The core thesis: **modern smartphone cameras "aren't just
cameras anymore" — they're computational, and the photo is "partly photons, but primarily processing."**
Two theories. **(1) The big-sensor transition overprocesses:** Google's jump to a 50 MP sensor (Pixel 6)
initially made photos worse because the old high-sensitivity/noise-reduction/sharpening pipeline was still
running — it took until the Pixel 7 to dial it in; the **iPhone 14 Pro's new 48 MP sensor is now in that
same awkward, over-processed phase** and Apple will fix it by the 15 Pro ("I bet there's one new word they
use on stage"). **(2) Skin-tone rendering:** because every test photo is of *his own face*, the results
exposed how phones handle skin — **Google's "Real Tone"** trains on diverse skin tones and represents his
accurately, while **Apple "simply likes to evenly light faces"** (an invisible "bounce fill" that sometimes
erases the real direction of light). He ties this to why he **liked RED cameras** early — accurate skin-tone
rendering over Sony/ARRI/Canon. Why the trophy despite the losses: the blind test only judged tiny,
compressed exposure/color — **not sharpness, autofocus speed/reliability, app speed, or video**, where the
iPhone leads. **"The pretty pictures... are partly photons, but primarily processing."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-01-05] **Cameras aren't just cameras anymore — primarily processing.** "'It's a whole computational
  event now — sampling the scene several times, merging exposures, tone mapping, noise reduction, HDR. So
  it's not about the best sensor gathering the most light; software makes a much bigger difference to how
  the image looks than anything else. The pretty pictures are partly photons, but primarily processing.'"
- [2023-01-05] **The big-sensor transition overprocesses; the iPhone is in that phase now.** "'Google's jump
  to a 50 MP sensor on the Pixel 6 initially made photos worse — they were still running the old
  high-sensitivity/noise-reduction/sharpening pipeline as if the sensor were small, so photos looked
  overprocessed. It took until the Pixel 7 to dial it in. The iPhone 14 Pro's new 48 MP sensor is going
  through the same thing — a little too processed — and they'll work on it by the 15 Pro (I bet there's one
  new word they use on stage).'"
- [2023-01-05] **Real Tone vs. Apple's even-lighting.** "'Google's Real Tone trains their camera on data
  sets with a large variety of skin tones to represent them correctly — that turns out to be a big part of
  why Pixels finished higher when I'm the subject. Apple simply likes to evenly light faces across the board
  — almost like someone added a subtle bounce fill — and sometimes it looks off, erasing the shadow so you
  can't even tell where the light is coming from.'"
- [2023-01-05] **Using his own face revealed the skin-tone issue — and why he liked RED.** "'I wanted the
  test subject to be a human because that's what people care about photographing — and using my own face
  revealed how differently phones handle skin tones. It's actually one of the earliest reasons I liked RED
  cameras: beyond 8K and color science, they rendered my skin tone accurately over a lot of the Sonys,
  ARRIs, and Canons I'd tried.'"
- [2023-01-05] **Why the trophy despite losing the blind test.** "'The scientific test judged one thing —
  small, compressed, postage-stamp exposure and colors. It did NOT test sharpness/detail, autofocus speed
  and reliability, the open-close time of the camera app, or video (mic quality, HDR, file formats). That's
  where the iPhone 14 Pro leads, and that's why it still got best-overall-system.'"
