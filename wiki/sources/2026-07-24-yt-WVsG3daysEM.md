## Summary
A big-gadget-week episode: the "did they even test this?" pet-peeve segment (the **Apple Weather app's
misleading all-day rain icon**), **Garmin's new screenless fitness tracker** (a Whoop competitor), the **new
Light Flip** phone, and the week's headline — **Samsung's Unpacked foldables** (Z Flip 8, the renamed Z Fold 8

## Key claims / discussion (2026-07-24)
- **"Did they even test this?" — the Apple Weather app (mixed hosts).** The app shows a **rain/thunderstorm icon
  for the whole day** even when it only rains 1 a.m.–2 a.m., and doesn't update after that hour passes. Consensus:
  it should be customizable ("tell me if it rains during my waking day / commute"). Weather-app recs surfaced
  (Carrot Weather ← Dark Sky layout; the "Not Boring" weather widget). Running gag: Marques is "the weather-app
  guy" — "your weather app just calls Marques."
- **Garmin screenless tracker (a Whoop competitor).** Andrew (the long-time Garmin user) is the enthusiast; the
  panel spans the wearable spectrum (Andrew = Whoop; another = Fitbit; solo Whoop holdout). Nice bands/materials,
  but disappointment that the side button isn't a customizable control.
- ★ **Marques on the Samsung foldables (corroborates his solo hands-on).** The **"Ultra" rename is pure
  positioning** — "the Ultra didn't get any new functionality to deserve the name; they just added it to slot the
  new smaller Fold underneath." He likes the **new passport-style Fold 8** a lot ("I was curious to see if others
  who got hands-on liked it as much as I do"): **4:3 inner display** is "a nice aspect ratio for reading… and
  **medium-format camera sensors are 4:3, a lot of the photos you take are 4:3**," so photo review is fullscreen;
  201 g (≈ an S26+) "feels like the same weight as a slab phone but a totally different shape." His **biggest
  beef: the $1,900 price** (256 GB base). The panel notes the clever anti-Apple timing (everyone expects an
  Apple passport-style fold later in 2026) but pans the naming ("should've been the **Fold Wide** or Fold Edge,
  not Ultra") and warns Samsung boxed itself in if Apple later goes tall (as Google did after its first
  passport-shaped fold).
- **The new Light Flip (Light Phone).** A **$300 flip phone** running the minimalist Light Phone UI — "looks
  adorable, like it was made by IKEA or Teenage Engineering," fun colors, raised inner buttons. Take: at **$300
  it's "not as bad"** as the prior **$900 Light Phone 3** ("cool and interesting, but $900 and hard to use").
