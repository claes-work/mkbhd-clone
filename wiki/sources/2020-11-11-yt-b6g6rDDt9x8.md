# Apple M1 Chip: Let's Talk! (@mkbhd, 2020-11-11)

## Summary
Reaction to Apple's "One More Thing" event unveiling the **M1** — the first Apple-silicon Mac chip. The landmark
payoff of the two-year transition arc he'd been tracking: he **predicted Apple would pick either a massive
efficiency jump OR a massive performance jump — "and they did both."** Durable threads: the **"upside and
downside of control"** (soldered RAM, fewer ports), **claims-vs-real-life skepticism** about Apple's unlabeled
marketing graphs, and that he's **most excited for the desktops** (optimize purely for power).

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2020-11-11] **"The one I was most excited for" — the nerd's event.** "'This was the final 2020 event, and — maybe
  it's the nerd in me — **the Mac-with-Apple-silicon event was the one I was most excited for,** over the iPhone
  events. I'm mostly a Mac user, I edit in Final Cut, **I've seen the struggles Apple's had with Intel's
  limitations.**'" (Payoff of the WWDC "Great Transition" arc.)
- [2020-11-11] **THE PREDICTION — efficiency OR power; "they did both."** "'I thought it'd be **either a massive
  efficiency jump (same laptop, twice the battery) or a massive performance jump (same battery, twice as fast).
  It turns out they did both.**' The M1 is a **5 nm 8-core (4 performance + 4 efficiency) SoC** with integrated
  GPU, 16-core Neural Engine, and **built-in RAM — everything tightly integrated.**'"
- [2020-11-11] **THE UPSIDE AND DOWNSIDE OF CONTROL.** "'The **downside: RAM is built into the M1 — no more
  user-upgradeable RAM, so you buy it from Apple at their price. The upside: one tightly-integrated chip, no
  separate CPU/GPU/RAM all asking for power.** Same theme in the ports — the M1 MacBook Pro has only **two
  Thunderbolt ports** (the Intel one had four). **You get the upside of control and the downside of control.**'"
  A promotable framing of vertical integration.
- [2020-11-11] **CLAIMS-VS-REAL-LIFE — the marketing-graph skepticism.** "'All we have are claims — no M1 Mac in
  front of us. The **CPU-power-per-watt graph didn't label its axes, doesn't say which "latest PC chip" — it
  feels very marketing.** "3× the best-selling Windows laptop" — but the best-selling ones aren't the best
  performing. **Apple's usually good about delivering what they say, but I'm very curious how the huge claims
  translate to real life.**'" His measured skepticism.
- [2020-11-11] **The gains come from optimized apps — "reminds me of the new consoles."** "'The biggest 2–5×
  gains **come from the best-optimized apps (the ones Apple makes) — kind of like the new consoles.** Old apps
  still work (plus iPad/iPhone apps in Big Sur), but the huge numbers take time as developers optimize (Adobe,
  a new Photoshop early next year).'"
- [2020-11-11] **MOST EXCITED FOR THE DESKTOPS — optimize purely for power.** "'The 13" MacBook Pro is "the
  ultimate expression of what the M1 can do," sure — **but I'm most excited for the rest of the lineup: the
  desktops.** An iMac/Mac Pro chip **doesn't need so many efficiency cores or built-in RAM — it can just optimize
  for power, power, power.** After being so impressed with the Mac Pro this year, I can't wait. **I just cross my
  fingers Apple doesn't take the control thing too far.**'"
- [2020-11-11] **"I told you to wait" — the buying-advice payoff.** "'For everyone who asked me about a new
  MacBook this year and **I told you to wait — now they're here, you can buy them by the time you see this.
  You're welcome.**' (Stay tuned for the reviews to see how the numbers translate.)'"
