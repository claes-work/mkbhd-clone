# OnePlus Watch 2 Review: Total Redemption! (@mkbhd, 2024-02-27)

## Summary
A review of the **$300 OnePlus Watch 2** (three years after a poor first attempt) whose **one interesting trick** is
its battery: **OnePlus claims 100 hours** (four days), in the *default* Smart Mode. It's a mature, well-built
circular Wear OS watch (stainless steel, sapphire glass, IP68 + MIL-STD, 1,000-nit AMOLED, dual-frequency GPS). The
twist is *how* it gets the battery: **two separate processors and two operating systems** — a high-power Snapdragon
W5 running **Wear OS 4** for intensive tasks (maps, music), and an always-on **BES 2700 running RTOS** for the basic
low-power stuff (heart rate, notifications, calls) — **"like a hybrid car"** (the responsiveness of electric + the
range of gas). Plus a notably big **500 mAh battery.** In a four-day head-to-head, it beat an **$800 Apple Watch
Ultra** on battery (with the always-on display off it clears 100 hours; power-saver mode reaches ~12 days). The
downsides: a **~2-second notification delay** (you can watch Wear OS "snap on"), so-so haptics, a clunky charger, no
iPhone support, and a decorative freely-spinning crown (there for durability). His verdict: **"OnePlus has redeemed
itself"** — most impressive that they kept it at $300 given two chips and two OSes.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-02-27] **The trick is the dual-chip, dual-OS hybrid.** "'The way it achieves this battery life is more
  interesting than the battery itself. Most smartwatches have one chip with high-power and efficiency cores. This has
  two entirely separate processors and two operating systems — a Snapdragon W5 running Wear OS 4 for intensive tasks,
  and an always-on BES 2700 running RTOS for basic low-power tasks. It's like a hybrid car — the responsiveness of
  electric with the range of gas.'"
- [2024-02-27] **100 hours — and it beat an $800 Apple Watch Ultra.** "'OnePlus claims 100 hours in the default Smart
  Mode. I wore it against an Apple Watch Ultra for four days; the $800 Ultra died around hour 56, and this $300 watch
  still had ~15% (and with the always-on display off — off by default — it clears 100 hours; power-saver reaches ~12
  days).'"
- [2024-02-27] **The big battery matters too.** "'I don't think the dual-chip system is all the magic — it also has a
  500 mAh battery, bigger than you'd expect for a smartwatch. If you gave the Pixel Watch 2 (also a W5) a 500 mAh
  battery, would it last 50 hours? I don't know, but there you go.'"
- [2024-02-27] **The downside — a ~2-second notification delay.** "'The main weakness: I feel the buzz, lift my
  wrist, and it takes a full ~2 seconds to show the notification — you can actually see the Snapdragon W5 and Wear OS
  snap on before it appears. Two seconds isn't a disaster, but it's longer than you expect and it annoys me. Also
  so-so haptics, a clunky charger, and no iPhone support.'"
- [2024-02-27] **Total redemption — and they kept it at $300.** "'For $300, OnePlus has redeemed itself from its
  previous smartwatch efforts. Most impressive is that they kept the price this low — two chips and two OSes sounds
  expensive, and it's really not missing anything drastic. In a world of smartwatch gimmicks (earbuds inside, the
  fitness one), maybe this is the one with the hybrid system and great battery life.'"
