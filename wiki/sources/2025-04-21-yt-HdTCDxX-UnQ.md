# I Have A Problem with the Light Phone III (@mkbhd, 2025-04-21)

## Summary
A review of the **Light Phone III** in the context of **the "dumb phone" / anti-distraction movement** (a companion to
his Boox Palma review). Smartphones are a net positive, but they're **designed to be addicting** (apps whose sole
purpose is maximizing screen time) — so the pitch is to strip all that out. The Light Phone III is **"the most premium-
feeling dumb phone" — the one a smartphone lover like him would be tempted to use**: not e-ink but an **OLED under matte
glass in an all-metal body**, clicky buttons, a nice brightness dial (push to toggle the flashlight), a clean text-only
UI (a Zune HD nostalgia trip), a SIM tray, and essential "tools" (calls/texts, alarms, calculator, a basic camera,
Maps with turn-by-turn, a podcast player) managed from an online dashboard. **"I will never waste my time on this
phone" — but that's the exact moment it goes from a cool idea to frustrating**: no copy-paste, no autocorrect, no
mobile-wallet pay (despite NFC), no RCS (so photos go as compressed unencrypted MMS; 2FA only via insecure SMS), no
third-party apps (WhatsApp/Slack/Spotify), no ebook reader, a bad vibration motor, a fingerprint reader that doesn't
work yet. The deeper question: **is it your *only* phone, or a second phone?** — "two very different products" (the
**bicycle analogy**: some people's only transport, most people's weekend cruiser) — and it could genuinely work for a
kid's first phone or an offline professional. **But the overarching problem is price: $800 ($600 pre-order) for a device
that does *less* on purpose is ridiculous** (a Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 is in sub-$200 phones; the cost is materials + unique-
software dev + low volume + tariffs). His sharpest line: **"the Light Phone company could have made a launcher app
instead of a phone — but then they couldn't charge $800 for it"**, and it "feels like they're taking advantage of
people's lack of discipline with a shiny $800 box." A cool, unique piece of hardware — "I like that this exists" — but
not worth the money for what's almost certainly a second phone. (Ridge Power Bank sponsor.)

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2025-04-21] **The most premium dumb phone — the one a smartphone lover would be tempted by.** "'Smartphones are a net
  positive, but they're designed to be addicting. The Light Phone III is the most premium-feeling dumb phone — not
  e-ink but an OLED under matte glass in an all-metal body, clicky buttons, a nice brightness dial. It's the one that
  feels most at home for a regular smartphone user. If someone asked me to design a dumb phone a smartphone lover would
  use, it's about what I'd make.'"
- [2025-04-21] **A cool idea until you use it — then it's frustrating.** "'"I will never waste my time on this phone" —
  but that's the exact moment it goes from a cool idea to frustrating: no copy-paste, no autocorrect, no mobile-wallet
  pay (despite NFC), no RCS (photos go as compressed unencrypted MMS; 2FA only via insecure SMS), no third-party apps,
  no ebook reader. The more I ask it to do, the more I'm just turning it back into a regular smartphone.'"
- [2025-04-21] **Is it your only phone or a second phone? The bicycle analogy.** "'A big part of the review was figuring
  out: is this supposed to be your only phone, or do you also carry a smartphone in your other pocket? Those are two
  very different products. Like a bicycle — for some people it's their only transport, for most it's a weekend
  cruiser. It could genuinely work for a kid's first phone or an offline professional. Just because it's not for me
  doesn't mean it's bad.'"
- [2025-04-21] **$800 for a phone that does less is ridiculous.** "'The overarching problem is price: no way anybody
  should pay $800 ($600 pre-order) for a device that does less on purpose. A Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 is in sub-$200 phones.
  The real cost is materials, unique software, low volume, and tariffs — but it's hard to get to $800 for something
  that does less.'"
- [2025-04-21] **A launcher couldn't charge $800 — it feels like exploiting a lack of discipline.** "'The Light Phone
  company could've made a launcher app instead of a phone — but then they couldn't charge $800 for it. The point is you
  can't get trapped doom-scrolling on this, which a launcher can't guarantee. Still, it's hard to get behind charging
  that much for what's almost certainly a second phone — it kind of feels like taking advantage of people's lack of
  discipline with a shiny $800 box.'"
