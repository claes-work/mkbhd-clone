# iPhone 16/16 Pro Review: Times Have Changed! (@mkbhd, 2024-09-23)

## Summary
The full iPhone 16 / 16 Pro review — opening with the launch of **Panels**, his own **wallpaper app** (iOS + Android;
where all his review wallpapers have come from for a year; a 50/50 profit split with artists; a subscription to fund
ongoing improvement — "it's fun to be on the other side, where now people review what we make"). The review's thesis:
**"this is the most unfinished I've ever seen a new iPhone launch."** All the marketing (billboards, the "Glowtime"
event, "designed for Apple Intelligence from the ground up") is about **Apple Intelligence that isn't on the phone** —
none of it ships at launch. This forces his golden rule to the surface: **"never buy a product based on the promise of
future software updates — buy it for what it is today... but yet here we are; times have changed."** He draws the
**iPhone-vs-Rabbit-R1/Humane-Pin comparison**: he could only review what shipped in the mail then too — the difference
is the iPhone is **a fine standalone product without the promised stuff**, and Apple **has a track record of shipping
what it promises**, so **"Apple is leaning heavily on how much you trust them"** ("it's really bold to ship like this").
The hardware is "very incremental": the wobbliest slab phone he's seen, new colors, an **8 GB RAM floor across the line**
(for the eventual AI), ~45 W wired / 25 W MagSafe faster charging, and the new **Camera Control** ("nobody at Apple is
allowed to call it a button; I'm gonna call it a button") — a finicky capacitive slider that **"99% of people will only
ever use as a shutter button."** His camera framing: **"smartphone cameras are like headphones' EQ"** — the iPhone
still over-brightens shadows, but Camera Control's **tone slider (he runs ~-0.5)** gives real control back, pushing the
iPhone back to the top of his favorites to shoot with; the **16 Pro camera is a significant step up** (best primary
sensor, 4K120, 5x). Verdict: buy the 16 if you're on a 13-or-older; buy the Pro for cameras/display; **"I personally
cannot use a 60 Hz $800 phone in 2024"** — and **"buy it for what it is today, not what it could be tomorrow."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-09-23] **Panels launch — being on the other side.** "'We finally launched Panels — the wallpaper app. It's
  where all my review wallpapers have come from for the past year, on iOS and Android, with a 50/50 profit split with
  the artists and a subscription to fund improving it over time. It's fun to be on the development side now, where other
  people get to review what we make.'"
- [2024-09-23] **The most unfinished iPhone launch ever.** "'This is the most unfinished I've ever seen a new iPhone
  launch. All the marketing — the billboards, the "Glowtime" event, "designed for Apple Intelligence from the ground
  up" — is about Apple Intelligence, and none of it is on this phone at launch. The only way to square that is that it's
  unfinished.'"
- [2024-09-23] **Never buy for future software — but times have changed.** "'If I've said it once I've said it a million
  times: never buy a product based on the promise of future software updates — buy it for what it is right now. That's
  been the golden rule forever. But yet here we are; times have changed. Apple's a public company and, like others,
  caved to the pressure to appear AI-first, presenting a ton of AI that isn't good yet — it's not even finished, or
  they'd ship it.'"
- [2024-09-23] **The iPhone-vs-Rabbit/Humane comparison — it comes down to trust and a track record.** "'The new iPhone
  is suddenly in the same boat as the Rabbit R1 and Humane Pin — I can only review what's in front of me, what ships in
  the mail. The difference: the iPhone is a fine standalone product even without the promised stuff, and Apple has a
  track record of shipping what it promises, where Humane and Rabbit didn't. So Apple is leaning heavily on how much you
  trust them. It's really bold to ship like this.'"
- [2024-09-23] **Camera Control — it's a button; cameras are like headphones' EQ.** "'Nobody at Apple is allowed to call
  it a button; I'm gonna call it a button. It's a finicky capacitive slider — 99% of people will only ever use it as a
  shutter. But smartphone cameras are like headphones' EQ: the iPhone still over-brightens shadows, but the tone slider
  (I run about -0.5) gives real control back, which pushed the iPhone back to the top of my favorites to shoot with. And
  the 16 Pro camera is a significant step up — my favorite primary sensor, plus 4K120 and a useful 5x.'"
- [2024-09-23] **Buy it for what it is today; I can't use a 60 Hz $800 phone.** "'Buy the 16 if you're on a 13 or older;
  buy the Pro for cameras or display. The cheap iPhone has never looked better. But I personally cannot use a 60 Hz $800
  phone in 2024 — lots of people like me will upgrade to the Pro just for ProMotion. Either way: buy it for what it is
  today, not what it could be tomorrow. Even if the AI never comes, these are rock-solid, very incremental upgrades.'"
