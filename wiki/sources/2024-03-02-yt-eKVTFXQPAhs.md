# Reviewing EVERY Samsung Galaxy S Ever! (@mkbhd, 2024-03-02)

## Summary
A retrospective through every main Galaxy S (S1 → S24, 2010–2024) — "the story of how we got here." The through-line
is his long-standing Samsung thesis: **"Samsung will try anything — throw stuff at the wall to see what sticks"**
(a phone for every carrier; head-tracking scrolling; the heart-rate sensor; the S6 Edge curved screen; DeX; the
Bixby button; a dual-aperture camera). Durable observations: **they've aimed for "one day of battery" the whole
time** (capacity grew, but the target never moved — the battery-acceptance pattern); the **Note 7 was "the greatest
PR disaster in smartphone history"** (a thin-separator battery defect, recalled worldwide, banned on planes — yet
Samsung was big enough to survive); the **Exynos-vs-Snapdragon split "failed spectacularly"** (Snapdragon versions
consistently outperformed; buyers imported them — "ahead of their time, but a big backfire"); **TouchWiz** vs stock
Android; and the arc where **base models got boring** while the **Ultra fights the Note at the top and the FE/A-series
fight at the bottom**, leaving the base phone in a play-it-safe no-man's-land ("no leadership here, just enough brand
cache that people buy the Samsung one next to the phone everyone's buying"). His fun argument: **"the S20 was
arguably peak Samsung"** (same screen size and battery as today's base). Plus a note that **the only brand whose
phones' batteries swell in his long-term storage is Samsung.** Ends with the signature **graphs** (screen size,
battery, megapixels, price over time). (dbrand sponsor.)

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-03-02] **Samsung will try anything.** "'Samsung will try anything — that's their thing. They have a ton of
  phones in every price bracket, and they throw stuff at the wall to see what sticks. The tell is when they try
  something for one generation and it's gone the next — head-tracking scroll, the heart-rate sensor, the dual
  aperture, the snap-on keyboard (top-five worst accessories of all time).'"
- [2024-03-02] **They've aimed for one day of battery the whole time.** "'They just keep aiming for one day of
  battery life — that's been the theme since the beginning. These get more powerful and do more every year, but as
  long as they hit one day, they seem fine with it. Capacity grew a lot; the target never moved.'"
- [2024-03-02] **The Note 7 — the greatest PR disaster in smartphone history.** "'The Note 7 blowing up has to be the
  greatest PR disaster in the history of smartphones, by a long way. A thin-separator battery defect; it got banned
  on planes, recalled worldwide. A lot of companies would never recover — but Samsung was established enough to
  weather it. (Fun not-so-fun fact: the only brand whose phones swell up like balloons in our long-term storage is
  Samsung.)'"
- [2024-03-02] **The Exynos-vs-Snapdragon split failed spectacularly.** "'The S8's choice to split Exynos and
  Snapdragon by region was smart in theory (make your own chips, don't pay Qualcomm) — ahead of their time. But it
  failed spectacularly: Snapdragon versions consistently outperformed, and buyers went into forums to import them.
  They finally gave up and went all-Snapdragon with the S23 — good (everyone gets the good chip) and bad (less
  competition for Qualcomm).'"
- [2024-03-02] **Base models got boring; the S20 was arguably peak Samsung.** "'Since ~the S21 the base models got
  boring — the action moved to the Ultra (which stole the S Pen from the Note) and the FE/A-series at the bottom,
  leaving the base phone in a play-it-safe no-man's-land. There's no leadership there, just enough brand cache that
  people buy the Samsung one next to whatever everyone's buying. By the graphs, you can argue the S20 was peak
  Samsung — same screen size and battery as today's base phone.'"
