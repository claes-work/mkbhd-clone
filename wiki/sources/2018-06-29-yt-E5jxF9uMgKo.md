# Dear YouTube! (@mkbhd, 2018-06-29)

## Summary
A "Dear X" open letter to YouTube — constructive, not a rant. Triggered by YouTube's silent thumbnails A/B test,
it lays out his **over-communication principle** for platforms and two concrete asks (tell everyone; make it
opt-out). A cornerstone creator-business/platform-critique node.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2018-06-29] **The "Dear X" frame — love first, then critique.** "**I love you guys** — there's a lot YouTube does
  for creators that no one else does. But there are things you could do better." His constructive-critic register
  (same family as Dear Snapchat/Dear Apple).
- [2018-06-29] **The trigger — the silent thumbnails experiment.** YouTube ran a test where **0.3% of viewers got
  an auto-generated thumbnail** instead of the creator's custom one, announced only in an obscure forum post.
  "**0.3% of 1.9 billion monthly users is still 5+ million people** — a lot of creators whose livelihoods depend
  on those thumbnails, blindsided." He does the math to show scale.
- [2018-06-29] **"I went to business school — I get it's a business decision."** "You want to improve auto-generated
  thumbnails for the millions who use them, so you A/B test. **I get it — this is how you make YouTube better.**"
  He explicitly grants the company's rationale before criticizing — his signature both-sides method (+ a dated
  biography tell: **business school**).
- [2018-06-29] **Ask #1 — TELL everyone (put it where creators look).** "Don't bury it in a Google product forum
  <1,000 people saw, or a video on a 60k-sub channel. **Put it in the Creator Studio we check every single day**
  — a labs/experiments section with the details and a link." Specific, actionable.
- [2018-06-29] **Ask #2 — make it OPT-OUT.** "Opt-in won't get you a big enough sample. **Opt-out is the way:** tell
  us, and if we don't like it, we uncheck the box. Casual users who ignore the studio stay in your sample; the
  creators who rely on custom thumbnails can protect their business. **Everyone wins.**" A designed compromise.
- [2018-06-29] **The principle — "err on the side of over-communication, especially with creators."** "Usually
  you've erred on the side of **under-communication — that's the main crux of why people get disappointed or mad.**
  Sometimes you'll need a test no one can opt out of, and people will be upset — but at least they'll **know**.
  **We just want to feel somewhat in control, and part of that is knowing what's going on.**" The durable takeaway.
