# The Future of Smartphone Cameras? Sony Xperia 1 IV! (@mkbhd, 2022-05-24)

## Summary
A review of the Sony Xperia 1 IV built around one landmark feature: **continuous optical zoom.** Where every other
phone has fixed focal lengths (so anything between them is digital zoom that adds no detail), the Xperia 1 IV's
telephoto is a **real moving-lens zoom (85–125 mm / 3.5×–5.2×) — actual optical zoom throughout the range.** His
thesis: **this is, or logically should be, the future of smartphone cameras** — we went from one camera to many to
cover focal lengths; as the tech improves we could go back from many to fewer to one — **but "you can't beat
physics"** (a true do-everything lens is a 53-lb, $165K microwave), and shrinking optics into a phone magnifies
every problem, so "one camera" probably isn't fully reachable. Classic Sony otherwise: an overkill 4K 120 Hz OLED,
an enthusiast Alpha-like camera app (no simple point-and-shoot modes), a headphone jack, **no notch ever**, announce-
now-ship-in-September, just-the-phone-in-the-box — and Sony's software is quietly **one of his favorite near-stock
Androids.** "Glad it exists."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-05-24] **Continuous optical zoom — real optics, not digital.** "'We've gotten used to lots of cameras on the
  back (a status thing), but between the fixed focal lengths it's all **digital zoom — no more detail, just
  convenience** (that "snap" when it switches to the next lens is the tell). The Xperia 1 IV brings back **legendary
  continuous optical zoom: a moving-lens telephoto (85–125 mm) with real optical zoom throughout** (there are
  moving parts inside). It's like having dozens of focal lengths in one telephoto — it maintains sharpness across
  the range.'"
- [2022-05-24] **This is (or should be) the future — but you can't beat physics.** "'This is, or logically should
  be, **the future of smartphone cameras** — we went one → two → three cameras to cover focal lengths, and as the
  tech improves we could go three → two → **one.** But the more I think about it, **you can't beat physics** — even
  a mirrorless camera changes lenses; a true do-everything lens (I found an 8–800 mm) is **the size of a microwave,
  53 lbs, $165K** — and every pain point is magnified shrinking it into a phone with tiny sensors. So "one camera"
  probably isn't fully reachable, but high-quality optics is the key, and the tech has come a long way since the
  2015 Zenfone Zoom.'"
- [2022-05-24] **Classic Sony — the overkill enthusiast phone.** "'The rest is exactly what you'd expect from Sony:
  an overkill 6.5" 4K 120 Hz OLED (now up to 1,000 nits — a battery hog), a bigger 5,000 mAh battery, an 8 Gen 1,
  all three rear cameras shoot 4K 120 fps with Alpha-like eye-tracking AF — but it leans hard into the Alpha
  enthusiast experience: **no simple point-and-shoot portrait or night mode; you dive into Sony's camera app.**'"
- [2022-05-24] **The classic Sony downsides — and the box.** "'The downsides: **announced now, doesn't hit the US
  until September** (so the hype curve drops), and the box **has just the phone — no brick, no cable, no
  paperwork.** Expensive.'"
- [2022-05-24] **Sony's software is quietly a favorite — glad it exists.** "'I really enjoy these Sony phones —
  **the software has quietly become one of my favorite near-stock Android experiences** (smooth, well-updated),
  they consistently deliver innovative enthusiast features, and they **refuse to drop the notch [never had one] or
  the headphone jack. I'm glad it exists.**'"
