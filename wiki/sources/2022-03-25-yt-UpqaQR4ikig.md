# Does Fast Charging ACTUALLY Ruin Your Battery? (@mkbhd, 2022-03-25)

## Summary
An explainer answering the universal comment-section fear that ultra-fast charging (120 W Xiaomi, a demoed 240 W
Oppo) must be destroying phone batteries. His research-backed conclusion: **fast charging does NOT have to ruin
your battery** — because **heat is the #1 factor that degrades a battery**, and companies engineer around it. He
explains the mechanism (lithium ions through an electrolyte; **the "wattage" number is only a brief peak — charging
follows a curve** that tapers after ~80%), the anti-heat techniques (**power management in the brick, parallel
charging** — splitting into two cells for less heat at a slight capacity cost, and more cooling hardware), and the
**industry-standard target: 80% battery health after 800 cycles** (~2 years). His practical takeaway: **use the
phone like normal and just don't give it extra reasons to get hot** (no hot dashboards, no plugged-in gaming) —
that's the best thing you can do for battery health.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-03-25] **Heat is the #1 battery killer.** "'Batteries naturally degrade through charge cycles, and they hate
  being at 100% or 0% — but **the number one factor that degrades a battery faster than normal is heat.** The
  electrolyte (like salts) can crystallize and clog the anodes/cathodes; and inefficient charging spills excess
  energy as heat. So the whole game is minimizing heat around the battery.'"
- [2022-03-25] **The wattage number is a brief peak — charging is a curve.** "'A battery is like a sponge — it
  absorbs the most when nearly empty, then tapers. **The "120 W / 150 W" numbers are the peak, not the constant
  rate — a 65 W charger only hits 65 W for the first few minutes, then trickle-charges after ~80%** (you can watch
  it on one of those nerdy display cables).'"
- [2022-03-25] **How they beat the heat — the brick, parallel charging, cooling.** "'The techniques: **move power
  management into the brick** (OnePlus's Warp Charge 30T — same power, less heat near the battery, but a bigger
  brick); **parallel charging** — split the battery into two cells so 25 W into each ≈ 50 W total with far less heat
  (the downside: slightly smaller overall capacity); and **more cooling hardware** (vapor chambers, even fans —
  every announcement now has a cooling section).'"
- [2022-03-25] **The industry standard — 80% after 800 cycles.** "'The generally-accepted target is **80% battery
  health after 800 charges (~2 years of daily charging).** My 13 Pro is at 97% after 5 months, on track for ~85%
  at 2 years — above standard. Xiaomi rates its 120 W tech at 80% after 800; Apple says 80% after just 500 (but
  seems to outperform its own claim — **under-promise, over-deliver**); Oppo/OnePlus claim 80% after 1,600.'"
- [2022-03-25] **The practical advice — use it like normal, don't let it get hot.** "'Fast charging doesn't have to
  ruin your battery (though the trade-offs — a thicker phone, a bigger brick, a smaller cell — are real). Batteries
  are smart now (temperature sensors, optimized overnight charging that holds at 80% until you wake, pass-through
  charging while gaming). **The best thing you can do is use the phone like normal and don't give it extra reasons
  to get hot** (no sunny dashboards, no long plugged-in gaming). Fun fact: the Note 7 explosions weren't from fast
  charging — it was a battery-supplier dimensions error.'"

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "The number one factor that damages batteries, that degrades them faster than normal, that ruins your battery, is
> heat."
> "Those numbers that you keep seeing with the 100 watts and 150 watt fast charging — that's not the constant rate,
