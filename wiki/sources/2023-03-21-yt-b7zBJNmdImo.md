# This Smartphone Hardware is Getting Crazy! (@mkbhd, 2023-03-21)

## Summary
Using the China-only **Oppo Find X6 Pro** as the example, an appreciation of how **ridiculously capable top-end
smartphone hardware has gotten — "we might actually be taking it for granted."** He walks through three areas.
**Battery:** "all-day" meant a 2,000–3,000 mAh cell a decade ago; now it's all-day-plus from a 5,000 mAh cell that
**charges at 100 W wired / 50 W wireless** while doing dramatically more work — so all-day today is far more
impressive than all-day then (plus a wild **ultra-low-temperature charging trick**: in extreme cold the phone runs
an artificial benchmark load to heat itself so the battery can fast-charge). **Display:** a 6.82" 3168×1440 (510
PPI) LTPO AMOLED, 1–120 Hz, 2,500 nits — numbers that "would be nuts" to someone from eight years ago. **Camera:**
here's the key structural point — **you can brute-force a good display or battery just by buying good parts, but
cameras are mostly software, so to separate yourself at the very top you have to combine both — give the software
the best possible information** (a 1-inch-type 50 MP main, big ultrawide/tele sensors). The trade-off/upside of
throwing hardware at cameras is **bigger and bigger camera bumps.** "These pieces of hardware would be unthinkable
six or seven years ago."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-03-21] **We're taking top-end hardware for granted.** "'Smartphone hardware has gotten so ridiculously
  capable — this being at the top of the heap is so capable — that I think we might actually be taking it for
  granted a little bit.'"
- [2023-03-21] **All-day battery is more impressive now than it was.** "'A decade ago all-day meant a 2,000–3,000 mAh
  battery; a few years ago you chose between a big battery OR fast charging. Now it's 2023 and we can have both — a
  5,000 mAh cell at 100 W wired / 50 W wireless. Even if all-day is still all-day, it's way more impressive now given
  how much more these phones are doing.'"
- [2023-03-21] **The ultra-low-temperature charging trick.** "'It has an ultra-low-temperature charging mode: in
  extreme cold, batteries can't charge quickly, so the phone runs an artificial load through the processor —
  basically benchmarking itself over and over — to generate heat and warm the battery to a better temperature.'"
- [2023-03-21] **You can brute-force a display or battery — but not a camera.** "'Display and battery are less
  software-dependent — you can brute-force a good one by buying a good enough part. Cameras are different: it's
  mostly software that determines the character of the photos. So to separate yourself at the very top, a flagship
  has to combine both — give the software the best chance by giving it the best information (a 1-inch-type sensor,
  big optics).'"
- [2023-03-21] **Throwing hardware at cameras works — and means bigger bumps.** "'We're throwing hardware at cameras
  now and it works — massive sensors, big optics. The downside, or upside depending how you see it, is bigger and
  bigger camera bumps. These pieces of hardware would be unthinkable six or seven years ago.'"
