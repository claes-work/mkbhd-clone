# I Tried the Weirdest "Open" Headphones (@mkbhd, 2024-11-29)

## Summary
A roundup of five unusual **open-back** audio devices — headphones that let air (and the room) through so you can hear
your environment. He opens with context: his **daily-driver desk pair is the Sennheiser HD 650** (open-back, so
studio-mates can get his attention without a shoulder-tap). Then he rates five "weird" ones on **sound (/5)** and
**transparency (/5)**: the **Sony LinkBuds Open** (a ring-with-a-hole in the ear — inconspicuous, decent with EQ; 3/4);
the **Bose Ultra Open** (clips on the ear — the most comfortable, "people can tell you can still hear them," but no
multipoint; 3/5); the **Nothing Ear (Open)** (over-ear hook, floats with an air gap — specs are right but feels least
secure and bleeds the most; 4/4); the **NWM open-back headphones** (a floating driver, "like a speaker next to your
head," the **lightest headphones he's ever worn**; his favorite — 4/5, though cheaply built); and an unreleased **Move
Engine UM 600** prototype (looks the most like real headphones but sounds worst — 2/2). The durable technical takeaway:
**the driver-in-the-middle-with-air-around-it design beats the ring-with-a-hole design**, because pointing audio
directly into the ear works better than routing it around a cavity. His closing frame: **modern transparency mode has
mostly tackled this niche and is more versatile** (mics pass the world through, plus ANC, hearing-aid modes, loud-noise
attenuation — "two pairs of headphones in one"). But not all transparency modes are equal (some are muffled/delayed),
so if you want the *best* transparency, nothing beats actually hearing the real world. His analogy: **"if transparency
mode is a VR headset, then open-back headphones are AR glasses."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-11-29] **Open-back explained; his daily driver is the HD 650.** "'Open-back headphones let air through so you
  also hear the room you're in. My daily-driver desk pair is the Sennheiser HD 650 — I edit in them, so if someone in
  the studio wants my attention they can just say something and I still hear them.'"
- [2024-11-29] **The five, rated on sound and transparency.** "'The Sony LinkBuds Open (a ring-with-a-hole — 3/4), the
  Bose Ultra Open (clip-on, the most comfortable but no multipoint — 3/5), the Nothing Ear (Open) (over-ear hook, floats
  with an air gap, least secure and bleeds most — 4/4), the NWM (a floating driver, the lightest headphones I've ever
  worn — my favorite, 4/5), and an unreleased Move Engine UM 600 prototype (looks most like real headphones but sounds
  worst — 2/2).'"
- [2024-11-29] **The driver-in-the-middle beats the ring.** "'The UM 600 is a proof of concept that the ring design
  with the hole in the middle is actually worse than a driver in the middle with air all around it — because pointing
  the audio directly into your ear works better than routing it around a cavity.'"
- [2024-11-29] **Modern transparency mode is more versatile — but open-back is AR glasses.** "'Modern earbuds/headphones
  have tackled this niche with transparency mode — mics pass the world through — plus ANC, loud-noise attenuation, even
  hearing-aid modes (like the AirPods Pro 2). That's two pairs of headphones in one, much more versatile. But not all
  transparency modes are equal (some are muffled or delayed), so for the *best* transparency nothing beats actually
  hearing the real world. If transparency mode is a VR headset, then open-back headphones are AR glasses.'"
