# iPhone Air Review: Beauty is Pain ★★

## Summary

The full iPhone Air review, built on Marques's **"every product design is a series of
trade-offs"** framework — a slider between form and function that Apple pushed hard toward
form. He walks the trade-offs in tiers (small / medium / big concerns), lands two shockers
(durability is a top-tier strength, not the expected weakness; battery is a C-minus), and
closes with a **third lens**: the Air is really a **proving ground for the foldable iPhone**.
A landmark for the thin-phone thesis and the "reviewing the box iOS comes in" line. ★★.

## Key claims

- **[2025-09-23]** Framework: **"Every product design is a series of trade-offs... a slider
  you can push toward form or toward function."** The Air is pushed way toward form —
  "thinner than a number two pencil... thinner than the one that bent." "**Beauty is pain.**"
  (quote)
- **[2025-09-23]** **Small concerns**: only **one speaker** (the earpiece — tinny, mono in
  landscape); **eSIM-only globally** (worse for him as a phone-swapper); **no mmWave 5G**
  (barely matters); the new **C1X modem + in-house N1** perform great; but the **USB-C is only
  USB 2.0 speeds** — "that's just dumb." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-09-23]** **Medium concerns**: **less cooling** (Pro chip, no vapor chamber, no
  aluminum body → it heats up and must be throttling under sustained load, though it never
  showed an overheating message); and the **single camera** (no ultrawide, no real zoom past
  ~5x) — "I can't tell you how many times I went to take a picture and realized I can't switch
  to the ultrawide." The one camera you get is genuinely good (same as the base 17). (paraphrase
  + quote)
- **[2025-09-23]** **Shocker #1 — durability is a top strength, not a weakness.** It survives
  the JerryRigEverything bend test and is "one of the most impressively durable smartphones of
  all time" — titanium flexes back to flat, IP68, and **Ceramic Shield 2** resisted scratches
  even beyond level 6/7. "Maybe they had some incentive to not repeat a certain past incident."
  Also a **7/10 iFixit** repairability with dual-glass entry + day-one disassembly guides.
  (quote)
- **[2025-09-23]** **Shocker #2 — battery is a C-minus.** He'd said silicon-carbon batteries
  would be a 2025 pillar, but **"two of the biggest manufacturers, Samsung and Apple, just
  didn't wanna take the dive"** — so the Air is thinner than ever *and* uses a normal-density
  battery the **same size as the iPhone 11's**. He ends the day at ~15% in Low-Power Mode with
  ~4h screen-on; standby is good but the Pro chip spikes hard (even wireless CarPlay drains it),
  and it charges slower than the rest of the 17s. "Makes me concerned for how this will look in
  three or four years." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-09-23]** "Pretty **classic Apple move to make the problem and then sell you the
  solution**" — the $100 MagSafe battery is "not a very good accessory": the same-size cell that
  only charges the phone to ~65% due to wireless heat loss. (quote)
- **[2025-09-23]** Cameras + battery are **his two deal-breakers** for dailying it — "the
  battery you can kind of work around; the camera you can't." (quote)
- **[2025-09-23]** The **third lens**: the Air is "one half of the upcoming **foldable iPhone**"
  — a good foldable's halves are each even thinner than this. All the miniaturization (N1, C1X,
  reorganized internals) is really groundwork for the first new iPhone form factor since the X.
  Just like Samsung shipped the S25 Edge, then the ultra-thin Z Fold 7. (paraphrase)
- **[2025-09-23]** Meta-critique of the iterative iPhone: **"Now it's like I'm reviewing the box
  that iOS comes in"** — almost zero new hardware features besides the selfie camera. But the
  Air's best effect is that it **"freed Apple up to make the Pro phones even more pro"** (thicker,
  bigger battery, unibody metal) — the one he'll daily. (quote)
