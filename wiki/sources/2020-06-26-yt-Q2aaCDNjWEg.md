## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
### Marques-attributed (persona-eligible)
- [2020-06-26] **"DESIGN IS SUBJECTIVE — you might get used to it and still not like it."** "'Federighi's Big Sur
  defense is an eloquent way of saying **"you'll get used to it" — and he's got a point, you probably will.** But
  **design is subjective; sometimes there are designs you get used to and still don't like.** My example: **I use
  Waze for the features, but I hate looking at it every time — it's not clean or elegant — yet I don't switch.**'"
  A promotable design principle (get-used-to-it ≠ like-it).
- [2020-06-26] **The "people complain and buy the next iPhone anyway" read.** "'Apple knows **people complain about
  iPhones all the time and then buy the next one** — so they know people won't leave macOS over new icons.'" A
  media-literacy observation about how design outrage rarely changes behavior.
- [2020-06-26] **His own default-apps + privacy takeaway.** "'The browser point is a good one: **if any app can
  become your browser, and that app has different tracking/privacy policies, it undermines your own privacy
  policy** — so sometimes Apple takes a stance against convenience to hold its strict privacy line. Makes sense.'"
  (Framing Federighi's answer in his own privacy-values terms.)
- [2020-06-26] **The interviewer's own asks (his priorities).** "Marques's questions reflect his own advocacy: an
  **iPad weather/calculator app** ("my favorite app, Dark Sky, is no longer on Android"), his **iPad-multitasking
  advocacy** (wanting Siri to stay put on the bigger screen), and the **fix-this-battery-icon** plea." His stated
  wishes, in his voice.
- [2020-06-26] **BIOGRAPHY — the Federighi conversation on Waveform.** "A full, longer version aired on the
  **Waveform podcast** — a datable high-profile interview milestone.'"
