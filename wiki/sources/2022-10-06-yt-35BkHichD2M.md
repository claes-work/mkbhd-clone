# iPhone 14 Plus & The Problem with Benchmarks! (@mkbhd, 2022-10-06)

## Summary
A commentary hung on the iPhone 14 Plus (the mini's replacement — a bigger 14 with the best-ever iPhone battery,
lighter than the smaller Pro thanks to aluminum). His main point: **benchmarks are less relevant than ever.**
Years ago, big year-over-year performance jumps **unlocked new things** (4K video, HD games, HDR) — but smartphones
are now so complete that every phone does the fundamentals well, so the GeekBench bump "doesn't really mean
anything significant." The real story is **custom silicon accelerators** (Apple's ProRes engine / neural engine /
ISP / display engine; Google's Tensor ML block) that **make a bigger difference than any benchmark will show.** His
**Rivian-R1T-vs-Porsche-911-GT3 analogy**: on raw numbers you'd pick the truck, but you should **pick by the
specific features/things you want to do, not general benchmarks.** Part two: a check-in a month later — **one of the
buggier iPhone launches in years** (a lock-screen auto-rotate bug, Dynamic Island glitches, and, most concerning,
**battery drain**), plus the tip that **disabling the always-on display improved his 14 Pro battery.**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-10-06] **Benchmarks used to unlock things — now they don't.** "'Years ago, big year-over-year performance
  jumps were exciting **because they unlocked new things — 4K video, HD games, HDR photos** (before the jump you
  couldn't; after, you crushed a threshold). **But smartphones are so evolved and complete now that every phone
  does all the fundamentals well** — so the GeekBench score still creeps up, but it means almost nothing
  significant (a few more fps, an app launching slightly faster). And the 14 Plus has the same A15 as last year, so
  it means almost nothing.'"
- [2022-10-06] **The real story is custom-silicon accelerators.** "'What's really happening: companies design their
  own silicon with **special sections that accelerate what they think their users prioritize.** Apple: a ProRes
  accelerator, a neural engine, an ISP that fuses exposures fast, a display engine for the Dynamic Island. Google:
  Tensor's ML block for speech-to-text and HDR+. Even Oppo adds a specialized ISP. **Those focused pieces make a
  bigger difference than any benchmark will show.**'"
- [2022-10-06] **The Rivian-vs-Porsche analogy — pick by what you want to do.** "'Imagine choosing a track vehicle
  by benchmarks alone: Vehicle A (0–60 in 3.5s, 502 hp, 339 lb-ft) vs Vehicle B (0–60 in 3.0s, 835 hp, 98 lb-ft).
  On numbers you'd pick B — **you just picked the Rivian R1T over a Porsche 911 GT3.** That's how I feel about
  general benchmarks: **pick based on the specific features and things you want to do** (a huge trunk, aero,
  cameras, or the longest battery — get the big one).'"
- [2022-10-06] **One month later — the buggiest launch in years.** "'It's been one of the notably buggier new-iPhone/
  iOS launches in years. From my ~thousands of Twitter replies, the top three: **(1) a lock-screen auto-rotate bug
  when customizing; (2) Dynamic Island glitches** (the bubble moving/animating wrong — it breaks the illusion);
  **and (3) battery drain** — an alarming number of people reporting warm phones with nearly-dead batteries
  overnight — **the one I'm most concerned about and the hardest to fix.** (My only issue: slow/failed wireless
  CarPlay.)'"
- [2022-10-06] **The always-on-display battery tip.** "'I noticed **better battery on the 14 Pros after disabling
  the always-on display** — it does make a difference (a follow-up from the review).'"

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "In the smartphone world these days, the benchmarks are less and less relevant than ever, and I almost don't even
> pay attention to them anymore."
> "Those pieces make a bigger difference than any benchmark will show."
> "You still should be picking based on the specific features and things you want to do."
