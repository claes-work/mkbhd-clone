# iPhone vs Android (The Real Winner)! (@mkbhd, 2023-09-01)

## Summary
His **"definitive" iPhone-vs-Android** video, structured to dodge the entrenched debate: **seven meaningful
categories** (an odd number, so there's a winner) — but crucially, **"there is no objective winner to a decision
this personal,"** so he gives you a **rubric to find *your* winner** (rank the seven by importance, rate each 1–5,
tally to your side). His category calls: **Customization → Android** (Apple keeps adding abilities but only "the
Apple way"); **Features → Android** (both mature and copy each other, but Android has endless little OS-level tweaks
and hardware choice); **Ease of use → iPhone** (consistency for 17 years, no bloatware, better support — ease of use
has an *inverse* relationship with customization); **Support/updates → iPhone** (iOS 17 back to the 2018 XR = five
versions; Samsung's 4–5 years leads Android but none match Apple); **Apps → iPhone** (quality over quantity;
developers prioritize iOS for efficiency — one aspect ratio — so updates land there first, even Google's own);
**Excitement → Android** (variety — folding/gaming/compact/fast-charging; ironically the iPhone's excitement is
often "will it finally get a feature Android had for years"); **Ecosystem → iPhone (slight)** (Samsung has congruent
pieces, but Apple's seamlessness/continuity + US popularity win). Tally: **iPhone objectively wins 4/7 — but put your
pitchforks down.** He mains Android himself. **"Buying a phone is one of the most personal decisions you make... the
winner, not to be cheesy, is us"** (they compete to get better).

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-09-01] **Seven categories — but rate and weight them yourself.** "'Seven meaningful categories, an odd number
  so there's a winner. But we're not psychos — one winner doesn't apply to every human. So I'll give you a rubric:
  put the seven in order of importance to you, rate each 1–5 by how much you care, and tally to your winner. For me,
  customization and features matter most, so my winner is clearly Android.'"
- [2023-09-01] **Android wins customization, features, excitement.** "'Customization goes to Android — Apple keeps
  adding abilities, but always "the Apple way," which is why iPhone setups still look the same; Android just lets you
  do whatever you want (you can also make a truly ugly Android setup). Features → Android (endless little OS-level
  tweaks and hardware choice). Excitement → Android (variety — and ironically the iPhone's excitement is often "will
  it finally get a feature Android had for years").'"
- [2023-09-01] **iPhone wins ease of use, support, apps, ecosystem.** "'Ease of use → iPhone (it has an inverse
  relationship with customization; 17 years of consistency, no bloatware, better support). Support → iPhone (iOS 17
  back to the 2018 XR). Apps → iPhone (quality over quantity; developers prioritize iOS for efficiency, so updates
  land there first, even Google's own). Ecosystem → iPhone, slightly (Samsung's pieces are congruent, but Apple's
  seamlessness and US popularity win).'"
- [2023-09-01] **iPhone wins 4/7 — but there's no objective winner.** "'Tally it up and the iPhone objectively wins
  four of the seven categories I made up — but put your pitchforks down. There's no objective winner to a decision
  this personal when the options are this close. I main an Android alongside an iPhone; my main customized setup is
  Android.'"
- [2023-09-01] **Buying a phone is deeply personal — the winner is us.** "'It's like buying a car — add your own
  heavily-weighted category (Cool Factor) if you want. Buying a phone is one of the most personal decisions you make
  — it's what you spent your money on and carry everywhere, which is why people get so worked up. At the end of the
  day, the winner, not to be cheesy, is us: they compete against each other to get better and win you over.'"
