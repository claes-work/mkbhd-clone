# Apple's Forbidden Words (@mkbhd, 2023-06-15)

## Summary
A branding/media-literacy essay: watching WWDC, Apple spent **45 minutes on the Vision Pro and never once said
"virtual reality"** — and it **never says "AI"** either (contrast Google I/O, "AI, AI, generative AI" nonstop). Apple
prefers "machine learning" / "a Transformer model on device." **Why? Two reasons: control and comparison.**
**Control:** attach your product to a word the public or competitors already use heavily, and if sentiment around
that word sours, it drags your product down — so Apple won't risk associating with a controversial word (like "AI,"
with its art-stealing "dark side") it can't control. **Comparison:** Apple **avoids acknowledging other companies at
all** (it pretends it's the only company in the world) and, crucially, **names everything** — instead of "120 Hz
LTPO" it's **ProMotion**, instead of "optical fingerprint reader" it's **Touch ID**, instead of a hole-punch it's a
**Dynamic Island** — which makes its version **harder to compare** to everyone else's spec race. The cherry on top:
**Apple often won't even state the spec** ("when's the last time you saw an iPhone's mAh in a keynote?" — just "the
longest battery life of any iPhone"). With the Vision Pro, it **brands VR as "environments"** so it can steer you
toward thinking of it as AR. His takeaway: **"pay attention to the words."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-06-15] **Apple never says VR or AI — and it's on purpose.** "'Apple spent 45 minutes on the Vision Pro and
  never said "virtual reality" once. And they never say "AI" — at WWDC they used "AI"/"artificial intelligence"
  exactly zero times (they said "machine learning," "a Transformer model on device"). Go back to Google I/O for
  contrast: AI, AI, generative AI, nonstop.'"
- [2023-06-15] **Reason one — control.** "'A big reason for what Apple does is control, and that extends to branding.
  If you associate your product with a word the public or competitors already use, you risk sentiment around that
  word souring and dragging your product down. AI has a public persona with a dark side (art-stealing accusations) —
  so if you care that much about your image, you just don't say it.'"
- [2023-06-15] **Reason two — comparison; so they name everything.** "'Apple avoids comparing itself to or even
  acknowledging other companies — it pretends it's the only company in the world. Instead of using everyone's term,
  it names everything: ProMotion, Touch ID, Taptic Engine, Ceramic Shield, Dynamic Island. Branding it for yourself
  specifically makes your version harder to compare — while everyone else races 120 → 165 → 244 Hz, Apple just says
  "this ProMotion is smoother than any ProMotion we've made."'"
- [2023-06-15] **They often won't even state the spec.** "'The epitome of control and comparison: a lot of times
  Apple won't say the actual spec at all — when's the last time you saw the mAh of an iPhone in a keynote? They'll
  just tell you "the longest battery life of any iPhone we've ever made."'"
- [2023-06-15] **The Vision Pro is a VR headset they call AR.** "'They never want to call the Vision Pro a VR headset
  even though it totally is (the theater demo is literally a VR headset), because their ambitions are to steer it
  toward AR — easier if you never say "virtual reality" out loud. They branded VR as "environments." Next time you
  watch a keynote — the graphics, the drone shots, the power stances — pay attention to the words.'"
