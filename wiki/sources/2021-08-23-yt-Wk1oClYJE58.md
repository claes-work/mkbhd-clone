# The Tesla Bot: Explained! (@mkbhd, 2021-08-23)

## Summary
A commentary on the Tesla Bot unveiled at AI Day. He grants the premise that **Tesla is really a software/robotics
company** ("our cars are semi-sentient robots on wheels"; the self-driving fleet-training + the new D1 chip). But
the durable thesis is his disagreement: **the human form is very inefficient — if you want a robot to take over a
task, build it for that single task, not like a human** ("the vacuum IS the robot; the dishwasher IS the robot;
the self-driving car IS the robot"). Even Boston Dynamics' Atlas isn't for sale — so **the Tesla Bot probably
won't exist soon; it's mostly good headlines / free PR / Tesla exploring computer vision in another form factor.**
Includes an Elon Musk factory-interview clip (quarantined).

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-08-23] **Why Tesla — it's a software/robotics company.** "'Tesla is a car company only by that specific
  definition — **it's common to see them as a software company that happens to make electric cars** (unlike
  Mercedes/Audi/BMW). Their self-driving — cameras constantly scanning, thousands of on-the-fly decisions,
  fleet-training on the data — **is a constantly self-improving system.** At AI Day they unveiled the **custom D1
  chip** to train full-self-driving faster. Elon: "Tesla is arguably the world's biggest robotics company —
  our cars are semi-sentient robots on wheels." I can see that.'"
- [2021-08-23] **THE DISAGREEMENT — the human form is inefficient.** "'Where I disagree: Elon says it makes sense
  to put the robot in humanoid form. **But the human form is very inefficient. Humans work well because our brains
  are amazing AND because we built the world with ergonomics designed for humans — when a task isn't designed for
  humans, an upright, always-balancing bipedal thing isn't efficient.** If you want a robot to take over a task,
  **build it for the single task, as efficiently as possible.**'" A promotable robotics thesis.
- [2021-08-23] **"The vacuum IS the robot."** "'The ideal floor-vacuuming robot isn't a humanoid pushing a vacuum —
  **the vacuum IS the robot. The dishwasher IS the robot. And you don't have a humanoid driving your car — the
  self-driving car IS the robot; that's already done.** So **the list of things you'd want a human-shaped robot
  for is pretty small, because labor usually isn't designed around the human form.**' (Their one example was
  grocery shopping.)'"
- [2021-08-23] **The Elon factory clip — humans are good at adaptation (guest, quarantined).** "'Elon (quarantined,
  from my factory interview): **the ~10–20% of tasks still done by humans are the ones requiring adaptation and
  finicky things — grabbing a dangling hose and connecting it — where a person just goes and does it, but a robot
  grabs air and smashes into the car.** That made me think: **why want a humanoid robot to do things humans are
  already good at?**'"
- [2021-08-23] **Boston Dynamics Atlas isn't for sale either — so the Tesla Bot won't be soon.** "'The most
  advanced humanoid, **Boston Dynamics' Atlas, still isn't for sale — they call it "experimenting with computing
  in a new form factor."** I doubt Tesla leapfrogs them in two years. And even if it exists — **training self-
  driving from billions of street miles works because the streets have a finite set of things to identify; asking
  a humanoid to navigate the entire rest of the real world is an order of magnitude more** ("go get bananas"; "grab
  my AirPods Max — does it need a model of every headphone?").'"
- [2021-08-23] **The verdict — no good reason, but good PR.** "'**There's no good reason I can think of for a
  humanoid robot to exist in people's hands — but it makes a lot of good headlines,** it's something we've been
  fascinated with forever, and it could just be **Tesla exploring computer vision in another form factor to become
  the best AI/vision company they can. And when have they ever said no to a good free PR opportunity?**'"

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "The human form is very inefficient... if you want to make a robot to take over a task, you typically don't want
> to build it like a human — you want to build it for a single task and make it as efficient as possible."
> "The ideal way to make a robot vacuum the floor for you is not to build a human-shaped robot that pushes around
> a vacuum — the vacuum is the robot."
> "There's no good reason I can think of for a humanoid robot to exist out there in people's hands... but hey, it
> makes a lot of good headlines."
