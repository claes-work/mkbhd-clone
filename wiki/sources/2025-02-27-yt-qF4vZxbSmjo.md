# iPhone 16e Review: Who Are You? (@mkbhd, 2025-02-27)

## Summary
A review (opening with a "Honesty Wireless" comedy sketch) of the **$599 iPhone 16e** whose thesis is **"this is not a
budget phone anymore."** The SE used to be the easiest iPhone to recommend (old body, new chip, **$399/$429**); at
**$599** ("the phone I'm testing is $900 with storage") it must be judged differently — and after a week **"I don't
think there's anyone I'd recommend it to."** It's the classic **parts-bin strategy** (mostly an iPhone 14 body: flat
sides, USB-C, IP68, a 6.1" 60 Hz notched screen + Face ID, a new Action Button). The cuts range from fine (a binned
A18 with one fewer GPU core) to harder to swallow: **no colors, no MagSafe** ("feels worse" — just slow 7.5 W Qi with
no alignment magnets), a **smaller single-camera 48 MP sensor** (Pixel-A-like — softer as light drops, no cinematic/
macro/spatial), no Wi-Fi 6E/7, no UWB, no Camera Control. The **sneaky X-factor is Apple's first in-house C1 modem**
(after buying Intel's modem business for ~$1B): **"is this the Apple silicon of modems? You probably won't notice —
which is a good thing"**; no mmWave yet, but the win is **efficiency** (a physically smaller modem → a bigger battery →
battery life on par with his 16 Pro), and debuting it in a **low-stakes phone** makes sense before the iPhone 17 — and
it **plus silicon-carbide batteries may enable the rumored thin iPhone Air.** But the verdict returns to **the Apple
price ladder**: "it exists to push you up to an iPhone 16 — but it fits the ladder, not the price tag." His sharp
recommendation: **"everyone considering the 16e should start by considering the $600 refurb iPhone 15 Pro"** (brighter/
higher-refresh screen, triple cameras, same battery, Apple Intelligence — "just start with that instead") — the same
**identity crisis the Pixel A-series had.** "It's not a bad phone; the new price just doesn't match the phone."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2025-02-27] **This is not a budget phone anymore.** "'The SE used to be definitively budget ($399/$429) and the
  easiest iPhone to recommend to parents, kids, first-time buyers. At $599, this is not a budget phone — it's the
  cheapest new iPhone, but it's not cheap, which makes it much harder to recommend. After a week I don't think there's
  anyone I'd recommend it to.'"
- [2025-02-27] **The parts-bin strategy, and the cuts.** "'The SE trick has always been the parts-bin strategy —
  frankensteining already-engineered parts. This is mostly an iPhone 14 body. The cuts range from fine (a binned A18)
  to harder to swallow: no colors, no MagSafe (just slow 7.5 W Qi with no alignment magnets — feels worse), a smaller
  single-camera sensor (Pixel-A-like), no Wi-Fi 6E/7, no UWB, no Camera Control.'"
- [2025-02-27] **The C1 modem — "the Apple silicon of modems," and efficiency is the win.** "'The sneaky X-factor is
  Apple's first in-house C1 modem (they bought Intel's modem business for ~$1B). Is this the Apple silicon of modems?
  You probably won't notice — which is a good thing. No mmWave yet, but the real win is efficiency: it's physically
  smaller, making room for a bigger battery, so battery life is on par with my 16 Pro. Debuting it in a low-stakes,
  lower-volume phone makes sense before the iPhone 17.'"
- [2025-02-27] **The C1 + silicon-carbide may enable the thin iPhone Air.** "'Apple's always wanted to make super-thin
  stuff, but battery life suffers when they do. Maybe this more-efficient modem, alongside silicon-carbide battery
  tech, is the missing piece that enables a super-thin iPhone Air whose battery doesn't suck.'"
- [2025-02-27] **The price ladder — but it doesn't fit the price tag; start with the $600 refurb 15 Pro.** "'The more I
  think about it, the 16e exists to push you up to an iPhone 16 in Apple's classic price ladder — the next rung is a
  small jump away with MagSafe and an ultra-wide. It fits the ladder, but not the price tag. Everything wrong with this
  phone is the price. Everyone considering the 16e should start by considering the $600 refurb iPhone 15 Pro —
  brighter/higher-refresh screen, triple cameras, same battery, Apple Intelligence. Same identity crisis the Pixel
  A-series had. It's not a bad phone; the new price just doesn't match the phone.'"
