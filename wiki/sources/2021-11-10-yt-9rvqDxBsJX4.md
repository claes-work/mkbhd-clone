# The Best Camera in Any Phone... With a Catch! (@mkbhd, 2021-11-10)

## Summary
A look at the $1,800 Sony Xperia Pro-I — "the most impressive hardware camera setup of any smartphone right now,
but there is a catch." Two durable ideas. First, a **tech-industry thesis: how much a phone matters to a company
depends on how much money it makes them** (Apple/Samsung pour energy in; Microsoft lives on enterprise, Google on
ads, Sony on the PlayStation/cameras/movies — so their phones can be niche and quirky). Second, **the catch:** the
Pro-I has the same physical **1-inch sensor as Sony's RX100 VII pocket camera**, but the lens's image circle
doesn't cover the whole sensor, so it's **effectively a ~1/1.3" crop** — and at f/2 max it actually gathers LESS
light/bokeh than an iPhone 13 Pro. And **computational photography wins**: the iPhone's brighter, better-dynamic-
range shot beats Sony's more natural-but-flat one. A specialist device he's **glad exists** but would recommend
only to those who "really know what they're getting into."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-11-10] **Money-priority determines phone quality — the industry thesis.** "'Some companies treat their
  phone lines based on how much money it makes them. **Apple and Samsung spend a ton of energy because a ton of
  their money comes from phones. Microsoft makes its money in enterprise, Google is mostly ads, Sony makes the
  PS5, Alpha cameras, and movies — so for them a phone doesn't move the needle, which is why they keep shipping
  ridiculous, super-niche ones.**'"
- [2021-11-10] **The incredible hardware — a camera-first phone.** "'A 4K 120 Hz OLED in a 21:9 body, a dedicated
  shutter button, microSD, a headphone jack, a wrist-strap eyelet — and a **1-inch main sensor, the largest ever
  in a phone; literally the same CMOS as the RX100 VII pocket camera.** Expensive aspherical glass lens elements
  inside (not the usual plastic), variable f/2–f/4 aperture. **It kind of feels like a camera first that happens
  to have a phone attached.**'"
- [2021-11-10] **THE CATCH — the image circle doesn't cover the sensor.** "'Here's the catch: **the phone doesn't
  use the whole 1-inch sensor. The optics are pushed so close that the lens's image circle is smaller than the
  sensor, so it's shooting a 12 MP crop of the 20 MP 1-inch sensor — effectively a ~1/1.3" sensor.** And f/2 max
  is actually narrower than rival flagships at ~f/1.5–1.6. So combined, **the Xperia Pro-I gathers less light and
  has less bokeh than an iPhone 13 Pro, S21 Ultra, or Pixel 6.** A wild quirk: a crazy huge sensor it can't fully
  use.'"
- [2021-11-10] **Computational photography wins.** "'Side by side, most people pick the iPhone shot — brighter,
  better dynamic range. **Sony doesn't do nearly as much computational photography, so its result is more natural,
  more true-to-life technically, but often duller and flatter.**'"
- [2021-11-10] **Glad it exists — but know what you're getting into.** "'I'd only recommend it to people with money
  to burn who really want the manual control Sony's apps offer (the video focus slider, etc.). **You've got to
  really know what you're getting yourself into — not a daily for most people. But I'm really glad it exists;
  smartphone cameras leveling up is one of the biggest things still differentiating the good phones at the high
  end. Love to see it.**'"
