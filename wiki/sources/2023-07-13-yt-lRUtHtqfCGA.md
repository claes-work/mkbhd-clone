# Nothing Phone 2 Review: A Real Personality! (@mkbhd, 2023-07-13)

## Summary
A review of the **Nothing Phone 2** ($599 start) that opens by defending its **similar-to-Phone-1 design**: people
were disappointed it looked so alike, but **"that's missing the point"** — a two-year-old company **can't
dramatically change its DNA year to year** or it never establishes an identity. And anyway **"the design matters,
but it's not the only thing that matters"** — most changes here are more-than-skin-deep (a brighter 1,600-nit LTPO
display, a centered selfie cutout, the Snapdragon 8+ Gen 1, a bigger 4,700 mAh battery with 45 W charging). The
core thesis: **the phone is defined by its character-filled software** — "I feel the same way about it that I do
about the Pixel: it has its own character version of Android" (London-isms like "the bin," the dot-matrix fonts,
interactive lock-screen widgets, the transparent-everything aesthetic). The **Glyph LEDs are a semi-gimmick** (some
useful bits — a progress indicator, an essential-notification light — buried in novelty; he wishes for fuller
customization). Battery is much improved; **cameras are a bummer** (average, with a rare ghosting bug). On price
(now up to ~$700 configured), his verdict: **"if you think 'worth it' is purely a specs question, you won't see
this as worth it"** — the two loud camps (Reddit "just an average mid-ranger" vs the "greatest design ever" fan
club) both usually haven't used it; **"the truth, per usual, falls somewhere in between,"** and once you use it you
realize it's **"really the software"** — character-filled and fun, even if a little buggy — that makes it the
Nothing Phone 2.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-07-13] **A new company can't change its DNA year to year.** "'People were disappointed it looks so similar to
  the Phone 1, but that's missing the point. If you're a two-year-old smartphone company trying to establish your ID,
  your DNA for how your phones look, you can't dramatically change things year to year — if your second phone looks
  wildly different from your first, it's hard to establish that. It still looks like a Nothing phone, on purpose.'"
- [2023-07-13] **Design matters, but it's not the only thing.** "'The design matters, but it's not the only thing
  that matters — a lot of the changes here are more than skin deep (brighter LTPO display, centered selfie cutout,
  the 8+ Gen 1, a bigger battery, 45 W charging). It feels more premium overall.'"
- [2023-07-13] **Defined by its character-filled software — like the Pixel.** "'This phone has a lot of character —
  little UI tweaks and features that made me go "oh, that's really nice" (resizable home-screen icons, lock-screen
  widgets, quick-settings widgets). I feel the same way about NothingOS that I do about the Pixel: it's its own
  character version of Android — London-isms like "the bin," the dot fonts, the transparent-everything aesthetic done
  really well.'"
- [2023-07-13] **The Glyphs are a semi-gimmick.** "'The Glyph LEDs — some think total gimmick, some think greatest
  thing ever; I'm in between. There are useful bits (a progress indicator, an essential-notification light) buried in
  novelty you probably won't use. I don't get why they won't give fuller customization of the lights.'"
- [2023-07-13] **"Worth it" isn't purely a specs question.** "'If you think "worth it" is purely a specs question,
  you won't see this as worth it — it doesn't have amazing specs. Two camps (Reddit "average mid-ranger" vs the
  "greatest design ever" fan club) both usually haven't used it. The truth, per usual, falls in between — once you
  use it, the specs and design aren't the primary thing; it's really the character-filled software, and that part,
  even a little buggy, is really fun.'"
