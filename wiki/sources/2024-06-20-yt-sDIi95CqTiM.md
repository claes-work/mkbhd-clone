# AI the Product vs AI the Feature (@mkbhd, 2024-06-20)

## Summary
A framework essay sparked by a Wired-podcast line that "nailed" how he thinks about emerging tech: **"WWDC basically
proved that AI is a feature, not a product."** He reframes it as the recurring **"is it a product or a feature?"**
question. The cautionary tale is **Clubhouse**: a meteoric pandemic-era hit (live audio rooms) that **died once
Spotify, Discord, Slack, and Twitter each built the same thing as a feature** inside their bigger apps — "the success
of all the features meant Clubhouse would just die." Now the same question hits AI: 2024 gave us **AI as a product**
(the Humane Pin, the Rabbit R1, or ChatGPT's website), and then, months later, **AI as a feature** — Apple's WWDC and
Google I/O **sprinkled AI throughout the OS** (writing tools, a smarter Siri, image/emoji generation). His read: **"if
history is any indication, more people will end up using AI as a feature than going to a standalone thing"** — he
struggled to find counter-examples, the best being **TikTok** (a standalone still beating Reels/Shorts) and maybe
Snapchat, but "do you have to get all the way to TikTok's level to survive as a standalone?" So **"Humane and Rabbit
were kind of doomed from the start"** — no way they'd also develop all the other benefits of a smartphone. He flags a
second, under-discussed point: **all the Apple Intelligence models are built and trained by Apple** (ChatGPT is barely
integrated — it asks permission every time), an **enormous barrier to entry** — **"this might be the last big set of
models we get to see"**; unless you're Apple/Google/Microsoft/OpenAI, "you probably don't have much of a shot."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-06-20] **WWDC proved AI is a feature, not a product.** "'A Wired-podcast line nailed how I think about a lot of
  emerging tech: WWDC basically proved AI is a feature, not a product. That's the exact product-vs-feature question
  we've been asking here for years.'"
- [2024-06-20] **The Clubhouse cautionary tale.** "'Clubhouse skyrocketed in the pandemic — live audio rooms — then
  within a year or two Spotify, Discord, Slack, and Twitter all built the same thing as a feature inside their bigger
  apps, and Clubhouse just died. That's why the product-vs-feature question keeps coming up.'"
- [2024-06-20] **Features usually beat standalone products — TikTok is the rare counter-example.** "'If history is any
  indication, more people will end up using AI as a feature than going to a standalone thing. I found it really hard to
  find examples of the opposite — the best is TikTok (a vertical-video algorithm that still beats Reels and Shorts as a
  standalone), and maybe Snapchat. But do you have to get all the way to TikTok's level to survive as a standalone?'"
- [2024-06-20] **Humane and Rabbit were doomed from the start.** "'It struck me that Rabbit and Humane were kind of
  doomed from the start, because there's no way they'd also develop all the other benefits of the big thing — the
  smartphone.'"
- [2024-06-20] **Apple built its own models — a high barrier; maybe the last big set.** "'All the Apple Intelligence
  models — diffusion, image, language — are built and trained by Apple. ChatGPT is actually barely integrated; it asks
  permission every single time, about as unintegrated as it gets. Making and training these models costs millions and
  is a really high barrier to entry. This might be the last big set of models we get to see — unless you're Apple,
  Google, Microsoft, or OpenAI, you probably don't have much of a shot.'"
