# Driving 1000 Miles in 3 Cars: Gas vs Electric! (@mkbhd, 2021-09-09)

## Summary
A landmark EV experiment: a 1,000-mile loop in a **Tesla Model S Plaid, a Ford Mustang Mach-E, and a gas Audi Q5
(control)**, all starting full at the same time. Results: gas fastest; **the Tesla added just +1h32m per 1,000 mi
("really not bad"); the Mach-E added +7h32m (mostly day-1 broken chargers).** The durable, counterintuitive
finding: **the biggest factor isn't range or even the Supercharger network — it's the accuracy of the information
in the car** (Tesla's software shows live stall availability/speed/occupancy; Ford navigates you to broken
chargers it doesn't know are down). Plus **Tesla quotes max-EPA range (you get ~15% less)** while Ford/Porsche
under-promise-over-deliver.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-09-09] **The results.** "'Over 1,000 miles (with a ±10% caveat): the **gas Audi Q5 was fastest; the Tesla
  added +1h32m ("really not bad"); the Mach-E added +7h32m — but ~6.5 hrs of that was day one** (broken/offline
  chargers, in-car nav routing to a dead charger). On day two, the Mach-E team ignored the Ford nav and used their
  own apps to hit only Electrify America — much better (+1h03m).'"
- [2021-09-09] **THE COUNTERINTUITIVE FINDING — information accuracy is the real factor.** "'The Tesla road-trip
  experience is far ahead — **but not for the reason you think. Part is the longer range and the Supercharger
  network, sure — but the biggest factor was the accuracy of the information in the car.** Tesla's software tells
  you whether you'll make it, routes you through Superchargers, how long to charge — and **for each charger, how
  many stalls, the speed, how many are out of order, and how many are currently occupied.** Ford tells you as much
  as it knows — but it often doesn't know a charger is down until you get there.'" A promotable software-is-the-
  moat insight for EVs.
- [2021-09-09] **Tesla quotes MAX EPA range — you get ~15% less.** "'Tesla goes by the **max EPA-estimated rating
  (highway-based), which requires near-perfect conditions.** On the first leg, the 345-mile-rated Plaid dropped to
  1 mile left over a 293-mile trip — **a 15% gap. Tesla owners calibrate for this: "if it says 40 left, get to a
  charger in the next 30."**'"
- [2021-09-09] **Ford/Porsche UNDER-promise-over-deliver on range.** "'The Mach-E was very consistent — it slightly
  **outperformed its quoted number, so Ford isn't quoting the max EPA rating.** The Taycan consistently outperforms
  its quote by a lot; the F-150 quotes range with 1,000 lb of cargo. **The general philosophy from the Fords and
  Porsches: under-promise, over-deliver, so the customer doesn't have to worry.**' (So the 345-mi Tesla and the
  305-mi Mach-E "go about the same distance.")'"
- [2021-09-09] **THE ANALOGY — the Supercharger is Lightning; CCS is USB-C.** "'The **Supercharger network is
  built/owned by Tesla to the exact spec of their cars — like Lightning, tightly controlled, vertical integration
  that helps the experience.** Every other EV uses public networks (CCS/Electrify America) — **like USB-C, a more
  open standard: some faster, some slower, some broken, a whole mix.** ~1,100 Superchargers vs Ford's FordPass
  looping in ~600 EA fast chargers plus slow/offline ones.'" A promotable framing of the charging divide.
- [2021-09-09] **The cost + the caveat.** "'Cost: **~$85 gas vs ~$66 Tesla-equivalent vs ~$61 Mach-E** (~30–35¢/
  kWh). **If you don't road-trip much, none of this matters — a big part of EV ownership is starting every day at
  100% in your garage.** But this is constantly changing — faster chargers, more chargers, longer ranges, the 4680
  batteries coming. I'd love to redo this in a year or two.'"
