# Reviewing a Bionic Hand! (@mkbhd, 2025-04-15)

## Summary
"Tech reviews are getting kind of weird" — a review of the **PSYONIC Ability Hand**, a **carbon-fiber-and-metal
motorized prosthetic hand** for people missing a hand: Bluetooth-connected with its own app, durable, **waterproof, and
cheap enough to be fully covered by Medicare.** He demos two control methods: a **control stick** (grips selected in
the app — Cylinder, Pinch, Handshake, Sleeve, Peace, even "Rude"; "the fastest-pairing Bluetooth device I've ever
used"), and — the real one for users — **EMG**, the same electrical-signal sensing as **Meta's Orion AR wristband**
(two skin sensors read the delta to open/close the grip, with haptic feedback and flex-speed control). As a fully-
limbed, untrained user he runs tasks — picking things up, peeling a banana, typing, using a touchscreen, writing with a
pen — noting **"this is the closest fully-limbed people can get to controlling a bionic hand with their brain,"** and
that even crazier demos (a **brain implant**) imply a far higher ceiling. His framing is a values statement: **"it's
really cool to see how far tech has come toward improving the human experience on planet Earth — that's all this stuff
is really about."** He's careful — **"I'm not in the target demographic, but that doesn't mean I can't appreciate how
much work went into this and how well it works"** — and, per his athletic self, notes the limits ("I'm not going to
play Ultimate Frisbee with it"). A companion to his **3D Pets** (3D-printed animal prosthetics) video: two ends of the
"tech that gives function back" spectrum. Sign-off: "technology is strong, but the flesh is weak."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2025-04-15] **A Medicare-covered, waterproof, carbon-fiber bionic hand.** "'The PSYONIC Ability Hand is an
  incredibly cool, incredibly niche, incredibly helpful piece of tech — a carbon-fiber and metal motorized articulating
  hand that attaches to prosthetics to regain functionality. It's Bluetooth-connected, durable, waterproof, and cheap
  enough to be fully covered by Medicare.'"
- [2025-04-15] **EMG control — the same tech as the Meta Orion wristband.** "'The real control method for users is EMG —
  the same technology as Meta's prototype AR glasses wristband, measuring the electrical signals from your brain
  through your nerves. Two skin sensors read the delta to open and close the grip, with haptic feedback and flex-speed
  control. This is the closest fully-limbed people can get to controlling a bionic hand with their brain.'"
- [2025-04-15] **Even as an untrained user, the functionality is impressive.** "'As a completely untrained, fully-
  limbed user I picked things up, peeled a banana, typed, used a touchscreen, and wrote with a pen. There are limits —
  I'm not going to play Ultimate Frisbee with it — but even the amount I could do has me impressed at how much function
  it gives back to the people who need it. And the crazier demos, like connecting it to a brain implant, imply the
  ceiling is way higher.'"
- [2025-04-15] **Tech toward improving the human experience — I'm not the target demographic, but I appreciate it.** "'In
  2025 it kind of feels like of course we should have carbon-fiber, waterproof, five-fingered hands lighter than a
  human hand, controlled by EMG. It's really cool to see how far tech has come toward improving the human experience on
  planet Earth — that's all this stuff is really about. I'm not in the target demographic, but that doesn't mean I can't
  appreciate how much work went into it and how well it works.'"
