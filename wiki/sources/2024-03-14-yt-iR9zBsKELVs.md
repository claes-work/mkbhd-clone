# Small Phones are Dead and We Killed Them (@mkbhd, 2024-03-14)

## Summary
An obituary for the **small flagship phone** — a "rare breed" he'd flagged for years, now **extinct**: the last one
standing, the Asus Zenfone (the near-perfect sub-6" Zenfone 9/10), became the **gigantic 6.8" Zenfone 11 Ultra** in
2024. The recurring pattern: every maker realizes screens are getting big, slots in a smaller alternative (the
Galaxy S Mini, the iPhone Mini), then kills it. His centerpiece is a **deep dive on the iPhone 12/13 Mini**:
supply-chain-master Apple (Tim Cook's thing) planned production knowing small phones don't sell — the Mini got
**glowing reviews** but sold at **~5% of the lineup** ("the least successful new iPhone of all time"), the 13 Mini
did worse, and it was replaced by a **Plus**. The lesson: **"positive reviews don't mean success, and negative
reviews from YouTubers aren't why it died"** — not enough people buy small phones for even Apple to justify the R&D.
This is **"right in line with the enthusiast-phone dilemma"**: a device that gathers a hot-but-small audience, and
when the maker branches out for success, it loses the core fans. And the kicker — **the most popular new iPhone is
still the Pro Max**, so Asus did the rational thing and made a big phone. Even budget small phones are growing (the
6.1" Pixel 7A, the Ultra-sized Nothing 2A). "Someone might briefly fill the void, but don't expect it to last. RIP,
the small flagship."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-03-14] **The small flagship is extinct.** "'I've said for years that small flagship phones are a rare breed.
  Today that's no longer true — that breed is now extinct. The last good one, the Zenfone, is now a gigantic 6.8"
  Ultra. Every maker makes a Mini (the Galaxy S Mini, the iPhone Mini), then kills it.'"
- [2024-03-14] **The Mini iPhone sold ~5% despite glowing reviews.** "'Supply-chain-master Apple planned production
  knowing small phones don't sell well. The 12 Mini got glowing reviews — tech reviewers, real users, YouTube all
  loved it — and it still sold at ~5% of the lineup, the least successful new iPhone of all time. The 13 Mini did
  worse, then it was replaced by a Plus.'"
- [2024-03-14] **Positive reviews aren't success; negative reviews aren't why things die.** "'The moral: the Mini
  reinforced what we knew — not enough people buy small phones for even a company Apple's size to justify the R&D.
  Some people think positive reviews mean success — not always true. And the counter, that negative YouTuber reviews
  are why something died — also not always true.'"
- [2024-03-14] **The enthusiast-phone dilemma.** "'This is right in line with the enthusiast-phone dilemma: whenever a
  maker starts making an enthusiast phone, it gathers a really hot but small audience — and when they branch out to
  make it more successful, they lose the core fans. People who liked the Zenfone for being small certainly won't
  like this one.'"
- [2024-03-14] **The most popular iPhone is the Pro Max — so Asus went big.** "'The fun fact: even today, the most
  popular new iPhone is the Pro/Pro Max — the biggest, highest-end phone. So what did Asus do? They made a super-high-
  end big phone. Even budget small phones are growing (the 6.1" Pixel 7A, the Ultra-sized Nothing 2A). The void is
  open — someone might briefly fill it, but don't expect it to last. RIP, the small flagship phone.'"
