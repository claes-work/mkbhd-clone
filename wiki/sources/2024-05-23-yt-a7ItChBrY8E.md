# I Gave A Commencement Speech! (@mkbhd, 2024-05-23)

## Summary
Marques delivers (and shares) the **commencement speech for the Class of 2024 at Stevens Institute of Technology**, his
own alma mater (he graduated 2015 — "N9 years ago"), which also gave him an **honorary doctorate** (the running joke:
auto-captions used to garble MKBHD into "MK PhD" — "kind of prophetic"). Given 7 minutes, he offers **two things — "one
for now, one for later."** **For now** (via an Ultimate Frisbee captain's huddle wisdom): **"take a beat"** and
appreciate the moment, "because you're going to blink and the whole season's over faster than you think." **For later**:
he admits he **struggles to give advice** — **"a lot of my success was the state of the world, a niche, capitalizing on
good luck; there wasn't really a playbook or set of things I strictly followed"** — so the best he can give is **"find
something that gets you excited."** Graduates are "at a launching pad" with tools already, and just need somewhere to
point that energy "all the way to 100": ask the big questions (who am I, what do I care about, how do I want to make an
impact); **"purpose is also a tool — and to me it's the most powerful one."** For some it'll take a risk; **"for some
of you, like me, it may be something that literally doesn't exist yet"** — and **"making a dent in the universe is much
easier when you care deeply about that part of the universe"** (for him, tech videos on the internet). He closes:
**"you graduate many times as a person"** — first job, a new skill, overcoming a fear, a conversation that unlocks
something — "I wish you many more graduations."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-05-23] **Take a beat (the "for now").** "'Just take a beat and look around at your classmates, your friends,
  your family — this is your day. But I promise you're going to blink and it's going to be 10 years later. Take a
  minute to enjoy where you are now.' (From an Ultimate Frisbee captain before his first tournament at Stevens.)"
- [2024-05-23] **My success was luck + a niche, not a playbook.** "'I've always struggled with giving advice. A lot of
  my success was the state of the world, a niche, capitalizing on good luck — there wasn't really a playbook or set of
  things I strictly followed. It was just something I dove into because it was exciting to me.'"
- [2024-05-23] **Find something that gets you excited — purpose is the most powerful tool.** "'The best advice I can
  give is to find something that gets you excited. You're at a launching pad with a bunch of tools — you just need
  somewhere to point that energy and dial it all the way to 100. Ask the big questions: who am I, what do I care about,
  how do I want to make an impact. You have all these tools, but purpose is also a tool — and to me it's the most
  powerful one.'"
- [2024-05-23] **Making a dent is easier when you care deeply.** "'It won't necessarily be a smooth ride finding what
  that is. For some it takes a risk; for some of you, like me, it may be something that doesn't exist yet, which is
  exciting. But whatever it is, making a dent in the universe is much easier when you care deeply about that part of
  the universe. For me it was tech videos on the internet.'"
- [2024-05-23] **You graduate many times.** "'You actually graduate many times as a person — you continue to level up
  and build and graduate over and over: a first-job graduation, a new-skill graduation, an overcoming-a-fear
  graduation, or a conversation that unlocks a new part of your brain. This certainly won't be your last one.'"
