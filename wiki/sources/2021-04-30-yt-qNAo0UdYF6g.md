# Apple vs The Paradox of Choice! (@mkbhd, 2021-04-30)

## Summary
A landmark commentary using the **shark-and-remora** metaphor: Apple is the shark, and many companies live as
accessories attached to it — "a dangerous place to live." The durable thesis: **Apple can kill entire companies,
but does it "so nicely, by presenting it as a choice on a silver platter"** — a paradox of choice where **either
way Apple wins.** Anchored by AirTags-vs-Tile and a ranked **"kill list"** (Flux, iPad styluses, Duet Display,
Watson) of companies Apple absorbed by entering their category.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-04-30] **The shark-and-remora metaphor — a dangerous place to live.** "'The remora fish suctions to the
  shark — a symbiotic win-win, but **the remora needs the shark far more than the reverse.** In tech, **Apple is
  the shark, and a whole bunch of companies' primary business is an accessory that attaches to something Apple
  does — a dangerous place to live.**'"
- [2021-04-30] **AirTags — the Find My network advantage no competitor can match.** "'Bluetooth trackers aren't
  new (Tile since 2013). But AirTags plug into **the Find My network — nearly a billion iPhones/iPads/Macs
  passively updating locations, frictionless and silent.** Best estimates put Tile at ~1 user per block in
  Manhattan; Apple gets a dozen+ devices per block. **Apple's ability to plug a feature into the iPhone gave
  them a huge advantage instantly.** (Credit where due: the user-replaceable CR2032 battery — "I could've seen
  Apple going the opposite route; they didn't. Nice.")'"
- [2021-04-30] **THE PARADOX OF CHOICE — either way, Apple wins.** "'Apple opened Find My to third parties, so
  Tile has "a choice": **make a Find-My-compatible Tile — and now your new customers improve Apple's network
  instead of yours (you're selling a product that hurts your own business) — OR don't, and slowly die as people
  buy the deeply-compatible ones.** "Look how friendly this guy is — he'd never force you; you can choose to swim
  with the shark or off into the ocean." It happens twice: for Tile AND for the customer.'" A promotable framing
  of ecosystem power.
- [2021-04-30] **THE KILL LIST — ranked 1–10 (how badly Apple left them).** "'**Flux (5/10)** — kicked from the
  App Store, then Apple built Night Shift in. **iPad styluses (6–7/10)** — the Apple Pencil (magnetic, instant-
  pair, low-latency) makes any $100+ stylus hard to justify. **Duet Display (7/10)** — Sidecar does it built-in.
  **Watson (10/10, RIP)** — Sherlock/Spotlight does exactly the same, everywhere (like the flashlight app baked
  into the lock screen). **All on the back of that paradox of choice.**'"
- [2021-04-30] **The antitrust tension — no easy right answer.** "'The day after AirTags, **Tile testified to
  Congress that Apple leveraged an unfair advantage.** Apple's counter: "we let people choose." **There's no easy
  right answer — should Apple not be allowed to leverage its own ecosystem to make great products, or does it
  have too much power?** Watch how much they plug in when they enter the NEXT category (an AR headset, a camera).'"

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "Apple can kill entire companies, but they don't do it menacingly — they do it so nicely, by presenting it as a
> choice on a silver platter."
> "It's actually a bit of a paradox of choice, because either way you choose, Apple still wins."
> "Look how friendly this guy is — he would never force you to do anything... you could choose to swim off into
> the rest of the ocean, or you could stay swimming with me, the shark."
