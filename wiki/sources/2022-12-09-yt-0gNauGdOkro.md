# The Truth About AI Getting 'Creative' (@mkbhd, 2022-12-09)

## Summary
A landmark essay on the ChatGPT/generative-AI moment, opening with a killer gag: **the entire "why AI can't replace
creators" intro was itself written by ChatGPT** and just recited. His "actual human take": **it's amazing to watch
these tools evolve — but in 2022 it's just that, a really impressive tool, not a Creator.** The ideal use for a
creator like him: **brainstorm earlier in the process, then put the human touch on top** (like Photoshop's AI
subject-select, then refine yourself) — human judgment decides what publishes. He flags two dangers: **(1) the
error rate** (ChatGPT confidently gets facts wrong — it claimed the iPhone 14 Pro had a 12 MP main and old colors,
and called a peregrine falcon the "fastest sea mammal"); and **(2) the credit problem** (Lensa-style apps returning
**mangled artist signatures** = a tell that copyrighted art was scraped without consent; the **LAION-5B dataset
includes his own thumbnails**). His reframe: the deep question shifts from **"what is art?" to "what is
inspiration?"** — **AI art is "speedrunning inspiration"** (dumping recorded human history into a black box) — and
the Taylor-Swift/YouTube-monetization analogy for the unsettled copyright question. Optimist's hope: it makes us
**appreciate human-created art more.**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-12-09] **The gag — the intro was written by ChatGPT.** "'"AI can't replace online creators because it's not
  capable of imagination, creativity, and a human perspective — it's a tool, not a Creator." **Every word I just
  read came directly from asking ChatGPT to write a script for an MKBHD video on why AI can't replace creators.**'"
- [2022-12-09] **It's a tool — brainstorm, then add the human touch.** "'My actual human take: (1) it's amazing to
  watch these tools evolve in front of us; (2) **that's all it is — a really impressive tool.** The ideal use for a
  creator like me is **not to take my job but to brainstorm earlier in the process, then put my human touch on
  top** — like the AI subject-select in Photoshop, then you refine the edges. Human judgment decides what
  publishes.'"
- [2022-12-09] **Danger #1 — the error rate.** "'The catch with generative AI is confident wrongness. **ChatGPT's
  iPhone 14 Pro script called the main camera 12 MP (it's the first 48 MP) and listed old iPhone 12 colors — it
  gets at least one thing wrong every time.** Asked the fastest sea mammal, it said **the peregrine falcon** (not a
  sea mammal). Like DALL·E's ~90% accuracy — impressive, but you can't rely on it to be factual. I expect error
  rates to drop over time.'"
- [2022-12-09] **Danger #2 — the credit problem.** "'The #1 app right now is **Lensa AI** (upload your face → AI
  avatars). You consent to your face — **but the artists whose work was scraped to train it did not.** A tell: people
  get back **mangled recreations of artists' signatures**, because the source images had signatures. These apps
  scrape free datasets (**Common Crawl → LAION-5B, which includes a ton of my own work/thumbnails**) then charge
  for the output. How do you credit the artists feeding the machine? (Ask DALL·E for a cat "in the style of Monet"
  and the source material is obvious — Monet wouldn't be happy.)'"
- [2022-12-09] **The reframe — from "what is art" to "what is inspiration."** "'The interesting question shifted from
  "how do we define art" to **"what is inspiration?"** A human drawing is a unique expression, but they were
  inspired by everything they've seen — **AI art is basically speedrunning inspiration: dumping all of recorded
  human history into a black box.** The copyright analogy (like the Taylor-Swift-in-a-free-YouTube-video rule) has
  no precedent yet. Optimist's hope: **this makes us appreciate human-created art more** — but keep an eye on the
  unanswered questions.'"
