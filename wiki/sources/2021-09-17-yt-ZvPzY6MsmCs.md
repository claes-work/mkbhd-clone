# 3 Features the iPhone 13 Didn't Add! (@mkbhd, 2021-09-17)

## Summary
Three bleeding-edge hardware features other phones have that the iPhone 13 avoided — each with a real reason,
illustrating that **the iPhone notoriously plays it safe with hardware.** The durable content: **120 W charging
(iPhone at 20 W) vs. long-term battery health** ("Apple would rather throttle your CPU than overwork your
battery"); a **2nd-gen under-display selfie camera** (nearly invisible now, but photos still bad — "maybe gen 3
graduates from gimmick"); and **high-resolution/108 MP cameras** (iPhone stuck at 12 MP since the 6s) — with a
promotable **pixel-binning** explainer on why more megapixels usually get binned back down.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-09-17] **The frame — the iPhone plays it safe with hardware.** "'The iPhone **plays it safe with hardware,
  notoriously — more obvious this year than ever.** These three phones each have a bleeding-edge feature that's
  risky enough there's a reason it isn't in the iPhone. The difference between Apple's four phones/year and these
  companies' dozens/year is massive.'"
- [2021-09-17] **#1 — 120 W charging vs. battery health.** "'Xiaomi's 11T Pro does **120 W (0–100% in <20 min);
  the iPhone 13 is still 20 W.** It's not for no reason — split cells shrink capacity, and the heat needs cooling.
  But the top reason is **long-term battery health: my iPhone 12 Pro is at 95% after a year. Apple would rather
  throttle your CPU and make your phone slower than risk overworking the battery** (they shipped a 5 W brick
  through the iPhone 11). Still — the convenience of fast charging is indispensable once you're used to it.'" A
  promotable Apple-priorities read.
- [2021-09-17] **#2 — the 2nd-gen under-display camera (much better hiding, still-bad photos).** "'ZTE's Axon 30
  has a **2nd-gen under-display camera — way better than gen 1: doubled pixel count, nearly invisible, only mildly
  pixelated.** But **the photos are still trash — foggy, hazy, blooming highlights, despite the defogging
  algorithm.** The hiding is functionally perfect; how many generations until the photo is only slightly soft?
  Buyers are people who don't care about the best selfies — the opposite of the iPhone (biggest notch for years).
  The iPhone finally shrank the notch 20%. **Maybe gen 3 graduates from gimmick.**'"
- [2021-09-17] **#3 — high-resolution cameras + the pixel-binning explainer.** "'108 MP has trickled down to a
  sub-$300 phone; the iPhone has stuck with **12 MP since the 6s.** I don't want a 100 MP iPhone — but I'm curious
  what Apple could do with more resolution: **12 MP looks fantastic full-screen, but breaks down the second you
  crop in** (little detail to work with), and **8K video needs a 33 MP sensor.** The catch: **every high-res
  sensor uses pixel binning by default** — more megapixels = smaller photosites = less light each = more noise in
  low light, so they **combine groups of photosites for accuracy (and lower final resolution).** So the marketing
  says "108 MP" but normal shots are binned to 12 MP; the high-res mode is rarely used. **How often do you switch
  to the high-res mode? I hardly do.**'" A promotable camera-physics teaching moment.
- [2021-09-17] **The throughline — and foldables too.** "'Each of these is a hardware risk the iPhone avoids —
  and **foldables are another huge hardware risk Apple probably won't jump into for a long time, until it's fully
  ready.** Which of these would you actually want in an iPhone? I bet fast charging is the one most people want.'"
