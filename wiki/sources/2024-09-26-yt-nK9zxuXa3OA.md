# Google Pixel 9 Pro Fold Is So Good! But… (@mkbhd, 2024-09-26)

## Summary
Back from Australia, first thing he did was put his SIM in the **Pixel 9 Pro Fold** — and came away impressed. The
framing: **"the second generation of anything tells you a lot"** — the first Pixel Fold (his favorite foldable to use
*closed*, passport-shaped) had trade-offs; this one **makes big strides**. It's much thinner (~10 mm folded), flat
squared sides, matte finish, a full flagship-size 6.3" cover screen (OnePlus-Open-sized, near the one-hand-use max),
and an 8" nearly-square inner display up to 2700 nits with dramatically improved even bezels. His striking observation:
**"almost everything about the hardware of this Google phone is at or above average — it's like the harder the part is
to make, the more above-average they are"** (the smooth hinge, the thinness "barely thicker than the USB-C port"). The
Pixel **software** is what differentiates it over flashier Vivo/Xiaomi foldables ("their software typically isn't
nearly as good") — Gemini's getting good, Circle to Search, the foldable two-column layouts, PiP. **But** the two known
downsides land: the **cameras are downgraded** (smaller sensors, ~Pixel 7 Pro level — worse in low light), and the
**Tensor chip is underpowered** (fine for the phone, but holding back gaming, longevity, and battery on a $1,800 phone
promised 7 years of updates). So the verdict is a paradox: **"this is both one of the best folding phones I've ever
used and you should probably wait"** — because the rumored **next-gen fully-custom 3 nm Tensor** could be the big jump,
and a $2,000-foldable buyer does care. If that doesn't speak to you, it's great today: **"two phones in one — a full
flagship closed, a mini tablet open — that's the ideal foldable for me,"** and **his favorite flagship foldable ever**
(the OnePlus Open won last year; this beats it). (Ridge wallet — his own design — sponsor.)

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-09-26] **The second generation tells you a lot — big strides.** "'The second generation of anything tells you a
  lot. The first Pixel Fold was my favorite foldable to use closed but had trade-offs; the Pixel 9 Pro Fold makes big
  strides — much thinner (~10 mm folded), flat squared sides, matte finish, a full flagship-size 6.3" cover screen and
  an 8" inner display with dramatically improved even bezels. It feels more like a regular phone than ever.'"
- [2024-09-26] **The harder the part, the more above-average Google is.** "'Almost everything about the hardware of this
  Google phone is at or above average, which is crazy — it's like the harder the part is to make, the better and more
  above-average they are. The hinge is smooth, it unfolds fully flat unlike last year, and it's barely thicker than the
  USB-C port. This hardware feels actually deserving of "Pro."'"
- [2024-09-26] **The Pixel software is the differentiator.** "'There are flashier foldables from Vivo and Xiaomi with
  razor-thin, head-turning hardware — but their software typically isn't nearly as good. Here you get the whole Pixel 9
  experience (Gemini's getting good, Circle to Search) plus the foldable optimizations — two-column layouts, PiP,
  multitasking. That's what differentiates it.'"
- [2024-09-26] **The two downsides: downgraded cameras and an underpowered Tensor.** "'The cameras are smaller sensors —
  worse, about Pixel 7 Pro level, softer in low light — a tough pill on an $1,800 phone if you shoot a lot (I rely on my
  phone's photos, so that's tough for me). And the Tensor chip has never been best-in-class; it holds these phones back
  on gaming, longevity, and battery. It's promised 7 years of updates — will the phone last that long?'"
- [2024-09-26] **Both one of the best and "you should probably wait."** "'It's weird to say this is both one of the best
  folding phones I've ever used and that you should probably wait — because the rumored next Tensor may be the first
  fully custom 3 nm Google chip, a big power/efficiency jump, and a $2,000-foldable buyer does care about that. But if
  that doesn't speak to you, it's great today: two phones in one — a full flagship closed, a mini tablet open — the
  ideal foldable for me, and my favorite flagship foldable ever (the OnePlus Open won last year; this beats it).'"
