# Testing Real 5G: Part 2! (@mkbhd, 2019-12-05)

## Summary
A follow-up to the "5G Explained" landmark — testing T-Mobile's new **low-band (600 MHz) 5G** in LA. The durable
payload: a clear framework for the **two 5G rollout strategies** (coverage-first low-band vs. speed-first
millimeter-wave) and why "5G is a race" is the wrong frame.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2019-12-05] **"It's not a race — there's no finish line."** "'Some call it a race to 5G. I agree with **Dieter
  from The Verge — it's not really a race because there's no finish line; you just keep getting better over
  time.**'" A reframing of the 5G-hype narrative.
- [2019-12-05] **THE FRAMEWORK — two opposite rollout strategies.** "'**Verizon** started with **millimeter wave**
  — incredible speeds but **doesn't travel far or through objects** (couldn't even cover a whole stadium). **T-
  Mobile** is doing the opposite: **low-band first** (covers **miles from a single tower** — I drove 15 min away,
  behind buildings/under trees, still locked to 5G), then mid-band (via the Sprint merger), then fill gaps with
  millimeter wave. **Bottom-up vs top-down.**'" A crisp, reusable comparison.
- [2019-12-05] **The low-band trade-off — coverage over headline speed.** "'Low-band won't get you the **sexy
  1,000 Mbps** headline speeds of standing next to a mmWave tower — I got **40-140 Mbps** (≈20-50% faster than
  good 4G). But **everyone with a 5G device is affected on day one**, not just those in a specific city block. The
  headline speeds are better for PR; the coverage is better for people.'" A PR-vs-real-benefit read.
- [2019-12-05] **No clear winner — a genuine open question.** "'One of the most interesting parts of 5G is the
  **rollout strategy** — mmWave-first (headline speeds now) vs low-band-first (coverage now). **There's no right or
  wrong answer.** Which is better? I'm curious.'" His even-handed framing.
- [2019-12-05] **The McLaren GT quirks-and-features aside (production/EV crossover).** "'While testing (in a 2020
  McLaren GT), I walked through its quirks — hidden door handles, no glovebox, a front trunk bigger than a Model
  S's, a power liftgate, and my favorite: an **electrochromatic tint sunroof** you adjust on a touchscreen.'" An
  Auto-Focus-style gadget walkthrough (defers the drive/review to Doug DeMuro).
