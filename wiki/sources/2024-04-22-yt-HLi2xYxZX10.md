# $20K vs $2,000,000 Hybrid Car! (@mkbhd, 2024-04-22)

## Summary
Part explainer, part supercar-drive: three hybrids at wildly different prices, each hybrid **for a different reason**.
The framing thesis: EVs are "having a renaissance" (the world's best-selling new car is electric) but still have heavy
batteries, slow recharge, and thin infrastructure — so **"there's this window now where a hybrid is actually the best
option for most people."** (1) The **~$24K Toyota Corolla Hybrid — for efficiency**: a small 1.6 kWh battery, no
plug, self-charges via regen, gamifies MPG with an "eco score," good for **50 mpg**; you buy it just to spend *less*
(not zero) on gas. (2) The **~$100K BMW 750e — for versatility**: a plug-in hybrid (14.4 kWh, ~500 hp combined, ~30
electric miles) that does everything the Corolla does *plus* plug-in EV commuting; his takeaway — **"plug-in hybrids
make the most sense for the most people I see driving them"** (a short commute → never buy gas, but road trips still
work). He reprises his **"heavy cars are the best ones to go electric"** rule (a big luxury sedan already carries the
weight). (3) The **$2.5M McLaren Speedtail — for performance**: a tiny 1.6 kWh battery + V8 = 1,000+ hp, **no
electric-only mode, no efficiency goals** — a demo pull drops the battery 100%→74% in seconds then regens right back,
**exactly like an F1 turbo-hybrid powertrain**. The durable EV insight lands here: **super-fast EVs need a huge heavy
battery** to export that power without draining (Model S Plaid's 100 kWh; the Lucid/EV6 GT "fight their weight"),
whereas a hybrid supercar can use a *tiny* battery that dumps power and drains in seconds. Verdict: **"electrification
is still the future at every level of cars, but in the meantime hybrids make a lot of sense — they've been right under
our noses the whole time."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-04-22] **There's a window where hybrids are the best option for most people.** "'EVs are awesome — instant
  torque, no emissions — but the batteries are heavy, they take forever to recharge, and there isn't enough
  infrastructure for everyone. So there's this window now where, for most people, a hybrid is actually the best
  option.'"
- [2024-04-22] **Three hybrids, three reasons.** "'The Corolla is a hybrid purely for efficiency (small battery, no
  plug, self-charging via regen, 50 mpg — you just want to spend less on gas). The BMW 750e is a hybrid for versatility
  (a plug-in — everything the Toyota does, plus ~30 electric miles you can charge from the wall). The McLaren Speedtail
  is a hybrid purely for performance — no electric-only mode, no efficiency goals at all.'"
- [2024-04-22] **Plug-in hybrids make the most sense for the most people.** "'Plug-in hybrids at this moment make the
  most sense for the most people I see driving them: a short commute means you can plug in and basically never pay for
  gas, but on the off chance you road-trip, you still have all the benefits of a gas car.'"
- [2024-04-22] **Heavy cars are the best ones to go electric.** "'Because batteries weigh so much, cars that are
  already heavy are the best ones to go electric — a big four-door luxury sedan isn't trying to be small, so it's a
  great candidate (which is why the i7 is good).'"
- [2024-04-22] **Super-fast EVs need a huge heavy battery — a hybrid supercar doesn't.** "'A Model S Plaid makes 1,000
  hp but needs a 100 kWh battery so exporting all that power doesn't drain it instantly — so super-high-horsepower EVs
  always come with the sacrifice of weight (the Lucid Air, the EV6 GT fight their weight). A hybrid supercar isn't
  electric all the time, so it can use a tiny battery that dumps a ton of power and drains in seconds — a demo pull
  went 100% to 74% then regenerated right back. That's exactly what F1 cars run: small turbocharged hybrid
  powertrains recovering energy under braking and spending it on acceleration.'"
- [2024-04-22] **Electrification is still the future — but hybrids were under our noses.** "'I still fully believe
  electrification is the future at every level of cars. But in the meantime it's pretty clear hybrids make a lot of
  sense — they've been right under our noses the whole time.'"
