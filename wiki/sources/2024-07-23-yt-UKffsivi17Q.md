# When Companies Copy Each Other... (@mkbhd, 2024-07-23)

## Summary
An essay built around the **Samsung Galaxy Watch Ultra vs. the Apple Watch Ultra** — a copy "so far it's hard to
ignore" (same name, same "top of the lineup" premium position, matched titanium/sapphire/100m/3000-nit/dual-GPS specs,
a matching orange custom "Quick Button" (Apple's "Action Button"), lifted watch faces, the app-blob, even the
double-pinch gesture — with a "Marine Band" that's a barely-changed "Ocean Band": **"can I copy your homework but
change it just a little so it's not too obvious?"**). But his thesis, restated: **"I actually don't care that these
products and companies copy each other — as long as they're copying the good stuff, we as customers still win."**
Copying goes both directions (Samsung copies Apple, Apple copies Samsung) and **"if you copy the good stuff, you can
end up with a great product no matter how shameless it is."** The twist: **the one thing Samsung copied that hurt its
case was dropping the rotating bezel** — the beloved differentiator from the Watch6 Classic — likely for the 810H
military certification (a moving part), but **"99% of users would rather have the rotating bezel over the military
cert; the 1% who don't will get a Garmin."** He grants the watch real strengths (a distinctive "cushion" design, great
One UI performance, an easier keyboard, battery that matched/beat the Apple Watch Ultra with ~25% better standby) —
but the conclusion is funny: **the simultaneously-updated Watch7 has the same screen, dual-GPS, and sapphire for half
the price**, cannibalizing the Ultra. The rotating bezel would've justified the separation. "It won't be the last
shameless copy, from either side."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-07-23] **I don't care that companies copy — as long as they copy the good stuff.** "'There have been many copies
  before, in both directions — Samsung copies Apple all the time and Apple copies Samsung all the time. I actually
  don't care that they copy each other. As long as they're copying the good stuff, us as customers still win.'"
- [2024-07-23] **This one is a shameless, near-total copy.** "'Same name, same top-of-lineup idea, matched durability
  specs (titanium, sapphire, 100m, 3000 nits, dual-frequency GPS), a matching orange custom button, lifted watch faces
  and the double-pinch gesture. The "Marine Band" vs Apple's "Ocean Band" is "can I copy your homework but change it up
  a little so it's not too obvious?" — and they didn't change it up enough.'"
- [2024-07-23] **Copy the good stuff and you still get a great product.** "'If you copy the good stuff, you can still end
  up with a great product, no matter how shameless it is. People call it shameless copycat behavior — but the product
  can still be great.'"
- [2024-07-23] **The one thing they shouldn't have copied: dropping the rotating bezel.** "'There's only one thing
  Samsung copied that I have a problem with — it's pure touchscreen, no rotating bezel. On a fitness watch, sweat,
  gloves, sleeves, and swimming all mess with a touchscreen; the rotating bezel would've been a banger differentiator.
  They probably dropped it for the 810H military cert (a moving part) — but I say do it anyway: 99% of users would take
  the rotating bezel over the military certification, and the 1% who wouldn't will get a Garmin.'"
- [2024-07-23] **The Watch7 cannibalizes the Ultra.** "'The funny conclusion: Samsung also updated the Watch7 at the
  same time — same screen size, dual-frequency GPS, sapphire crystal, three sporty bands — for half the price. That
  makes the $650 Ultra harder to recommend. The rotating bezel would've made it easier to justify and separate
  them.'"
