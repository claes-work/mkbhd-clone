# Galaxy S25 Edge and the Thin Phone Problem (@mkbhd, 2025-05-30)

## Summary
A deeper thesis on **ultra-thin phones** built from dailying the **5.8 mm Galaxy S25 Edge**. The obvious take: **"nobody
asked for this — the universal thing everyone wants is more battery, not thinner phones"** (thicker with less camera
bump). "I could end the video there — but what if I actually gave it a shot?" The Edge is a Galaxy S25 Plus from the
front, borrowing the Ultra's **200 MP main sensor** (but **no telephoto**) and One UI 7. The **battery is the price of
physics**: a **3,900 mAh cell (25% smaller than the same-size Plus's 4,900)** → ~4.5–5 hr screen-on vs the ~6 he wants,
and **"when it gets old it'll be even worse."** But the surprise: **"holding it in person has sold way more people than
any video or photo comparison ever could"** — like the Galaxy Tab Ultra and the M4 iPad Pro, it's a genuinely
impressive thin sheet of metal that only clicks in the hand (163 g, very light). On durability, Samsung used **Gorilla
Glass Ceramic 2 + titanium** ("no chance they repeat the bending-in-pocket mistakes of the past"). The **core thesis**:
**thin phones only make sense *with* silicon-carbon batteries** — the tech (from his OnePlus 13 review) lets you either
keep the size and get a *much* bigger battery (the OnePlus 13's 6,000 mAh, ~7 hr SOT — the path he prefers) **or** go
ultra-thin and keep roughly the same battery. **The S25 Edge chose thinness but is NOT silicon-carbon** (Samsung
couldn't source enough, or is battery-shy post-Note-7), so battery suffered. And the **rumored iPhone 17 Air reportedly
won't be silicon-carbon either** — rumors point to **~2,800 mAh** ("so much worse than the rest of the lineup —
fascinating how they'll justify it"). His verdict: **ultra-thin phones *can* work — a thin phone with a 4,500–5,000 mAh
silicon-carbon battery would be amazing** (Realme's normal-thickness 10,000 mAh concept shows the ceiling); "maybe the
S26 Edge gets there."
