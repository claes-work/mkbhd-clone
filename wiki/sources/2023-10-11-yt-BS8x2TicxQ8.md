# Google Pixel 8/8 Pro Review: We'll Fix It In Post! (@mkbhd, 2023-10-11)

## Summary
The full Pixel 8 review built on a filmmaker's metaphor: **"we'll fix it in post."** "A good filmmaker tries to get
it reliably good in-camera instead of relying on software — and the 'we'll fix it in post' smartphone is definitely
the Google Pixel." Its best features literally fix it in post (**Face Unblur, Magic Editor, Audio Eraser**), and
"with AI it actually works — that's where the Pixel shines." This is a **rare W for Pixel hardware**: flat Pro
display, satin backs, and **screens (2,000/2,400 nits) that finally put it alongside any flagship** — though the
fingerprint reader is still slow optical and dust still gathers under the camera bar. Cameras are "software pushed to
its absolute maximum" (great HDR/color; a bit more watercolory noise reduction; some over-HDR), and **Magic Editor
"lowered the barrier to entry to just lie" — "what is a photo?"** But he hits the **software-locking**: the same G3
+ same sensors in both phones, yet **Pro Controls, 50 MP capture, Zoom Enhance, and a better Magic Eraser are locked
to the Pro** — **"it was obvious when Apple did it, and it's even more obvious when Google does it... same computer."**
The counterpoint: **"you can't fix battery in post"** — good hardware still matters, and he's had genuinely good
battery (with the usual Pixel-aging question). Verdict: **this is finally the generation of Pixel right alongside
the other flagships**, supported like one (7 years), at **$700 — worth it**; the +$300 Pro is a harder sell.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-10-11] **The Pixel is the "fix it in post" smartphone.** "'"We'll fix it in post" means the shot isn't
  perfect but we'll fix it in post-production. A good filmmaker tries to get it reliably good in-camera instead of
  relying on software — and the "we'll fix it in post" version of a smartphone is definitely the Google Pixel. So
  many of its best features (Face Unblur, Magic Editor, Audio Eraser) are literally fixing it in post — and with a
  little AI, they actually work. That's where the Pixel shines.'"
- [2023-10-11] **A rare W for Pixel hardware.** "'The screens tie the phone together up at the level of any flagship
  — flat Pro display, satin backs, super-bright (2,000/2,400 nits), even bezels. This is a rare W for Pixel
  hardware; you can put it right alongside any flagship. (Though the fingerprint reader is still slow optical and
  dust still gathers under the camera bar.)'"
- [2023-10-11] **Magic Editor lowered the barrier to lie — what is a photo?** "'Magic Editor goes beyond fixing it in
  post — select anything and remove/move/resize it, switch the scene to golden hour, replace the sky. Zoomed out
  these are decently convincing to regular people, so it just lowered the barrier to entry to just lie, I guess.
  What is a photo?'"
- [2023-10-11] **The software-locked Pro/non-Pro separation is obvious.** "'The good thing Google did was put the
  same G3, ISP, primary and selfie cameras in both. So the artificial software-locking is glaring: Pro Controls, 50
  MP capture, Zoom Enhance, a better Magic Eraser — all locked to the Pro, even though it's the same computer
  (someone already ported 50 MP to a Pixel 6). It was obvious when Apple did it; it's even more obvious when Google
  does it.'"
- [2023-10-11] **You can't fix battery in post — and this is finally a flagship-tier Pixel.** "'You can't AI your way
  out of a 2,400-nit screen — you need the hardware, a big battery, an efficient chip. I've had genuinely good
  battery (6–8 hrs screen-on); the only question, given Pixel history, is how long it stays good. But between fit,
  finish, hardware, and software, this is finally the generation of Pixel right alongside the other flagships, and
  supported like one — 7 years — for $700. Worth it. The +$300 Pro is a harder sell.'"
