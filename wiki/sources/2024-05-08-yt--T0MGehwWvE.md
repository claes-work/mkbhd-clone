# M4 iPad Pro Impressions: Well This is Awkward (@mkbhd, 2024-05-08)

## Summary
Event-day impressions of Apple's new iPads, anchored to his long-running refrain: **"it kind of doesn't matter how
powerful they make the iPad — it's still an iPad, still iPadOS."** The new **iPad Air** is a predictable spec bump (M2,
128 GB base, landscape webcam "where it belongs," now an 11" and a 13"). The **iPad Pro** is where Apple shows off:
the **thinnest Apple device ever made** (5.1 mm on the 13" — thinner than an iPod Nano; "I don't know who asked for a
thinner iPad"), the **first M4 chip** (50% faster CPU / up to 4× GPU vs M2, or same perf at half the power), and a
**tandem OLED display** ("maybe the most interesting part of the whole event" — two stacked OLEDs the M4 driver
addresses at once for 1,000-nit full-screen / 1,600-nit HDR brightness). He flags the new **Nano-texture matte** option
(anti-reflective but at the cost of black level/contrast, only on 1–2 TB models, with **durability concerns** — you'll
likely be stuck using only the in-box microfiber). The new **Apple Pencil Pro** (haptics, Find My, squeeze sensor,
rotation) is only compatible with the newest iPads. But the verdict is the same **"hardware outpaces the software"**
tension: **"there's almost nothing these iPads can do that my three-year-old M1 iPad Pro can't"** — a tiny sliver of
apps take advantage, some ("new Final Cut") ship later. The **awkwardness is timing**: **WWDC and iPadOS 18 are right
around the corner**, and could be the biggest iPad software update yet — so these land in an odd meantime. (Also
disclosed: as **Ridge's chief creative partner**, his first MKBHD gear — carbon-fiber and matte-black wallets.)

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-05-08] **However powerful, it's still an iPad.** "'We've been saying this for years: it doesn't matter how
  powerful they make the iPad — it's still an iPad, still iPadOS. The last thing we needed was another spec-bumped
  iPad… well, here they are.'"
- [2024-05-08] **The thinnest Apple device ever — nobody asked for it.** "'The 13" is 5.1 mm — the thinnest Apple
  device ever, thinner than an iPod Nano. It feels like an impossible sheet of metal. I don't know who was asking for a
  thinner iPad, but that's the form factor the M4 specifically enables. Fun fact: this makes the iPad Air heavier than
  the iPad Pro — keeping the mess of an iPad lineup going.'"
- [2024-05-08] **Tandem OLED is the most interesting thing here.** "'Two OLED panels stacked, with the M4 display
  driver addressing both layers at once for really high local brightness — 1,000 nits full-screen, 1,600 nits HDR on
  an iPad-sized OLED. That's maybe the most interesting part of the whole event.'"
- [2024-05-08] **Be careful with Nano-texture matte.** "'The new Nano-texture matte deals with reflections better, but
  just like the desktop version that's at the expense of sharpness and contrast — the blacks don't look as black even
  at zero degrees. And I worry about durability: you'll probably only be able to rely on the microfiber that comes in
  the box. It's a $100 add-on only on the 1–2 TB models — they know you shouldn't just grab it in the store.'"
- [2024-05-08] **Hardware outpaces software — same as the M1.** "'The hardware is genuinely amazing — bright OLED, M4,
  new neural engine. But taking advantage of all this power is nearly impossible: a tiny sliver of apps make it feel
  worth upgrading, some ship later this year. There's almost nothing these iPads can do that my three-year-old M1 iPad
  Pro can't.'"
- [2024-05-08] **The awkwardness is timing — WWDC is around the corner.** "'The awkwardness comes from timing. WWDC is
  right around the corner — a new iPadOS 18 that could be one of the biggest iPad software updates yet, Apple showing
  its hand on AI features. So we get the most powerful, thinnest iPads ever in an awkward meantime where the list of
  things they can do is the same as my M1. What a time to be alive.'"
