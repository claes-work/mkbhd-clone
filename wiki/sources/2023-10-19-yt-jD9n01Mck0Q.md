# OnePlus Open is Awesome — What's Happening with Folding Phones? (@mkbhd, 2023-10-19)

## Summary
A review of the **OnePlus Open** ("not a good name, but a very good first-gen foldable") that reopens a recurring
question: **if folding phones are this good, why aren't regular people buying them?** The Open is genuinely
excellent — **it nails all five pillars** (a co-crown "best foldable to use closed" alongside the Pixel Fold, the
**brightest smartphone display he's ever seen at 2,800 nits**, one of the least-visible creases of 2023, top specs,
great battery, a decent camera, and a "get stuff done" triple-panel multitasking hub) — and yet **"a phone can hit
all five pillars and not be a great buy for everyone."** The three things holding folds back: **(1) price** ($1,700
— "twice an iPhone 15," though a bargain vs the $1,800 Pixel/Samsung folds); **(2) durability** (he already got
**dead pixels near the hinge after three weeks of babying it**); and **(3) the power-user problem — "folding and
unfolding is a power-user feature, and power-user features don't sell a ton of phones."** He backs #3 with the
graveyard: LG's manually-adjustable cameras (gone), Sony's pro-camera phones (in danger), even the excellent Asus
Zenfones ("clearly not doing super well"). The best fold hardware he's seen is the Honor Magic V2, but it has the
same regular-person downsides. Flips are getting closer to mainstream via lower prices (the $1,000 Flip 5, the ~$600
Razr 40). "Folds are simultaneously very evolved and still have a long way to go — for now it's more fun for us
nerds."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-10-19] **It nails all five pillars — but that's not enough.** "'The OnePlus Open nails all five of my pillars
  of a great smartphone — screen, battery, build, performance, camera — and I'm not being generous. But it turns out
  a phone can hit all five pillars and not be a great buy for everyone.'"
- [2023-10-19] **Why regular people don't buy folds — three things.** "'It comes down to three things. Price — $1,700
  is a lot no matter how you slice it, twice an iPhone 15 (though right in line with the $1,800 folds, so this is
  kind of a bargain). Durability — questionable; I got dead pixels near the hinge after three weeks of babying it.
  And the power-user problem.'"
- [2023-10-19] **Folding is a power-user feature — and those don't sell phones.** "'Folding and unfolding is a
  power-user feature, and power-user features, as much as we love them, don't sell a ton of phones. We learned this
  many times: LG made the most manually-adjustable cameras and don't make phones anymore; Sony can't stop making
  pro cameras in a phone body and their sales are in danger; the Asus Zenfones are incredible for enthusiasts and
  clearly aren't doing super well either.'"
- [2023-10-19] **The best fold hardware — but the same downsides.** "'The most compelling fold hardware I've seen is
  the Honor Magic V2 — super thin, a 3D-printed titanium hinge, a 5,000 mAh battery. It helped me appreciate what
  fold hardware can be. But it still has the obvious regular-person downsides: ~$1,400 price, questionable
  durability, and the power-user thing of carrying a slightly thicker phone isn't something everyone actually
  wants.'"
- [2023-10-19] **Flips are getting closer via price.** "'The flips are finally coming closer to mainstream because
  they're lower in price — the Z Flip 5 is down to $1,000, and Motorola's Razr 40 launches around $600. That's
  getting there, but we need a lot more of that. Folds are simultaneously very evolved and still have a long way to
  go.'"
