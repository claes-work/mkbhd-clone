# How Does Sony Keep Doing This? (Xperia 1 Mark V) (@mkbhd, 2023-06-03)

## Summary
A review of the **$1,400 Sony Xperia 1 V** that turns into a strategy essay: these phones are **"endangered species
for a reason"** — loaded with incredible tech and manual controls but aimed at **"the narrowest band of people who
will actually appreciate everything they do"** (we already lost HTC and LG). His fresh framing: **Sony smartphones
today look like Porsche 20 years ago** — a maker of expensive enthusiast products with anemic sales that was
actually in danger, until it **"gave the people what they want"** and built the **Cayenne** SUV, which became its
best-seller and **funded the continued making of the 911s.** So — **not** stop making enthusiast phones, but **make
a mass-market ($600–800) phone that keeps the Sony character** so reviewers can recommend it and fund the rest. His
three fixes: **(1)** smartly cut specs (1080p not 4K, a bit smaller/more reachable — but keep the 21:9 flat screen,
front speakers, headphone jack, SD slot, IP68); **(2)** — the big one — **a much better *automatic* camera** (Sony
makes the world's best sensors and has world-class manual modes, yet its full-auto mode overexposes and finished
near dead-last in his blind test — "most people just want a quick auto snap to be the best possible"); **(3)** treat
it like a flagship — **ship it 1–2 weeks after announcing** (the months-long gap kills the hype). "Until then, the
most underrated phones on the planet."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-06-03] **Endangered species for a reason.** "'There's incredible tech here, but I've had to realize these
  phones are an endangered species for a reason — the narrowest band of people who will appreciate everything they
  do. Nothing wrong with that existing, but it worries me: we lost HTC, we lost LG, just in the past couple years.'"
- [2023-06-03] **The Porsche-Cayenne analogy — give the people what they want.** "'Sony today looks almost exactly
  like Porsche 20 years ago — toiling on expensive enthusiast sports cars with anemic sales, the business in danger.
  Their strategy was to give the people what they want: they built the Cayenne SUV, it became their best-seller by a
  mile, and it propped up the business so they could keep making the 911s and GT3s. Sony needs its Cayenne — a
  mass-market phone that keeps the Sony character so people like me can recommend it and fund phones like this.'"
- [2023-06-03] **Fix #1 — smartly cut specs, keep the character.** "'For the mass-market phone: 1080p not 4K
  (they've done 1080p before, the Xperia 5); a bit smaller and more reachable. But keep what makes it a Sony — the
  tall 21:9 flat panel, no hole punch, big front speakers, the headphone jack, the SD slot, IP68, the combo power
  button/fingerprint reader and dedicated shutter.'"
- [2023-06-03] **Fix #2 — a much better AUTO camera (the big one).** "'These are some of the best cameras in any
  phone *because* they're so manually controllable — but full-auto is a different world. Sony literally makes all
  these sensors, so they should have the best vertical integration on earth, yet they get blown out by a Pixel or
  Samsung in basic point-and-shoot (overexposes, worse HDR, near dead-last in my blind test). Most people just want a
  quick auto snap to be the best possible — fixing that would make a massive difference.'"
- [2023-06-03] **Fix #3 — ship 1–2 weeks after announcing.** "'Treat it like a flagship: put it out a week, maybe
  two, after you announce it. This was announced May 11 and estimated for July 28 — almost three months, better than
  the old six, but still enough time for people to forget and spend their $1,400 on something else.'"
