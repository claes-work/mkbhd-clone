# Apple vs Right to Repair: Part 2! (@mkbhd, 2021-11-17)

## Summary
Commentary on Apple announcing its Self-Service Repair program — "a pretty major flip on their stance on right to
repair, and yes there's still a bit of a catch." His two reactions: **(1) "I ain't never seen you act like this
before"** — Apple, notoriously anti-repair, adding ports back to the MacBook Pro AND now flipping on repair; and
**(2) "why?"** His verdict: **a great first step** — real choice, less e-waste, industry ripples — but **still a
first step** because the supported repair list is short (battery, screen, camera) and deeper integrated repairs
(soldered memory, ports, mics) remain off-limits without official parts/docs. The "why": **impending legislation
pressure** (FTC, EU, a US presidential order) plus **it's fantastic PR.** Because it's Apple — one of the biggest,
most influential consumer companies — any step is a big deal for the whole industry.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-11-17] **"A new look Apple" — but why?** "'Apple announced Self-Service Repair — individuals like you and
  me can now get genuine Apple parts, tools, and manuals (starting with the iPhone 12/13, then M1 Macs), do the
  repair, and send old parts back for a recycling credit. **My first reaction: "I ain't never seen you act like
  this before" — first ports back on the MacBook Pro, now flipping on right to repair. My second reaction: why?**'"
- [2021-11-17] **Great first step — but still a first step.** "'On the surface this is huge — the e-waste removed,
  the repairs unlocked, the real choice offered, the industry ripples. **But it's still a first step:** (1) the
  list is short (battery, screen, cameras) because modern phones/laptops are more tightly integrated than ever —
  I could do a battery or screen, but soldered memory means replacing the whole board; (2) as independent shops
  have aired, **anything outside those three (ports, buttons, speakers, mics) still has no official parts or
  documentation.** The new MacBook Pro battery does look noticeably more repair-friendly (pull tabs, fewer
  screws) — so this seems at least a little premeditated.'"
- [2021-11-17] **The "why" — legislation pressure + fantastic PR.** "'Why now, when they've been notoriously
  against this forever? **(1) Impending legislation — FTC regulation, EU law, even a US presidential order — has
  put pressure on them, and they're getting out ahead of it. (2) This is fantastic PR — a great headline,
  especially for Apple.**' We still don't know part prices or who qualifies.'"
- [2021-11-17] **Because it's Apple, it moves the whole industry.** "'A lot is still unchanged and unanswered — but
  it's a great first step, and **since it's a great first step for Apple, one of the biggest companies out there,
  it's a great first step for the industry as a whole.** It feels like a new-look Apple — whether it's just
  getting ahead of legislation or a little bit of actually listening. Either way, it got us here — a good day for
  the beginning of the movement.'"
