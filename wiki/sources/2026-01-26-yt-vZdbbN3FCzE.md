# The Downfall of OnePlus will be Studied ★★

## Summary

A video-essay on **the enthusiast-brand arc** — "why enthusiast brands will always betray
you" (crediting an ~8-year-old TechAltar video) — using OnePlus's full journey as the case
study. The thesis: winning over enthusiasts is "a hell of a start" but unsustainable,
because the enthusiast is **the worst possible prime customer** (smallest, most fickle,
least loyal, highest standards); to survive you must build a mass audience **faster than you
lose the enthusiast** — and the very things that won the enthusiasts are the opposite of
what the masses reward. Most brands die on that leap (Nextbit, Essential, ASUS ROG/Zenfone);
OnePlus barely survived, becoming "the blueprint for survival." A landmark market-analysis
essay. ★★.

## Key claims

- **[2026-01-26]** Prompted by a clickbait "OnePlus is being dismantled" article ("reads like
  it was written by ChatGPT — I'm not even gonna link it"); the durable frame is **TechAltar's
  "why enthusiast brands will always betray you"** (8 years old, "crazy how evergreen"). Many
  brands **die on the leap from enthusiast success to mass appeal** (Nextbit, Essential, ASUS).
  (paraphrase)
- **[2026-01-26]** The origin: the **OnePlus One** (2014) — flagship Snapdragon 800 specs at
  **$300**, clean CyanogenMod software, an **invite-only** buying system — was "a breath of fresh
  air," the **"flagship killer,"** and sold 1M+. "Right recipe, right time." (paraphrase)
- **[2026-01-26]** **"The second generation of anything tells you the most about it"** — it's
  where you see what the company learned and which direction it chases (the OnePlus 2 kept the
  formula but nervously; the OnePlus X was "a little wobble" off the flagship-killer identity).
  (quote)
- **[2026-01-26]** The core problem: **the enthusiast is the worst prime customer** — "the
  smallest group, the most fickle, highest standards, least loyal" (they leave for slightly
  better specs). So you must **leverage that success to reach the mass, brand-loyal audience** —
  **"but the thing that won over the enthusiasts is the exact opposite of what the masses want."**
  Cost-cutting, spec-maxing, skipping the IP rating to save money, no carrier deals — enthusiasts
  eat it up; the masses want **the safe option with the IP rating on the carrier-store shelf.**
  (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-01-26]** The graveyard: **ASUS ROG Phone** (slowly softened for the mainstream → dead)
  and the **Zenfone** ("mega-enthusiast hit" 9/10, then a panicked pivot to a huge Zenfone 11
  Ultra because **"the masses don't buy small phones — they want big screens"** → failed). The
  mission: **"build the new audience faster than you lose the old one."** (paraphrase)
- **[2026-01-26]** OnePlus's peak-and-drift: **"peak OnePlus" for enthusiasts was the 6/7 era**
  (OxygenOS came into its own — "the best bang for the buck in the industry"); his **favorite of
  all time is the OnePlus 7T Pro** ("a phone I genuinely believe I could still use today"). Then
  the slow peel to the mainstream: glass backs, official IP ratings, **price creep to $900**, the
  **Nord budget line**, US carrier deals, the **Hasselblad** partnership, **co-founder Carl Pei
  left**, and **OxygenOS faded into Oppo's ColorOS**. By the OnePlus 10 they'd "almost completely
  lost their identity." (paraphrase)
- **[2026-01-26]** Where it lands: the **OnePlus 15** (he's dailied it a couple months) is a
  "solid phone, generic-looking, cameras aren't great, but fine" — now **recommendable to everyday
  people.** "So… success? Technically. Barely." OnePlus is **"the most successful *and* least
  successful jump from enthusiast to mainstream we've ever seen"** — but **still alive**, so
  **"the blueprint for survival."** (quote)
- **[2026-01-26]** Coda: **Carl Pei "is speedrunning the same process with Nothing"**; and
  **"enthusiasts do not forget."** Rumors that OnePlus's next foldable and its compact-flagship
  sequel got canceled; a OnePlus 16 is likely but the future's unclear. (paraphrase)
