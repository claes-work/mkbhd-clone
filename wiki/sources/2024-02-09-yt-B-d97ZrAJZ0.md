# OnePlus 12 Review: Better Than You Think! (@mkbhd, 2024-02-09)

## Summary
A review of the **$799 OnePlus 12** (marked down to ~$699 with a trade-in) that "flew under the radar" during the
Vision Pro chaos — and is a legit **flagship killer** against the $1,000–1,300 Ultras. It looks nearly the same as
last year, which frames a durable point: **"that's what smartphones are nowadays — finding a bunch of clever little
things and stacking a bunch of little improvements"** (vs the "gigantic total shift" from the OnePlus 1 to the 2). A
much brighter display, the Snapdragon 8 Gen 3, and a **battery champ** (a huge 5,400 mAh, 80 W wired / 50 W
wireless, two light days). The cameras are the honest caveat: **"acceptable for a flagship, but not quite beating
the best"** (they fall apart in low light — cross-referenced to his 20-phone blind test, where the near-identical
OnePlus Open scored top-3 in daylight but fifth-from-bottom in low light). The "little things" he loves:
**per-app frame-rate forcing**, an **IR blaster**, **Aqua Touch** (a genuinely-works wet-screen mode), and
**2160 Hz PWM dimming** (for flicker-sensitive people). The real downsides: **no AI features at all despite the 8
Gen 3** (a notable observation — "this phone kind of just skipped the AI part") and only **4/5 years of updates** vs
the 7 from Pixel/Samsung/Apple. Verdict: **"is this a flagship killer? Depends on your definition"** — for
performance/screen/battery/charging, it's "better than you probably think."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-02-09] **Smartphones now stack small improvements.** "'This is a case of a bunch of little changes stacking
  up to equal a large difference from last year. That's what smartphones are nowadays — I remember the gigantic total
  shift from the OnePlus 1 to the OnePlus 2. Now it's finding clever little things and stacking little improvements
  to make the experience a little better.'"
- [2024-02-09] **A battery champ.** "'This prioritizes long battery life over squeezing out every last FPS — a huge
  5,400 mAh battery (bigger than a lot of Ultras), 80 W wired, 50 W wireless. Leave it in default settings and you
  get two full light days; near zero, you charge to half in 12 minutes. Contender for battery champ of the year.'"
- [2024-02-09] **The camera is acceptable, not the best.** "'Cameras have been a relative weak point for OnePlus's
  high-end phones — I'd say now it's on the "acceptable for a flagship" level, but not quite beating those. It's good
  most of the time, but falls apart a little too quickly in adverse conditions, especially low light — in our blind
  test the near-identical Open was top-3 in daylight but fifth-from-bottom in low light.'"
- [2024-02-09] **No AI features despite the 8 Gen 3.** "'A notable downside: this phone skipped the AI part. Qualcomm
  put huge new AI capabilities in the 8 Gen 3, and other flagships use them — but there's no Magic Editor, no live
  call transcription/translation, no browser summaries here. The one AI thing is a color-overlay "AI Palettes." Those
  were genuinely useful features.'"
- [2024-02-09] **Only 4/5 years of updates — and "is it a flagship killer?"** "'The biggest legitimate downside vs
  the flagships: it only promises 4 years of OS / 5 years of security updates, where the Pixels, Samsungs, and even
  iPhones give six or seven. Is this a flagship killer? Depends on your definition. If camera, long-term support, or
  AI features are high on your list, go with another flagship. For everything else — performance, screen, incredible
  battery, fast charging — it's better than you probably think.'"
