# How Do YouTubers Make Money? (And Other Questions We Have to Answer) (@mkbhd, 2021-07-16)

## Summary
A landmark creator-business explainer — the top-10 FAQ about being a YouTuber, framed as an Uber-ride conversation.
The durable content: the **three revenue buckets** (YouTube/AdSense ads, self-sold in-video ads, selling
products/direct-audience), how the **embargo/NDA** system works, the **"we're treated like tech journalists"**
relationship (companies send products early; he often **buys the thing after the review**), how he decides what to
make (**the product is the subject — it's on the tech industry to make interesting stuff**), and the promotable
**basketball-in-the-park** analogy for turning a creative hobby into a job.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-07-16] **THE THREE REVENUE BUCKETS.** "'For every creator, revenue divides into three buckets: **(1)
  YouTube's built-in ads (a company pays YouTube, YouTube splits it with the uploader — the AdSense program;
  tech companies pay more to be alongside tech channels), (2) creators selling their own in-video ads (better-
  integrated, more tailored, better analytics — so they cost more), and (3) selling things — a direct
  relationship with the audience** (paid content like Patreon/OnlyFans "which I don't do," or products: ICONS,
  LTTstore, Beast Burger). We make free content, supported by ads or the audience directly.'"
- [2021-07-16] **The embargo/NDA + "treated like tech journalists."** "'In 2021, tech video creators are **treated
  essentially like tech journalists** — companies send products early to test. We sign an **NDA (non-disclosure
  agreement)** to keep it secret until a date/time it expires — the **embargo** — which is why all the reviews
  drop at once. There's risk for the company (a bad product gets a wave of negatives), but it's good for a good
  product.'"
- [2021-07-16] **Do you keep the stuff? — often buy it after.** "'Sometimes — depends on the company/product (cars
  go back to the next reviewer; keeping an older gen to compare is useful). **We don't just give them away — and
  we often end up buying the thing after the review process, so we can compare it later when the next gen comes
  out.**'"
- [2021-07-16] **How do you decide what to make? — "the product is the subject."** "'Like asking a basketball
  player how he picks a shot — you have your go-to's but always probe for the best option. A tech channel's
  advantage: **the subject IS the product, so it's not on me to be interesting — it's on the tech industry to
  keep making the most interesting new stuff; I point a camera at it.** The hard part isn't the idea — it's
  making the video (a 12-min video can take days or weeks of writing, research, testing, shooting, editing).'"
- [2021-07-16] **Self-taught + the "YouTuber is the #1 desired job" note.** "'Most people I know didn't go to
  school for this — **it's mostly self-taught** (the closest thing is our Skillshare course). **YouTuber is now
  reportedly the #1 job young people want — crazy, since in 2009 literally zero people made a living making
  internet videos.**'"
- [2021-07-16] **THE BASKETBALL-IN-THE-PARK analogy — the job vs. the hobby.** "'Turning YouTube into a job is
  like basketball: **it's never been easier to pick it up (all you need is a camera/phone) — but doing it as a
  job takes luck, timing, hard work, and skill; it's almost two different categories of the same activity.** My
  advice: **imagine it like basketball in the park — if you'd have a great time doing it every day and never
  making a dime, do it — but don't set the expectation of turning it into a job.**'" A promotable creator-advice
  framing.
- [2021-07-16] **Will you do this forever? — creator burnout is real.** "'"If you love what you do you never work
  a day" — partially true; it's still work. **I'll do it as long as it's fun** — but **creator burnout is very
  real**: an independent creator with writer's block/burnout feels "if I step off the path, how do I get back
  on?" **If there's ever a day I'm not having fun making tech videos, something went very wrong.**'"

## Notable verbatim quotes
