# I Tried Real Augmented Reality Glasses! (@mkbhd, 2024-11-01)

## Summary
A hands-on with **both** of 2024's rare true-AR glasses — **Meta's Orion** and **Snap's AR Spectacles** — "the best
look into the future of tech I've ever seen." He reprises his **AR-Goldilocks-zone** framing (VR headsets shrink down /
smart glasses add tech, both racing toward real AR glasses in the middle), then contrasts the two very different
approaches — **neither of which will ship, and "that's probably a good thing."** **Meta Orion**: a **3-part system**
(glasses + a wireless compute puck within ~15 ft + an **EMG wristband** reading the electrical impulses in your arm),
delivering the most convincing post-smartphone AR demo he's seen (floating Instagram windows, AI ingredient-labeling
dots that track objects, shared-space 3D Pong). The standout is the **EMG wristband — "maybe the coolest input device
I've tried in a long time"** (~80% accuracy, works with your hand in your pocket, could someday read midair
handwriting). But it's a **technical marvel that's thermally constrained**: ~100 g magnesium frames, 70° FOV, 2–3 hr
battery, an assumed **~$25,000** price from the silicon-carbide waveguides — Meta's shipping it "as PR / to keep
iterating" toward a sellable v2/v3, and **he agrees with that strategy**. **Snap Spectacles**: everything's **in the
glasses** (no puck) so they're huge (228 g), but **higher resolution** (near Vision-Pro sharp) with a **much smaller
46° FOV** (very noticeable — things clip when you move), a slick **electrochromic tint** (same tech as the new Rivian
sunroof), and — crucially — **an actual developer kit** you can get today ($100/mo, 1-yr commit) with real apps
(Beatboxer, shared-space experiences that map the room with no QR code). His take: he **likes Snap's "ship it to
developers early and see what they make" strategy**, and shares his two **AR dream use cases** — learning any instrument
via a Guitar-Hero-style overlay, and an airplane-window app that outlines territories/landmarks you fly over. Neither is
ready (Orion's puck + 2 hr; Snap's 45-min battery + bulk), but "you can't help but picture a future where we look at our
phones less."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-11-01] **The best look into the future I've seen — the AR Goldilocks zone.** "'I'm one of very few people who's
  tried both of 2024's true-AR glasses, and this might be the best look into the future of tech I've ever seen. VR
  headsets are shrinking down and smart glasses are adding tech — both racing toward the Goldilocks zone in the middle:
  real AR glasses. These pull back the curtain on what that looks like with today's tech.'"
- [2024-11-01] **Meta Orion — a 3-part system with a standout EMG wristband.** "'Orion is glasses + a wireless compute
  puck (within ~15 ft) + an EMG wristband that reads the electrical impulses running to your fingers — which may be the
  coolest input device I've tried in a long time (~80% accurate, works with your hand in your pocket, could someday
  read midair handwriting). The AR is convincing — floating windows, AI dots labeling ingredients and tracking them,
  shared-space 3D Pong.'"
- [2024-11-01] **Orion is a thermally-constrained ~$25k marvel not shipping — as PR.** "'It's a technical marvel but
  thermally constrained: ~100 g magnesium frames (a heat sink), 70° FOV, 2–3 hr battery, and an assumed ~$25,000 price
  from the silicon-carbide waveguides. Meta isn't shipping it, ever — think of it as PR, iterating behind the scenes
  toward a sellable v2/v3 without the pressure of marketing the first one. I actually agree with that strategy.'"
- [2024-11-01] **Snap Spectacles — no puck, higher res, smaller FOV, a real dev kit.** "'Snap's put everything in the
  glasses (no puck), so they're huge (228 g), but higher resolution (near Vision-Pro sharp) with a much smaller 46° FOV
  you really notice — things clip when you move your head. They have a slick electrochromic tint (same tech as the new
  Rivian sunroof). And crucially they're an actual developer kit you can get today ($100/mo) with real apps and
  no-QR-code shared spaces. I like Snap's "ship it to developers early, see what they make" strategy.'"
- [2024-11-01] **My AR dream use cases — and the future is close but not ready.** "'My two dream use cases: learn any
  instrument via a Guitar-Hero-style overlay (piano notes coming down), and an airplane-window app that outlines the
  territories and pops up landmarks you're flying over. Neither pair is ready — Orion needs a pocket computer and lasts
  2 hours; Snap's is 45 minutes and looks like that. But you can't help picturing a future where we look at our phones
  less. Someday.'"
