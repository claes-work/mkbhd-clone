# Here's the Thing about the M3 Macbook Air... (@mkbhd, 2024-03-20)

## Summary
A review whose hook is: **"the most interesting things about this new laptop are the reasons not to buy it."** The
M3 Air is a boring update — a barely-different anodization and the M3 chip (a ~10–20% bump, more on graphics, plus
ray tracing; fixed the M2's slow base-256GB SSD; dual-external-display support with the lid closed). The MacBook Air
is normally "one of the easiest laptops in the world to recommend," but this one isn't, for two reasons. **One:** with
the M3, Apple **dropped the M2 to $999 and discontinued the M1** (still ~$649–699 at Walmart/Best Buy) — so for the
"massive group of people doing normal, baseline computing" (browsing, tabs, a bit of photo/video), **"don't buy the
M3 — get the cheaper M2, or even the M1."** **Two:** the **base-spec fallacy** — **"'starting at' is the biggest lie
in tech"** (credit Linus; the car industry does it too), and **Apple's above-base markups are brutal** ($200 for 16
GB RAM, $200 for 512 GB — "the phone I'm using has more than 8 GB / 256 GB") — a few clicks makes it a **$1,500 Air**.
His nuanced take: **"is 8 GB / 256 GB usable in 2024? Sometimes yes"** — for light users you rarely hit memory swap,
and even then it's fine (it's heavy encoding/export/gaming that breaks it). The durable insight: **"what Apple
Silicon did to the lineup is bring down the minimum level of machine you can get away with for your workflow"** — he
now edits on a MacBook Air / Mac Studio, not just a Mac Pro. So for heavy users the M3 Air is "amazing, easy to
recommend"; for light users, **get the cheaper one.**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-03-20] **The interesting things are the reasons not to buy it.** "'It's crazy that the most interesting
  things about this new laptop are the reasons not to buy it. The M3 Air is a boring update — a slightly better
  anodization (barely makes a difference) and the M3 chip.'"
- [2024-03-20] **Don't buy this one — get the M2 or M1.** "'The Air is normally one of the easiest laptops to
  recommend, but with the M3 they dropped the M2 to $999 and discontinued the M1 (still ~$649–699 at Walmart/Best
  Buy). For the massive group of people doing normal baseline computing — browsing, tabs, some photo/video — don't
  buy the M3; get the cheaper M2, or even the M1.'"
- [2024-03-20] **"Starting at" is the biggest lie in tech.** "'The base-spec thing has become a fallacy — Linus did a
  video on how "starting at" is the biggest lie in tech, and he's right; the car industry does it too (nobody gets
  the bare base model). It just tells you how much you're about to spend when you option it up.'"
- [2024-03-20] **Apple's above-base markups are brutal.** "'The base is 8 GB / 256 GB (the phone I'm using has more
  than that). The first RAM upgrade to 16 GB is $200; 512 GB storage is another $200 — brutal, ridiculous pricing vs
  what it costs Apple at their scale. Do just that and it's a $1,500 Air. Since Apple Silicon can never be upgraded
  after purchase, you're incentivized to splurge — which makes the base price feel like even more of a lie.'"
- [2024-03-20] **Apple Silicon lowered the minimum machine for your workflow.** "'Is 8 GB / 256 GB usable in 2024?
  Sometimes yes — for light stuff you rarely hit memory swap, and even then it's fine; it's heavy encoding/export/
  gaming that's another story. The big insight: what Apple Silicon did is bring down the minimum level of machine you
  can get away with for your workflow. I used to need a high-end desktop to edit; now I edit on a MacBook Air / Mac
  Studio. So for heavy users the M3 Air is amazing — but if you're doing lightweight normal stuff, get the cheaper
  one.'"
