# The Tesla Robotaxi is Confusing... (@mkbhd, 2024-10-14)

## Summary
On-the-ground reaction to Tesla's Robotaxi ("We, Robot") event — **"maybe the weirdest tech event I've ever been to."**
He rode the **Cybercab**, explored the Robovan, and talked to an Optimus robot, and left confused. Three pillars: (1)
The **Cybercab** — a mini-Cybertruck-looking two-seater with scissor doors, no steering wheel or pedals, a 21" screen,
and **wireless charging only (no charge port at all — can't even supercharge or road-trip; it's purely a taxi)**;
grilling Tesla engineers, he learns there's no front trunk, no stated battery/range, and an efficiency target of **~5.5
mi/kWh** (A+, enabling a small ~40 kWh pack). But the pitch is thin: **"just look at this thing, there's just concepts
of a plan."** (2) His **biggest red flag is the timeline — "notorious Elon stuff"**: promised "under $30,000 before
2027," which he flatly doubts, citing precedent — the **$35k Model 3** that shipped briefly then died, and the
**Cybertruck that went from a promised $39k to $95k** ("I'll shave my head on camera if they ship it by 2027 — I'm not
confident they *won't*"). He raises the **philosophical question**: what's the real difference vs. a Model 3 Tesla has
long promised will also self-drive? (mostly cheaper/smaller/fleet-friendly), and captures Elon dodging the Hardware-3
"can old cars do FSD?" question. (3) The **Robovan** he dismisses as **CES-style vaporware** ("50 of these no-wheel
concept vans every year; none ship"). And the **Optimus robots** — dancing, serving drinks, chatting — were **clearly
teleoperated** ("it's just a person talking through a speaker... it stutters and makes human mistakes; different robots
had different voices"; Tesla only confirmed "the LLMs run on-device"), which **overshadowed the event** ("now we don't
know how much was real"). His conclusion is generous-but-skeptical: the *vision* is honorable and optimistic, but as a
public company they overpromise on price and timeline — **"if I had to bet on one company to actually get there, it'd be
Tesla, for the massive real-world data advantage. I'll believe it when I see it."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-10-14] **The weirdest tech event, with "concepts of a plan."** "'The Robotaxi event might be the weirdest tech
  event I've ever been to. I rode the Cybercab, explored the Robovan, talked to Optimus — and left confused. There were
  shockingly few actual details: just look at this thing, there's just concepts of a plan.'"
- [2024-10-14] **The Cybercab: no wheel/pedals, wireless charging only.** "'It's a two-seater with scissor doors, no
  steering wheel or pedals, a 21" screen — and it charges only by wireless pad, with no charge port at all, so it can't
  supercharge or road-trip; it's purely a taxi. No front trunk, no stated battery or range, just an efficiency target
  of ~5.5 mi/kWh (A+), which enables a small ~40 kWh pack. The drivetrain logic checks out — but you're telling me this
  while doing scissor doors and wireless charging.'"
- [2024-10-14] **The timeline is the biggest red flag — notorious Elon stuff.** "'"Under $30,000 before 2027" — no
  they're not; there's just no way. This is notorious Elon timeline stuff. They promised a $35k Model 3 (shipped
  briefly, then killed it), and the Cybertruck went from a promised $39k to $95k today. I'll shave my head on camera if
  they ship the Cybercab by 2027 — and I'm not fully confident they won't.'"
- [2024-10-14] **What's the difference vs. a Model 3?** "'There's a philosophical question: what's the difference
  between this and a Model 3, which Tesla has promised for years will also do full self-driving? Mostly the Cybercab is
  cheaper, smaller, and easier to run in a fleet. If my car can self-drive, I think I'd want the one that can also
  supercharge and carry things — with a steering wheel I can use sometimes.'"
- [2024-10-14] **The Robovan is vaporware; the Optimus robots were teleoperated.** "'The Robovan is basically CES
  vaporware — every year there's a no-steering-wheel concept van; none ship. And the Optimus robots chatting with
  people were clearly teleoperated — it's just a person talking through a speaker; it stutters and makes human
  mistakes, and different robots had different voices. Tesla only confirmed "the LLMs run on-device." That became the
  talking point and overshadowed the event — now we don't know how much was real. If I had to bet on one company to
  actually get there, it'd be Tesla, for the massive real-world data advantage. I'll believe it when I see it.'"
