# Galaxy S22 Review: The iPhone of Android! (@mkbhd, 2022-02-18)

## Summary
The full review of the S22/S22+ — "**the iPhone of Android**." The durable framing device: **the NBA analogy** —
the early-2000s Spurs and the current Suns are "drama-free, a bit boring, and quietly really, really good" — no
one standout superstar, no weaknesses, just great fundamentals. **The S22 is boring because it's so good across
the board that there are no glaring flaws, which makes it incredibly easy to recommend** (kin to the Camry
analogy). The bigger thesis: **One UI has become the default Android experience** — as OxygenOS declined and the
Pixels got buggy, Samsung's software evolved into one of the most stable, feature-packed Androids, and Samsung is
now **doing some Google features better than Google's own phones** (a Samsung-skinned Google Messages, Google Duo
"watch together" S22-first, and **4 years of updates vs Google's 3**). Notes one real regression (the vibration
motor) and a "When Tech Companies Lie" callback on the LTPO spec confusion.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-02-18] **The NBA analogy — boring because there are no weaknesses.** "'What do the early-2000s Spurs, the
  current Suns, and the S22 series have in common? **They're all drama-free, a bit boring, and quietly really,
  really good** — no single standout MVP, but the whole team is great with no weaknesses. **This phone is boring —
  but that's what happens when a phone gets so good across the board that there aren't any weaknesses**, because
  they took a good thing and made it better instead of taking big risks.'"
- [2022-02-18] **The small update — glass back, boxier, one real downgrade.** "'A small update in every department:
  a bit more boxy/squared, a **real glass back** (up from plastic), and the smaller S22 (6.1") "dare I say has a
  compact feel." The concern going in was battery (both phones are smaller, ~300 mAh less, and the new Snapdragon
  8 Gen 1 runs hot) — but it turned out **"San Antonio Spurs boring-level good"** (a solid day-plus).'"
- [2022-02-18] **The camera — "more Harrison Barnes than Kevin Durant."** "'A major camera upgrade would've been
  like the 2016 Warriors signing Durant — but **this new system is more Harrison Barnes than Kevin Durant: really
  good, not mind-blowing.** Balanced colors, not over-sharp, no HDR haloing, minimal shutter lag — "boring Tim
  Duncan bank-shot solid." And finally **8K no longer crops into the telephoto** (the high-res is now the main
  camera) — though it's still 24 fps (the wrong frame rate; give us a 30 toggle).'"
- [2022-02-18] **The real downgrade + the "lie" callback.** "'One thing I like less than last year: **the vibration
  motor switched from a z-axis to an x-axis** — just as strong in the hand, but way weaker on a desk/in a pocket
  (I missed notifications). And on the LTPO confusion: Samsung told me these aren't LTPO, but the spec sheet said
  10–120 Hz; a few days later they quietly changed the site to 48–120 Hz — **"it's almost like some other
  companies sometimes lie to people by expressing things in non-standard ways."**' (Callback to
  [[2022-01-13-yt--njHjebtIg4]].)'"
- [2022-02-18] **One UI is the default Android — and beats Google at its own features.** "'I used to rag on Samsung
  software as clunky bloatware — but **now that OxygenOS has gone downhill and the Pixels are a buggy mess, One UI
  has evolved into one of the most stable, performant, feature-packed Android experiences**, and it's solidified
  as the default Android phone (crazy, given what the Pixel 6 was supposed to be). Samsung even does Google
  features better: a **Samsung-skinned Google Messages** (only they pulled that off), **Google Duo "watch
  together" launched S22-first** (even before Google's own Pixel), and **4 years of updates vs Google's 3.** In the
  classic "what should I buy" question, **the first phones I bring up are the iPhone 13 and the Galaxy S22+.**'"
