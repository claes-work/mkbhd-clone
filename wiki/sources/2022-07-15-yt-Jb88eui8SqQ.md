# Nothing Phone (1) Review: Seeing Through the Hype! (@mkbhd, 2022-07-15)

## Summary
The full review of the Nothing Phone (1) — a rare "new phone from a new company," from **Carl Pei (ex-OnePlus)
running the familiar hype playbook.** The differentiating bet is **design** (where OnePlus once leaned on
specs-for-a-low-price): the transparent back and the **Glyph interface** (900+ LEDs). His verdict: a genuinely
distinctive-looking mid-ranger (~$399+) that behaves **"exactly like a good mid-range Android in 2022 — and I mean
that as a compliment"** ("a tweaked Pixel"). But the Glyph's functionality is **limited (no per-app light patterns
— "buy a phone for what it is now, not what it's promised to be")**, the **cameras are average** (the same IMX766 as
the ROG/Zenfone), and it requires the phone face-down (against his habits). The bigger read: Nothing's strategy —
**a solid user experience + a distinctive design (not top specs)** — is smart, and he's glad for **"more
cool-looking stuff in a world of not-as-cool-looking stuff."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-07-15] **A new company running the old playbook — design as the differentiator.** "'A new phone from a new
  company — but **Carl Pei (ex-OnePlus chief) running the hype playbook we've seen** (slow reveals to drum up
  interest). To give people a reason over the boring established phones, **where OnePlus leaned on specs-for-a-low-
  price, Nothing leans on design** — the transparent Gorilla Glass back with indicators, exposed screws, text, and
  the 900+ LEDs. I've never seen a phone quite like it. (Squint and it's an iPhone shape — their channel-sponsor
  skin proves it accomplishes ~90% of the look.)'"
- [2022-07-15] **The Glyph interface — cool, but limited granularity.** "'The Glyph LEDs serve a purpose: they sync
  to your ringtone, show charge status, and light up when you talk to Assistant. **But I wish for more granularity
  — back in the day RGB notification lights told you which app; here it's all white, so I can't tell what
  notification I have. I assumed I could set per-app dot patterns — that seems like the whole point.** They might
  add it via software, but **buy a phone for what it is now, not what it's promised to be.** (The lights sip
  battery — ~2–3%/hr; a slight green tint on one, and they double as a decent fill light.)'"
- [2022-07-15] **A tweaked Pixel — a good mid-ranger.** "'At £399+ it behaves **exactly like a good mid-range
  Android in 2022 — and I mean that as a compliment** (alongside the Pixel 6a, Galaxy A53). A Snapdragon 778G+, a
  great flat 6.55" 1080p OLED (up to 1,200 nits, even bezels, a not-terrible under-display reader, adaptive
  120/60 Hz), near-stock Nothing OS with tasteful dot-matrix touches and no bloatware. **It basically feels like a
  tweaked Pixel — a compliment.**'"
- [2022-07-15] **Average cameras — set expectations.** "'The cameras are where mid-tier separates from flagship —
  and these are **particularly average** (a pair of 50 MP, the primary being the **same IMX766 I just saw in the
  ROG 6**): decent in good light, but noise and slow shutter as soon as light drops (forget moving subjects
  indoors). **Set your expectations right — this is not a flagship-killer camera** (also modest speakers, only
  IP53). Sustainability: 100% recycled aluminum, 50%+ recycled plastics.'"
- [2022-07-15] **The strategy is smart — more cool stuff, please.** "'Nothing set out to **hit a price point, then
  separate from the pack with a good UX + a unique aesthetic — that's smart.** They're building an ecosystem (buds
  → phone, both "solid UX + distinctive design, not top specs") — expect the same from whatever's next. Even if
  it's not for a pixel-peeper like me, **I like that we're getting cool-looking stuff — more cool stuff, please.**'"
