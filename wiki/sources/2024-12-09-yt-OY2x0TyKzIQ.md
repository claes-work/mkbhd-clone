# This Video is AI Generated! SORA Review (@mkbhd, 2024-12-09)

## Summary
**"The first-ever Sora review: the good, the bad, and the ugly"** — a week of full hands-on access to OpenAI's video
generator (a follow-up to his Feb 2024 "AI video changed forever"), opening with a **real-or-AI guessing game** and
the verdict that the results are **"both horrifying and inspiring at the same time."** He tours the UI (browse others'
prompts + see exactly what they typed; remix with a subtle→strong slider; storyboard to chain prompts) and shares his
core observations of what gives AI video away: **no object permanence** (things pass in front of/behind each other
wrong, materialize and vanish — "the smartphone in this imaginary tech reviewer's hands, just gone"; walking legs swap
front/back), and **it struggles with physics in general** (movement looks slow-mo then randomly fast — "physics
requires some understanding of what you're making a video about," the video-model analog of LLM hallucination). It's
decent at **fluid dynamics and fire**, and genuinely useful for a narrow set: **abstracts/screensavers, title slides
(it's gotten better at requested text), and stop-motion/cartoon/claymation** (where physics errors read as stylistic).
The guardrails: it refuses photorealistic people, public figures, logos, under-18 subjects, and **watermarks every
clip — "but you can still crop watermarks."** The unresolved worries: the **training-data question** (it added a
specific fake plant he never prompted — "are my videos in the source material?"), energy use, and the fact this is
**"about to be in the hands of millions all at once,"** pushing us **"further into the era of not being able to believe
anything you see online."** And these clips are silent, seconds-long, 1080p — **"this is the new baseline; this is the
worst they'll ever be."**
