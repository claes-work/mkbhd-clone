# Wait... Smart Glasses are Suddenly Good? ★★

## Summary

Mid-iPhone-17-reviews, Marques flies back to California for Meta's event and comes away
"shockingly" impressed by the **Meta Ray-Ban Display** — a real, shipping, $800 product with
a monocular in-lens HUD and an EMG **neural wristband**, arriving just **10 months** after
the $10,000, overheating, puck-tethered Orion prototype he reviewed. The through-line is
**"the rate of improvement is kind of insane"** — light leak nearly gone, mid-air text input
now genuinely usable. He calls them "the best smart glasses with a screen I've tried, hands
down," flags Meta's first-party lock-in and privacy questions, notes the keynote's demo
disasters, and muses on a still-distant post-smartphone world. ★★ L3-candidate.

## Key claims

- **[2025-09-19]** The core observation: **"The rate of improvement is kind of insane."** Ten
  months ago Orion was a $10k, cumbersome, hour-of-battery, puck-tethered proof of concept;
  now the **Meta Ray-Ban Display** is "a finished product with a price tag and a release date"
  and "massively better." (quote)
- **[2025-09-19]** It's a **monocular** full-color HUD in the right lens (42 pixels/degree, up
  to 5,000 nits, visible outdoors) — "a real-life little HUD in the corner of your vision." The
  **Orion light leak is nearly completely gone** — from most angles no one can tell you're
  looking at a screen. (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-09-19]** Control is via a **surface-EMG neural band** reading electrical impulses in
  your arm from discreet finger gestures. **Mid-air text input** — an idea 10 months ago — is
  now "maybe the most reliable, quickest method of text input I've tried with these... writing
  whole sentences first try, quickly, with no errors." (quote)
- **[2025-09-19]** Fully self-contained (no puck), 69g ("a little heavy" for a non-glasses
  wearer), black or sand with a matching band; **"you cannot underestimate how much aesthetics
  matter if you're gonna be wearing this computer on your face."** The folding charging case
  (4 extra charges) is "pure genius." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-09-19]** **What a display adds** over the audio-only Ray-Bans (whose #1 use he
  believes is POV video): (1) a visible UI for silent gesture control; (2) a real camera
  **viewfinder** + review; (3) **messaging/video calls** (POV feed to the other side); (4)
  **head-tracked turn-by-turn maps** — "so clutch" for someone with a bad sense of direction;
  (5) **live subtitling / translation** of whatever's in front of you via beamforming mics.
  (paraphrase)
- **[2025-09-19]** **$800** — "the price of a whole phone," but less than he'd have guessed; he
  suspects **Meta is losing money on the hardware** to get them into hands. (paraphrase)
- **[2025-09-19]** Biggest downside: **it's Meta** — privacy questions about data collection,
  and **everything is first-party** (WhatsApp for messaging/calls, Meta's own maps "won't have
  Google Maps' real-time traffic," Instagram Reels; only Spotify is a third-party exception; no
  app store yet). (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-09-19]** As of now, **"the best smart glasses with a screen I've tried, hands down"**
  — but "you wouldn't really get that from the presentation at all": the keynote was "brutal"
  because the **live demos failed** (Wi-Fi issues). He gives Meta props for "trying to do real
  demos instead of faking everything." (quote)
- **[2025-09-19]** Muses whether this brings a **post-smartphone world** closer (still far off;
  these still need a phone) — the appeal of snapping a photo on a hike "instead of putting a
  computer between you and the scene," but also the social awkwardness of someone glancing at a
  HUD mid-conversation: "you don't know if they're actually paying attention to you." (quote)
