# Golf Tech is Hidden in Plain Sight! (@mkbhd, 2024-06-27)

## Summary
A sports-tech deep dive (a companion to his Formula 1 explainer) on **the technology hidden in plain sight on a golf
course** that makes a broadcast possible. Biography note: **Marques is a longtime golfer — his very first YouTube
uploads, before any tech videos, were golf videos.** The core problem: like F1, golf is **hard to watch** — 18
"courts" spread over hundreds of yards with a tiny invisible ball — so there are **three escalating ways to watch**,
each needing more tech. (1) **TV broadcast + the shot tracer** — crucially, **the trace is precisely measured, not an
AI prediction**: towers around the course each carry a camera, a **TrackMan radar** (measuring launch speed/angle/spin
the instant of impact), and a transmitter (solar + battery powered). (2) **The PGA Tour website portal** — on-fairway
cameras plus volunteers with laser rangefinders; the **ShotLink system projects a probability polygon of where the
ball will land before it lands** (accounting for wind, temperature, humidity, elevation) and **self-calibrates every
~10 shots**; a volunteer lasers the final position so it's live within ~3 seconds (their fingerprint also closes the
live betting window at the golfer's backswing). (3) **The Apple Vision Pro app** — the exact F1-mockup dream (broadcast
+ live 3D minimap) **made real**: drone-mapped, extremely detailed 3D holes with **actual shot shapes** rendered from
launch data. His closing reflection ties to why broadcasts matter: **"only ~1% or less of people watching are there in
person — everyone else knows the sport through the broadcast,"** which is always evolving (the mid-90s NHL glowing puck
→ today tracking a white ball across three football fields to within a couple yards).

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-06-27] **Biography: he's a golfer — golf came before tech.** "'I am a golfer — I've been golfing longer than
  I've been doing anything else. My first ever YouTube uploads, before any tech videos, were a couple of golf videos.'"
- [2024-06-27] **The shot tracer is measured, not predicted.** "'A lot of people don't know how the shot tracer is
  made — it's not a computer drawing where it sees the ball going, and it's not an AI prediction. It's precisely
  measured: every tower has a camera, a TrackMan radar (launch speed, angle, spin the instant of impact), and a
  transmitter. From that launch data they project the shot shape onto the hole.'"
- [2024-06-27] **ShotLink predicts the landing polygon before the ball lands.** "'The second a shot is hit, ShotLink
  projects a yellow probability polygon of where the ball will end up — accounting for launch, spin, speed, wind,
  temperature, humidity, elevation — and the ball rolls right into it. A volunteer lasers the final spot to confirm,
  and it's on the website within ~3 seconds. The system self-calibrates every ~10 shots; I watched it get more
  accurate over the day.'"
- [2024-06-27] **The Vision Pro app is the F1 mockup made real.** "'Remember the fake Vision Pro F1 mockup — broadcast
  up top, a live 3D minimap below — that no one had actually built? The PGA Tour's Vision Pro app is exactly that, and
  it works: drone-mapped, extremely detailed 3D holes with the actual shot shapes (a draw, a fade, a low shot)
  rendered from the launch data.'"
- [2024-06-27] **The broadcast is ~99% of how people know a sport.** "'For every sport, the broadcast is so important —
  only about 1% or less of people watching are there in person. Everyone else knows the sport through the broadcast and
  the media, and that's always evolving — from the mid-90s NHL glowing puck to tracking a tiny white ball across three
  football fields to within a couple yards.'"
