# CMF Phone 2 Pro: Budget Phone of the Year! (@mkbhd, 2025-05-01)

## Summary
A review of the **$279 CMF Phone 2 Pro** (CMF is Nothing's even-more-budget sub-brand) whose one non-mixed verdict is
**"it's a damn good deal."** ("Pro" is basically honorary — it just means triple cameras.) It **looks and feels far
higher-end than it is**: a big, bright 120 Hz AMOLED (up to 3,000-nit HDR / 1,300-nit full-screen; 2,160 Hz PWM), an
under-display reader, thin even bezels, light despite a 5,000 mAh battery. Its selling point is **personality in a sea
of tame, colorless slab phones** ("look at this orange — that's personality"). It's **modular again** (though not
compatible with last year's modules): a **swappable accessory back** adds **camera-lens mounts** (clip-on macro +
fisheye over the 50 MP main — "a crazy idea that may only last a year") and **"the strongest MagSafe magnet array I've
ever seen"** (a locked-on wallet/kickstand — better than Apple's too-easily-falling MagSafe wallet). The real star:
**"the best software in any new phone under about $400"** — Nothing OS 3.2 (the OnePlus→Nothing→CMF software lineage),
smooth, ad-free, and crucially **with no artificially removed features** (nothing locked to a higher-end chip; it even
has the Essential Space AI button). The specs he barely discusses (a MediaTek Dimensity 7300 Pro ≈ a 5-year-old
Snapdragon 865 — "perfectly fine"; NFC returns; a mono speaker that's terrible; IP54; 33 W charging), because the
**cameras are "fine — the triple cameras are kind of a stunt to look more Pro"** (a warm/pink-cast, over-exposing 50 MP
main, no OIS; a weak 2x tele; a tiny 8 MP ultra-wide). He wishes the US had all the bands, muses about a genuine
high-end version (a **Moto Z / Moto Mods callback**), and concludes: **for sub-$300, especially outside the US, "the
best budget phone out this year. I'm a fan."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2025-05-01] **A damn good deal with real personality.** "'There are mixed thoughts on the looks and the specs, but no
  mixed opinions on one thing: it's a damn good deal ($279). It looks and feels far higher-end than it is — a big
  bright 120 Hz AMOLED, thin bezels, light despite a 5,000 mAh battery. In a sea of tame, colorless slab phones, this
  is personality — look at this orange.'"
- [2025-05-01] **Modular again — clip-on lenses and the strongest MagSafe magnets I've seen.** "'It's modular again (not
  compatible with last year's modules): a swappable accessory back adds camera-lens mounts (a clip-on macro and fisheye
  over the 50 MP main — a crazy idea that may only last a year) and the strongest MagSafe magnet array I've ever seen —
  a wallet/kickstand that actually locks on, better than Apple's too-easily-falling MagSafe wallet.'"
- [2025-05-01] **The best software under $400 — with no artificially removed features.** "'This is the best software in
  any new phone under about $400 — Nothing OS 3.2, the OnePlus-to-Nothing-to-CMF software lineage, smooth, ad-free, and
  none of the features are artificially removed or locked to a higher-end chip. It even has the Essential Space AI
  button. This is what makes it easy to forget it's such a cheap phone.'"
- [2025-05-01] **The triple cameras are a stunt; the specs aren't the reason.** "'The triple cameras are kind of a stunt
  to look more Pro. The 50 MP main is fine (a warm/pink cast, over-exposes, no OIS); the 2x tele is weak on a small
  sensor; the ultra-wide is a tiny 8 MP. The chip (a MediaTek Dimensity 7300 Pro ≈ a 5-year-old Snapdragon 865) is
  perfectly fine. The specs aren't high on the list of reasons I'd get it — the software and display and modular fun
  are.'"
- [2025-05-01] **Best budget phone of the year.** "'It makes me wonder what a genuinely high-end modular version would
  be like — remember the Moto Z and Moto Mods? For $279, especially outside the US (I wish it had all the US bands),
  this is the best budget phone out this year. There's been a lot of action in the ~$400 space (the iPhone 16e, the
  Pixel 9a), but for sub-$300, this is where it's at. I'm a fan.'"
