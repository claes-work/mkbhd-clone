# Google Pixel 10A Impressions: (Never) Seen This Before

## Summary

First look at the Pixel 10a — which Marques calls **"the most close to a re-release I've
ever seen in a phone."** It's effectively identical to the Pixel 9a: same dimensions, same
screen, same cameras, **the same year-old Tensor G4 chip** (not even the G5), same $499
price. He walks the complete list of the handful of tiny differences, then turns it into a
commentary: the Pixel is now so **software-defined** that the hardware barely matters — which
raises "why release a new phone at all?" His answer: **"that's just the routine now"** —
capitalism's default of a new-year model on the shelf.

## Key claims

- **[2026-02-18]** **"This is the most close to a re-release I've ever seen in a phone… it feels
  like the same phone with a new name on it."** Same dimensions/shape, plastic back, the thicker-
  body-flush-camera trick, 5,100mAh battery, 6.3" 60–120Hz display (still 60Hz by default), same
  48MP+13MP dual cameras, **the same Tensor G4** (not the G5), 8GB/128GB, and the **same $499
  price.** (quote)
- **[2026-02-18]** The *entire* list of differences vs the 9a: ~10% brighter (3,000 nits),
  Gorilla Glass 7i (from 3), slightly thinner bezels, 30W charging (from 23), a fully flush camera,
  10W wireless (from 7.5), more recycled content, Satellite SOS in some regions, and **new colors.**
  "And that's it." (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-18]** He's careful about wishing for change for its own sake ("if it ain't broke,
  don't fix it"), but names obvious misses: **Pixelsnap magnets** ("the whole world was baffled at
  the lack of magnets in the iPhone 16E"), **256GB base** ("it's 2026"), or **at least the Tensor
  G5** — the A-series historically gets the flagship chip, and this year it doesn't. (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-18]** The read: **Google is "leaning all the way into the software-defined product
  that the Pixel really is."** A-series buyers "wouldn't notice G4 vs G5 as much as the software
  features that make the Pixel the Pixel" — Best Take, Camera Coach, **Call Screening, Hold for Me,
  Now Playing**, 7 years of updates, and **Quick Share that now works natively with AirDrop.**
  (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-18]** The commentary: **"why even release a new phone at all?"** If the hardware barely
  matters and it's all software, why churn one out yearly (Nothing publicly said you don't have to)?
  **"It's just 'cause that's the routine now."** The launch "felt like an afterthought," "the default
  that capitalism… falls into." The justification: even if you upgrade every 2–4 years, **"every year
  is someone's third year with a phone,"** so there's always a buyer for a new-year model on the shelf.
  (quote)
- **[2026-02-18]** Verdict: "Good thing it's still a decent deal." (quote)
