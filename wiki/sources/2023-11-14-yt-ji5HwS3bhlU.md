# To Beat the iPhone, You Must... Be the iPhone? (Nothing Chats) (@mkbhd, 2023-11-14)

## Summary
On **Nothing's attempt to break into the US by bringing iMessage to Android** (the Nothing Chats app, co-developed
with Sunbird, exclusive to the Phone 2). The framing is the iMessage lock-in: the iPhone has **~50% US market share
(growing), and 87% of US teens have an iPhone** — **"the most damning stat, because it means they got them early"**
(first phone → iPhone customer for life; it's the only way into the group chats, FaceTimes, and inside jokes). A
startup trying to break in can give up on the US, chase a carrier deal, or **"lean all the way in and embrace the
biggest asset of the other side"** — so Nothing built iMessage in. It works (sign in with an Apple ID — even without
Apple hardware — and you become a blue bubble, with full-res media and typing indicators). **But it's an enormous
security risk: you're signing into your Apple ID on a Mac Mini in some server farm you'll never see** (same for
Beeper). He asked **Carl Pei what Apple will do; the answer: "probably nothing"** — because Apple is under so much
antitrust pressure that publicly acknowledging/killing it would **shine a light on exactly the lock-in it doesn't
want seen** (they'll quietly point at the security risks instead). His counter: **if Apple cares so much about
security, why not just support RCS** so the millions of daily Android↔iPhone messages aren't unencrypted SMS? The
real question — will it work? **"There's a non-zero number of people"** who'll try it for the blue-bubble life, "but
there's a lot of baggage."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-11-14] **87% of US teens have an iPhone — they got them early.** "'The iPhone has ~50% US share and it's
  growing, and 87% of US teens have one — that's the most damning stat, because it means they got them early. For a
  lot of them it's their first phone, all their friends have iPhones, and they don't even consider anything else —
  it's the only way into the group chats, the FaceTimes, the inside jokes. It's a very baked-in, US-centric thing.'"
- [2023-11-14] **To break in, embrace the other side's biggest asset.** "'You can give up on the US, chase a carrier
  deal, or lean all the way in and embrace the number-one problem — the biggest asset of the other side. That's what
  Nothing did: they built iMessage into their Android phone (kind of). Sign in with an Apple ID — even without Apple
  hardware — and you become a blue bubble, with full-res media and typing indicators.'"
- [2023-11-14] **It's an enormous security risk.** "'This could lead to enormous security issues — signing into your
  Apple ID on a device you don't own. I confirmed with Nothing: it's literally signing in on a Mac Mini in a server
  farm that does the routing. It's encrypted, but you're logging into your Apple ID on someone else's computer — on
  its face, a big enough risk that many people will never try it (same for Beeper).'"
- [2023-11-14] **Carl Pei: Apple will probably do nothing — antitrust.** "'I asked Carl Pei what Apple will do. His
  answer: probably nothing — because we're in a weird 2023 timeline where Apple can't really retaliate publicly.
  Everyone knows they use iMessage to keep people from leaving; acknowledging this as a competitor to shut down would
  shine a light on the lock-in in a way they don't want. They'll quietly point at the security risks instead.'"
- [2023-11-14] **If Apple cares about security, support RCS.** "'The other side of the coin: if Apple cares so much
  about security, wouldn't they want to support RCS so the millions of daily Android↔iPhone messages aren't
  unencrypted SMS? That's another story. The real question: will it work? There's a non-zero number of people who'll
  try this for the blue-bubble life — but there's a lot of baggage.'"
