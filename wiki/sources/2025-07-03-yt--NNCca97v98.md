# My Nintendo Switch 2 Review (Doesn't Matter?)

## Summary

Marques reviews the Nintendo Switch 2, framing console reviews as fundamentally
different from phone reviews: with a console the reason you buy it is almost never the
hardware — it's the games. The Switch 2 is "a vehicle for the games." His recurring
"second-generation is the most interesting" thesis applies: the original Switch fused
Nintendo's two prior product lines (a home console like the Wii U and a handheld like
the 3DS) into one two-in-one, and the sequel refines both modes rather than reinventing.
He identifies battery life as the clear weak point, defends the $450 price as
inflation-reasonable, but flags an ecosystem that has gotten expensive around the
hardware. A characteristic "does the spec even matter to the buyer" review.

## Key claims

- **[2025-07-03]** Console reviews are unusual because "the reason you buy a console is
  almost never the hardware, it's the games — this thing is just a vehicle for the
  games." (paraphrase + partial quote)
- **[2025-07-03]** The **second generation is the most interesting** generation:
  the original Switch replaced both the Wii U (home console) and the 3DS (handheld) with
  a single two-in-one; roughly half of Switch owners use it in BOTH handheld and docked
  modes, so Nintendo improved both — a bigger screen for handheld, and 4K60 / HDR / a
  cooling fan / top-mounted USB-C / ethernet for docked. (paraphrase)
- **[2025-07-03]** The **magnetic Joy-Cons** "are what make the Switch the Switch" —
  the detachable-controller identity is preserved and now snaps on magnetically.
  (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2025-07-03]** New **mouse mode** for the Joy-Cons is responsive but "no games
  actually use it yet." (paraphrase)
- **[2025-07-03]** The extra performance "breathes new life into old games" — e.g. Bomb
  Rush Cyberfunk goes from 24–30fps to 60fps. (paraphrase)
- **[2025-07-03]** THE weak point is **battery life** — intense games can drain
  100→0% in ~3 hours. (paraphrase)
- **[2025-07-03]** The **$450 price is "actually not that bad"** adjusted for inflation:
  the 2017 Switch's $300 is roughly $400 in today's money, and this is 8 years later.
  (paraphrase)
- **[2025-07-03]** BUT the surrounding **ecosystem got pricey**: microSD Express cards,
  $80 games, a $9 *paid* Welcome Tour pack-in ("worse than the free Astro's Playroom"),
  and game-key-cards that still require a download. (paraphrase)
- **[2025-07-03]** The sticks are **not hall-effect** — "will it drift? unknown." A
  pointed callback to the original Switch's stick-drift saga. (paraphrase)
- **[2025-07-03]** Bottom line: "it comes down to which games you want to play."
  (paraphrase)
