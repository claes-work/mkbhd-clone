# Samsung Galaxy S25/Ultra Impressions: What Happened? (@mkbhd, 2025-01-22)

## Summary
First-look impressions of the **Galaxy S25 series** with a blunt opener: usually "same as last year" is exaggeration,
but here **"I can honestly say there is nothing dramatically new."** The base S25/S25+ are visually identical to last
year (the one tell: a moved 5G antenna) — new Snapdragon 8 Elite for Galaxy, a bigger vapor chamber, 12 GB base RAM. The
Ultra gets a token cosmetic update (slightly squarer sides, ~15% smaller bezels, a new 50 MP ultra-wide) but the same
battery/brightness/most cameras. **"This is the purest form of the old every-other-year 'S' spec-bump — basically a
Galaxy S24S."** He half-excuses it (the S24 sold well / won his phone of the year — "if it's not broke"), but notes the
design's felt stale for several years running. **Everything new is software/AI**: the headline is a new **power-button
assistant that's a hybrid of Gemini + Bixby** ("Ask Gemini" — defaults to Gemini, but keeps Bixby's in-app actions; oddly
no voice trigger); **AI Select** (context-aware selection → summarize text / make a GIF); **natural-language search** in
Settings and Gallery (genuinely useful for less-technical people); a **Now brief** widget; and an **audio eraser** (like
the Pixel's). His recurring verdict on AI features: **"cool if they actually work, but debatable how much you'll
actually use them."** The kicker: because Samsung now promises **seven years of updates**, **most of this software will
come to the S24 in a couple weeks** — so it's hard to use it as a reason to upgrade. His bigger-picture take: **One UI 7
(the software overhaul + fresh coat of paint, with some iOS-inspired bits) makes a bigger difference than any of the
S25 hardware changes.** Bottom line: **"an absolute textbook spec bump — zero risk for Samsung, and probably zero risk
for the target demographic too."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2025-01-22] **There is nothing dramatically new — a textbook spec bump.** "'Usually "same as last year" is
  exaggeration, but here I can honestly say there is nothing dramatically new about the S25s. The base phones are
  visually identical (the one tell is a moved 5G antenna); the Ultra gets slightly squarer sides, ~15% smaller bezels,
  and a new 50 MP ultra-wide. This is the purest form of the old "S" spec-bump — basically a Galaxy S24S. An absolute
  textbook spec bump, zero risk.'"
- [2025-01-22] **Everything new is software/AI — a hybrid Gemini+Bixby assistant.** "'Everything new here is software.
  The headline is a new power-button assistant that's a hybrid of Gemini and Bixby — it says "Ask Gemini," defaults to
  Gemini, but keeps Bixby's ability to reach into apps and take in-app actions (adding a calendar event for you).
  Oddly, there's no voice trigger. Plus AI Select (summarize text / make a GIF), natural-language search in Settings and
  Gallery, a Now brief, and an audio eraser.'"
- [2025-01-22] **AI features: cool if they work, debatable how much you'll use them.** "'These are decently useful — some
  clever, some work offline, some power users will love — but it's the recurring lesson: cool if they actually work,
  debatable how much you'll actually use them and how much value they bring.'"
- [2025-01-22] **Seven years of updates means the S24 gets most of this anyway.** "'Samsung now promises seven years of
  updates, so most of this software will come to the S24 in a couple weeks. That makes it hard to use as a reason to
  upgrade if you already hold a decently new phone.'"
- [2025-01-22] **One UI 7 matters more than the hardware.** "'One UI 7 — the software overhaul and fresh coat of paint,
  with some iOS-inspired bits like the lock-screen customization — makes a bigger difference than any of the S25
  hardware changes. Give me the new ultra-wide, the slightly bigger screen, even the new chip, and I might not notice.
  The UI I notice.'"
