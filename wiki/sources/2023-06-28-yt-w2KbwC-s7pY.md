# Why Does the M2 Mac Pro Exist? (@mkbhd, 2023-06-28)

## Summary
Apple **completed its Apple Silicon transition** ("said two years, took closer to three") — the last Intel Mac, the
**Mac Pro**, finally got Apple Silicon, "in a weird way." The chip ladder tops out at the **M2 Ultra** (two M2 Maxes
fused) in the **Mac Studio** — "absurdly impressive," 15–20% faster than the M1 Ultra he'd edited on all year (his
5-min ProRes export dropped from 1:45 to a hilarious **0:57 — ~80% improvement**). Hardware nerds hoped the Mac Pro
would get an even bigger "M2 Extreme"... **it didn't — it's the exact same M2 Ultra as the Studio.** So the question
writes itself: **why spend $3,000 more for the same performance in a bigger machine?** He tests the theories and
dismisses them: the thermal/sustained-performance advantage is a **rounding error** (single-digit %), and the extra
I/O isn't worth $3,000 (get dongles). **The real reason the Mac Pro exists is PCI slots** — six expansion slots the
Studio lacks — used by pros for sound/IO/storage/networking cards (**notably NOT video cards** — the GPU is baked
into the SoC; you literally can't add a GPU). **Most people should get the Mac Studio** ("a revelation — top-of-line
performance for way less than the old top of line"); the Mac Pro is for specific workflows (his own is **storage** —
a 64 TB PCI SSD at 25,000 MB/s). His closing **theory: this is a "lazy refresh" (same Intel-era chassis, new chip),
and Apple's pattern is a redesign the next generation** — so he's already thinking about the M3.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2023-06-28] **The transition is complete — and the Mac Pro got the same chip as the Studio.** "'Apple completed
  the Apple Silicon transition — every Mac that ran Intel a few years ago now runs Apple Silicon. The last was the
  Mac Pro, and it got it in a weird way: it's the exact same M2 Ultra chip that's in the Mac Studio. Hardware nerds
  hoped for an "M2 Extreme" — two Ultras fused — but it didn't happen.'"
- [2023-06-28] **The real reason is PCI slots — and most people should get the Studio.** "'Why spend $3,000 more for
  the same performance in a bigger, heavier machine? The real reason the Mac Pro exists is PCI slots — six expansion
  slots the Studio doesn't have. Next time someone asks "why does the Mac Pro exist when the Studio has the same
  chip," it's the PCI slots, that's really it. Most people should get the Mac Studio — it's a revelation, top-of-line
  performance for way less than the old top of line.'"
- [2023-06-28] **The thermal/IO theories don't justify it.** "'I ran the theories down. The bigger chassis's thermal
  advantage is a rounding error — single-digit percent, not enough to spend $3,000 on. The extra I/O (eight
  Thunderbolt, two HDMI, two Ethernet) is real but you'd just get dongles. It's PCI or nothing.'"
- [2023-06-28] **The slots aren't even for GPUs — the Apple Silicon quirk.** "'The PCI slots aren't for video cards
  at all — the GPU is built into the Apple Silicon chip (up to 192 GB shared memory). You could pull an AMD Radeon
  from the old Mac Pro, put it in this, and nothing would happen. The slots are for sound/IO/storage/networking cards
  — really specific pros who already had PCI desktops and literally couldn't make a Mac work without them. For me
  it's storage — a 64 TB PCI SSD at 25,000 MB/s.'"
- [2023-06-28] **The lazy-refresh-then-redesign theory.** "'You can predict Apple from the past: this Mac Pro is a
  classic lazy refresh — the same Intel-era chassis with the new chip inside — just like the first Apple Silicon
  MacBook Air and Pro reused the old design, then the next generation got the redesign. So I'm already thinking about
  the M3 Mac Pro redesign. Probably unhealthy, but that's where my mind goes.'"
