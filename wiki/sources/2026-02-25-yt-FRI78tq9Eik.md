# Samsung Galaxy S26/Ultra Impressions: 1 Crazy Display Feature!

## Summary

A remote first-look at the Galaxy S26 lineup — Marques was snowed in (2+ feet shut the
flights), so teammate **Brandon** shot the hands-on footage in California and Marques
assembled it from spec sheets. The story: **price creep** (the base is up $100), a modest
spec bump, **still no magnets and still no silicon-carbon**, a lot of "decently useful to
full-on slop" AI, and one genuinely exciting Ultra-only feature — a hardware-level
**Privacy Display** that fades off-axis, which he calls "the most Samsung thing ever."

## Key claims

- **[2026-02-25]** Production note: he couldn't fly (2+ feet of snow shut East-Coast flights for
  two days); **Brandon from the team shot the footage** in California and sent it back. (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-25]** **Price creep** is the first thing that stuck out: **S26 $900 / S26+ $1,100 /
  S26 Ultra $1,300** (last year the S25 started $799/128GB; the S26 is $899/256GB — a base-storage
  bump but $100 more; the + is also +$100). It feels like they're making room for an ~$800 Fan
  Edition. (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-25]** The expected spec bump: **Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy** (NPU-optimized
  for AI). The **Ultra** is slightly thinner/less boxy, **back to aluminum sides (from titanium)**
  with a redesigned vapor chamber, faster charging (25/45/60W), and **slightly wider apertures** on
  the otherwise-unchanged main (f/1.7→1.4) and 3x (f/3.4→2.4). "Pretty modest hardware changes."
  **Still no magnets and still no silicon-carbon batteries.** (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-25]** The standout: the Ultra's new **Privacy Display** — **hardware-level control over
  the pixels** that, when on, looks normal straight-on but **fades to gray/black off-axis** (both
  horizontal and vertical), replacing the always-on privacy screen protector. Highly customizable:
  auto-enable per location or per app (banking, photos), or **just part of the screen** (a password
  field, the notification area). "Genuinely really sick." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-02-25]** **"This is the most Samsung thing ever."** Samsung is conservative on its flagship
  designs but "will try absolutely insane stuff" — and **all its big swings are display innovations**
  (the Note Edge's curved side, the waterfall S-edges, the Note's huge screen, the Fold, the tri-fold,
  and now this privacy screen). "I hope it catches on." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-02-25]** The AI features "range from decently useful to **full-on slop**." Welcome:
  **Call Screening** (a Pixel copy) and **Nudge** (their Magic Cue — context-aware keyboard
  suggestions). Claimed: **LLM-powered Bixby** (with Gemini + Perplexity integrations — "new Bixby
  could be good"). Meh/borderline useless: **Photo Assist** (Google-Photos-style AI editing) and
  **Creative Studio** (an Image-Playground clone). (paraphrase)
- **[2026-02-25]** He dislikes Samsung **marketing it as "an AI phone"**: "in an age where it's more
  important than ever to get real photos and videos from people's cell phones, the slippery slope of
  all this AI stuff in our photos… I'm not sure I love how far down the slope we're going." (quote)
- **[2026-02-25]** Verdict: "no real massive surprises… not one of their big Hail Marys." The Ultra
  is "a little bit of a refresh" made interesting by the privacy screen; the base going up $100 with
  little else new — "not so excited about that." (paraphrase)
