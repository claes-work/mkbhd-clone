# The Huawei Ban: Explained! (@mkbhd, 2019-05-22)

## Summary
A landmark **industry-commentary explainer** — not a review. Marques breaks down the May 2019 US executive order
adding Huawei to the trade blacklist, cutting it off from Google/Android and US component suppliers. His
throughline: **less competition is bad for consumers**, whether or not you like Huawei.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2019-05-22] **What happened.** "A **US executive order** blacklisted Huawei from doing business with any US
  company. **Google complied** by pulling Huawei's Android license — 'not a choice they made,' just compliance.
  Huawei is the **#2 smartphone vendor** (behind Samsung, above Apple) and the fastest-growing." Neutral, sourced.
- [2019-05-22] **Past/present phones — the 90-day exemption.** "Existing Huawei/Honor phones get **security and
  software updates for ~90 days** (into August); after that they're **frozen** at whatever Android version they
  have. Google Play and Play Protect keep working **for now** on existing devices."
- [2019-05-22] **Hardware is survivable; software is the real problem.** "Huawei spent **$70B on components** last
  year, **$11B from US firms** — but they make their **own Kirin silicon** and built a **3-month stockpile**, so
  in theory they could swap suppliers. **The software is the killer**: AOSP is open-source, but bare AOSP has
  **no Google services (Gmail, YouTube, Play Store).** Building your own OS *and* app store is 'an immense
  challenge.'" His core analysis.
- [2019-05-22] **Why an alt app store is nearly impossible.** "Look at **Amazon's app store** — a multi-billion-
  dollar company and still a 'sad version.' And if you **can't do business with US companies**, you can't
  negotiate Netflix/Facebook/Instagram onto your store." A durable platform-moat insight.
- [2019-05-22] **THE THESIS — competition is good for consumers.** "As someone who **likes tech**, this is
  terrible: **one less company means less competition, less incentive** for everyone to work hard. Even if you're
  not a Huawei fan, **having them around is good for us.** Their phones have gotten good *and* pushed everyone
  around them to get better." (Also flags the knock-on risk to **folding phones** — the Mate X.)
- [2019-05-22] **The ZTE precedent + the reputational damage.** "A **similar 2018 cutoff hit ZTE** and it was
  'never the same.' Even if the ban reverses, **the lasting damage may be how people think about buying Huawei**
  — a hard sell at $1,000 vs. an iPhone. But Huawei is a 'goliath,' so maybe it goes differently." Hedged,
  source-critical; he expects it could reverse.

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "Having one less company means less competition, which is less incentive for these companies to work hard and
> make great stuff... even if you're not a fan of Huawei, we have to admit that having them around is good for us."
> "How do you make a phone without Google, without the Android license, without the Google Play Store?"
