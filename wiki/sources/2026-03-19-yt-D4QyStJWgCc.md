# Nothing Phone 4A/Pro Review: I Have a Theory

## Summary

A budget-phone review of the Nothing Phone 4A (€349) and 4A Pro ($499) — "the two best-looking
Nothing phones of all time" — that becomes a market-analysis piece. The tell is the naming:
**there is no Nothing Phone 4**, just the 4A and 4A Pro. Marques's theory: **Nothing can't
make a competitive high-end flagship right now** (a small company pays more for parts, and
RAM prices have skyrocketed), so CEO Carl Pei's "we won't do a flagship every year" framing —
thoughtful as it sounds — is really economics. The phones are good in the value slot the
underwhelming Pixel 10A and iPhone 17E left open; **"the 4A is where it's at."**

## Key claims

- **[2026-03-19]** Both are "good phones… the two best-looking Nothing phones of all time." The
  **4A** (€349, Snapdragon 7s Gen 4, triple cameras, 5,000+mAh) is the most competitively priced;
  the **4A Pro** ($499 US, unibody aluminum, a Glyph Matrix, a Snapdragon 7 Gen 4) is "a slightly
  higher-end version." They slot perfectly between the **underwhelming Pixel 10A ($499)** and the
  **iPhone 17E** — with **triple cameras** where those have one/two. (paraphrase)
- **[2026-03-19]** Praise for **Nothing OS 4.1** (smooth/responsive even without a flagship chip;
  UFS 3.1; more home-screen customization; the community-driven **Playground** widget store) — "I'll
  move to another phone and find myself missing the Nothing home-screen features." The **Glyph**
  lights (a 7-LED bar / the 4A Pro's fuller **Glyph Matrix** that can show notification icons) let
  you "keep the phone face down most of the time." The red video-recording dot: "maybe more phones
  should have that." (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-03-19]** **The theory: Nothing doesn't make a flagship right now because it *can't.*** The
  last high-end try, the **$800 Nothing Phone 3 (2025)**, "did not go well." A **relatively small
  company places smaller orders → pays more for the same parts**, and combined with **skyrocketing
  RAM prices**, "it's harder to compete" (the giants locked in old prices with larger orders / longer
  lead times). So a full flagship (8 Elite Gen 5 + 16GB + a 7,000mAh silicon-carbon battery) is "all
  super expensive and harder to get" — **and not why people buy Nothing phones.** (paraphrase)
- **[2026-03-19]** On Carl Pei's public "we're not going to make a new flagship every year just for
  the sake of it": the framing (make each upgrade meaningful) is **"very clever and thoughtful… totally
  valid,"** but the underlying reason is the economics above. And they know their **savvy fans would
  call out a "flagship" without the flagship chip**, so instead they made a **mid-range + upper-mid-
  range** pair — hence no Phone 4. (paraphrase)
- **[2026-03-19]** The 4A Pro "emulates the big flagship without being one" (unibody aluminum, a
  flagship-style plateau, a good-not-best Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 — "most people would be hard-pressed to
  tell the difference"). But some "flagship-looking" specs don't matter: **5,000 vs 4,500 nits** (no
  visible difference); **IP65 vs IP64**; and a **"144Hz" display that basically never hits 144** (only
  a couple supported games — the same trick as those old ASUS phones). Cameras are **"perfectly
  average"** (a bit HDR-y/noisy); neither shoots 4K from the 8MP ultrawide; the Pro's only extra is a
  **"world's first 140x ultra zoom"** — "why stop there? just go to 1,000 — it's just digital crop."
  (paraphrase + quote)
- **[2026-03-19]** Verdict: the 4A Pro is **"less a Pro version and more a slightly up-badged version
  for $150 more" for the US** — "still a fine phone, **but the 4A is where it's at.**" Neither has many
  AI features ("music to some people's ears"). (quote)
