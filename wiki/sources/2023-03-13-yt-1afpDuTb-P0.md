# What is Happening with Samsung's Camera? (Moon Mode) (@mkbhd, 2023-03-13)

## Summary
A **"time to own it"** correction video and part two to the iPhone-camera essay. Marques had posted a short zooming
to 100× "Space Zoom" on the S23 Ultra to shoot the Moon, saying it *wasn't* doing AI fakery — **"well, I was wrong,
so time to own it"** (he won't delete the short). **What he should have done: test it, run an A/B experiment** — as
Reddit user u/ibreakphotos did. The finding: point the phone at a **blurred** photo of the Moon (detail obscured)
and it still **spits out a sharp, detailed Moon with detail that wasn't in the source image.** The mechanism (also
documented on a Samsung forum, in Korean): past ~25× it recognizes the Moon, drops the exposure, sets focus to
infinity, locks stabilization, and **runs noise-reduction + detail-enhancement that AI-sharpens the viewfinder
toward what it *knows* the Moon is supposed to look like** — matching the phase, keeping color. It's **not quite
Huawei's alleged brute-force overlay fakery**, but it "feels weird, feels fake." His bigger point (a callback):
**this is just one of many photos our phones are already editing for us** — greener grass, bluer skies, punchier
food — **"the stuff that comes out of the camera isn't so much reality as this computer's interpretation of what it
thinks you'd like reality to look like."** You can disable it (Scene Optimizer off). The refrain: **"what is a
photo?"**

- [2023-03-13] **"I was wrong, so time to own it" — I should have run the experiment.** "'I made a short zooming to

  more complicated than nothing.'"

  skies bluer, food more saturated. The Moon is just the most visually obvious one.'"
- [2023-03-13] **You can turn it off — and "what is a photo?"** "'You can disable it by turning off Scene Optimizer
