## Summary
A week-long digital-wellbeing experiment: Ellis (Minimal Phone) and Adam (Boox Palma 2)
try to replace their smartphones with e-ink "finite scrollers." Team-authored; useful as
consumer-tech-culture context (the studio's digital-wellbeing sensibility), not as subject
persona data.

## Key claims (team-attributed — CONTEXT ONLY)
- [2025-06-13] The **"dumb phone paradox"** (Ellis's thesis): the only people who know
  these phones exist are enthusiasts who *like* smartphones — so the buyer must "be both an
  enthusiast about the tech but also want less of it in their life." Verdict: these are
  "objectively worse than regular smartphones… pretty bad daily drivers," but if you sit
  in the gap between loving tech and wanting less of it, they can help. (context)
- [2025-06-13] Practical findings (context): the Boox Palma 2 isn't a real phone (no
  reliable notifications/tap-to-pay) but pairs well *alongside* a smartphone for healthier
  habits; the Minimal Phone runs full Android 14 with a loved physical keyboard but has
  bad half-toning, always-on double-tap-to-wake killing battery, and carrier-lottery
  connectivity issues.
