# In-Glass Fingerprint Sensor: Testing the limits! (@mkbhd, 2018-05-15)

## Summary
A hands-on stress-test follow-up to the February in-glass fingerprint explainer — now with the shipping **Vivo
X21**. Marques deliberately abuses the optical under-display sensor (water, food, dust, screen protectors,
scratches) and benchmarks it against Touch ID. A good example of his methodical "test the limits" approach.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2018-05-15] **The setup — test the future now.** "If we're going to see this in a future iPhone or Samsung
  phone, it's worth exploring the limits now." Reprises the mechanism (an OLED lights the finger; a fingernail-
  sized optical sensor reads the reflection) from [[2018-02-21-yt-dRtqFjYYUY0]].
- [2018-05-15] **The stress tests, with honest controls.** **Water/sweat:** struggles — "but I'm not surprised;
  **Touch ID struggles with wet fingers too**" (fair, controlled comparison). **Saucy fingers (hot wings):** fails
  on both — "phones just don't like saucy fingers." **Dusty fingers (SunChips):** the in-glass fails first;
  **Touch ID unlocked on the first swipe** where the optical sensor needed several tries. He always cross-checks
  against Touch ID rather than judging in a vacuum.
- [2018-05-15] **Screen protectors — the surprising results.** A **tempered-glass** protector: no problem. A
  **privacy shield** (polarized, off-axis blackout): "I'd have bet against it — but it **works**." Even a
  **shattered** protector with cracks over the sensor: "really slow, but success is success." Empirical, willing
  to be surprised.
- [2018-05-15] **Scratch/wear test.** Deliberately box-cutter-scratches the glass over the sensor ("painful to do
  on purpose, but for science") — it keeps reading through micro-scratches and even heavy damage. "**If your phone
  ever gets this scratched, the fingerprint reader still working means you're good for a long time** — and you're
  probably in the market for a new phone anyway."
- [2018-05-15] **The verdict — "first-gen, like early Touch ID."** "It's clearly **not as fast as Touch ID** — but
  that's just where the tech is. **Remember first-gen Touch ID wasn't great either**, then it matured into the gold
  standard. If a bunch of companies work on this, I see it in a future iPhone/Samsung within a couple years."
  Calibrated optimism grounded in precedent.

## Notable verbatim quotes
