# Why Does This $10,000 Gucci Xbox Exist? (@mkbhd, 2021-12-02)

## Summary
An unboxing of the one-of-100 $10,000 Gucci Xbox Series X used as a springboard into a **framework for brand
collaborations.** His core lens: **every collab is ultimately about a win-win for both brands** (awareness,
trending, overlapping audiences). He splits tech collabs into **"make sense" (tech-on-tech, which can actually
improve the product — Apple×Nike, OnePlus×Hasselblad, Leica) vs "seem dumb" (tech×fashion, which do NOT make the
product better — this is just a monogrammed Xbox).** Threaded through: **"the smartphone is the most personal
purchase you make"** (why tech companies want to feel fashionable), the lesson that **Apple couldn't buy its way
into fashion alone** (the discontinued $10K gold Apple Watch Edition → the successful Hermès partnership), and
**hype** (Microsoft chasing PS5 buzz). Self-aware punchline: "it's working — I'm sitting here talking about it."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-12-02] **Every collab is a win-win — that's the whole game.** "'Brands love to collaborate — it's Corporate
  101 for awareness, trending, and overlapping audiences. **Fundamentally what they're always looking for is a
  win for both sides.** Apple×Nike+ makes sense (fitness features + Nike exposure via Apple's sales machine);
  OnePlus×Hasselblad makes sense (OnePlus borrows camera trust, Hasselblad looks fashion-forward). Some make
  sense, some seem dumb — but seen as a win-win, they all kind of make sense.'"
- [2021-12-02] **Tech-on-tech can improve the product; tech×fashion can't.** "'I divide them into two buckets. **The
  tech-on-tech collabs you could argue actually make the product better (a Hasselblad camera, Nike+ features). But
  this is just a monogrammed Xbox Series X — it's not a better Xbox.** The Dolce & Gabbana Razr was just a gold
  Razr. This kind of collab is about the experience of taking it out of the box, the packaging, the fashion — not
  performance.'"
- [2021-12-02] **The smartphone is the most personal purchase.** "'Tech is supposed to be purely functional, but
  there's a personal element. **I've always said the smartphone you buy is one of the most personal choices you
  make — you hold it every day, take it everywhere, it wakes you up, you feel empty without it.** No wonder
  companies want you to feel they're fashionable, not just good tech.'"
- [2021-12-02] **Apple couldn't buy into fashion alone.** "'The best example: the 2015 $10,000 solid-gold Apple
  Watch Edition — real people actually bought it, then it was discontinued a year later. **Even Apple, as massive
  as it is, can't break into fashion just on its own name** — which is why the Hermès collaboration that followed
  (custom bands, packaging, higher prices) has been way more successful: Hermès gets a tech crowd, Apple gets the
  fashion crowd. Win-win.'"
- [2021-12-02] **Hype — and "it's working."** "'The X factor is hype. With the PS5 the most hyped thing in gaming,
  Microsoft's brand team wants people talking about Xbox — so a limited-edition hyped Xbox with a hyped fashion
  brand. **The product isn't better, but the brand awareness is a win-win — and it's working: I'm here on video
  talking about it, and it's now on YouTube going into their brand-sentiment calculator (and of course there are
  no dislikes on this video).**'"
