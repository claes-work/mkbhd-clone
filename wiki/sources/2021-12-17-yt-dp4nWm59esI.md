# The Blind Smartphone Camera Test 2021! (@mkbhd, 2021-12-17)

## Summary
The fourth annual **Blind Smartphone Camera Test** — a canonical MKBHD format. 16 phones, bracket-style blind
voting on Instagram (3M+ votes), labels removed to defeat **confirmation bias.** The **$399 Google Pixel 5A won**
(75% in the final over the OnePlus 9 Pro). The durable, repeated findings: **(1) the brighter photo almost always
wins** (a reflection of the side-by-side format, NOT necessarily the better photo); **(2) sharpness doesn't matter**
(social-media compression erases it — and pixel-peeping "is not the point"); **(3) most people want the most
finished photo straight out of camera** (they don't even tap to focus). New this year: **price had almost nothing
to do with which camera people preferred** (the more expensive phone lost most rounds; the cheapest won it all).

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2021-12-17] **Why blind — confirmation bias.** "'We all know which cameras we think we like — but that's heavily
  influenced by **confirmation bias.** Show you a photo you KNOW is an iPhone vs one you like less and your eye
  finds every little thing better about the iPhone. **Remove the labels — 16 phones, bracket format, blind
  voting** — and narrow down to the People's Choice best camera.'"
- [2021-12-17] **Finding #1 — the brighter photo wins.** "'Four years running, the biggest lesson: **all else
  equal, in a side-by-side, people almost always pick the brighter photo as the winner. That doesn't mean it's
  the better photo — it's a reflection of the format** (put two images side by side and people are drawn to the
  brighter one).' (Exception this year: on the red pool table, the too-bright photos overexposed the reds and
  LOST to the more neutral ones.)'"
- [2021-12-17] **Finding #2 — sharpness doesn't matter on social.** "'While I love a crispy photo, **sharpness is
  never really a factor — social-media compression erases any advantage.** People ask why I don't post
  downloadable files to pixel-peep — that's not the point; the point is the gut reaction to a photo as you'd
  actually see it posted online.'"
- [2021-12-17] **Finding #3 — people want the finished photo.** "'The theory I find most interesting: **most people
  just want the most finished photo out of the camera** — the data shows most people take the phone out, open the
  camera, hit the shutter (they don't even tap to focus), and that unedited photo is the one they share. Done.'"
- [2021-12-17] **New finding — price doesn't predict the winner; the Pixel 5A won.** "'I look for patterns — and
  the most interesting thing this year: **the price of the phone had almost nothing to do with whether you voted
  it the better camera.** The more expensive phone won 5 / lost 3 in round one, lost all four in round two, and
  **the $399 Pixel 5A won it all** (75% in the final). One of the hardest videos to make every year (seating,
  shooting, tallying 3M+ votes) — but always a lot of fun.'"
