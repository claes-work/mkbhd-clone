## Summary
A myth-buster on what a "hologram" actually is (real holograms = interference-pattern
recordings; "holograms" like Miku/Pepper's-Ghost are 2D projection), building to the Voxon
VX2 swept-volume LED display as a genuine 3D "digital campfire." Ellis carries the
technical load. The subject's contribution is a compact statement of his reviewer's
philosophy about judging a product whose use-case doesn't exist yet.

## Key claims (Marques-attributed only)
- [2026-04-13] Marques's **reviewer doctrine on judging early/use-case-less tech:** as a
  reviewer, evaluating the swept-volume display is like judging **"a mechanically complex
  McLaren P1 before we had roads — it would be a bad take to say the car is useless."** The
  tech can be great *for a technologist* while being a hard sell *to a buyer*, and a good
  reviewer holds both frames at once. (tech-reviews — a reusable "display before the
  content" / "car before the roads" principle)
- [2026-04-13] He affirms Ellis's "digital campfire" framing as the interesting angle — a
  narrative, experiential, 360°-viewable display people can stand around — while still
  flatly noting the honest cons: it's **not** the highest-resolution or most color-accurate
  display in the studio, and it **does flicker** in person. (tech-reviews — pros-and-cons
  honesty even about a thing he finds cool)

## Notable verbatim quotes (Marques)
- "It's like if someone invented [a] mechanically complex McLaren P1 before we had roads —
  it would be a bad take to say that the car is useless."
- "If you were trying to explain it to a technologist, you'd be like, 'This is great.' If
  you were trying to convince someone to buy it, you'd be like…" (the reviewer's two frames)
