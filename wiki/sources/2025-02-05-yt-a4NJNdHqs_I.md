# Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra Review: The Tables Have Turned! (@mkbhd, 2025-02-05)

## Summary
A review/rant on the **$1,299 S25 Ultra** that argues Samsung **"played it incredibly safe — took last year's award-
winning flagship and did the bare minimum, a spec bump."** Of the **three big 2025 flagship pillars** (Snapdragon 8
Elite, super-bright displays, silicon-carbide batteries), the S25 Ultra has **only the chip** — no IP69, no Qi2, no
returning 10x, no high-frequency PWM, and a **downgraded S Pen** (Bluetooth removed). His thesis for *why*: **Samsung
has no competitive pressure in the US because Vivo/Xiaomi/Oppo have no meaningful US traction**, and Americans buy from
carriers — so **"Samsung is the go-to safe option,"** and as long as they ship phones that "play it safe and don't
totally suck," people keep buying. The chip is genuinely great (the best part vs last year — rivals the A18 Pro,
excellent efficiency/battery), but that'll be true of *every* 2025 Android flagship, so it's not a reason to pick
*this* one. His sharpest critique lands on the **S Pen Bluetooth removal**: Samsung justified it with "<1% used it," but
**"follow that logic and most people never use the stylus at all — the Ultra is *supposed* to be crammed with extra
features for the few who'll use them; that's what we pay the premium for."** The real tell is **"we could save a few
cents per unit and keep appealing to the masses"** (also why no silicon-carbide battery, no IP69, no Qi2). He flags the
**unknown AI cost** ("Galaxy AI free until the end of 2025 — then what? Samsung may not even know yet"). The upgrade
that *is* worth it: the new 50 MP ultra-wide. Verdict: **"awesome, but not the best — and at $1,299 it's fair to expect
the best."** The competition caught up: **"we went from last year's 'why would you buy anything else?' to this year's
'you could buy anything else.' The tables have turned."** (The base S25/S25+ at $799/$999 have all the same software +
chip and are the better value.)

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2025-02-05] **They played it incredibly safe — only one of the three 2025 pillars.** "'The three big 2025 flagship
  pillars are the Snapdragon 8 Elite, super-bright displays, and silicon-carbide batteries. The S25 Ultra has only the
  chip — no IP69, no Qi2, no 10x zoom, no high-frequency PWM, and a downgraded S Pen. They took last year's award-
  winning flagship and did the bare minimum, a spec bump.'"
- [2025-02-05] **No pressure because the competition has no US traction.** "'Samsung has no pressure to compete against
  the Vivos, Xiaomis, and Oppos because those have no meaningful US traction, and Americans buy from carriers. So
  Samsung is the go-to safe option — and as long as they ship phones that play it safe and don't totally suck, people
  keep buying and they never feel threatened.'"
- [2025-02-05] **The S Pen Bluetooth removal betrays what "Ultra" means.** "'They removed the S Pen's Bluetooth,
  justifying it with "<1% used it." But follow that logic and most people never use the stylus at all. The Ultra is
  *supposed* to be crammed with extra features for the small number who'll use and love them — that's what we pay the
  premium for. The real reason is "we could save a few cents per unit and keep appealing to the masses."'"
- [2025-02-05] **The unknown AI cost.** "'Galaxy AI is free until the end of 2025 — but then what? They haven't said.
  Part of me thinks Samsung doesn't even know yet. It's not great to have that looming over your head as a user.'"
- [2025-02-05] **Awesome but not the best — the tables have turned.** "'This phone is awesome — great chip, great
  battery, one of the best displays, fine cameras — but it's not the best, and at $1,299 it's fair to expect the best.
  We went from last year's "why would you buy anything else?" to this year's "you could buy anything else." The tables
  have turned. The base S25/S25+ at $799/$999 have the same software and chip and are the better value.'"
