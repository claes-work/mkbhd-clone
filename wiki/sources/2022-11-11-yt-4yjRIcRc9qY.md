# Lucid Air: How to One-up Tesla! (@mkbhd, 2022-11-11)

## Summary
A week with the $154K Lucid Air Grand Touring, framed around Lucid's strategy: **one-up Tesla at everything.** On
paper it beats the Model S (446 mi range, 350 kW charging, 1,000+ hp). In person the **interior genuinely one-ups
Tesla** (better materials, a quieter cabin, dramatically better back seat, a huge front trunk, a 21-speaker Dolby
Atmos system, real buttons/knobs). The bold in-house software (no CarPlay) is "pretty good" and improving (v2.0),
with quirks (can't show music + maps at once). And it **drives** — with **three genuinely distinct personalities
(Smooth/Swift/Sprint)** that make it a car he'd be **"equally happy driving or being driven in"** — though he wishes
you could **decouple the modes** (Swift acceleration + Smooth suspension). His hot-take dissent: **he doesn't like
the exterior** (the two-tone and the bulbous trunk). The catch is the strategy itself: **Lucid starts at its most
expensive, highest-margin trims and works down** (a $250K Sapphire coming) — so **"when you only tackle Tesla's top
trims, [you] might be trending towards trouble."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-11-11] **The strategy — one-up Tesla at everything.** "'Different makers enter EVs differently (Ford a sedan,
  Tesla a sports car, Rivian a truck). **Lucid's strategy is to one-up Tesla at everything** — on paper the Air GT
  beats the Model S: **446 mi range, 350 kW charging, 1,000+ hp, stickier tires, more luxury.** Got my attention.'"
- [2022-11-11] **The interior genuinely one-ups Tesla.** "'Tesla interiors were never much to write home about — and
  **Lucid one-ups pretty much all of it**: better materials (leather/Alcantara/wood), a quieter cabin, better seats,
  and a **dramatically better back seat** (doors open 90°, way more legroom, real HVAC controls). A **huge powered
  front trunk I'm tempted to use more than the rear**, a 21-speaker Dolby Atmos system (I came home and just sat
  listening), and real buttons/knobs.'"
- [2022-11-11] **The software — bold, good, improving, quirky.** "'Like Tesla, no CarPlay — their own bold UI, and
  it's **pretty good (v2.0 — faster and better laid out than the 1.x versions), with useful features (a top-down
  parking view Tesla still doesn't offer).** The quirk: **you can't show music and maps at the same time.** But it's
  software, and this car actually gets meaningful updates.'"
- [2022-11-11] **It drives — three personalities; happy to drive or be driven.** "'It's a 5,000+ lb sedan, but it
  has **three genuinely distinct personalities — Smooth/Swift/Sprint** (great regen/one-pedal calibration). It
  feels lighter than it is, with real mechanical grip — **confidence-inspiring, and it happens to have 1,000 hp.**
  My gripe: **you can't decouple the modes** (I'd want Swift acceleration with Smooth suspension). Unlike the
  executive-seat prototype I drove 5 years ago (a car to be driven in), **this is a car I'd be equally happy
  driving or being driven in — an accomplishment.** And it really gets its ~400–430 mi.'"
- [2022-11-11] **The dissent + the strategy's trouble.** "'My hot take: **I don't like how it looks from the
  outside** (the two-tone silver reads like an old Lincoln, and the regular-trunk decision gives it a bulbous back
  — great for rear luxury/aero, but the trunk opening is small, hard to fit a flower pot). Overall it one-ups Tesla
  in the ways that matter — **but it's more expensive across the board ($154K GT, a $250K Sapphire coming — basically
  the Plaid Plus Tesla never made). They start at the most expensive, highest-margin trims and work down — so when
  you only tackle Tesla's top trims, [you] might be trending towards trouble.**'"
