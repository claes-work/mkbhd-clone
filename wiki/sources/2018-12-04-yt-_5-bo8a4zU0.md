# The Blind Smartphone Camera Test 2018! (@mkbhd, 2018-12-04)

## Summary
The annual Blind Camera Test at full scale — a **16-phone bracket** decided by **6M+ public votes** (5M+ on
Instagram, 1M+ on Twitter). The **Huawei Mate 20 Pro won** as "the people's champ," and the results deliver his
sharpest media-literacy insight yet: **the brighter photo almost always wins — and brighter ≠ better.**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2018-12-04] **The format — take ALL of them, let instinct decide.** "Instead of my subjective top few, **we took
  all 16 phones** from the past year or two (Pixel 3/2, iPhone XS/X, Note 9, P20 Pro, Mate 20 Pro, RED Hydrogen,
  V40, 6T, Razer 2, U12+, Moto Z2 Force, Pocophone, BlackBerry Key2, Palm) — seeded, randomized, **people's-choice
  bracket, winner takes all.** Same photo on every phone each round." A scaled-up, un-gameable version of the
  franchise.
- [2018-12-04] **THE INSIGHT — "the brighter, punchier photo almost always won."** "**9 times out of 10 the
  brighter/more-saturated/more-contrasty photo won every single round.** When you put photos side by side, people's
  eyes just go to the brighter one — and **Twitter/Instagram compression ruins the sharpness/detail advantage** the
  actually-great cameras rely on, so it comes down to which looks brighter." A durable media-literacy finding.
- [2018-12-04] **The iPhone XS vs BlackBerry upset (the illustration).** "I thought the iPhone was a gimme. But the
  BlackBerry photo was brighter/warmer (even though its smaller sensor was so starved it **mistook my sweatshirt's
  texture for noise and smoothed it away**). **228,000 people picked the BlackBerry; 63,000 the more-neutral
  iPhone.** That's how the Pocophone beat the iPhone X, how the P20 Pro beat the Pixel 3, how the Mate 20 Pro won it
  all — the brighter, less-shadowy photo." A concrete, memorable case.
- [2018-12-04] **"Brighter ≠ better" — his own preference stated.** "The brighter photo isn't necessarily the
  better one. **I'm a fan of the Pixel 3's flatter, more natural, more neutral photo with lots of dynamic range and
  detail — the more information the better, because I can bend and adjust it later.** But side by side, people's
  eyes go straight for the finished-looking one." Separates crowd preference from his craft preference.
- [2018-12-04] **The takeaway — two types of people + "nothing actually sucks."** "There's no scientific winner
  here — the pixel-peeping advantages got eliminated. **Know what you want: a photo that looks great straight out of
  camera (AI post-processing), or a natural/neutral one with max information to edit. Two different types of people,
  and clearly there are more of the first.**" And (via friends' reactions — Dave2D et al.): "**we're in a time when
  nothing actually sucks** — I don't care enough about my camera in 2018 to switch phones for it; they're all so
  good."
