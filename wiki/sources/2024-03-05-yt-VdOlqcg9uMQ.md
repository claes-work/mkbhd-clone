# Nothing Phone 2A Review: Flashy Good Deal! (@mkbhd, 2024-03-05)

## Summary
A review of the **$350 Nothing Phone 2A** — framed by his budget-phone rule: **"with all of them, if you poke hard
enough, you find the corner they cut; picking one is just picking the trade-offs you're okay with."** This one **"is
the prettiest $350 phone"** (the transparent Nothing aesthetic, dual center cameras "like eyes," an NYC-subway-map-
inspired PCB look, the Glyph lights). The cuts are reasonable: clear *plastic* (not glass), a plastic frame, IP54,
no wireless charging. But the strengths land: a big, bright 6.7" 120 Hz AMOLED with **2160 Hz PWM dimming** ("if you
don't like the back, at least the front is impressive for $350"), a **battery champ** (5,000 mAh — bigger than
Nothing's flagships — 45 W charging), and a **MediaTek Dimensity 7200 Pro** that's **"respectable — a testament to
the software"** (Nothing OS 2.5 is clean, fast, no bloat — "a throwback to old OnePlus magic"). The thing that left
the biggest imprint: **it keeps almost all the flagship Nothing features with no artificial cuts** (the only missing
one is macro mode — same Pro camera modes, same home-screen features, same Glyph functionality) — **"it feels like
they're trying to give people all of the Nothing experience for less money."** Cameras are "serviceable, not good,
but fine." With three years of Android updates promised, **"a good, solid, all-around budget phone. Well done,
Nothing."**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2024-03-05] **The budget-phone rule — find the corner they cut.** "'Budget phones are fun because if you poke
  around hard enough, you'll find the thing they had to cut corners on. Picking one for you is just a matter of
  picking the one that makes the trade-offs you're okay with (build+cameras → iPhone SE; a big screen → Galaxy A54;
  software+cameras → Pixel 7A). And this — this is the designer one.'"
- [2024-03-05] **The prettiest $350 phone.** "'It's not going to win on raw specs, the best camera, or the best
  battery — but you could argue it's the prettiest $350 phone. If the mission is to look like a Nothing phone and not
  be mistaken for anything else, mission accomplished. Even if you don't like it, at least it's visually
  interesting.'"
- [2024-03-05] **A respectable chip — a testament to the software.** "'My biggest question mark was the MediaTek
  Dimensity 7200 Pro — Nothing conveniently only benchmarks it against the two-year-old Phone 1. It won't win prizes
  on raw specs, but in actual use it's respectable, and that's a testament to the software: Nothing OS 2.5 is clean,
  no bloat, fast and smooth — a throwback to old OnePlus magic.'"
- [2024-03-05] **It keeps the flagship features — no artificial cuts.** "'What left the biggest imprint: compared to
  their flagships, almost all the features are still here — the only one missing is macro mode. All the Pro camera
  modes, experimental features, home-screen features — it's all on this cheaper phone. It feels like they're trying
  to give people all of the Nothing experience for less money, without artificial cuts.'"
- [2024-03-05] **A good, solid budget phone.** "'Yes, there are hardware cuts (plastic, IP54, no wireless charging).
  The cameras are serviceable — not good, but fine (autofocus works, captures a moment, document scanning). With
  three years of Android updates promised, this is a good, solid, all-around budget phone. Well done, Nothing.'"
