## Summary
A short segment from Marques's **Retro Tech** series covering the **1989 Reebok Pumps** — the basketball shoe
with **inflatable chambers** you hand-pump to create a "custom fit," plus a heel **"energy return system"**
(cylinders Reebok claimed acted as springs). Marques inflates the shoe and **tests whether the hyped
performance claims hold up** with a vertical-jump test, deadpan-debunking the marketing.

## Key claims / observations
- The Reebok Pumps' pitch was **enhanced athletic performance** via the custom inflatable fit + heel energy
  return — classic late-'80s wearable-tech marketing. [2021-03-15]
- **The performance claims don't really hold up on test.** His baseline vertical maxed at 10'8"; with the
  Pumps he hit 10'9" once (a marginal inch) and on a second metric actually came in *below* baseline (9'2" vs a
  9'3" usual). Verdict, dryly: "they need to sell these along with jumping lessons from instructors." [2021-03-15]
- Fits his recurring reviewer instinct: **test the hyped claim empirically rather than repeat the spec/marketing.**
