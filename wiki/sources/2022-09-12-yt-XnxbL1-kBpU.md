# The State of Foldables 2022: Samsung vs The World! (@mkbhd, 2022-09-12)

## Summary
A state-of-the-category essay. Two facts up top: **foldable sales rise every year (but are still a tiny fraction),
and it's still early adopters/enthusiasts** (they're expensive and need a material-science breakthrough to feel
like a regular phone). The core thesis: **Samsung steers the ship.** He sorts the market into **the Fold, the Flip,
and "the others,"** and finds **everyone is converging on the Samsung form factor** (the Oppo Find N, Xiaomi Mix
Fold 2, the newly Z-Flip-like Razr). Fold 4 notes: the standout is **the new taskbar/dock** ("maybe the iPad should
experiment with a permanent dock like this"), the under-display selfie **takes "the worst selfies I've seen from a
modern phone."** Flip 4 notes: the big win is finally **all-day battery**, and the small-fold's **"barrier to
mindless scrolling"** is an upside for people trying to use their phone less. His explanation for Samsung's lead:
**they try a lot of weird stuff and can afford to subsidize the R&D** — "that's the Samsung thing."

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2022-09-12] **Samsung steers the ship — the fold/flip/others taxonomy.** "'Foldable sales rise yearly but are a
  tiny fraction; it's still enthusiasts. The most interesting thing: **Samsung so clearly steers this category.**
  The market has coalesced into three form factors — **the Fold (normal → big), the Flip (normal → small), and "the
  others"** — and **everyone is converging on the Samsung version.**'"
- [2022-09-12] **The Fold 4 — the dock is the best thing; the under-display camera is the worst.** "'The Fold 4
  looks like last year (trimmer bezels, an 8+ Gen 1 that gives 15–25% more battery, flagship-quality cameras) —
  the hardware evolution has basically stopped. **The absolute best thing is the new dock/taskbar — it massively
  improved how easy it is to multitask; maybe the iPad should experiment with a permanent dock like this.** And the
  under-display selfie camera hides itself impressively — **but it takes some of the worst selfies I've ever seen
  from a modern phone; just use the back camera.**'"
- [2022-09-12] **The Flip 4 — all-day battery, and a barrier to mindless scrolling.** "'The Flip (the most popular
  foldable) is iterative, but the biggest win is finally **all-day battery** (the 8+ Gen 1 + a bigger 3,700 mAh
  cell, and the best hinge they've made). A small fold means you have to open it to use it — **for a power user
  like me that wears on you, but for people intentionally trying to use their phone less it's a huge win: a small
  barrier against grabbing it and mindlessly scrolling.**'"
- [2022-09-12] **The others — riskier, and the Razr lost its character.** "'"The others" (the outward-folding Mate
  Xs, TCL's tri-folds/rollables) are the most interesting because there's no Samsung — but **their future is way
  more questionable** (outward-folding can't be as durable until material science advances). Even the **Razr, the
  one flipper with real character, now looks and feels more like a Z Flip than ever** (are they uncreative, copying
  the popular one, or converging on the most advanced design?).'"
- [2022-09-12] **Why Samsung leads — they subsidize the weird R&D.** "'It's risky to pour R&D into something
  unproven — **which is why Samsung is at the forefront: that's what they do (remember they made the first huge
  "phablet"). They try so many weird things that some are bound to stick, and they make so much money elsewhere
  they can subsidize the R&D of wild ideas.** Someday a foldable Pixel or even iPhone — but for now, **Samsung
  steers the foldables market, until the next material-science breakthrough, and I'd bet Samsung is the one
  spending the most to find it.**'"
