# The Truth about Drone Deliveries! (@mkbhd, 2025-03-25)

## Summary
An investigative deep-dive (his return-to-explainer lane) on **drone delivery — very different from the "propellers
carry your box" image.** The setup: **85% of Amazon's deliveries are under five pounds**, yet Amazon's drone (drops the
package from 15 ft, blows it away with propwash — "I'd never trust it with a GPU") and Alphabet's Wing (a promising
site with no findable coverage) aren't nailing it. The neat use cases that *do* make sense: baby formula for a stuck
new parent, first aid somewhere a car can't reach, a burrito instead of "a 4,000-lb private taxi." The company that's
way ahead is **Zipline** (didn't sponsor; just pulled back the curtain) — the **largest autonomous drone-delivery fleet
in the world**, doing it since 2016. **Platform 1** (a fixed-wing craft slingshot 0→60 in a quarter-second, parachute
drop, caught out of the air by a hook) has saved thousands of lives delivering **blood/medical supplies in Rwanda**.
**Platform 2** (the current gen) is "the most advanced drone delivery we've ever seen": a fixed-wing + articulating-
prop drone that **stays ~100 m up** and lowers a small **"Zip" droid on a zipline** to place the package with **dinner-
plate precision** (styrofoam body, carbon-fiber wing, weight at a premium). He gets the logical questions answered:
**it's shockingly quiet** (acoustic engineering + never dropping below a football-field up), **~3–5 minute delivery**
(70 mph cruise, 20-mi range), **weatherproof** (flies in anything short of hurricane winds), and **safe** (redundant
everything — "cut a wire and it flies home"; can lose two propellers; 500 safety checks/sec; a parachute fallback) with
**100 million miles flown, zero human-safety incidents.** His verdict: **"not for everything — trucks aren't going
anywhere — but for very specific deliveries that are both small and time-sensitive, drone delivery makes the most
sense"** (his own example: forgetting a memory card at a tech event). Rwanda was an ideal first market (small, hilly,
stormy, a cooperative government — because **US regulation is "a whole department's worth of work"**). His one worry is
**visual/night-sky pollution** (a Starlink parallel), not noise.

  propwash (I'd never trust it with a GPU), and Alphabet's Wing has a promising site but no coverage I could find.
  Maybe if the giants aren't nailing it, it's just not practical — or maybe there are perfect little use cases.'"
- [2025-03-25] **Zipline is way ahead — Platform 1 saved lives in Rwanda; Platform 2 is the most advanced yet.** "'The
  largest autonomous fleet in the world is Zipline (since 2016). Platform 1 — a fixed-wing craft slingshot 0→60 in a
  quarter-second, parachute drop, caught by a hook — has saved thousands of lives delivering blood in Rwanda. Platform
  2 stays ~100 m up and lowers a small "Zip" droid on a zipline to place the package with dinner-plate precision.'"
- [2025-03-25] **The logical questions, answered: quiet, fast, weatherproof, safe.** "'It's shockingly quiet (acoustic
  engineering + never dropping below a football field up); ~3–5 minute delivery (70 mph, 20-mi range); weatherproof
  (flies in anything short of hurricane winds); and safe — redundant everything ("cut a wire and it flies home"; can
  lose two propellers; 500 safety checks/sec; a parachute fallback), with 100 million miles flown and zero human-safety
  incidents.'"

  impressive, but not for everything — trucks aren't going anywhere. For very specific deliveries that are both small
  and time-sensitive, it makes the most sense. My example: I fly to a tech event, didn't rent a car, forgot my memory
  card and need it quick — drone delivery. It unlocks a new dimension for a few specific things, and sometimes means
  one less vehicle on the road.'"
- [2025-03-25] **Rwanda was ideal; US regulation is the real barrier; the worry is the night sky.** "'Rwanda was an
  ideal first market — small, hilly, stormy, with a government willing to work on regulation. In the US, regulation is

  fast enough to be basically invisible — but I am a little worried about visual pollution, particularly the night sky,
  the same way Starlink is useful but concerning for the view of the sky.'"
