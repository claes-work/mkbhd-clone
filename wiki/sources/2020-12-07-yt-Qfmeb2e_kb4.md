# The Portless iPhone: Let's Talk! (@mkbhd, 2020-12-07)

## Summary
Commentary on rumors of a portless iPhone, built on a promotable framework: **when Apple removes something, they
always do two things — "solve and justify."** Threads in **"Apple reporting is pattern-matching"** (project the
past forward), the **control double-edge** ("you're along for the ride for the misses too"), and a read on how
Apple would frame killing the Lightning port (MagSafe must be in the box). Includes a **Mark Gurman guest
interview** — his predictions are attributed to Gurman, not the subject.

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2020-12-07] **The control double-edge — "you're along for the ride for the misses too."** "'All the best AND
  worst things about the iPhone come from **the level of control Apple has.** The vertical integration (like the
  M1) enables the efficiency and image quality people love — but **if you're an Apple person, you're along for
  the ride for the misses too** (a keyboard that breaks after two years, antennagate).'" Ties to the M1 "upside
  and downside of control" ([[2020-11-11-yt-b6g6rDDt9x8]]).
- [2020-12-07] **"Apple reporting is pattern-matching."** "'One of the truest facts in tech: **most Apple
  reporting boils down to looking at what one of the biggest, most consistent tech giants has done and applying
  that pattern to the future, assuming they'll keep it up.**'" A promotable meta-observation about Apple analysis.
- [2020-12-07] **THE FRAMEWORK — "solve and justify."** "'With everything Apple removes, they always: **(1) solve
  the problem it caused (and probably charge you for it), and (2) justify on stage why they removed it.** Home
  button → solved with Face ID, justified with more screen. Headphone jack → solved with AirPods (a wildly
  profitable product), justified with water resistance + "courage." **Create a problem, then sell you the
  solution.**'" A promotable, reusable Apple-behavior model.
- [2020-12-07] **How they'd frame the portless iPhone — MagSafe must be in the box.** "'The **justify is easy
  (you don't use the port for much but charging; a bit more water resistance, in-screen fingerprint). The solve
  is harder — MagSafe.** They can't pull it off unless **MagSafe is in the box** (they could make a cheaper
  plastic version, like the two Apple Watch puck versions). If they go portless and DON'T include it, I'll lose
  my mind.'" (Gurman guest: predicts the rationale mirrors the headphone-jack one — quarantined as guest.)
- [2020-12-07] **Buttons are (probably) here to stay — the HTC U12+ lesson.** "'After the home button, jack, and
  port — **are buttons next? I think they're here to stay a couple years:** buttons are hard to solve and hard
  to justify removing. **Pressure-sensitive/haptic buttons were done before (I reviewed the HTC U12+ in 2018) and
  it wasn't great** — what if the phone freezes and you can't hard-reset? The buttonless-holeless **Meizu Zero
  got 39 Indiegogo backers and died** — the world wasn't ready.'"
- [2020-12-07] **"It's not if, it's when" — and Apple starts the trend.** "'It's **not if, it's when** Apple gets
  around to it — and **one or two years after Apple does something "ridiculous" (like killing the headphone jack),
  it becomes a trend everywhere.**'" His recurring Apple-sets-the-trend read.

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "With everything they've removed from the iPhone, they always do two things: one, they try to solve the problem
> that is caused by the thing they got rid of, and probably charge you for it. And two, they will always try to
> justify on stage why they got rid of that thing."
> "If you're an Apple person, you're along for the ride for the misses too."
> "Most of Apple reporting can be boiled down to looking at the past... and applying that pattern to the future."
