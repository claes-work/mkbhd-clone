# The Blind Smartphone Camera Test 2019! (@mkbhd, 2019-12-17)

## Summary
The second annual crowd-voted blind camera test — 16 phones, a March-Madness bracket, **4M Instagram + 2M Twitter
votes.** Winner: **Galaxy Note 10+.** The value is the **media-literacy findings** about how people actually judge
photos: they pick the **brighter** one, and — new this year — the one with **more in focus** (a sign of a *worse*
sensor).

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2019-12-17] **FINDING 1 (confirmed) — people pick the brighter photo.** "'Same as last year: when two photos
  sit next to each other on **compressed social media, sharpness/detail/texture/contrast matter far less** — when
  all else is equal, **people just pick the brighter photo.** The ones that brightened my face most tended to win.'"
  Reinforces the 2018 blind-test thesis (brighter wins ≠ brighter is better).
- [2019-12-17] **FINDING 2 (NEW) — when brightness is equal, people pick "more in focus."** "'People started voting
  for the photo with **more in focus** — but that's **backwards.** A soft, out-of-focus background is the more
  expensive, big-sensor look (why portrait mode exists to fake it). Huawei stuffs a **huge sensor**, so its
  background blurs even without portrait mode — yet **the masses picked the more-in-focus (smaller-sensor) photo as
  "better," even though it's technically worse.**'" A sharp new media-literacy insight.
- [2019-12-17] **The iPhone 11 Pro's upset — lost on white balance.** "'The fluke: the **iPhone 11 Pro lost round
  one because of white balance** — when the iPhone misses, more than ever this year it **goes cool/blue** — and the
  warmer, more accurate OnePlus won.'" A specific, dated camera-behavior note.
- [2019-12-17] **The finals were the same camera twice — decided by a reflection.** "'The final was **Note 10+ vs
  S10e — essentially the same camera.** The S10e photo was magenta-shifted at the bottom because the **pink phone
  was reflecting in the window** I shot through. Credit to the sharp-eyed voters who caught it.'"
- [2019-12-17] **THE META-LESSON — people make fascinating decisions in side-by-sides.** "'What we learn: (1)
  people like brighter photos; (2) with equal brightness, they prefer more-in-focus (a sign of a worse photo, but
  they don't care); (3) **people make fascinating decisions when presented with side-by-sides.**' (Caveat: Twitter
  is about to stop compressing JPEGs — the landscape may shift.)" His enduring "how audiences actually perceive
  quality" framework.

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "When all else is the same and you put them right next to each other, people are going to just pick the brighter
> photo. This has not changed."
> "The masses decided that they would select the photo with more in focus as better, even though technically
> speaking it's in theory worse, because it's a telltale sign of a smaller sensor."
