# Why Don't People Buy Sony Smartphones? (@mkbhd, 2020-10-05)

## Summary
A landmark market-analysis video built around the Sony Xperia 1 II — a genuinely great enthusiast phone (4K
display, alpha-camera tech, headphone jack, expandable storage). The question: why does Sony have ~0% smartphone
market share? Two answers: **enthusiasts are a tiny sliver of the market — to sell volume you must appeal to
everybody**, and **Sony's launch execution is terrible** (unveiled in May, in hand in October, successor already
out). He's **still rooting for Sony because competition makes all products better.**

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2020-10-05] **The phone is genuinely great — and full of unique enthusiast stuff.** "'Flagship basics, "which
  technically anybody can build," **plus a lot of unique, really useful stuff I haven't seen elsewhere**: a
  **6.5" 4K 21:9 display** (creator mode), a **tool-less microSD/SIM tray that keeps water resistance**, a
  **kept headphone jack** ("good guy Sony" — they sell great wireless buds and kept it anyway), alpha-line
  **autofocus + Photo Pro/Cinema Pro** manual control with focus racking. "Next-level dedication to
  enthusiasts."'"
- [2020-10-05] **ANSWER #1 — enthusiasts are a tiny market; to sell volume, appeal to everybody.** "'This
  partly answers the question: **enthusiasts make up a very small percentage of the market, especially in
  smartphones.** Enthusiast features sell things sometimes, but **for true high volume you need to appeal to
  everybody** — and this doesn't: no simple night mode like Pixel's Night Sight, only a 1080p30 selfie camera
  (which a growing mass cares about most), and **the people who'd use 4K manual control mostly already own a
  mirrorless camera.** A 4K screen almost nobody would even notice.'" A cornerstone market principle.
- [2020-10-05] **ANSWER #2 — nobody knows about them; launch execution is terrible.** "'The other reason:
  **people don't know about these.** It's crazy the company behind these cameras, the PlayStation, and the TVs
  **can't leverage anything to get above 0% share.** Like LG, Sony **can't properly launch a phone to the
  masses.** Apple/OnePlus/Samsung do it fast — event, pre-order a week later, in the streets a week after that,
  hype doesn't die. **This was unveiled in MAY and I just got it last week (October) — a long time for the hype
  to die,** and its successor (the Xperia 5 II) is already announced. "It doesn't look like a priority."'"
- [2020-10-05] **The reviewer's-eye frustration.** "'It's bad for reviewers too — you want the most updated
  info; **why review a phone whose sequel just came out and that people won't even buy?** A poorly thought-out or
  poorly executed launch just doesn't look good, period.' (Also: **Sony is terrible with names** — Xperia 1 II,
  Xperia 5 II.)'"
- [2020-10-05] **STILL ROOTING FOR SONY — competition makes everything better.** "'I'm **still rooting for
  Sony because I don't want to see this die** — **the more competition in any field, the better the products
  get.** That downward curve makes their mobile division look like life support, and we've seen what that looks
  like before. I don't want it to die, for both of us.'" His recurring pro-competition value (cf. LG).

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "Enthusiasts really only make up a very, very small percentage of the overall market of anything, but
> especially of smartphones... if you want that true high level volume of sales, you need to appeal to everybody."
> "There's one more reason that people don't buy these... and that's because they don't know about these."
> "The more competition in any field, the better the products get."
