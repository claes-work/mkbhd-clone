# Moto RAZR 2 Impressions: Nostalgia Reloaded? (@mkbhd, 2020-09-09)

## Summary
First look at the $1,399 second-gen Moto Razr ("Tech Timber," brought to you by the Waveform shirt). The durable
thesis: **the second generation is where the interesting stuff happens** — where feedback gets acted on — but
the Razr 2 is mostly a **minor doubling-down update** that reveals the thin-vertical-flip form factor **can't
yet fit flagship hardware.** The open question: does the **nostalgia still justify $1,400** now that the novelty
has worn off?

## Key claims (paraphrase, dated)
- [2020-09-09] **"The second generation is where the most interesting stuff happens."** "'First-gen stuff is
  cool but it's a lot of proof-of-concept, testing-the-waters. **The second generation is where the adjustments
  are made — where they heard our voices, know what tech and design to improve, and what to drop.**'" His
  recurring gen-1-vs-gen-2 lens (cf. the Surface Duo review).
- [2020-09-09] **The good little adjustments.** "'Motorola doubled down on almost everything: **tapered chin,
  fingerprint moved to the back Motorola-logo dimple** (a nice throwback), a more traditional bottom, three
  colors that run into the bezels so **you'll never not know which color you have.** A new 48 MP OIS main
  camera — an improvement, but still a single small module.'"
- [2020-09-09] **THE FORM-FACTOR LIMIT — cameras (and specs) are the ceiling.** "'I get the feeling **cameras
  are going to be the limitation of this form factor.** It's so thin you can't fit a flagship array — you
  literally can't fit the Note 20 Ultra's camera bump in the top of a Razr. And the **Snapdragon 765G, 2,800
  mAh battery — this form factor isn't ready for peak flagship hardware yet.** You can't fit a flagship battery,
  camera, or processor in here, at least not yet.'" A promotable form-factor-constraint framework.
- [2020-09-09] **The outside display is the Razr's real advantage — so double down on it.** "'The **outside
  screen is the Razr's biggest advantage over the Z Flip's tiny one** — new card UI, quick-launch apps, a full
  keyboard, even watching YouTube on it. Motorola knows it and lets you do anything you want with it. Plus the
  underrated Moto software (double-chop flashlight, double-twist camera).'"
- [2020-09-09] **THE NOSTALGIA-TAX QUESTION.** "'The first one sold on nostalgia — "I don't care how much it
  is, I gotta have it." **Now that it's evolving a little, is it still a phone you want for $1,400,** knowing
  it'll probably get better next generation? That's the harder question this time.'" A durable read on
  novelty-priced hardware.

## Notable verbatim quotes
> "The second generation is where the adjustments are made. It's where they heard our voices... and what to
> actually work on, and what to drop, what to forget about."
> "I kind of get the feeling that cameras are going to be the limitation of this form factor in general."
> "Now that it's starting to evolve a little bit, is this still gonna be a phone you want for 1,400 bucks?"
